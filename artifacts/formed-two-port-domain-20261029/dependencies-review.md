## A valid extension of the existing potential floor

Let h(s)=1-cos(s), with a fixed a in (0,pi/2), and define

    r_a(s) = h(s)-h(a)-sin(a)(s-a).

For -pi<=s<=a, sin(s)<=sin(a): it is nonpositive on [-pi,0] and
increases from zero to sin(a) on [0,a]. Therefore r_a'(s)<=0 there.
For a<=s<=pi-a, sin(s)>=sin(a), so r_a'(s)>=0. Since r_a(a)=0,

    h(s) >= h(a)+sin(a)(s-a),       -pi<=s<=pi-a.             (D1)

This proof uses a supporting affine function, not convexity of h on the
whole interval. The interval extends past pi/2; h is not convex everywhere
on it. For a in the stated open range equality in (D1) occurs only at s=a.
No scalar root or extremum has been evaluated to obtain these endpoints.

For a nine-edge cycle with consistent oriented gaps satisfying
sum_j g_j=9a and -pi<=g_j<=pi-a, summing (D1) cancels its affine terms:

    sum_j h(g_j) >= 9h(a).                                  (D2)

Apply (D2) to the two original cycles with a_D=4pi/9 and a_R=2pi/9.
Their fixed affine lifts retain sums 4pi and 2pi. The same potential floor
as M4 is consequently valid whenever all donor gaps belong to [-pi,5pi/9]
and all receiver gaps belong to [-pi,7pi/9]:

    U >= 9[1-cos(4pi/9)] + 9[1-cos(2pi/9)] = U_floor.        (D3)

The two contact contributions are nonnegative for every real contact gap;
no extra contact restriction is needed for this floor statement itself.
Symmetric restrictions |g_D|<=5pi/9 and |g_R|<=7pi/9 are also sufficient
subsets, but are not selected here as a new transient domain or policy.
The old source upper bound and E0=1/4 therefore survive on any actual-phase
domain contained in (D3), with the unchanged source and contact accounting.

These are pointwise statements. They establish neither forward invariance,
reference arrival, principal winding preservation outside an admitted branch,
nor positive curvature. The fixed affine cycle identities by themselves
do not imply a global potential floor: concentrating a 4pi ring increment
on one edge and setting the others to zero has that affine sum and zero
ring potential. Branch/domain information cannot be discarded.

## What could be reused beyond an edgewise acute domain

The broader mathematical condition is H_cos(g)=B^T diag(cos(g)) B>=0,
on the actual full relative phase space and every required segment. The repo
already establishes that some negative edge cosines can be compensated by
other graph paths: `SINE_PREPARATION_ROBUSTNESS.md` section 8 gives a triangle
and a segment collar; `SINE_COLLECTIVE_IDENTITY_TRANSITION.md` section 10
proves a full-network path comparison through nonacute edge crossings.
This is existing methodology, not a newly discovered generic theorem.

A concrete reusable sufficient test is as follows. For each possibly negative
edge e=(i,j), choose a path of positive edges connecting i and j. If those
positive edges have admitted weights c_p>0, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives

    (v_j-v_i)^2 <= (sum_{p in path(e)} 1/c_p)
                         * sum_{p in path(e)} c_p (Bv)_p^2.

Multiplying by an upper bound on the negative magnitude and summing accounts
for every reused positive edge. If the aggregate load on each positive edge
is at most one, its quadratic energy dominates all negative terms. Strictly
less than one on a connected positive graph gives relative coercivity.
This test retains all nodes, their actual paths and shared-edge loads. No
sign or load for the current formed-source transient is asserted here.

Combining a proved full-domain Hessian condition with (D3) would retain the
energy-informed argument on a wider convex phase domain, provided the
reference collar also keeps theta, y and their segments there. Convexity of
the domain is useful; convexity of each edge potential is not separately
required once the full Hessian condition is proved. A statewise PSD check
only along psi is insufficient for the y-to-psi comparison.

If only a positive growth bound is available, the error comparison acquires
an integrating factor and reference speed need no longer stay below F0.
Then M6--M9's numerical bounds must be rederived; retaining their old Q and
tail while changing only the Taylor kernel's growth input is invalid. The
existing global estimate in `SINE_FORM_PHASE_REDUCTION.md` already supplies
a different, potentially much less sharp finite comparison. Its existence

reference without changing M1--M9 if the exact reference remains sufficiently
