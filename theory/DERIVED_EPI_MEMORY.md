## 1. What is derived, and from what

The microscopic dynamics is the existing pure-EPI channel of the TNFR nodal
equation. It contains no imposed delay or additional memory parameter:

$$
\dot x_i=\nu_i\Delta\mathrm{NFR}_{\mathrm{epi},i},\qquad
\Delta\mathrm{NFR}_{\mathrm{epi},i}
=\frac{\sum_j W_{ij}x_j}{d_i}-x_i,
\qquad d_i=\sum_jW_{ij}.
$$

Here $x_i$ is the real scalar EPI coordinate. Assume finite fixed effective
conductance $W=W^\top\geq0$, positive row strengths $d_i>0$, and fixed finite
positive capacities $\nu_i$. These are the hypotheses of this restricted
channel, not conclusions about all TNFR executions. Set

$$
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_i),\quad B=D-W,\quad
H=\operatorname{diag}(d_i/\nu_i),\quad
A=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_i)(I-D^{-1}W)=H^{-1}B.
$$

The nodal law is $\dot x=-Ax$. In particular,
$A^\top H=HA=B$, and $B$ is positive semidefinite. Algebra in this note is over
the exact real coefficients of the declared model. Floating-point construction
and evaluation of the associated matrices have separate numerical residuals.

An observer retains the weighted EPI means of a partition and discards the
remaining within-block coordinates. The partition specifies which TNFR nodes
are observed together; it is not an additional evolution law or a proof that
nature selects those particular blocks. Eliminating the discarded coordinates
derives the memory below from the same nodal law. It does not add a fundamental
memory mechanism to that law.

## 2. The reversible observation geometry

Let $P\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}$ lift one macro value to every node of its
nonempty partition block: $P_{i\alpha}=1$ when node $i$ belongs to block
$\alpha$, and zero otherwise. Define

$$
\bar H=P^\top HP,\qquad
R=\bar H^{-1}P^\top H,\qquad Q=I-PR.
$$

Thus $R$ takes the $H$-weighted block means, $RP=I$, $Q^2=Q$, $RQ=0$, $QP=0$,
and $Q^\top H=HQ$. The macro and hidden coordinates are

$$
y=Rx,\qquad z=Qx,\qquad x=Py+z.
$$

The hidden subspace is $\mathcal Z=\ker R=\operatorname{im}Q$. It is the
$H$-orthogonal complement of $\operatorname{im}P$. The instantaneous macro
generator is fixed by the same graph and capacities:

$$
\bar A=RAP=\bar H^{-1}P^\top BP.
$$

This is the existing reversible quotient construction. Its off-diagonal macro
conductances aggregate the microscopic conductances between blocks. When a
macro node has positive external strength $\bar d_\alpha$, its corresponding
capacity is $\bar\nu_\alpha=\bar d_\alpha/\bar H_{\alpha\alpha}$; it is not a
separately fitted parameter. The shared implementation additionally requires a
strict reduction to at least two blocks and connected macro support.

## 3. Exact elimination, including the initial hidden state

Applying $R$ and $Q$ to the nodal equation gives

$$
\dot y=-\bar A y-RAz,\qquad
\dot z=-QAPy-QAQz.
$$

Let $T=QAQ$ and $C=QAP$. The subspace $\mathcal Z$ is invariant under $T$.
Since $z_0=Qx_0$ and $Cy(s)$ lie in that subspace, variation of constants gives

$$
z(t)=e^{-Tt}Qx_0-\int_0^t e^{-T(t-s)}Cy(s)\,ds.
$$

Substitution into the first equation proves the exact Volterra equation

$$
\boxed{
\dot y(t)=-\bar A y(t)
+\int_0^t K(t-s)y(s)\,ds+f(t)
}
$$

with

$$
\boxed{K(t)=RAe^{-Tt}C},\qquad
\boxed{f(t)=-RAe^{-Tt}Qx_0}.
$$

The plus sign on the convolution follows from substituting the negative hidden
forcing into $-RAz$. The source $f$ is the contribution of the initial hidden
state, not an external drive or random noise. Omitting it generally changes the
solution. Conversely, $Qx_0=0$ removes this source but does not generally remove
the convolution generated by later within-block evolution.

Writing $RAQ$ instead of $RA$ is equivalent in these two expressions: the inputs
to the exponential are hidden and the exponential preserves $\mathcal Z$.
Without that subspace fact, dropping projection factors would be unjustified.

This equation reproduces $y(t)=Re^{-At}x_0$ for all $t\geq0$ and every initial
state under the declared fixed-coefficient hypotheses. The converse follows by
recovering $z$ from its formula: the reconstructed $x=Py+z$ solves the same
linear initial-value problem. No empirical fitting or extra constitutive law
enters this equivalence.

## 4. Kernel positivity and the exact closure criterion

The hidden generator is self-adjoint and nonnegative in the $H$ metric:

$$
T^\top H=HT,\qquad
v^\top HTv=(Qv)^\top B(Qv)\geq0.
$$

Consequently $H^{1/2}TH^{-1/2}$ is symmetric positive semidefinite, and its
exponential is symmetric positive definite at each finite $t\geq0$. Using
$Q^\top H=HQ$ and $A^\top H=HA$ gives

$$
\boxed{\bar H K(t)=C^\top H e^{-Tt}C}.
$$

Indeed, $C^\top H=P^\top HAQ$ and $Qe^{-Tt}C=e^{-Tt}C$. For every macro vector
$v$, this identity yields

$$
v^\top\bar H K(t)v
=(H^{1/2}Cv)^\top
 e^{-H^{1/2}TH^{-1/2}t}(H^{1/2}Cv)\geq0.
$$

Thus $K(t)$ is self-adjoint and positive semidefinite in the macro metric. This
does not assert that all its individual entries are nonnegative. At zero lag,
$\bar H K(0)=C^\top HC$, so positive definiteness of $H$ proves

$$
\begin{aligned}
K(0)=0
&\iff QAP=0\\
&\iff AP=P\bar A\\
&\iff RA=\bar A R.
\end{aligned}
$$

For the final equivalence, $QAP=0$ says that the lifted block-constant subspace
is invariant under $A$. Self-adjointness in $H$ makes its orthogonal complement
invariant too, hence $RAQ=0$ and $RA=\bar A R$. Conversely, $RAQ=0$ has weighted
adjoint $QAP=0$. Therefore the kernel vanishes identically exactly when every
microstate admits the autonomous macro law $\dot y=-\bar A y$. In that case the
initial hidden source also vanishes for every $x_0$.

Zero hidden source for one state or agreement of one initial derivative is a
strictly weaker condition. A numerical residual below a tolerance is likewise
not an exact zero-kernel proof.

If microscopic positive-conductance support is connected, $B$ has only the
constant null direction. Because $\mathcal Z$ is orthogonal to that direction,
$T$ restricted to $\mathcal Z$ is positive definite. The ambient matrix $T$
still has the lifted macro subspace in its kernel. Connected macro support
alone does not establish connected microscopic support, so it cannot justify
the stronger hidden-space assertion for every accepted graph.

The positive convolution sign does not create energy. The original nodal
balance remains

$$
\frac12 x^\top Hx
=\frac12 y^\top\bar Hy+\frac12 z^\top Hz,\qquad
\frac{d}{dt}\frac12 x^\top Hx=-x^\top Bx\leq0.
$$

The macro equation retains the influence of hidden coordinates of this same
dissipative system. It does not establish a new source of sustained vibration.

## 5. Exact zero-memory control: P4

Take the unweighted path $0-1-2-3$, unit capacities and partition
$((0,3),(1,2))$. Here

$$
a=(x_0+x_3)/2,\qquad b=(x_1+x_2)/2,\qquad
\bar H=\operatorname{diag}(2,4).
$$

For every microscopic initial state, direct application of the nodal equation
gives

$$
\dot a=-a+b,\qquad \dot b=(a-b)/2,
\qquad
\bar A=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Thus $QAP=0$, $K(t)=0$, and $f(t)=0$. Reflection-antisymmetric hidden states
can evolve, but they cannot affect these macro observations. Grouping nodes
does not universally produce nonzero memory.

## 6. Exact nonzero-memory witness: P5

Take the unweighted path $0-1-2-3-4$, unit capacities and partition
$((0,4),(1,2,3))$. The reversible macro coordinates and one relevant hidden
coordinate are

$$
a=(x_0+x_4)/2,\quad b=(x_1+x_2+x_3)/3,\quad
u=x_2-(x_1+x_3)/2.
$$

The full hidden subspace has dimension three, but its other two coordinates
do not couple to these macro observations. No reflection-symmetry assumption
on the initial state is required. Direct substitution proves

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot a&=-a+b-u/3,\\
\dot b&=(a-b)/3+u/9,\\
\dot u&=(b-a)/2-5u/3.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
\bar H=\operatorname{diag}(2,6),\qquad
\bar A=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1/3&1/3\end{pmatrix},
$$

$$
K(t)=e^{-5t/3}
\begin{pmatrix}1/6&-1/6\\-1/18&1/18\end{pmatrix},\qquad
f(t)=u_0e^{-5t/3}\begin{pmatrix}-1/3\\1/9\end{pmatrix}.
$$

In particular,

$$
\bar H K(t)=\frac{e^{-5t/3}}3
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
$$

Three controls distinguish the claims:

1. **Same macrostate, different initial rates.** The nonnegative integer states
   $x^{(A)}=(0,1,1,1,0)$ and $x^{(B)}=(0,0,3,0,0)$ both have $(a,b)=(0,1)$.
   Their hidden coordinates are $u_A=0$ and $u_B=3$, and their initial macro
   rates are $(1,-1/3)$ and $(0,0)$. No autonomous first-order right-hand side
   depending only on $(a,b)$ can represent both.
2. **Zero source, nonzero generated memory.** For $x_0=P(1,0)$, $u_0=0$ and
   $f$ vanishes. Nevertheless $\dot u(0)=-1/2$. The second derivative differs
   from the source-free autonomous quotient prediction by
   $K(0)y_0=(1/6,-1/18)^\top$. Thus removing the convolution is already wrong
   at second order in time.
3. **Relaxation, not an emergent harmonic wave.** The contrast $d=a-b$ satisfies
   $\ddot d+3\dot d+2d=0$. Its two decay rates are 1 and 2. The added time
   derivative is a reformulation of the eliminated hidden coordinate, not a
   separately postulated inertia or proof of sustained oscillation.

All these coefficients follow from the path, the partition and the existing
unit-capacity nodal law. For a common fixed capacity $\nu>0$, the kernel is
$\nu^2 e^{-5\nu t/3}K_0$, with $K_0$ the displayed constant matrix, and the
source is $\nu u_0e^{-5\nu t/3}(-1/3,1/9)^\top$. The contrast decay rates are
$\nu$ and $2\nu$; no new relaxation parameter is fitted.

## 8. Finite-history approximation on the derived P5 model

The exact P5 kernel in section 6 decays with a rate already fixed by the nodal
law. This permits a separate approximation question: how much older observed
history can be omitted, and how much can that omission change the resulting
macro trajectory? A bound on the omitted forcing alone does not answer the
second question, because the approximate trajectory subsequently feeds back
through its retained history. The following theorem controls that feedback.

### 8.1 Full and finite-history equations

Keep the unweighted P5 graph, fixed partition and common positive capacity
$\nu$ of section 6. Define the macro contrast and conserved mean by

$$
d=a-b,\qquad m=(a+3b)/4,
$$

and set

$$
\alpha=\frac{4\nu}{3},\qquad
\beta=\frac{5\nu}{3},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{2\nu^2}{9},\qquad
k(t)=\gamma e^{-\beta t},\qquad
g(t)=-\frac{4\nu u_0}{9}e^{-\beta t}.
$$

Here $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ abbreviate derived coefficients; they are
not configurable laws or the REMESH mixing coefficient. Taking the contrast
of the exact macro equation gives

$$
\dot d(t)=-\alpha d(t)+g(t)+\int_0^t k(t-s)d(s)\,ds.
$$

For a selected retention window $\ell\geq0$, define $d_\ell$ by the same
initial contrast and the same initial-hidden-state source:

$$
\boxed{
\dot d_\ell(t)=-\alpha d_\ell(t)+g(t)
+\int_{\max(0,t-\ell)}^t k(t-s)d_\ell(s)\,ds,
\qquad d_\ell(0)=d_0.
}
$$

Thus only the driven memory integral is shortened. The source $g$ is retained
at every time; discarding it would define a different approximation requiring
an additional error term. The window $\ell$ is an approximation resource
chosen against an error target, not a new physical timescale. For each finite
window the continuous Volterra equation has a unique continuous trajectory
with the displayed derivative. No timestep or live-graph update is needed to
define it.

Both macro equations conserve the same $m$: the row vector $(1,3)$ annihilates
the instantaneous generator, memory kernel and source. Consequently

$$
y_\ell(t)-y(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}3/4\\-1/4\end{pmatrix}
\bigl(d_\ell(t)-d(t)\bigr),\qquad
\|y_\ell(t)-y(t)\|_\infty=\frac34|d_\ell(t)-d(t)|.
$$

### 8.2 Exact reference and omitted forcing

The full contrast has the closed form

$$
d(t)=c_1e^{-\nu t}+c_2e^{-2\nu t},\qquad
c_1=\frac{2d_0}{3}-\frac{4u_0}{9},\quad
c_2=\frac{d_0}{3}+\frac{4u_0}{9}.
$$

Its exact amplitude $D=\sup_{t\geq0}|d(t)|$ can be calculated without sampling.
Under $z=e^{-\nu t}$, the contrast is $c_1z+c_2z^2$ on the closed interval
$[0,1]$. Therefore $D$ is the maximum of $|d_0|$ and, only when $c_2\ne0$ and
$z_*=-c_1/(2c_2)\in(0,1)$, the additional candidate
$|-c_1^2/(4c_2)|$. The omitted endpoint $z=0$ has value zero. The simpler
$D\leq |c_1|+|c_2|$ supplies a conservative alternative. On a finite horizon
$[0,T]$, the corresponding $D_T$ follows from the endpoints and any interior
vertex of the same quadratic on $[e^{-\nu T},1]$.

Write $\delta=e^{-\beta\ell}$ and $q=t-\ell$. For $t\leq\ell$, the omitted
forcing $r_\ell(t)$ is zero. For $t>\ell$, its value on the full reference is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_\ell(t)
&=\int_0^{t-\ell} k(t-s)d(s)\,ds\\
&=\delta\left[
\frac{\nu c_1}{3}\bigl(e^{-\nu q}-e^{-5\nu q/3}\bigr)
+\frac{2\nu c_2}{3}\bigl(e^{-5\nu q/3}-e^{-2\nu q}\bigr)
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Direct integration also gives the sign-independent bound

$$
|r_\ell(t)|\leq
\frac{2\nu}{15}D\delta\bigl(1-e^{-\beta q}\bigr)
\leq\frac{2\nu}{15}D\delta.
$$

These are statements about a forcing evaluated on the exact full trajectory.
The error between the two actual solutions requires the next step.

### 8.3 Positive resolvent and the uniform trajectory bound

Let $k_\ell(t)=k(t)\mathbf1_{0\leq t\leq\ell}$, with causal convolution on
$[0,\infty)$. The value at the window endpoint does not change its integral.
Subtracting the two equations, $e=d_\ell-d$ satisfies

$$
\dot e=-\alpha e+k_\ell*e-r_\ell,\qquad e(0)=0.
$$

Its impulse resolvent $h_\ell$ is nonnegative. To see this, set
$p(t)=e^{-\alpha t}$ and $j_\ell=p*k_\ell$. Variation of constants gives the
nonnegative series

$$
h_\ell=p+\sum_{n=1}^\infty j_\ell^{*n}*p.
$$

The series converges in $L^1$ because

$$
\|j_\ell\|_1=
\frac{\|k_\ell\|_1}{\alpha}<1,
\qquad
\|k_\ell\|_1=\frac{2\nu}{15}(1-\delta).
$$

Nonnegativity permits integration term by term, yielding

$$
\|h_\ell\|_1
=\frac1{\alpha-\|k_\ell\|_1}
=\frac{15}{2\nu(9+\delta)}.
$$

As $e=-h_\ell*r_\ell$, the convolution inequality now proves

$$
\boxed{
\sup_{t\geq0}|d_\ell(t)-d(t)|
\leq\frac{D\delta}{9+\delta},\qquad
\sup_{t\geq0}\|y_\ell(t)-y(t)\|_\infty
\leq\frac{3D\delta}{4(9+\delta)}.
}
$$

This is a bound on the solution of the truncated equation, including its
feedback through the retained history. It assumes no unproved bound on that
approximate solution. For a finite horizon, $D_T$ can replace $D$ and the
suprema can be restricted to $[0,T]$.

### 8.4 Causal and decaying error bounds

The full-kernel resolvent is explicit:

$$
h(t)=\frac23e^{-\nu t}+\frac13e^{-2\nu t}.
$$

Its Laplace transform is
$(p+\beta)/((p+\nu)(p+2\nu))$, as follows directly from
$p+\alpha-\gamma/(p+\beta)$. Since $0\leq k_\ell\leq k$, the positive
resolvent series proves $0\leq h_\ell\leq h$. For
$q=(t-\ell)_+$, integration gives

$$
\int_0^q h(q-v)\bigl(1-e^{-\beta v}\bigr)\,dv
=\frac{5}{6\nu}\bigl(1-e^{-\nu q}\bigr)^2.
$$

Combining this identity with the forcing estimate and the uniform bound gives

$$
\boxed{
|d_\ell(t)-d(t)|\leq
D\delta\min\left\{
\frac1{9+\delta},\;
\frac{(1-e^{-\nu q})^2}{9}
\right\}.
}
$$

The macro infinity-norm bound is again three quarters of this expression.
The error vanishes through time $\ell$ and its bound starts quadratically
after that time. Thus $\ell\geq T$ gives exact agreement over $[0,T]$.

A bound that also resolves late-time relaxation follows by convolving the
absolute values of the two explicit forcing terms with $h$. With
$s=\nu(t-\ell)_+$, both bracketed expressions below are nonnegative, and

$$
|d_\ell(t)-d(t)|\leq\frac{2\delta}{9}\left[
|c_1|e^{-s}(s-1+e^{-s})
+|c_2|\bigl(e^{-s}-(1+s)e^{-2s}\bigr)
\right].
$$

This expression tends to zero as $O((1+s)e^{-s})$. The truncated solution
therefore converges to the same conserved macro mean, although its transient
trajectory generally differs. This conclusion concerns this derived scalar
P5 reduction with its retained source, not arbitrary memory truncations.

### 8.5 Window choice, limiting controls and scope

For a prescribed positive contrast-error target $\varepsilon$, a sufficient
uniform window is

$$
\ell_{\mathrm{req}}=
\begin{cases}
0,&\varepsilon\geq D/10,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{3}{5\nu}\log\dfrac{D-\varepsilon}{9\varepsilon},
&0<\varepsilon<D/10.
\end{cases}
$$

This is obtained by solving $D\delta/(9+\delta)\leq\varepsilon$; it is a
sufficient budget, not a proof of a smallest possible window. For a macro
infinity-norm target $\varepsilon_y$, substitute $4\varepsilon_y/3$ for
$\varepsilon$. On a finite horizon, retaining
$\min(T,\ell_{\mathrm{req}})$ suffices, since the $T$ branch is exact there.
If $D=0$, both $d_0$ and $u_0$ vanish, and every window is exact for these
macro observations. A zero error target for a nonzero trajectory is not
covered by the positive-target logarithmic formula.

At the other boundary, $\ell=0$ removes the convolution but keeps the initial
hidden source. Its solution is

$$
d_{\ell=0}(t)=
\left(d_0-\frac{4u_0}{3}\right)e^{-4\nu t/3}
+\frac{4u_0}{3}e^{-5\nu t/3}.
$$

This supplies an independent approximation control. In particular, it must
not be confused with the source-free instantaneous quotient, which would
give only $d_0e^{-4\nu t/3}$. Changing the common capacity replaces time by
$\nu t$ and the retention window by $\nu\ell$ throughout: the retention
budget scales as $1/\nu$, without an independently selected physical rate.

The theorem is over exact real model coefficients and applies to every
finite real initial P5 state, including states without reflection symmetry.
Binary64 evaluation of its exponentials or bounds is not automatically a
certified enclosure. In particular, exponential underflow to a displayed
zero cannot prove exact zero omitted history. Exact zero for $t\leq\ell$
instead follows from the integration domain itself. Any executable rational
reference must separately enclose the exponential evaluations and its
finite-history solution.

This approximation result neither evolves a live graph nor certifies a
canonical operator sequence, REMESH history, numerical solver timestep,
changing topology or empirical correspondence. Its memory and decay rates
remain consequences of the fixed P5 nodal dynamics already derived above.

### 8.6 Executable rational finite-history reference

[`bound_p5_memory_truncation`](../src/tnfr/physics/p5_memory_truncation.py)
implements this fixed P5 reference from five explicit initial coordinates,
the common capacity, a window and observation times. It accepts integers and
Fractions exactly; other real inputs retain the shared materialized binary64
rational value. It does not infer a P5 model from an arbitrary live graph.

The finite-history solution itself has an exact finite formula. Set
`sigma=nu*t`, `lambda=nu*ell`. Differentiating the derived convolution equation
and retaining its source gives

$$
d_\lambda''+3d_\lambda'+2d_\lambda
=-\frac29e^{-5\lambda/3}
d_\lambda(\sigma-\lambda)\mathbf1_{\sigma>\lambda},
\qquad d_\lambda'(0)=-4d_0/3-4u_0/9,
$$

where primes now use scaled time. The source remains in the initial
derivative even though it cancels on differentiating the equation. This
second derivative is a reformulation of projected diffusion, without an
independently added inertia. The value of the right-hand side at its single
switching time does not change the continuous solution.

For `lambda>0`, define polynomials for positive integers `p,q`:

$$
P_{pq}(r)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{p-k}
{p+q-k-1\choose p-k}\frac{r^{k-1}}{(k-1)!},\qquad
Q_{pq}(r)=\sum_{k=1}^{q}(-1)^p
{p+q-k-1\choose q-k}\frac{r^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}.
$$

Partial fractions show that
`P_pq(r)*exp(-r)+Q_pq(r)*exp(-2r)` is the inverse Laplace transform of
`1/((s+1)^p*(s+2)^q)`. The boundary cases are `P_10=1,Q_10=0` and
`P_01=0,Q_01=1`. Expanding the delayed transfer equation gives

$$
d_\lambda(\sigma)=
\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\sigma/\lambda\rfloor}
\left(-\frac29\right)^j
\left[
 (c_1P_{j+1,j}(r_j)+c_2P_{j,j+1}(r_j))
 e^{-(\sigma+2j\lambda/3)}
+(c_1Q_{j+1,j}(r_j)+c_2Q_{j,j+1}(r_j))
 e^{-(2\sigma-j\lambda/3)}
\right],\quad r_j=\sigma-j\lambda.
$$

Equivalently, each term is a delayed convolution power of the second-order
resolvent with the full reference. Terms with `j>=1,r_j=0` vanish exactly.
The implementation handles the zero-window formula in section 8.5 separately
and uses the full reference unchanged when `t<=ell`.

Every coefficient and exponential argument in this formula is rational for
rational inputs. The implementation reuses the rational negative-exponential
enclosure from
[reversible_eigenmode_reference.py](../src/tnfr/physics/reversible_eigenmode_reference.py)
and its shared Taylor bound in [_exact_time.py](../src/tnfr/_exact_time.py).
Signed interval sums preserve rigorous endpoints and exact cancellation of
equal exponents. Unlike the approximate matrix-exponential observer in section
7, these results explicitly enclose evaluation uncertainty. No integration
timestep or sampled-history interpolation error occurs in this formula.

The output keeps three quantities separate: the omitted reference forcing,
the error between full and truncated solutions, and the width of the numerical
enclosure. The theorem's macro error bound is rounded upward with rational
exponential bounds; the raw solution-error enclosure can be wider because
interval evaluation is conservative. Integer/Fraction interval endpoints
remain authoritative. The benchmark uses directed outward rounding for any
displayed binary64 error bounds, rather than ordinary float casts.

Resource admission currently allows at most 32 nonzero delayed corrections per
sample and `nu*t<=2048`, which keeps all required exponential arguments within
the existing limit of 4096. These are workload and representation limits;
neither is a physical scale or a bound on rational bit size. The theorem itself
has no such finite-time cutoff. In particular, an extremely large window is
handled by the exact `t<=ell` branch without exponentiating the window.

[Tests](../tests/physics/test_p5_memory_truncation.py) compare the enclosures with
independent high-precision full, zero-window and first-delay formulas, and
compare later windows with a separate block-matrix method of steps. They also
check the source, amplitude maximum, bounds, capacity scaling and resource
boundaries. The existing
[benchmark](../benchmarks/derived_epi_memory.py) now records three P5 initial
states at windows `0,1/4,1/2,2` and times `0,1/8,1/2,1,2`. Its separate B2.b
manifest labels the rational reference **DERIVED**, preserves exact endpoints
and records the working-source digest. Physical correspondence remains
untested.

## 9. Nested nodal closure and the REMESH comparison

The derived history integral and the configured REMESH echo both involve past
structure, but that description alone establishes no dynamical equivalence.
The following comparison retains the same fixed unweighted P5, common positive
capacity `nu`, and real scalar EPI channel. It separates a failure of direct
substitution from an exact way to compose the two declared dynamics.

### 9.1 A causal-history obstruction to direct substitution

Write the microscopic generator as `A5=nu*L_rw`. The reflection-even vector

$$
v=(1,0,-1,0,1)^\top,\qquad A_5v=\nu v
$$

gives the exact nodal trajectory
`x(t)=c0*1+epsilon*exp(-nu*t)*v`. Let `h>0` be an observation interval and take
the current state at zero together with its actual nodal past at
`-tau_local*h` and `-tau_global*h`. For positive integer delays and
`0<alpha<=1`, uniform unclipped REMESH uses

$$
\beta=(1-\alpha)^2,\quad \gamma=\alpha(1-\alpha),\quad
\delta=\alpha,\qquad \beta+\gamma+\delta=1.
$$

Its output is `c0*1+epsilon*r*v`, where

$$
r=\beta+\gamma e^{\nu\tau_{\rm local}h}
       +\delta e^{\nu\tau_{\rm global}h}>1.
$$

The exact forward nodal state at time `h` instead has amplitude
`exp(-nu*h)<1`. Thus no coefficient in this class makes the complete REMESH
map a forward nodal step on this same causal history. The original two-group
projection also sees `v`, so projection does not remove the contradiction.
Equivalently, no real decay factor `z` in `(0,1)` satisfies

$$
z=\beta+\gamma z^{-\tau_{\rm local}}
       +\delta z^{-\tau_{\rm global}}.
$$

This is not an argument based on fabricated constant past data. For any
finite set of these samples, a finite baseline and amplitude can keep every
coordinate nonnegative and inside one interval where clipping is inactive.
Neither the observation interval nor that preparation is a new physical law.

An exact dyadic control uses `nu=1`, `h=log(2)`, `c0=4`, `epsilon=1`,
`alpha=1/2`, and delays `1,2`. Newest first, its three rows are
`4*1+v`, `4*1+2*v`, `4*1+4*v`; every value lies in `[0,8]`. REMESH gives
`r=11/4`, whereas forward diffusion gives `1/2`. The current microscopic
disagreement energy rises from `2` to `121/8`. This does not contradict the
existing augmented-history theorem: with stationary weights `(4/9,1/3,2/9)`,
that energy decreases from `32/3` to `55/6`, an exact drop of `3/2`.

The obstruction concerns direct substitution of the complete echo map for a
forward nodal or projected evolution step. It does not rule out every possible
derived embedding, discretization of the memory term, or larger drift-plus-echo
construction. Such a construction would need its own exact observation map,
history initialization and dynamical correspondence; none follows from this
comparison, and fitting a coefficient to one trajectory would not prove it.

### 9.2 The memory is removed by a genuine nested nodal quotient

Retain the reflection-orbit coordinates

$$
q=(a,p,c)^\top,\qquad
a=(x_0+x_4)/2,\quad p=(x_1+x_3)/2,\quad c=x_2.
$$

The associated projection and lift are

$$
R_3=\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&0&0&0&1/2\\
0&1/2&0&1/2&0\\
0&0&1&0&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\\0&1&0\\1&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This is the existing reversible quotient construction for the equitable
partition `((0,4),(1,3),(2,))`. The quotient is a three-node path with
conductance `2` on each edge, strengths `(2,4,2)` and capacity `nu` at every
node. Direct application of the nodal law gives

$$
A_3=\nu\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&0\\-1/2&1&-1/2\\0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\dot q=-A_3q,\qquad
R_3J=I,\quad R_3A_5=A_3R_3,\quad A_5J=JA_3.
$$

These identities hold for every microscopic initial state; reflection symmetry
of that state is unnecessary. The original two-group observer is the further
weighted merge

$$
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&2/3&1/3\end{pmatrix}q,\qquad
b=(2p+c)/3,\quad u=c-p.
$$

Its projection is exactly this merge composed with `R3`. The first reduction
closes without memory. The second merges two quotient nodes with distinct
neighbor profiles, discards their contrast `u`, and produces the kernel of
section 6. Thus the memory-producing step is identified inside the same
hierarchy of TNFR node observations.

The chart `(a,b,u)` is related to genuine quotient EPI values by
`q=(a,b-u/3,b+2*u/3)`. Its generator is

$$
A_{abu}=\nu\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&1/3\\-1/3&1/3&-1/9\\1/2&-1/2&5/3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This is a coordinate change, not a proposal to treat `u` as a new TNFR node.
In particular, its consensus direction is `(1,1,0)`, not `(1,1,1)`.

### 9.3 Minimality of the retained linear state

Let `R2` denote the original two-group projection. Any autonomous linear state
containing both its outputs for every microscopic initial state must have a
row space containing `R2` and invariant under right multiplication by `A5`.
It must therefore contain the two rows of `R2` and the first row of `R2*A5`.
Their minor in columns `0,1,2` is

$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&0&0\\0&1/3&1/3\\\nu/2&-\nu/2&0
\end{pmatrix}=\frac{\nu}{12}>0.
$$

At least three independent coordinates are required. The invariant
three-coordinate quotient above attains that bound. This proves minimality
within autonomous linear realizations containing `(a,b)`; it does not prove
full microscopic observability, nonlinear state completeness, or minimality
of every possible history representation.

### 9.4 One metric and an exact visible/discarded energy split

The metrics are inherited from the same nodal capacity and conductance:

$$
H_5=\frac1\nu\operatorname{diag}(1,2,2,2,1),\qquad
H_3=J^\top H_5J=\frac1\nu\operatorname{diag}(2,4,2).
$$

Set `rho=(x0-x4)/2`, `sigma=(x1-x3)/2`, and
`w=x-J*q=(rho,sigma,0,-sigma,-rho)`. The lift and discarded component are
`H5`-orthogonal. The latter has zero weighted mean, while both `x` and `q`
have mean `m=(a+2*p+c)/4=(a+3*b)/4`. Consequently the centered disagreement
energy `E_H(x)=1/2*(x-m*1)^T*H*(x-m*1)` satisfies

$$
E_{H_5}(x)=E_{H_3}(q)+\frac{\rho^2+2\sigma^2}{\nu},\qquad
E_{H_3}(q)=\frac{3(a-b)^2}{4\nu}+\frac{2u^2}{3\nu}.
$$

In the `(a,b,u)` chart the induced diagonal metric is
`diag(2,6,4/3)/nu`, but centering must remove the direction `(1,1,0)`.
Applying an all-ones-centered node-energy routine directly to that chart would
change the meaning of consensus. Existing REMESH certificates can instead
read the genuine three-node `q` and `H3` without changing their semantics.

The reduced nodal balance is

$$
\frac{d}{dt}E_{H_3}(q)
=-2(a-p)^2-2(p-c)^2\leq0.
$$

The two-coordinate contribution alone need not decrease. For example,
`x0=(2,1,4,1,2)` has `a=b=2,u=3`: its visible contrast starts at zero and then
becomes `(4/3)*(exp(-2*nu*t)-exp(-nu*t))`, through the hidden source. The full
three-coordinate energy still decreases. Conversely, quotient consensus can
hide nonzero microscopic disagreement in `w`.

The distinction also affects rates. Quotient diffusion has eigenvalues
`0,nu,2*nu` and centered energy gain `exp(-2*nu*h)` over an interval `h`.
The discarded coordinates satisfy

$$
\begin{pmatrix}\dot\rho\\\dot\sigma\end{pmatrix}
=-\nu\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1/2&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\\sigma\end{pmatrix},
$$

with rates `nu*(1-1/sqrt(2))` and `nu*(1+1/sqrt(2))`. Therefore the faster
quotient gain cannot be promoted to all microscopic disagreement. Both rates
and the energy partition follow from the fixed P5 nodal generator.

### 9.5 Exact composition with a declared temporal echo

Uniform scalar REMESH mixing commutes with `R3`:

$$
R_3(\beta x_0+\gamma x_l+\delta x_g)
=\beta R_3x_0+\gamma R_3x_l+\delta R_3x_g.
$$

History shifts likewise commute with applying `R3` to every retained row.
The generator identities imply the same transport for exact diffusion
segments and for a declared Euler map `I-h*A5`. Hence any finite composition
of those fixed-support linear operations closes on `3*(m_delay+1)` state
coordinates when `m_delay` is the largest active delay and corresponding
fine/coarse history rows are supplied. This is a sufficient finite state
dimension, not a new minimality theorem for temporal companions. On each
diffusion segment, eliminating `u` recovers the derived memory with that
segment's actual initial hidden coordinate.

Adding a REMESH operation changes the trajectory; commutation proves that
its effect can be represented by the same quotient, not that it was already
present in the eliminated continuous dynamics. These claims require uniform
mixing and matching delays across nodes. General node-dependent coefficients,
clipping, changing support/capacity or other channels are outside this proof.

For fixed REMESH coefficients and delays, let `pi` be the existing companion's
stationary temporal weights. Summing the spatial identity in section 9.4
over retained rows gives the exact augmented-history split

$$
V_5=V_3+\sum_j\pi_j\frac{\rho_j^2+2\sigma_j^2}{\nu}.
$$

The before/after values and Jensen drops therefore split under one exact
REMESH transition. This reuses the
[uniform history theorem](../src/tnfr/physics/remesh_history_stability.py).
The [schedule policy theorem](../src/tnfr/physics/remesh_schedule_policy_stability.py)
can separately use the quotient diffusion gain when its hypotheses and
operation order are satisfied: it contracts a newly mixed head. It does not
automatically apply that gain to a diffusion-before-mixing cycle or to the
discarded microscopic modes. Neither theorem controls spatially uniform
temporal means merely from a spatial-disagreement bound.

### 9.6 Implementation, finite runtime defects and evidence boundary

Integers and Fractions remain exact; other accepted real inputs use the

[Exact tests](../tests/physics/test_p5_reduction.py) compare the quotient with

accounted for by the projected signed binary64 rounding defects. Exact-real
intertwining therefore does not promise bitwise runtime equality.

Clipping supplies an independent obstruction. At `alpha=1`, let an older
history row have endpoint values `(4,-2)` and zero elsewhere, with hard target
bounds `[-1,1]`. Clipping the microscopic endpoints gives mean zero; projecting
first gives the endpoint mean one, which the quotient clip leaves unchanged.
The first-orbit defect is `-1`, with zero rounding defect. The selected older
row is deliberately outside the target interval. This example does not
contradict the fact that an exact convex mixture of inputs all inside one
common hard interval remains inside it.

The causal decay fixture is also exercised by the production kernel with
inactive `[0,8]` clipping. Its input values are analytically compatible with
one exact nodal past, but direct caller-supplied history is not a sealed
executor-owned causal-history certificate. These finite production checks
establish neither repeated complete-runtime stability, solver convergence,
generic grammar/event admission nor future binary64 commutation.
