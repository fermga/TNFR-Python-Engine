    Integrated sensor response during prepared-pattern recovery¶

    Declared finite recovery question¶

    The loaded-window result resolves matrix response but proves overlap with a matched phase-blind reference during its short pulse. This candidate keeps that complete loaded law, pulse and sensor, and changes the observation to a signed integral covering recovery. The research program owns admission. The form/phase memory identities motivate retaining the endpoint information exactly; their decay coefficients are not transferred to the loaded model.

    Let L be the unit C5 Laplacian, v=(1,-1,0,0,0), W_r=I+r*L^2, gamma=1/(1023*pi), and keep the declared normalized clock tau, unit held capacities, circular phases and continuous centered perturbation lift theta about winding w=+1 or w=-1. The complete law is

    \[ W_r U'=(-LU+\gamma S(\Phi))/4+f-kv(y-z),\qquad \Phi'=\gamma LU/4,\qquad \tau_s z'=y-z,\qquad y=v^TLU. \]

    The alternative omits the sine-current branch, consumes only U and z, and retains the same r, U(0), z(0), input, clock and sensor. It is the passive linear reference of the preceding owner, not a different response matrix.

    Finite nonlinear result¶

    Declaration revision 46df23f1 fixes the candidate above. The area intervals are disjoint by more than 2^-14 under those premises. The proof combines exact integrated evolution rows with a new decay estimate for the actual loaded nonlinear law. It evaluates no trajectory or physical response. The result distinguishes restoring phase feedback from the declared passive phase-blind reference. A separate countermodel below prevents interpreting that distinction as unique identification of sine dynamics or winding.

    Pulse admission and shared preparation¶

    Retain the loaded storage and pulse bounds:

    \[ E_s=\tfrac12U^TLW_rU+V(\Phi)-V_*+\tfrac{k\tau_s}{2}z^2, \qquad E_s'=-\tfrac14\|LU\|^2-k(y-z)^2+(LU)^Tf. \tag{1} \]

    The unchanged pulse gives E_s(T)<=Ebar<2^-16, and its first-exit argument preserves max|Dtheta|<1/10 throughout the pulse and all later unforced time. Here Ebar includes initial sensor energy and the allowance 2*a^2*T; it is not delivered work. The full preparation cost still includes V_*. There is no event or state reset at pulse release. After release the supplied work is zero and the sum of the two continuous losses over any recovery interval is at most Ebar, by (1) and nonnegative excess storage within the retained collar.

    The common preparation calculation (4+32*r_max)*delta^2, collar radius and barrier now have one shared owner. The unloaded and loaded consumers retain distinct observations, gains, sensor terms and balances. A separate loaded-pulse owner re-admits the seven pulse primitives and rebuilds sensor energy, forced growth and identity. Both observation consumers use this energy calculation; recovery requires no short-window gain or mean-response interval. The energy bound itself does not require the short-window norm denominator to be positive. Passing H as the pulse horizon would change the supplied pulse and sensor law: sensor time remains tau_s=T=2^-10 throughout recovery.

    A decay estimate that retains the sensor¶

    Put q=LU, d=y-z, sigma=k*tau_s=2^-16, and N=||U||^2+||theta||^2+sigma*z^2. On the admitted centered collar, I<=L<=4I on the neutral space, I<=W_r<=(13/5)I, and

    \[ \tfrac1{10}\|\theta\|^2\le V-V_*\le2\|\theta\|^2, \qquad \theta^T\nabla V\ge\tfrac15\|\theta\|^2. \tag{2} \]

    These use the full phase potential's Hessian throughout the collar, not just the tangent at the twist. N and the modified energy below use the fixed normalized chart; they are not additional physical observables or invariant metrics. Define

    \[ \mathcal F=E_s+ \gamma\theta^T(W_rU+\sigma vz)+\tfrac12\|\theta\|^2. \tag{3} \]

    The sensor-corrected quantity obeys (W_rU+sigma*v*z)'=-q/4-gamma*grad(V)/4 after release. The derivative of the last term in (3) cancels the mixed theta^T*q term. Thus, exactly along the full nonlinear recovery,

    \[ \mathcal F'=-\tfrac14\|q\|^2-kd^2+ \tfrac{\gamma^2}{4}U^TLW_rU+ \tfrac{\gamma^2\sigma}{4}yz- \tfrac{\gamma^2}{4}\theta^T\nabla V. \tag{4} \]

    This cancellation uses the actual matrix input and sensor state. A cross term inserting L^+ before the nonlinear potential gradient would need a further sign proof; its matrices need not commute.

    For completeness, E_s+||theta||^2/2 lies between N/2 and 26*N/5. Since ||v||=sqrt(2)<3/2, the absolute mixed term in (3) is at most (23/10)*gamma*N<N/4. Consequently

    \[ N/4\le\mathcal F\le8N. \tag{5} \]

    Also U^TLW_rU<=(13/5)||q||^2 and yz=y^2-yd<=3||q||^2+d^2/2. Applying gamma<1/3000 and the fixed sensor coefficients to (4) gives

    \[ \mathcal F'\le-\|q\|^2/8-kd^2/2-\gamma^2\|\theta\|^2/20. \tag{6} \]

    Because z=y-d, one has N<=2||q||^2+||theta||^2+2*sigma*d^2. Equations (5)-(6) therefore imply

    \[ \mathcal F'\le-\gamma^2N/20 \le-\gamma^2\mathcal F/160 \le-2^{-32}\mathcal F. \tag{7} \]

    The last inequality uses gamma>1/4096, following from pi<4. These coefficients belong to this loaded C5 model and its fixed collar and sensor. Earlier reciprocal-potential energy methods motivate (3); their different-law coefficients are not reused as certificates.

    At pulse release, N(T)<=10*Ebar, so F(T)<=80*Ebar. At any later duration D, let n=floor(D/2^32). Since exp(-n)<=2^-n,

    \[ N(T+D)\le320\overline E\,2^{-n}<2^{-7-n}. \tag{8} \]

    The phase-blind reference has its own storage E_L=Uhat^T*L*W_r*Uhat/2+sigma*zhat^2/2 and loss E_L'=-||qhat||^2/4-k*dhat^2. With N_L=||Uhat||^2+sigma*zhat^2, the bounds N_L/2<=E_L<=8*N_L and N_L<=2||qhat||^2+2*sigma*dhat^2 give E_L'<=-E_L/64. The same forced growth allowance bounds E_L(T)<=Ebar. It therefore satisfies the weaker common endpoint envelope (8) as well. No phase coordinate is invented for this reference.

    An entirely rational norm bound follows without an exponential evaluator or a trajectory grid:

    \[ \chi=2^{-\lfloor(n+7)/2\rfloor},\qquad \|U_H\|,\ \|\theta_H\|,\ \|\widehat U_H\|\le\chi, \qquad |z_H|,|\widehat z_H|\le256\chi. \tag{9} \]

    For the declared D, n=256 and chi=2^-131. This is a conservative finite deadline, not an optimized settling time or a proposed laboratory duration. The rational tail avoids losing small bounds to a fixed absolute interval grid. The detached consumer limits n to 4096 as an arithmetic-work policy; that limit is not a physical restriction or a failed convergence claim.

    Exact finite area and separation¶

    Integrating the live phase and sensor rows gives, at every finite H,

    \[ A_H=\frac4\gamma v^T(\theta_H-\theta_0) -\tau_s(z_H-z_0). \tag{10} \]

    This is an identity for the raw sensor integral. It neither adds a phase measurement nor deletes sensor endpoint memory from the proof. Integrating the actual phase-blind form row, with the same pulse, gives separately

    \[ \widehat A_H=8aT+4v^TW_r(U_0-\widehat U_H) -(1+8k)\tau_s(\widehat z_H-z_0). \tag{11} \]

    In particular, the factor 1+8k retains the sensor's back-action as well as its observation memory. Omitting either changes this identity. Use the common endpoint envelope (9) and define

    \[ \begin{aligned} b_F&=6(4096)\delta+\tau_s\delta_s +[6(4096)+256\tau_s]\chi,\\ b_L&=6(13/5)\delta+(1+8k)\tau_s\delta_s +[6(13/5)+(1+8k)256\tau_s]\chi. \end{aligned} \tag{12} \]

    The permitted observed record sets are enclosed by

    \[ \mathcal A_F=[-b_F-\eta_A,b_F+\eta_A],\qquad \mathcal A_L=[8aT-b_L-\eta_A,8aT+b_L+\eta_A]. \tag{13} \]

    For the frozen candidate, exact rational arithmetic yields

    \[ \inf\mathcal A_L-\sup\mathcal A_F =8aT-b_F-b_L-2\eta_A>2^{-14}. \tag{14} \]

    One coarse check is that the input area minus the full-law initial-phase allowance is 2^-13, the remaining initial allowances sum to less than 2^-22, the two observation errors sum to 2^-17, and the two terminal allowances sum to less than 2^-115. These bounds imply (14) without sampling a trajectory or fitting a gain. The result holds over the entire declared preparation and r interval, not only at exact twist initialization.

    At exact zero perturbation and sensor preparation, (10) tends to zero as recovery completes, while (11) tends to 8aT=1/2048. The positive early sensor response of the earlier theorem is therefore followed by a negative signed contribution in the full model. This compensation is a consequence of return to the same phase lift and the complete sensor law. A small late value of z by itself would not certify that accumulated area. Signed voltage area and dissipated energy have different units and balances; the late sign reversal is consistent with both nonnegative losses in (1).

    What the area identifies, and what it leaves unresolved¶

    A distinct restoring law shares the decisive endpoint property. In the declared local lift, replace V-V_* by Q(theta)=cos(2*pi/5)*theta^T*L*theta/2 and its sine source by -cos(2*pi/5)*L*theta. Keep both evolution rows, W_r and the sensor. This local quadratic law matches the twist and its full tangent but differs from the nonlinear sine field away from that state. It consumes phase information and is not the phase-blind reference of (11). The law is declared on the retained local lift; it is not a globally periodic potential.

    Its own potential satisfies the same collar Hessian bounds, so its own storage, trapping and calculation (3)-(9) apply. For matched initial states, the initial terms of (10) agree exactly between the sine and quadratic laws. Their finite areas differ by at most twice the full terminal allowance in (12), less than 2*eta_A at the declared horizon. Their actual permitted records overlap by the midpoint-error construction. Thus this area detects restoring feedback relative to the calibrated phase-blind alternative, but does not uniquely select the sine law or its nonlinearity. The two winding signs also retain the same bounds. Further physical identification must state which property and which alternative it intends to distinguish.

    Physical scaling and remaining admission¶

    Under the existing electrical dictionary, tau=b*t_lab, V_s=v0*z and v0=a0*b*gamma/4. The laboratory voltage area and its corresponding error requirement are

    \[ \mathcal A_{\rm lab}=\int_0^{H/b}V_s(t)\,dt =\frac{a_0\gamma}{4}A_H,\qquad \varepsilon_{\rm area}\le\frac{a_0\gamma}{4}\eta_A. \tag{15} \]

    Changing b rescales duration and voltage together; it does not change this area conversion. The integral allowance is a new observation premise with different units from the short-window mean-error allowance. Neither implies the other.

    Operational admission of the frozen candidate¶

    Physical status: not admitted. Experimental evaluation: not tested. The review below concerns direct voltage-area measurement with the supplied Josephson conversion and voltage-only initialization checks. Its published instrument error allowance does not certify the frozen integral error, while those initialization checks cannot establish the full phase preparation. These are separate scoped obstructions. They preserve the conditional theorem and do not establish a universal physical impossibility or an experimental rejection of either law.

    Clock, duration and joint residual error¶

    Let p denote the laboratory pulse duration and h the total observation duration, avoiding confusion with Planck's constant h_P. The unchanged candidate and sensor imply

    \[ p=T/b,\qquad h=H/b=Rp,\qquad R=H/T=1+2^{50},\qquad (\tau_s)_{\rm lab}=p. \tag{16} \]

    If an independently admitted input-delivery mode requires p>=p_min>0, and preparation, calibration and measurement coverage hold only through h_max, any compatible b must satisfy

    \[ H/h_{\max}\le b\le T/p_{\min},\qquad Rp_{\min}\le h_{\max}. \tag{17} \]

    This constrains delivery of the actual input, not sampling of its short pulse. A continuous integrator need not resolve that pulse in separate samples. Conversely, a finite rise time requires a justified input-error bound; a nominal pulse-width setting does not realize the ideal rectangular current exactly.

    For the supplied conversion a0=h_P/(4*pi*e), the SI defining constants give h_P=6.62607015e-34 J s and e=1.602176634e-19 C exactly. Then

    \[ C_A=\frac{a_0\gamma}{4} =\frac{h_P}{16(1023)\pi^2e},\qquad B_A=C_A\eta_A=\frac{h_P}{1023\,2^{22}\pi^2e} \simeq9.7658568\,10^{-26}\ {\rm V\,s}. \tag{18} \]

    The nominal phase-blind area is C_A/2048, approximately 1.2500297e-23 V s; the proved structural gap corresponds to more than 1.5625371e-24 V s. These are unit translations, not measured responses. The physical constants and Josephson reference remain supplied premises.

    Suppose an independent residual-error model after calibration has |offset(0)|<=e0 and |offset'(t)|<=d0. Over [0,h], its sharp worst-case absolute integrated error is e0*h+d0*h^2/2, attained by aligned affine offset and drift. Allocating the entire integral allowance to these errors would require

    \[ e_0h+\tfrac12d_0h^2\le B_A, \qquad e_0Rp_{\min}+\tfrac12d_0(Rp_{\min})^2\le B_A. \tag{19} \]

    For constant offset alone, e0*p_min<=B_A/R, approximately 8.6738233e-41 V s. As a scale illustration only, a 1 ns minimum delivered pulse entails an observation longer than 1.1258999e6 s and requires a residual constant-offset bound below 8.6738233e-32 V even if it consumes the whole allowance. Neither value is a hardware specification or a fundamental noise floor. This calculation assumes the stated error class; it does not assert that all noise accumulates coherently.

    Independent calibration can subtract a known offset, but its residual and drift still need joint coverage. A final small voltage cannot certify a small accumulated error. A gain error, gate-timing error, sensor loading, input tracking or uncertain matrix changes further terms and cannot be assigned the same full allowance simultaneously. Finite-time calibration uncertainty is not an admitted perturbation of the exact dynamical law. Changing the horizon, using paired pulses or resetting/subtracting part of the response requires a new observation or input declaration and proof.

    Independent preparation information¶

    The voltage map V=v0*L*U and sensor voltage V_s=v0*z contain no initial relative-phase information. This fails even locally: at U=z=0, both theta=0 and theta=2*delta*(e_0-e_1) have identical voltage readings and winding and lie in the retained collar, while only the first satisfies ||U||+||theta||<=delta. The equal-energy counterexample separately prevents zero voltage, winding and target storage from globally certifying the collar. Allowing the reference law to omit phase does not remove the full law's preparation obligation.

    A phase-sensitive measurement or independently validated preparation process must therefore bound the missing relative coordinates without using the reserved recovery area. Any reading's loading, disturbance, uncertainty and transport to pulse start belong to that certificate. A winding label does not replace the four relative phases, and an acquired 45-node source does not transfer to this supplied C5 matrix law without its own reduction and state-error proof. Common phase origin can be quotiented; response-relevant relative phase cannot.

    For example, admit the neutral form leaf, a calibrated v0>=v_min>0, projected nodal voltage estimate Vhat, sensor estimate Vshat and an independently admitted centered perturbation lift thetahat. Let their joint errors at pulse start be bounded in the respective Euclidean/absolute norms by eps_V, eps_s and eps_theta. Since ||L^+||_2<1 on C5, sufficient bounds are

    \[ B_U=\frac{\|P\widehat V\|+\varepsilon_V}{v_{\min}},\qquad B_\theta=\|\widehat\theta\|+\varepsilon_\theta,\qquad B_s=\frac{|\widehat V_s|+\varepsilon_s}{v_{\min}}, \quad B_U+B_\theta\le\delta,\quad B_s\le\delta_s. \tag{20} \]
