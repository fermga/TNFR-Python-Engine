    \[ K=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_i/d_i),\qquad q=Lx,\qquad S_i(\theta)=\sum_{j\sim i}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i). \]

    \[ a=\frac w\pi,\qquad b=\frac{w}{\beta\pi},\qquad a=\beta b. \]

    \[ \dot x=-eKq+aKS(\theta),\qquad \dot\theta=bKq. \]

    It is distinct from native neighbor-argument pressure and its phase mobility. Holding capacity and support fixed is a premise, not an inferred universal law.

    \[ E(x,\theta)=\frac12x^\mathsf TLx+ \beta V(\theta),\qquad V(\theta)=\sum_{\{i,j\}}\bigl(1-\cos(\theta_j-\theta_i)\bigr). \]

    3. The full equilibrium derivative¶

    Take an exact critical pattern x_*=c*1,S(theta_*)=0 and define its phase Hessian

    \[ H=\nabla^2 V(\theta_*) =L_{\cos(\theta_{*,j}-\theta_{*,i})}. \]

    Assume H is positive definite after removal of common phase rotation. Strictly acute critical edge gaps are a sufficient condition on connected support. This hypothesis is essential; not every phase configuration is a stable pattern.

    Let

    \[ h=K^{-1/2}\mathbf1,\qquad \mathcal Q=h^\perp,\qquad B=K^{1/2}LK^{1/2},\qquad C=K^{1/2}HK^{1/2}. \]

    Both matrices annihilate h and are positive definite on mathcal Q. Every inverse and square root below is restricted to this quotient, rather than inverting a singular full Laplacian. In transformed perturbations xi=K^(-1/2) delta x and eta=K^(-1/2) delta theta, with their common-origin components removed, the exact tangent is

    \[ \dot\xi=-eB\xi-aC\eta,\qquad \dot\eta=bB\xi. \]

    Consequently

    \[ \ddot\eta+eB\dot\eta+abBC\eta=0, \qquad B^{-1}\ddot\eta+e\dot\eta+abC\eta=0. \]

    This is derived from the joint nodal rows. It does not add a primitive oscillator, identify capacity with an angular frequency or reconstruct pressure from a response.

    The quotient tangent is asymptotically stable. For example, testing the second quadratic pencil on a possibly complex eigenvector v gives

    \[ m s^2+e n s+ab c=0,\qquad m=v^*B^{-1}v>0,\quad n=v^*v>0,\quad c=v^*Cv>0. \]

    A real root is negative. For a nonreal root, its imaginary part gives Re(s)=-e*n/(2m)<0. This establishes stability, but does not classify every root as real or nonreal.

    In general B and C do not commute. An isolated-ring discriminant cannot classify a different full graph without checking that reduction. If H=rho*L, then C=rho*B even with heterogeneous positive capacity: capacity changes modal rates and eigenvectors, but not the resulting common pole-class condition. Otherwise use the whole pencil and actual observations.     4. A finite-frequency maximum for every active collocated port¶

    Declare the probe and the measured response¶

    Introduce, solely for this response question, a supplied form-rate input f*u(t):

    \[ \dot x=-eKq+aKS+f\,u(t),\qquad \dot\theta=bKq. \]

    The fixed real vector f and scalar waveform u specify the probe. This does not derive a sinusoidal drive from an autonomous environment or install an input in the unforced engine. The exact balance now includes

    \[ \dot E=-e\,q^\mathsf TKq+u\,y,\qquad y=f^\mathsf Tq=f^\mathsf TLx. \]

    Thus y is the output conjugate to this input in structural storage work. It is not necessarily a laboratory power measurement. Changing the output to a single phase or a different region changes the response claim.

    Let P_Q project onto mathcal Q and put

    \[ F=P_{\mathcal Q}K^{-1/2}f,\qquad G=B^{1/2}F,\qquad \zeta=B^{1/2}\xi,\qquad A=abB^{1/2}CB^{1/2}. \]

    The common-origin input component is tracked separately in the weighted form mean and contributes no work to y. An active port means F!=0, equivalently G!=0. A nonzero spatially uniform form-rate input has F=0 and is an explicit excluded case: it moves only the common form origin.

    At the tangent level the transfer from u to y, with zero initial perturbation, is

    \[ \boxed{ \mathcal H(s)=sG^\mathsf T (s^2I+esB+A)^{-1}G.} \]

    Indeed, zeta'= -eB zeta-aB^(1/2)C eta+G*u and eta'=bB^(1/2)zeta; eliminating eta yields the displayed transfer, and y=G^T zeta. This is a tangent frequency response around the exact pattern. A finite nonlinear experiment needs its own amplitude, error and preparation admission.

    Conditional theorem and proof¶

    For every active port under the stated hypotheses, the scalar gain |mathcal H(i*omega)| has a positive global maximum at at least one finite omega>0.

    First, A and B are positive definite. For omega>0, the matrix

    \[ M(\omega)=A-\omega^2I+ie\omega B \]

    is nonsingular: if M v=0, the imaginary part of v^*M v would be e*omega*v^*Bv=0, forcing v=0. There is no imaginary-axis pole, in agreement with the stability argument above.

    For z=M(omega)^(-1)G, direct multiplication gives

    \[ \operatorname{Re}\mathcal H(i\omega) =e\omega^2 z^*Bz>0. \]

    The response is therefore nonzero at every positive frequency. It is continuous there, and its endpoint asymptotics are

    \[ \mathcal H(i\omega) =i\omega\,G^\mathsf TA^{-1}G+O(\omega^2) \quad(\omega\to0), \]
    \[ \mathcal H(i\omega) =\frac{G^\mathsf TG}{i\omega}+O(\omega^{-2}) \quad(\omega\to\infty). \]

    The gain tends to zero at both ends. Choose any positive interior value; outside a sufficiently large compact interval the gain is smaller than that value. Continuity on the interval supplies a positive finite-frequency global maximum.

    The gain is also bounded in terms of the admitted input and loss:

    \[ |\mathcal H(i\omega)|^2 \le\omega^2(G^\mathsf TB^{-1}G)(z^*Bz) =\frac{\|F\|^2}{e}\operatorname{Re}\mathcal H(i\omega), \]

    and hence

    \[ \boxed{|\mathcal H(i\omega)|\le\frac{\|F\|^2}{e}.} \]

    This is frequency selectivity of a specified structural-work port through reciprocal form--phase dynamics. It holds for noncommuting B,C and does not require complex temporal poles. It does not prove a narrow peak, large gain, a particular maximum frequency, selective response at every remote node or a maintained autonomous pulse. The normalization of f fixes the numerical gain.
