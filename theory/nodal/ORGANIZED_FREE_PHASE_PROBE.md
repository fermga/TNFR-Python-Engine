# A freely reacting organized probe through one full relative turn

<a id="organized-free-phase-probe"></a>

Two formed C9 constituents can traverse every relative registration on
one freely evolving history while both internal identities remain acute.
An initially supplied difference of their common forms drives the turn;
neither constituent is held, and no phase reset or continuous input is
used after contact. The actual mean-form record determines the relative
phase through the already declared kinetic row. An integral identity then
reads a current harmonic without differentiating that record.
The finite-record construction below bounds the same readout from
finitely many uncertain samples, under explicit timing and kinetic
calibration premises.

This is conditional operational access under the existing supplied
constitutive family. It does not select that family, establish physical
measurement, or replace source, event-work and observation admission.
The [execution plan](../research/FIVE_STAGE_EXECUTION_PLAN.md#free-phase-probe-admission)
owns the bounded gate. The
[phase-faithfulness classification](SINE_CONSTITUTIVE_INFORMATION.md#phase-response-faithfulness)
still distinguishes a complete operational alphabet from one current
channel's invisible shifts.

## 2. Exact mean rows and a monotone full turn

Put

\[
 r=X_A-X_B,\qquad \delta=\Phi_A-\Phi_B,\qquad
 v=\gamma r,\qquad s=\frac a2\tau,\qquad
 \overline J=\frac19\sum_{k=0}^8
 j_\lambda(\delta+q_{A,k}-q_{B,k}).
 \tag{5}
\]

Internal currents cancel in each child mean, and the matching edges
retain exactly the same relative mean form. Summing (3) therefore gives
the exact full-state equations

\[
 r_\tau=-\frac a2(r+\gamma\overline J),\qquad
 \delta_\tau=\frac{\gamma a}2r,\qquad
 \boxed{v_s=-v-\gamma^2\overline J,\quad \delta_s=v.}
 \tag{6}
\]

Both constituents react. On the invariant carrier this reduces to
\(\overline J=j_\lambda(\delta)\); no such substitution is needed
for the following transit bound.

Write \(c_0=\gamma^2J_0\). The canonical constants give
\(0<c_0<1/2\), hence also \(c_0\le1\).
Variation of constants with \(v(0)=12\) gives, for \(0\le s\le1\),

\[
 12e^{-s}-c_0(1-e^{-s})
 \le v(s)\le
 12e^{-s}+c_0(1-e^{-s})\le12.
 \tag{7}
\]

Using \(e^{-1}>1/3\) and \(c_0<1/2\) in the lower bound yields
\(v(s)>11/3\). Integration of the lower bound, with
\(e^{-1}<2/5\) and the weaker \(c_0\le1\), gives

\[
\begin{split}
 \delta(1)-\delta(0)
 &\ge12(1-e^{-1})-c_0e^{-1}\\
 &>\frac{34}{5}>2\pi.
\end{split}
 \tag{8}
\]

The elementary estimates on \(e\) follow from its positive series:
\(5/2<e<3\). The final comparison follows, for example, from
\(\pi<22/7\). Thus a unique \(s_*\in(0,1)\) satisfies
\(\delta(s_*)=2\pi\). In fact \(s_*>\pi/6\), since \(v\le12\).
No trajectory or numerical root is needed to prove this statement.
The flow continues freely; \(s_*\) merely ends the readout integral.

This establishes access to every intermediate relative registration in
one actual history, without holding either phase fixed or independently
preparing a separate contact for every query.

## 3. Fine-state retention through the nonacute transit

Let \(P\) project onto the separate zero-mean subspaces of the two rings.
It commutes with \(L\). Write \(L_f=L|_{\operatorname{ran}P}\).
The fine projection of the gradient is zero on the carrier for every
\(\delta\): each ring's two internal currents cancel and its cross current
is constant over slots. Consequently the exact fine rows are

\[
 q_\tau=\frac\gamma4 L_fz,\qquad
 z_\tau=-\frac14L_fz-\frac\gamma4 g(q,\delta),\qquad
 g(q,\delta)=A(q,\delta)q.
 \tag{9}
\]

Here \(A\) is the integrated projected Hessian on the straight
fine segment from zero to \(q\), at the current relative mean phase.
On the bootstrap tube \(\|q\|<1/100\), every internal gap differs
from \(\alpha\) by less than \(1/50\). In particular its magnitude
is below \(18/25\). With
\(C_6(u)=1-u^2/2+u^4/24-u^6/720\),

\[
 j_\lambda'(u)\ge\cos u-\frac2{243}
 \ge C_6(18/25)-\frac2{243}>\frac7{10}
 \quad\hbox{on those internal gaps}.
 \tag{10}
\]

The cross-edge curvature can be negative during the turn, but is
bounded below by \(-J_1\). On the two fine ring spaces the
internal Laplacian has lowest eigenvalue
\(2-2\cos(2\pi/9)>2/5\) and norm at most four;
the matching Laplacian has norm two. Therefore the actual bounds are

\[
 L_f\succeq\frac25I,\qquad
 \frac14I\preceq A(q,\delta)\preceq5I,
 \tag{11}
\]

uniformly in the complete relative phase circle. For the lower bound,
\((7/10)(2/5)-2aJ_1>1/4\); the upper norm is bounded by
\(J_1(4+2a)<5\). These are own-law bounds for every
\(0\le\lambda\le1\), not a transfer from the sine tangent.
For the internal spectral bound, \(\alpha>2/3\) and cosine
decreases on this interval, while
\(\cos(2/3)\le1-(2/3)^2/2+(2/3)^4/24=191/243<4/5\).

Define the positive functional

\[
 F=\frac12\|q+\gamma z\|^2+\frac12\gamma^2\|z\|^2,
 \qquad \|q\|^2\le4F,\qquad
 F\le\|q\|^2+\frac32\gamma^2\|z\|^2.
 \tag{12}
\]

Substitution of both fine rows gives the exact identity

\[
 F_\tau=-\frac{\gamma^2}{4}
 \left[q^\top g+z^\top L_fz+2\gamma z^\top g\right].
 \tag{13}
\]

Since \(\|g\|\le5\|q\|\), Young's inequality gives
\(2\gamma|z^\top g|\le\|q\|^2/8+200\gamma^2\|z\|^2\).
The canonical \(\gamma<1/3000\) satisfies
\(2/5-200\gamma^2>1/8\) and \(3\gamma^2/2<1\).
Equations (11)--(13) consequently imply

\[
 \boxed{F_\tau\le-\frac{\gamma^2}{32}F,\qquad
 F(\tau)\le F_0e^{-\gamma^2\tau/32}
                 =F_0e^{-125s/2}.}
 \tag{14}
\]

Initially (4) and (12) place \(q\) strictly inside the tube.
The same strict bound remains at any proposed first exit, so
continuity closes the bootstrap through the whole observation horizon.
Both internal cycles stay acute with winding one, including every
allowed fine direction. No stationary profile replaces the actual state.

The full relative turn necessarily passes nonacute matching gaps and
their phase cuts. This proves retention of the two constituent
identities, not a fixed acute identity of the joined graph or fixed
windings on every new mixed cycle. U3 at a support event is a separate
admission; it is not reapplied as a filter of the unchanged global
smooth current during (3).

For later observation, put \(d_k=q_{A,k}-q_{B,k}\).
Its sum is zero. Taylor expansion and (2) give the quadratic error

\[
\begin{split}
 |\overline J-j_\lambda(\delta)|
 &\le\frac{J_2}{18}\sum_kd_k^2
 \le\frac{J_2}{9}\|q\|^2\\
 &\le\frac{4J_2F_0}{9}e^{-125s/2}.
\end{split}
 \tag{15}
\]

The first-order correction cancels because the fine means are retained.

## 5. A derivative-free harmonic observation

The mean-form record gives \(v(s)=\gamma(X_A(s)-X_B(s))\).
The unchanged phase row gives exactly

\[
 \delta(s)=\delta(0)+\int_0^s v(u)\,du.
 \tag{18}
\]

This uses the declared initial relative lift and kinetic law.
It does not use an inverse of the current being tested, hidden
fine phases, or a held phase reference. It cannot simultaneously
validate an unknown kinetic row or recover an unadmitted
initial phase.

For any positive integer \(n\), define from the same continuing
record, up to its one-turn readout time \(s_*\),

\[
 B_n=\frac1{\pi\gamma^2}
 \left\{
 \frac n2\int_0^{s_*}\cos(n\delta)\,v^3\,ds
 -\int_0^{s_*}\sin(n\delta)\,v^2\,ds
 -\frac12[\sin(n\delta)v^2]_0^{s_*}
 \right\}.
 \tag{19}
\]

To verify it, multiply \(v_s+v=-\gamma^2\overline J\)
by \(v\sin(n\delta)\), integrate, and use \(\delta_s=v\)
to integrate the \(vv_s\) term by parts. The result is exactly

\[
 B_n=\frac1\pi\int_0^{s_*}
       \overline J(s)\sin(n\delta(s))\,v(s)\,ds.
 \tag{20}
\]

No derivative of a measured record remains. In the primary
preparation \(\delta(0)=0\), \(\delta(s_*)=2\pi\), so the
endpoint term in (19) vanishes. It is displayed because it
must be retained for an independently admitted nonzero starting
phase and a complete turn from that phase.

Strict monotonicity permits \(d\delta=v\,ds\).
If \(b_n\) is the actual sine Fourier coefficient of
\(j_\lambda\), orthogonality and (15) imply

\[
 \boxed{|B_n-b_n|\le\frac{8J_2F_0}{9}.}
 \tag{21}
\]

Indeed the absolute integral of the fine current error over a
turn is at most \(2\pi\) times its uniform upper bound.
For this supplied family,
\(b_1=1\), \(b_9=\lambda/2187\),
\(b_{18}=-\lambda/4374\), and the other positive-index
coefficients vanish. Formula (21) concerns the actual freely
reacting fine state, not a nominal carrier sampled at imposed
registrations.

### Exact access is not finite-resolution identification

Let \(A_n\) denote the expression in braces in (19). On the
ideal carrier its ninth-harmonic value is
\(\pi\gamma^2\lambda/2187\). This raw contrast is small;
the normalization in (19) does not increase measurement accuracy.
For the fixed declared \(\gamma\) and normalization, if a separately
admitted complete record and quadrature error
bounds \(|\widehat A_n-A_n|\) by \(E_n\), its total coefficient
uncertainty is at most

\[
 \frac{E_n}{\pi\gamma^2}+\frac{8J_2F_0}{9}.
 \tag{22}
\]

That error budget must include reconstruction of \(\delta\),
the readout endpoint and all affected integrals. If the kinetic
calibration or clock is uncertain, its uncertainty must instead
be propagated through the whole observation map, including the
normalizing denominator; a numerator-only error at nominal
\(\gamma\) does not suffice. For example, distinguishing two
deformations through \(n=9\) with a common such bound requires
twice (22) to be smaller than
\(|\lambda_1-\lambda_0|/2187\). The next section supplies a finite
sampling construction and its conditional uncertainty budget.
It does not admit an instrument or a physical observation map.

## 6. Finite records with explicit uncertainty

The mathematical question is whether finite observations can retain the
information in (20), rather than whether the ideal continuous integral
exists. Keep the complete law, primitive preparation, contact and horizon
of section 1. In particular the actual initial means remain
\(v(0)=12,\delta(0)=0\). The initial phase uncertainty introduced below
belongs only to the readout; it does not enlarge the preparation class.

### 6.1 Subtracting the known free relaxation

Define a reference and two residuals from the actual mean record:

\[
 h(s)=12e^{-s},\qquad w(s)=v(s)-h(s),\qquad
 R(s)=\frac{v(s)^2-h(s)^2}{2\gamma^2}
     =\frac{h(s)w(s)+w(s)^2/2}{\gamma^2}.
 \tag{23}
\]

The complete mean rows, without any carrier substitution, give

\[
\begin{split}
 w_s+w&=-\gamma^2\overline J,\qquad w(0)=0,\\
 R_s+2R&=-v\overline J,\qquad R(0)=0,\\
 |w|&\le\gamma^2J_0,\qquad |w_s|\le2\gamma^2J_0,\\
 |R|&\le R_0:=6J_0,\qquad |R_s|\le24J_0.
\end{split}
 \tag{24}
\]

The last two estimates follow by variation of constants and \(v\le12\).
Multiplying the second row by \(\sin(n\delta)\) and integrating yields,
at any \(b\in[0,1]\),

\[
 \frac1\pi\int_0^b\overline J\,v\sin(n\delta)\,ds
 =\frac1\pi\left\{
 \int_0^b R\bigl(nv\cos(n\delta)-2\sin(n\delta)\bigr)\,ds
 -[R\sin(n\delta)]_0^b\right\}.
 \tag{25}
\]

At \(b=s_*\) this is the same \(B_n\) as (19).
The factorized expression in (23) avoids forming the difference of
the two large integrals in (19). It does not remove the accuracy needed
to read the small form residual or to calibrate \(\gamma\).

The finite interpolation bound must also retain the actual fine state.
Writing \(d_k=q_{A,k}-q_{B,k}\), equations (9), (12) and \(s=a\tau/2\)
give

\[
 \|d\|\le2\sqrt{2F},\qquad
 \|d_s\|\le\frac{5\sqrt F}{a},\qquad \sum_k(d_k)_s=0.
 \tag{26}
\]

Here \(\|L_f\|<5\) and \(\gamma\|z\|\le\sqrt{2F}\).
Differentiate the actual average current and subtract the zero
quantity \(j_\lambda'(\delta)\sum_k(d_k)_s/9\). Then

\[
\begin{split}
 |\overline J_s|
 &\le12J_1+\frac{J_2}{9}\|d\|\|d_s\|\\
 &\le12J_1+\frac{10\sqrt2J_2}{9a}F
 \le12J_1+\frac{5J_2}{3a}F,\\
 |w_{ss}|&\le
 \gamma^2(2J_0+12J_1)+\frac{5000}{3}J_2F_0.
\end{split}
 \tag{27}
\]

Thus the fast fine relaxation has a declared contribution to the
interpolation budget; it is not replaced by the carrier derivative.

### 6.2 Finite sampling, reconstruction and stopping

Use \(N+1\) samples at nominal scaled times \(s_i=i/N\),
\(i=0,\ldots,N\), with \(N\) a positive integer and \(H=1/N\).
The following are independently
supplied observation premises:

- A raw reading \(\widehat r_i\) differs from the actual relative form
  \(r(s_i^{\rm act})\) by at most \(\varepsilon_r\).
- Its exposure time satisfies
  \(|s_i^{\rm act}-s_i|\le\xi\), with every actual exposure in \([0,1]\).
  This is an error in the declared scaled clock, including any error
  in converting a reading's timestamp to that clock.
- A single kinetic calibration obeys
  \(\widehat\gamma/\gamma\in[1-\kappa,1+\kappa]\), \(0\le\kappa<1\).
- The readout origin \(\widehat\delta_0\) obeys
  \(|\widehat\delta_0|\le\zeta\).

These premises do not follow from the nodal identity. In particular,
a sensor value has not been identified with EPI by this theorem.
The true kinetic law and \(\gamma=1/(1023\pi)\) remain unchanged;
\(\widehat\gamma\) is only an observation calibration. An arbitrary
unknown clock scale is not admitted unless it satisfies the uniform
exposure bound throughout this horizon.
The error allowances are deterministic joint uniform bounds and may
be correlated. Independent justification means justification apart
from the evaluated response; no statistical independence or stochastic
averaging is assumed.

For computable enclosures let \(g_-\le\gamma\le g_+\) be positive
rational bounds and let \(\pi_-\le\pi\le\pi_+\).
Use the norm-derived sufficient upper bound \(F_0\) from (4), replacing
\(\gamma\) by \(g_+\) there. Define

\[
\begin{split}
 C&=g_+^2J_0,\\
 E&=12\kappa+(1+\kappa)
       \bigl[g_+\varepsilon_r+(12+C)\xi\bigr],\\
 M_w&=g_+^2(2J_0+12J_1)+\frac{5000}{3}J_2F_0,\\
 \varepsilon_v&=E+\frac{M_wH^2}{8},\qquad
 E_\delta=\zeta+\varepsilon_v,\qquad
 G_-^2=(1-\kappa)^2g_-^2.
\end{split}
 \tag{28}
\]

Indeed \(|v_s|\le12+C\). Multiplication of the raw reading by
\(\widehat\gamma\), followed by comparison with \(v(s_i)\), gives
\[
 |\widehat v_i-v(s_i)|\le E,\qquad
 \widehat v_i=\widehat\gamma\widehat r_i.
 \tag{29}
\]
The term \(12\kappa\) retains the error in the large form mean; it
cannot be dropped merely because the constitutive contrast is small.

At each nominal time form
\(\widehat w_i=\widehat v_i-h(s_i)\), and let \(\widetilde w\)
be their piecewise linear interpolant. Define the continuous
reconstruction

\[
 \widetilde v=h+\widetilde w,\qquad
 \widetilde\delta(s)=\widehat\delta_0+
            \int_0^s\widetilde v(u)\,du,\qquad
 \widetilde R=\frac{h\widetilde w+\widetilde w^2/2}
                   {\widehat\gamma^2}.
 \tag{30}
\]

The elementary linear interpolation remainder bounded by
\(\sup|w_{ss}|H^2/8\), together with (29), proves
\[
 |\widetilde v-v|\le\varepsilon_v,\qquad
 |\widetilde\delta-\delta|\le E_\delta.
 \tag{31}
\]
The integral in (30) uses only an exponential and the integral
of a linear function on each cell; it requires no derivative
of any reading.

Require the strict stopping margin

\[
 \varepsilon_v<
 \min\left(\frac{11}{3},\,\frac{34}{5}-2\pi_+\right).
 \tag{32}
\]

Then \(\widetilde v>0\) and there is a unique
\(\widehat s_*\in(0,1)\) at which
\(\int_0^{\widehat s_*}\widetilde v\,ds=2\pi\).
This is a full reconstructed **increment**, so that
\(\widetilde\delta(\widehat s_*)=\widehat\delta_0+2\pi\);
it does not reset the true phase or absorb the readout offset.
Moreover

\[
 |\delta(\widehat s_*)-2\pi|\le\varepsilon_v,\qquad
 |\widehat s_*-s_*|\le\frac{3\varepsilon_v}{11}.
 \tag{33}
\]

The second bound uses the true lower speed in (7).
The readout origin error affects the harmonic factors and both
endpoint terms, but not this increment stopping rule.

### 6.3 Calibration, quadrature and endpoint errors

The residual normalization in (30) uses the uncertain kinetic
calibration. Its complete error, rather than a numerator-only
error, is bounded by

\[
 \varepsilon_R=
 \frac{(24+\varepsilon_v)\varepsilon_v}{2G_-^2}
 +R_0\left((1-\kappa)^{-2}-1\right),\qquad
 |\widetilde R-R|\le\varepsilon_R.
 \tag{34}
\]

To see this, first compare \(\widetilde v^2\) with \(v^2\),
using \(v\le12\); then compare
\(\gamma^2/\widehat\gamma^2\) with one in the true residual.
Both errors are retained in (34).

For an explicit finite quadrature, take a positive integer \(K\).
Subdivide each data cell into \(K\) equal subcells and retain
their portions before \(\widehat s_*\), clipping the last one
at that endpoint. Use the midpoint of each retained portion.
Each has length at most \(H/K\), no portion crosses a data
knot, and there are at most \(NK\) such evaluations. This splitting
matters because \(\widetilde w_s\) can jump at a data knot.

The following constants bound derivatives inside each data cell:

\[
\begin{split}
 W&=C+E,\qquad D=2C+2E/H,\qquad
 V=12+W,\qquad V_1=12+D,\\
 \widehat R_0&=(12W+W^2/2)/G_-^2,\\
 \widehat R_1&=(12W+VD)/G_-^2,\\
 \widehat R_2&=(12W+24D+D^2)/G_-^2,\\
 A_0&=nV+2,\\
 A_1&=nV_1+n^2V^2+2nV,\\
 A_2&=12n+3n^2VV_1+n^3V^3+2nV_1+2n^2V^2,\\
 M_{f,2}&=\widehat R_2A_0+
                    2\widehat R_1A_1+\widehat R_0A_2,\\
 E_Q&=\frac{M_{f,2}}{24(NK)^2}.
\end{split}
 \tag{35}
\]

For example \(|\widetilde w|\le W\),
\(|\widetilde w_s|\le D\) and \(\widetilde w_{ss}=0\)
inside a cell. Therefore
\[
 \widetilde R_{ss}
 =\frac{h\widetilde w-2h\widetilde w_s+
                       (\widetilde w_s)^2}{\widehat\gamma^2}.
 \tag{36}
\]
This proves the three residual bounds in (35).
For
\(\widetilde A=n\widetilde v\cos(n\widetilde\delta)
-2\sin(n\widetilde\delta)\), the bounds on
\(|\widetilde A|,|\widetilde A_s|,|\widetilde A_{ss}|\)
are respectively \(A_0,A_1,A_2\), using
\(\widetilde\delta_s=\widetilde v\) and
\(|\widetilde v_{ss}|=h\le12\).
The product rule bounds
\(|(\widetilde R\widetilde A)_{ss}|\) by \(M_{f,2}\).
The midpoint remainder summed over the separate subcells is
at most \(M_{f,2}(H/K)^2/24\), since their total length is
at most one. No differentiability across data knots is assumed.

Let \(Q_{N,K}\) be this quadrature of
\(\widetilde R\widetilde A\), and retain the reconstructed
endpoint correction:

\[
 \widehat B_n^{\,\rm exact}=\frac1\pi\left\{
 Q_{N,K}-[\widetilde R\sin(n\widetilde\delta)]
                               _0^{\widehat s_*}\right\}.
 \tag{37}
\]

Both sine factors at these endpoints are
\(\sin(n\widehat\delta_0)\); they need not vanish.
Define the deliberately conservative observation bound

\[
 E_O=\varepsilon_R(nV+4)
 +R_0\left[n\varepsilon_v+(12n^2+4n)E_\delta\right]
 +J_0\varepsilon_v.
 \tag{38}
\]

Here is a direct comparison with the true full-turn integral.
For \(A=nv\cos(n\delta)-2\sin(n\delta)\), equations
(31) and (34) imply
\[
 |\widetilde R\widetilde A-RA|
 \le\varepsilon_R(nV+2)
 +R_0\left[n\varepsilon_v+(12n^2+2n)E_\delta\right].
 \tag{39}
\]
Each endpoint difference is at most
\(\varepsilon_R+R_0nE_\delta\).
Finally the true current integral between \(\widehat s_*\)
and \(s_*\) is bounded by \(J_0\varepsilon_v\), using
\(d\delta=v\,ds\), \(|\overline J|\le J_0\) and (33).
Adding these terms proves (38), including the true and
reconstructed endpoints. The last term could be sharpened
near a full turn but no such improvement is used here.

Allow a further certified coefficient error
\(\varepsilon_{\rm num}\) in evaluating (37). Combining
(21), (35) and (38) gives the finite-record theorem

\[
 \boxed{|\widehat B_n-b_n|\le
 \mathcal E_n:=
 \frac{8J_2F_0}{9}+\frac{E_O+E_Q}{\pi_-}
                         +\varepsilon_{\rm num}.}
 \tag{40}
\]

The terms separately retain fine-state bias, raw readings,
clock exposures, calibration, phase readout, interpolation,
stopping, endpoints, quadrature and numerical evaluation.
This is a sufficient theorem domain; failure of (32) or of
a discrimination margin does not prove that a particular
record is uninformative.

The numerical allowance can be supplied constructively.
There are finitely many cell formulas and quadrature values;
bounded-argument exponential and trigonometric Taylor series,
with rigorous remainders, and convergent rational enclosures
of \(\pi\) enclose them to arbitrary positive accuracy.
The positive lower slope
\(11/3-\varepsilon_v\) in (32) gives an explicit inverse
bound for bisection of the reconstructed increment.
For the integrand in (37), put
\(M_0=\widehat R_0A_0\) and
\(M_1=\widehat R_1A_0+\widehat R_0A_1\).
Moving the clipping endpoint by \(e_s\) changes the midpoint
sum by at most \((M_0+HM_1/(2K))e_s\): the derivative of a
partial-cell term of length \(\ell\) is
\(f(\mathrm{midpoint})+\ell f_s(\mathrm{midpoint})/2\).
The corresponding endpoint product changes by at most
\((\widehat R_1+n\widehat R_0V)e_s\).
These bounds also apply when an endpoint crosses a data knot,
by continuity and the bounds on each side. They permit a
finite root enclosure and arithmetic precisions whose total
coefficient error is any prescribed
\(\varepsilon_{\rm num}>0\). This is an existence and error
allocation argument, not a claim about unchecked floating
point evaluation. Setting this allowance to zero describes
ideal exact evaluation, not a generic machine implementation.

### 6.5 A certified consumer of primitive records

The detached reader implements (30)--(40). Its inputs are the ordered
raw readings at the canonical nominal grid \(i/N\), the scalar
\(\widehat\gamma>0\), the initial lifted-phase readout
\(\widehat\delta_0\), and all primitive preparation, error and
integer work budgets. It rebuilds the family and fine-state bounds;
a cached budget, derivative bound or earlier report is not a premise.
Every raw scalar is admitted before interval arithmetic. Malformed
values, a wrong sample count and unsupported theorem budgets retain
their domain errors.

Some necessary consistency conditions can be checked before evaluating
the observation. In addition to
\(|\widehat\delta_0|\le\zeta\), they are

\[
\begin{split}
 1-\kappa&\le1023\pi\,\widehat\gamma\le1+\kappa,\\
 |\widehat w_0|&\le E,\qquad
 |\widehat w_i|\le C+E,\\
 |\widehat w_{i+1}-\widehat w_i|
   &\le2CH+2E,\qquad
 \widehat w_i=\widehat\gamma\widehat r_i-12e^{-iH}.
\end{split}
 \tag{43}
\]

The first line checks the declared calibration against the unchanged
model constant. The other lines follow from \(w(0)=0\),
\(|w|\le C\), \(|w_s|\le2C\), and the sample error (29).
A separated violation is incompatible with the joint supplied
premises. If an outward enclosure overlaps a threshold without
certifying its side, the reader abstains numerically. Passing these
conditions is not a sufficient test that a record came from the
declared dynamics, preparation or instrument.

All numerical enclosures concern the supplied represented readings
themselves. Sensor and calibration uncertainty are already propagated
by (28)--(40); they are not applied a second time as numerical
uncertainty on every raw value. The shared exact exponential bounds
and outward rational interval arithmetic enclose the reference heat
values, residuals and their cumulative integrals. For example, define

\[
 P_i=\sum_{j<i}\frac H2(\widehat w_j+\widehat w_{j+1}),
 \qquad d_i=\frac{\widehat w_{i+1}-\widehat w_i}{H}.
\]

For \(s=iH+u\), \(0\le u\le H\), the reconstructed increment is

\[
 D_{\rm inc}(s)=12(1-e^{-s})+
                    P_i+\widehat w_i u+\frac12d_i u^2.
 \tag{44}
\]

This exact cell formula avoids numerical integration of the phase.
Grid heat values may be formed by outward recurrence with the common
factor \(e^{-H}\), retaining the accumulated enclosure width.
The shared positive exponential enclosure on \([0,1]\) also encloses
\(e^{-s}\) by taking its reciprocal. No new decay law is introduced.

On each cell the lower bound
\[
 \widetilde v(s)\ge
 12e^{-(i+1)H}+\min(\widehat w_i,\widehat w_{i+1})
 \tag{45}
\]
gives a directly checkable positive slope \(\mu\) for the reconstructed
increment. The reader encloses \(D_{\rm inc}(1)-2\pi\) and brackets
the first complete turn. Bisection uses certified signs, not a
floating root followed by a claimed tolerance. If the enclosure of
\(D_{\rm inc}(m)-2\pi\) straddles zero, the inverse slope bound
still contains the root within
\(m\pm |D_{\rm inc}(m)-2\pi|/\mu\), using an upper bound on the
absolute residual. A root bracket is retained through every step.
The initial phase readout remains outside this increment equation.

Let the certified root interval be \(S\), let \(b\) be its rational
midpoint and let \(r_S\) be its radius. Evaluate the prescribed
midpoint sum clipped at \(b\), including both endpoint terms,
with outward function and arithmetic enclosures. Denote this
coefficient enclosure by \(I_b\). The root sensitivity derived
after (40) gives

\[
\begin{split}
 L_{\rm end}
 &=\frac{1}{\pi_-}\left[
 \widehat R_0A_0+\frac{H}{2K}
       (\widehat R_1A_0+\widehat R_0A_1)
       +\widehat R_1+n\widehat R_0V\right],\\
 I_{\rm obs}&=I_b+[-L_{\rm end}r_S,L_{\rm end}r_S].
\end{split}
 \tag{46}
\]

Thus \(I_{\rm obs}\) encloses the exact discrete functional (37),
including its exact reconstructed turn endpoint. The numerical
certificate requires the computed radius
\(\operatorname{rad}(I_{\rm obs})\le\varepsilon_{\rm num}\);
the requested numerical allowance alone never proves that inequality.
Quadrature truncation is accounted for separately, through \(E_Q\).
If certified, the final conditional coefficient enclosure is

\[
 I_{\rm coefficient}=I_{\rm obs}+[-E_{\rm an},E_{\rm an}],
 \qquad
 E_{\rm an}=\frac{8J_2F_0}{9}+\frac{E_O+E_Q}{\pi_-}.
 \tag{47}
\]

The measured numerical width already lies in \(I_{\rm obs}\).
Adding \(\varepsilon_{\rm num}\) again in (47) would count it twice.
The midpoint of \(I_{\rm obs}\) also satisfies the conservative
bound (40), conditional on the independent observation premises.

The implementation distinguishes five outcomes:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Certified | The necessary record checks, reconstructed turn and requested numerical width have been enclosed; (47) remains conditional on the supplied model and observation premises |
| Incompatible record | A checked necessary condition is separated from its admitted interval; the record and joint premises cannot all hold |
| Unresolved numeric | Fixed-precision enclosures cannot settle a required comparison or fit the requested numerical allowance |
| Unresolved endpoint | A sufficient monotone reconstruction or complete-turn bracket has not been certified within the endpoint iteration and precision limits |
| Work limit | The quadrature evaluation cap is insufficient for the reconstructed endpoint |

These statuses do not replace domain rejection and do not identify
a physical law. The interval owner uses a fixed dyadic precision;
its bounded computation need not attain every positive tolerance
allowed by the mathematical existence argument. In particular, zero
numerical allowance is an exact ideal and will generally remain
unresolved. Work limits are computation policies, not a failure of
TNFR dynamics. Input admission precedes a work-limit return.
The coefficient enclosure remains unavailable for every noncertified
status, even if intermediate endpoint or observation bounds are retained.

Independent controls use manufactured analytic records. For example,
an ideal constant residual \(w=c\) gives
\[
 v=h+c,\quad
 R=(hc+c^2/2)/\widehat\gamma^2,\quad
 R_s+2R=cv/\widehat\gamma^2.
 \tag{48}
\]
Its corrected continuous full-increment functional is zero for every
initial readout origin: the corresponding test current is constant
\(-c/\widehat\gamma^2\), whose sine integral over a turn vanishes.
For nonzero origin and \(c\), the endpoint term itself need not
vanish. Rational approximations to these analytic samples, with
independently bounded remainders, test the numerical reconstruction
and endpoint correction. Such records are controls of an observation
algorithm, not evaluated histories of the supplied constitutive
family. No scientific producer, source replay or physical-data
comparison is needed to verify this reader.

## 7. Scope and shared verification

This result replaces an abstract collection of separately imposed
registrations by one finite, freely reacting contact history.
It retains its supplied large form means, positive work, both
kinetic rows, complete fine-state allowance and constituent identities.
The finite-record theorem additionally exposes sufficient observation
and computation budgets, and a regime where reading sets can overlap.
The observations remain conditional on their declared preparation
and law. A free probe does not convert compatibility, faithfulness
or minimal information into an intrinsic constitutive selector.

and residual allowances.
