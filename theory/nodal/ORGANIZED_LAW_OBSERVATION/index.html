    Constitutive law and unknown collective initialization¶

    Analytical scope and result¶

    The unchanged joint form observations distinguish the sine law from its retained analytic countermodel throughout the declared initial class, even when initialization is independently unknown under each law. The whole-record theorem proves that their complete finite noisy record sets are disjoint. Its retained strict certificate includes both nonlinear histories and each law's fine-source error. This is a conditional information result; no measured record or physical law is selected. The earlier record-only forecast still predicts under its supplied sine law.

    This owner establishes the alternative's own carrier, hidden-state error, finite identity and numerical growth bounds. It also proves a local obstruction: two distinct admitted initial phase gaps under the two endpoint laws have exactly the same two ideal frozen-current quadratures. An admitted adjacent gap can also match the three ideal receiver currents. Neither local test uniformly selects the law. The complete 150-by-35 formal sampled map is injective, but a direction with exact low-order cancellations obstructs every global scalar or positive block bootstrap using the retained unconstrained coefficient and remainder bounds. Those preliminary results alone establish neither separation nor overlap of the complete noisy nonlinear record sets.

    A constrained continuation uses the actual shared harmonic phase. Two long-ramp contrasts cancel the fundamental without dividing by the short plateau duration, and their ideal joint signal has a uniform positive phase-circle margin. The middle ramp supplies stronger same-mode contrasts. Joint observations also force equal winding and bound the fundamental phasor difference. These restrictions, actual late-time heat decay and correlated phase transport close the nonlinear separation theorem. A separate heat bridge through the same readings retains its historical phase-increment error bound below 2.8e-8; the final theorem does not need to reconstruct an individual record's phase history.

On C5 all currents then equal one circulation. During a marked-child ramp three unactuated edges have constant command, so this circulation is constant. Each of its two actuated edges has a trigonometric polynomial of frequencies 1,9,18 equal to that constant on an open interval of command values. Independence of those sine/cosine functions and the constant forces all six coefficients on that edge to vanish. Every edge is actuated somewhere in the word, proving injectivity.

    This theorem neither supplies continuous observations in the actual protocol nor proves rank or conditioning of its 150-row sampled matrix. It concerns the formal linear response, before nonlinear endogenous phase drift.

    For the actual pair, let B_n=max(||b_s,n||infinity,||b_c,n||infinity) and D=4*g*X+2*g^2*J_1*T, bounding each endogenous edge-phase drift. Here the carrier has only two incident outer currents, so ||S_lambda,5||infinity<=2*J_1 and ||Y||infinity<=X+g*J_1*T/2. Integrating its live phase row gives coordinate drift at most 2*g*X+g^2*J_1*T, hence the stated edge bound D. This is separate from the fine-state drift F_lambda in (10). The fundamental difference remainder and the two one-law higher-harmonic remainders give

    \[ u'=-L_5u/4-B\sum_n[b_{s,n}\cos(nh)+b_{c,n}\sin(nh)]+r,\quad \sum_c r_c=0, \]
    \[ \|r(v)\|_\infty\le 3D(B_1+9B_9+18B_{18})+g^2\int_0^v\|u(s)\|_\infty\,ds. \tag{17} \]

    For the fundamental term, use the original complex-phasor difference identity and |d_0-d_1|<=2*g*integral||u||infinity. For a higher harmonic, |exp(i*n*d)-1|<=n*|d|; its scaled phasor norm is at most sqrt(2)*B_n. Two incident edges and 2*sqrt(2)<3 yield (17). The zero-sum heat integral bound of 6 therefore implies

    \[ \sup\|u-V\|_\infty\le 18D(B_1+9B_9+18B_{18})+6g^2T\sup\|u\|_\infty. \tag{18} \]

    The old 15-column gain does not certify (16)-(18).     5. Exact rank of the actual finite formal design¶

    The 150-by-35 sampled map of (16), at the original thirty observation times, has column rank 35. This is an exact statement about the formal linear heat response. It supplies neither a quantitative inverse bound nor a noisy or nonlinear law discriminator. No sampled matrix is evaluated in the proof.

    The recorded plateaus and first ramps determine scalar moments¶

    Suppose a coefficient vector produces zero V at every recorded time. The first reading gives V(0)=0, eliminating all five initial-form coefficients. Write A=L5/4. On each plateau its forcing is a constant vector f, and the two recorded endpoints satisfy

    \[ 0=e^{-hA}0+\left(\int_0^h e^{-sA}\,ds\right)f. \tag{19} \]

    The integral matrix is positive definite: its eigenvalues are h for the constant mode and (1-exp(-h*mu))/mu>0 for every positive eigenvalue mu of A. Thus f is zero on each of the fifteen plateaus. At command zero, this means that all baseline edge currents sum_n b_s,n,e equal one circulation, because the kernel of the oriented C5 incidence matrix is the constant edge vector.

    For an edge e define its command-dependent increments

    \[ E_e(q)=\sum_n b_{s,n,e}(\cos(nq)-1),\qquad O_e(q)=\sum_n b_{c,n,e}\sin(nq),\qquad n\in\{1,9,18\}. \tag{20} \]

    When a child is marked, only its two incident edges have nonzero commands, with opposite signs. The other three edges retain the baseline circulation. Zero divergence at the two nonzero plateau levels therefore gives E_e(epsilon)+O_e(epsilon)=E_e(epsilon)-O_e(epsilon)=0 on each incident edge. Every edge is incident to a marked child, so

    \[ E_e(\epsilon)=O_e(\epsilon)=0\quad\hbox{for every edge e}. \tag{21} \]

    Every child's first ramp starts at the recorded endpoint of its zero-level plateau and ends at the recorded beginning of its positive-level plateau. Both V values are zero. This includes child 4; its first ramp ends before the last reading. With local ramp time t in [0,1], put q(t)=epsilon*t^2*(3-2*t). After subtracting the zero-divergence baseline, the forcing is supported on the two incident incidence columns.

    The two distinct nonzero eigenvalues of A are

    \[ \mu_1=(5-\sqrt5)/8,\qquad \mu_2=(5+\sqrt5)/8. \tag{22} \]

    Each has a real two-dimensional Fourier eigenspace. The projections of two adjacent incidence columns onto either eigenspace are linearly independent: in complex Fourier coordinates their ratio is exp(plus_or_minus 2*pi*i*k/5), for k equal to 1 or 2, which is not real. The zero ramp endpoint consequently forces each incident edge's weighted forcing integral to vanish separately for each mu.

    Each edge is probed once from each endpoint in two child blocks. Their first ramps use the same q(t) and reverse that edge's command sign. Adding and subtracting the two integral equations therefore gives

    \[ \int_0^1 e^{-\mu_j(1-t)}E_e(q(t))\,dt=0,\qquad \int_0^1 e^{-\mu_j(1-t)}O_e(q(t))\,dt=0, \quad j=1,2. \tag{23} \]

    Only recorded endpoints and the known linear response between them were used. Neither derivatives nor extra within-ramp observations are supplied. Equations (21)-(23) reduce coefficient recovery on every edge to two three-by-three homogeneous systems. Their common matrix construction is

    \[ M_f=\begin{pmatrix} f_1(\epsilon)&f_9(\epsilon)&f_{18}(\epsilon)\\ \int_0^1 w_1(t)f_1(q(t))dt&\int_0^1 w_1(t)f_9(q(t))dt& \int_0^1 w_1(t)f_{18}(q(t))dt\\ \int_0^1 w_2(t)f_1(q(t))dt&\int_0^1 w_2(t)f_9(q(t))dt& \int_0^1 w_2(t)f_{18}(q(t))dt \end{pmatrix},\qquad w_j(t)=e^{-\mu_j(1-t)}, \tag{24} \]

    using either f_n(q)=sin(nq) or f_n(q)=1-cos(nq). The latter differs from (20) only by a common minus sign. Both matrices are nonsingular, as the following argument proves without computing their entries.

    Strict independence of both trigonometric triples¶

    For |z|<pi, the analytic function h(z)=z*cot(z) has expansion

    \[ h(z)=1-\sum_{m\ge1}a_m z^{2m},\qquad a_1=1/3,\qquad (2m+1)a_m=\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}a_k a_{m-k}>0\quad(m\ge2). \tag{25} \]

    The recurrence follows directly from z*h'=h-h^2-z^2; the first poles are at plus and minus pi. Thus for every positive d,

    \[ H_d(\sigma)=\sqrt\sigma\cot(d\sqrt\sigma) =\frac1d-\sum_{m\ge1}a_m d^{2m-1}\sigma^m \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_d'<0,\quad H_d''<0 \tag{26} \]

    on 0<d*sqrt(sigma)<pi, where the derivatives are with respect to sigma. Termwise differentiation is valid inside that convergence domain.

    First take f_n(q)=sin(nq), with 0<q<=epsilon=1/10 and n equal to 1, 9 or 18. Every f_n is positive because 18*epsilon=1.8<pi. For sigma=n^2, its logarithmic derivative and normalized second derivative are f_n'/f_n=H_q(sigma) and f_n''/f_n=-sigma. The first two-column Wronskian is negative by the strict decrease in (26). Factoring the positive functions from the three-column Wronskian leaves

    \[ \det\begin{pmatrix} 1&1&1\\ H_q(1)&H_q(81)&H_q(324)\\ -1&-81&-324 \end{pmatrix}\ne0, \tag{27} \]

    because strict concavity makes the three points on the graph of H_q noncollinear.

    For g_n(q)=1-cos(nq)=2*sin(nq/2)^2, every g_n is also positive and

    \[ g_n'/g_n=H_{q/2}(\sigma)=H,\qquad g_n''/g_n=(H^2-\sigma)/2. \tag{28} \]

    Again its first two-column Wronskian is negative. Invert the strictly decreasing map in (26) locally as sigma=sigma(H). Throughout the interval between its three values,

    \[ \sigma''(H)=-H_{q/2}''(\sigma)/(H_{q/2}'(\sigma))^3<0, \qquad \frac{d^2}{dH^2}(H^2-\sigma(H))>0. \tag{29} \]

    The three-column Wronskian is therefore nonzero by strict convexity of the last row in (28) as a function of the middle row.

    These Wronskian conditions imply that each triple has a nonzero evaluation determinant at any three distinct q values in (0,epsilon]. One direct Rolle argument is to divide a proposed three-zero linear combination by its positive first function. Put r=f_9/f_1 and s=f_18/f_1; r' never vanishes, and (s'/r')' is the three-column Wronskian divided by f_1^3*(r')^2, hence never zero. Applying Rolle twice forces the combination to be trivial. The evaluation determinant has a constant nonzero sign on the connected domain of ordered triples.

    The two weighted moments retain that independence¶

    Expanding (24) and interchanging its finite integrals gives exactly

    \[ \det M_f=\int_{0<t<u<1} [w_1(t)w_2(u)-w_1(u)w_2(t)] \det\begin{pmatrix} f_1(\epsilon)&f_9(\epsilon)&f_{18}(\epsilon)\\ f_1(q(t))&f_9(q(t))&f_{18}(q(t))\\ f_1(q(u))&f_9(q(u))&f_{18}(q(u)) \end{pmatrix}\,dt\,du. \tag{30} \]

    The weight bracket is strictly positive: mu2>mu1 and w2(t)/w1(t)=exp(-(mu2-mu1)*(1-t)) strictly increases. Also q(t) strictly increases on (0,1), so 0<q(t)<q(u)<epsilon on the integration domain. The evaluation determinant consequently has a constant nonzero sign. Thus (30) is nonzero for both bases. Equations (21)-(24) force all thirty edge coefficients to vanish. Together with the five initial forms this proves the claimed rank 35.

    This finite theorem strengthens the continuous formal injectivity result; it does not make the local collision in section 3 into a complete-history collision or transfer formal linear identifiability to nonlinear dynamics. No determinant lower bound, inverse norm, finite-error separation or numerical budget verdict follows from strict nonvanishing alone.

    6. Why the unconstrained global bootstrap cannot close the question¶

    This obstruction concerns a specified proof method, not the actual laws' record sets. It needs no coefficient-matrix evaluation, proposed inverse, nonlinear history or choice of an unknown initial state.

    One formal direction with a uniformly small response¶

    Set the five initial-form coefficients and all b_c coefficients to zero. Repeat the same b_s coefficients on every edge:

    \[ (a_1,a_9,a_{18})=(1,-323/19683,20/19683),\qquad b=(1,323/19683,20/19683). \tag{31} \]

    The vector b lists their three block infinity norms. Direct rational algebra gives

    \[ \sum_n a_n n^2=\sum_n a_n n^4=0,\qquad \sum_n|a_n|n^6=1+8721+34560=43282. \tag{32} \]

    The command-zero current is a constant circulation and has zero divergence. At any instant only one child c is marked, with scalar command q satisfying |q|<=epsilon. The two changed edge commands are opposite; cosine is even. Consequently the complete formal forcing is exactly

    \[ F(q)(e_{c-1}-e_{c+1}),\qquad F(q)=\sum_n a_n(\cos(nq)-1). \tag{33} \]

    Taylor's theorem through degree five, with (32), gives for the entire command range

    \[ |F(q)|\le f_*:=\frac{43282\epsilon^6}{720}. \tag{34} \]

    The dipole in (33) has zero sum and Euclidean norm sqrt(2). The smallest nonzero eigenvalue of L5/4 is greater than 1/3. Variation of constants, including every change of marked child, therefore yields

    \[ \sup_v\|V(v)\|_\infty \le\sqrt2 f_*\int_0^\infty e^{-s/3}\,ds <\frac92 f_* =:m=\frac{21641}{80000000}. \tag{35} \]

    This bounds the complete formal history and hence every actual sample. It does not assert a zero response; it is consistent with rank 35. The retained elementary remainder coefficient is strictly larger:

    \[ a=18D>m,\qquad D=4gX+2g^2J_1T. \tag{36} \]

    All quantities in (31)-(36) are fixed rational bounds. In particular, m=0.0002705125, whereas 18D>0.000304.

    Both scalar and block recovery inherit the obstruction¶

    Let M denote the exact sampled formal map. Any proposed recovery K of all thirty harmonic coefficients has row norm k and harmonic residual block norm e for J=[0 I]-KM. Applying that identity to (31), whose initial forms vanish and harmonic infinity norm is one, gives

    \[ 1\le e+k\|Mp\|_\infty\le e+km. \tag{37} \]

    A scalar bootstrap with remainder coefficient at least a would require 1-e-k*a>0. Equations (36)-(37) exclude this for every K, even one obtained from an exact inverse. Taking upper residual bounds cannot improve it.

    Keeping separate harmonic block norms also does not rescue this particular global envelope. Write B_n=max(||b_s,n||infinity,||b_c,n||infinity) for an arbitrary actual pair, and U_*=sup||u||infinity. Its last preceding sample is at most two units earlier, including the final unobserved ramp and tail. The static force and (17) then give

    \[ cU_*\le\delta+6\sum_n(1+nD)B_n,\qquad c=1-2g^2T>0. \]
    \[ R_*:=\sup\|u-V\|_\infty\le\sum_n A_nB_n+B_0\delta, \quad A_n=18nD+\frac{36g^2T(1+nD)}c,\quad B_0=\frac{6g^2T}c. \tag{38} \]

    Here delta=2*eta+E_0+E_1 retains both own-law source errors. For arbitrary block recovery matrices K_n, let k_n be their row norms and E_nm the nonnegative row-norm bounds of their residuals on harmonic block m. Their proposed componentwise bootstrap has feedback matrix

    \[ H=E+kA^T. \tag{39} \]

    Using the formal witness instead of an actual pair in the exact recovery identities gives b<=E*b+k*m. But A^T*b>=A_1>18D>m, so

    \[ Hb\ge b,\qquad b>0. \tag{40} \]

    Thus I-H cannot have a nonnegative inverse: otherwise multiplying (I-H)b<=0 by that inverse would imply b<=0. This excludes the positive block bootstrap for every choice of dual weights and their certified residual bounds. Positive diagonal changes of coefficient units preserve the same obstruction. A more accurate matrix or a different inverse cannot repair (38)-(39); a sharper correlated remainder or constrained argument is a different method and is not excluded.

    The obstruction is not an admitted cross-law history¶

    An actual alternative has ninth and eighteenth phasor radii r9=gamma/(4*2187) and r18=r9/2. In complex notation z_n=b_c,n+i*b_s,n, its coefficients on each edge satisfy

    \[ z_9=-r_9e^{9ia},\qquad z_{18}=(r_9/2)e^{18ia} =z_9^2/(2r_9). \tag{41} \]

    Both are tied to the same phase a that occurs in the fundamental cross-law difference. On each edge the witness has phasor modulus ratio 323/20, instead of 2, so even rescaling it cannot produce these actual coefficients. This uses its zero b_c coordinates; general block infinity norms need not have the same ratio as phasor moduli. It also supplies no pair of initial cycle phases or nonlinear histories. Its role is to prove a limitation introduced by allowing independent formal coefficients and independent norm remainders. Finite formal rank, exact local ambiguity and this obstruction do not by themselves decide the joint question. The constrained theorem resolves it under the same word, noise and horizon using additional joint phase restrictions and stronger contrasts.

    7. Constrained contrasts from recorded long-ramp endpoints¶

    The following construction avoids dividing a form difference by the short plateau duration. It cancels the formal initial-form response, constant forcing and fundamental harmonic before using the alternative's shared phase. It supplies a conditional finite separation criterion. The complete certificate in sections 9-11 instead uses the middle ramp as well, while retaining this construction as a control and reusable conditional result.

    An explicit functional of the unchanged readings¶

    Orient each edge as e -> e+1, with incidence column b_e=e_(e+1)-e_e. Choose edge 1 -> 2 and its two marked-child blocks, c=1,2. Let h_p=1e-5 and s_c=3*c*(1+h_p). The first and third ramps have recorded endpoints

    \[ a_{c,1}=s_c+h_p,\quad b_{c,1}=a_{c,1}+1,\qquad a_{c,3}=s_c+2+3h_p,\quad b_{c,3}=a_{c,3}+1. \tag{42} \]

    The third endpoint is the next child's zero-level plateau beginning. All eight endpoints in this construction belong to the existing thirty times. Use command-removed form Y, or a difference u of two carrier forms, for which the common command already cancels.

    For either positive eigenvalue mu in (22), let P_mu be its orthogonal spectral projector. Put Q_c,mu=[P_mu*b_(c-1),P_mu*b_c] and let R_c,mu,e be the row of its left inverse selecting edge e=1. Define

    \[ \mathcal T_{c,\mu,e}(w)=-R_{c,\mu,e} [w(b_{c,1})-e^{-\mu}w(a_{c,1}) -w(b_{c,3})+e^{-\mu}w(a_{c,3})]. \tag{43} \]

    The row already lies in the range of P_mu. Its norm is explicit: the Gram matrix of Q has diagonal d=8*mu/5 and off-diagonal d*(1-2*mu), with d*(1-(1-2*mu)^2)=1/2. Hence ||R_c,mu,e||2=sqrt(2) for both modes. Coordinate errors at most delta in the four inputs to (43) change its value by at most 2*sqrt(10)*(1+exp(-mu))*delta.

    For the frozen formal equation (16), the first-minus-third ramp difference cancels every constant edge current. Only the marked child's two incident edges remain, so (43) extracts their separate weighted current contrasts. The command on edge 1 is positive when child 2 is marked and negative when child 1 is marked. Therefore define its two parity moments by

    \[ \mathcal E_\mu(w)=-\frac{e^{\mu/2}}8 [\mathcal T_{2,\mu,1}(w)+\mathcal T_{1,\mu,1}(w)],\qquad \mathcal O_\mu(w)=\frac{e^{\mu/2}}8 [\mathcal T_{2,\mu,1}(w)-\mathcal T_{1,\mu,1}(w)]. \tag{44} \]

    Each has observation error at most sqrt(10)*cosh(mu/2)*delta<4*delta. This bound uses the recorded forms themselves, without estimating a plateau derivative or an unknown initial form.

    Positive kernels after removing the fundamental¶

    Write the centered ramp coordinate as r=t-1/2 and put xi(r)=epsilon*(3*r/2-2*r^3). On 0<=r<=1/2, xi increases from zero to epsilon/2. The first and third commands are epsilon/2+xi(r) and -epsilon/2+xi(r). Their trigonometric difference gives the exact kernels

    \[ C_{\mu,n}=\int_0^{1/2}\sinh(\mu r)\sin(n\xi(r))\,dr,\qquad D_{\mu,n}=\int_0^{1/2}\cosh(\mu r)\cos(n\xi(r))\,dr. \tag{45} \]

    For the formal history, (44) equals respectively sum_n b_s,n,e*sin(n*epsilon/2)*C_mu,n and sum_n b_c,n,e*sin(n*epsilon/2)*D_mu,n. Set

    \[ \alpha_E=C_{\mu_1,1}/C_{\mu_2,1},\qquad \alpha_O=D_{\mu_1,1}/D_{\mu_2,1},\qquad 0<\alpha_E,\alpha_O<1, \]
    \[ \mathcal E=\mathcal E_{\mu_1}-\alpha_E\mathcal E_{\mu_2},\qquad \mathcal O=\mathcal O_{\mu_1}-\alpha_O\mathcal O_{\mu_2}. \tag{46} \]

    The fundamental coefficients vanish exactly. The remaining coefficients are

    \[ K_{E,n}=\sin(n\epsilon/2)(C_{\mu_1,n}-\alpha_E C_{\mu_2,n}),\qquad K_{O,n}=\sin(n\epsilon/2)(D_{\mu_1,n}-\alpha_O D_{\mu_2,n}), \quad n=9,18. \tag{47} \]

    All four are strictly positive. Indeed, each numerator in (47) is an ordered double integral of a weight determinant times a function determinant. Both weight ratios sinh(mu2*r)/sinh(mu1*r) and cosh(mu2*r)/cosh(mu1*r) strictly increase for positive r. Both function ratios sin(n*x)/sin(x) and cos(n*x)/cos(x) strictly decrease for 0<x<=epsilon/2, where 18*x<=.9<pi/2. Consequently both determinants have the same positive orientation. This proves strict positivity without evaluating an integral.

    The same positive representation bounds their frequency ratios. For 0<x<y<=epsilon/2, the sine determinant sin(n*x)*sin(y)-sin(x)*sin(n*y) is (n^2-1)*sin(y) times the positive Dirichlet Green integral of sin(n*s) on [0,y], with operator -d^2/ds^2-1. Since sin(18*s)=2*sin(9*s)*cos(9*s), its n=9 to n=18 ratio is at most 40/(323*cos(.45)). For the cosine determinant use

    \[ \left(\frac{\cos(ns)}{\cos s}\right)' =-\frac{n^2-1}{\cos^2s}\int_0^s\cos(nt)\cos t\,dt. \tag{48} \]

    The n=9 to n=18 ratio is at most 80/(323*cos(.9)). Multiplying in each case by sin(.45)/sin(.9)=1/(2*cos(.45)) gives

    \[ 0<\rho_E:=K_{E,9}/K_{E,18} \le\frac{20}{323\cos^2(.45)}<\frac1{12},\qquad 0<\rho_O:=K_{O,9}/K_{O,18} \le\frac{40}{323\cos(.9)\cos(.45)}<\frac14. \tag{49} \]

    The strict rational comparisons already follow from cos(.45)>719/800 and cos(.9)>119/200. No sampled coefficient matrix or kernel magnitude is computed here.

    A finite certificate with explicit remaining obligations¶

    \[ \frac{2\mathcal E_{\rm fr}}{K_{E,18}} =r_9(\sin2\theta-2\rho_E\sin\theta),\qquad \frac{2\mathcal O_{\rm fr}}{K_{O,18}} =r_9(\cos2\theta-2\rho_O\cos\theta). \tag{50} \]

    For two histories sharing a noisy record, (13) bounds every input to (43) by delta. Their two final observation allowances are

    \[ N_E=\sqrt{10}[\cosh(\mu_1/2)+\alpha_E\cosh(\mu_2/2)]\delta<8\delta, \quad N_O=\sqrt{10}[\cosh(\mu_1/2)+\alpha_O\cosh(\mu_2/2)]\delta<8\delta. \tag{51} \]

    Suppose uniform, own-law arguments additionally establish |mathcal E(u)-mathcal E_fr|<=epsilon_E and |mathcal O(u)-mathcal O_fr|<=epsilon_O. A sufficient separation criterion for the actual record sets is then

    \[ \sqrt{\left(\frac{2(N_E+\epsilon_E)}{K_{E,18}}\right)^2+ \left(\frac{2(N_O+\epsilon_O)}{K_{O,18}}\right)^2} <\frac{r_9}{2}. \tag{52} \]

    This criterion alone supplies no passing verdict: it requires certified kernel magnitudes and actual-history departures from one shared reference phase. Reconstructing phase increments accurately does not make those increments zero: even known nonzero endogenous drift changes the frozen kernels. It must be incorporated into the contrasts or bounded, together with the uncertainty of the reconstruction and both laws' source errors. No trajectory, fit or reserved response is evaluated by either construction.

    8. The same form readings constrain historical phase transport¶

    The exact carrier phase row depends on the observed form history, not on an independently chosen phase velocity. This allows the initial phases to remain unknown while bounding their increments more tightly. This section uses only the historical interval 0<=v<=t_last=14+15/100000; it does not reconstruct the unobserved return ramp or the tail to time 16.

    Put A=L5/4 and w_lambda=gamma*S_lambda,5/4 in (7). Then

    \[ Y'=-AY+w_\lambda,\qquad \Psi'=\gamma AY,\qquad \|Y\|_\infty\le M_\lambda:=X+gJ_\lambda T/2. \tag{53} \]

    A bridge that retains arbitrary heat relaxation¶

    On each interval of length h between successive readings, define

    \[ Q(s)=\int_0^s e^{-rA}\,dr,\qquad V_h(s)=Q(s)Q(h)^{-1},\qquad \widehat Y(s)=(I-V_h(s))y_i+V_h(s)y_{i+1}. \tag{54} \]

    The matrix Q(h) is positive definite, including its constant-mode eigenvalue h. The eigenvalues of V_h(s) lie in [0,1]. This bridge exactly reproduces every solution with constant forcing, including arbitrary initial heat relaxation. It is an observation-based interpolation, not a new supplied evolution law. It need not reproduce the actual current. Joining the intervals yields one continuous form interpolant. Define the common edge-phase increment reference

    \[ \widehat d(v)=\gamma B^TA\int_0^v\widehat Y(s)\,ds. \tag{55} \]

    The initial circular edge phase is not reconstructed. For each law its actual carrier increment d_lambda=B^T(Psi_lambda(v)-Psi_lambda(0)) is compared with this same reference.

    With exact carrier endpoints, e=Y-hat Y obeys e''+Ae'=w_lambda', with zero values at both cell endpoints. For a spectral value 0<=mu<=1, let q_mu(s)=(1-exp(-mu*s))/mu, using s when mu is zero. The absolute scalar Dirichlet Green kernel is

    \[ G_\mu(t,s)= \frac{q_\mu(\min(t,s))q_\mu(h-\max(t,s))}{q_\mu(h)} e^{-\mu\max(t-s,0)}. \]

    For h at most one, q_mu(r)<=r and mu*h/(1-exp(-mu*h))<=1/(1-exp(-1))<8/5, with the continuous value one at mu zero. Consequently

    \[ 0\le G_\mu(t,s)\le\frac85G_0(t,s),\qquad \int_0^hG_0(t,s)\,dt=\frac{s(h-s)}2. \tag{56} \]

    The C5 geometry gives ||B||2<=2, ||A||2<=1, and each row of B^T A has squared Euclidean norm 5/4. The actual current derivative therefore satisfies, when the marked command level is q,

    \[ \|w_\lambda'\|_2\le \frac{\gamma L_\lambda}{2} (\sqrt2|q'|+2\gamma\sqrt5 M_\lambda). \tag{57} \]

    The bound uses B^T H', which has exactly two nonzero entries of opposite sign, and the complete phase row in (53). It is an own-law estimate, not an observed derivative.

    Separate reading, command and endogenous errors¶

    Thus a uniform historical bound is
