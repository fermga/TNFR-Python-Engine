    Collective response inherited from constituent deviations¶

    Analytical question and fixed comparison¶

    This comparison asks which collective response follows from the existing fine dynamics, instead of a separately supplied response matrix. The electrical protocol owns the complete five-child law and source. Its sine-covariance remainder, quadratic norm bound and uniform-child input/work pairing are starting results, not this question's answer. The ontology requires the effective object to retain its constituents' actual influence.

    An implicit hidden-state integral equation is not by itself a reduced solver or a finite-dimensional closure. Any approximation must retain its hidden initialization, input dependence and own residual. Earlier memory identities and source transport remain valid on their own models. This comparison neither derives primitive support nor establishes a new physical property.

    Result: nonlinear feedback without a carrier tangent kernel¶

    Declaration 78d460df fixes the comparison above. In its natural means and unchanged input map, no fixed positive-r response matrix is an exact all-input inheritance. The internal phase deviations instead generate a nonlinear current correction with a leading quadratic covariance term. Their evolution retains hidden initialization and collective phase history. On the exact carrier the hidden state stays zero, and the full tangent has no collective-hidden coupling. That zero tangent kernel does not remove the nonlinear correction away from the carrier.

    An explicit pair in the larger inference class has equal collective state, initial current correction, full storage and instantaneous loss, but opposite derivatives of that correction. This identifies information missing even after adding the instantaneous correction to the means. It is a local model obstruction, not a claim that both states were reached by the actual source preparation or that their finite responses were observed.

    1. Exact projected law and source domain¶

    Write L_f=I5 tensor L9+L5 tensor I9, P=RR^T/9, Q=I-P. The fixed template psi is repeated in each child. The observation gives U=R^T*x/9, Phi=R^T*(theta-psi)/9 on its declared continuous lift. Since L_f R=R L5 and Q L_f=L_f Q, the complete projected rows are

    \[ \begin{aligned} U'&=(-L_5U+\gamma S_5(\Phi))/4+f+\mathcal R(\Phi,z), &\Phi'&=\gamma L_5U/4,\\ u'&=-L_fu/4+(\gamma/4)Q S(\psi+R\Phi+z), &z'&=\gamma L_fu/4. \end{aligned} \tag{1} \]

    The exact correction is the one in the protocol, not an inferred external force. Uniform-child input obeys Q R f=0. This removes direct hidden forcing; it does not make the hidden dynamics independent of the collective phases. The input changes U and Phi, which enter its nonlinear sine map.

    For primitive bounds ||u0||<=a0, ||z0||<=b0, let g>=gamma, s=2gT<1, and retain the existing transport bounds

    \[ A=\frac{a_0+s b_0}{1-s^2},\qquad Z=\frac{b_0+s a_0}{1-s^2}. \tag{2} \]

    The shared transport rebuilds these from the primitive bounds. They apply to (1) because the heat part contracts, ||L_f||<=8, and the sine map is globally Lipschitz with that bound. Requiring 2Z<pi/2 retains the circular child observer. The earlier actual source satisfies its proved bounds under the matching law, support and input hypotheses. The identities below need no source replay. They do not assert that every point of its norm enclosure was formed. In particular, generic new input histories require their own outer-identity and physical observation arguments.

    2. Exact hidden-history dependence¶

    Put E(t)=exp(-t*L_f/4) and G(Phi,z)=Q S(psi+R Phi+z). Variation of constants in (1) gives

    \[ u(t)=E(t)u_0+\frac\gamma4\int_0^t E(t-s)G(\Phi(s),z(s))\,ds, \tag{3} \]
    \[ z(t)=z_0+\gamma[I-E(t)]u_0+ \frac{\gamma^2}{4}\int_0^t[I-E(t-s)]G(\Phi(s),z(s))\,ds. \tag{4} \]

    Equation (4) follows by integrating the live z row and exchanging the finite integrals in (3); integral_0^t L_f E(s) ds=4[I-E(t)] supplies its coefficient. No inverse on the common mode or phase reconstruction from a response is used. For a prescribed continuous Phi history the globally Lipschitz hidden rows have a unique solution. Therefore (4) defines an implicit causal functional z[Phi;u0,z0], and the actual mean correction is R(Phi(t),z[Phi;u0,z0](t)). Uniform input affects this functional through the coupled collective history. Setting either hidden initial term to zero without its preparation premise changes the problem.

    This is a model-specific reuse of hidden-state elimination. It still requires solving the hidden history. It is neither a known linear convolution of U nor a reduced numerical method, positive memory kernel, finite cutoff or new delayed operator. The new restrictions below concern this product support and its nonlinear feedback, beyond that generic identity.

    3. The invariant carrier and its decoupled tangent¶

    For every Phi, internal helical sine currents cancel and same-slot external currents are constant within each child, so G(Phi,0)=0. Thus u=z=0 is invariant for every admitted uniform-child input. Such an input cannot generate hidden departures from exactly zero in this law.

    At the carrier, let K(Phi)=-D S(psi+R Phi). Its exact form is

    \[ K(\Phi)=\cos(2\pi/9)(I_5\otimes L_9) +L_5^{\cos(\Phi)}\otimes I_9, \tag{5} \]

    where the weighted outer Laplacian uses the cosine of each current outer phase gap. It can be time dependent and need not be positive for arbitrary Phi. Both K and L_f commute with P and Q. Consequently R^T K Q=0 and Q K R=0: the full collective and hidden variational equations decouple along any carrier trajectory. The hidden tangent generator is

    \[ \mathcal A_h(\Phi)=\frac14 \begin{pmatrix}-L_f&-\gamma K(\Phi)\\ \gamma L_f&0\end{pmatrix} \quad\hbox{on }\operatorname{im}Q\oplus\operatorname{im}Q. \tag{6} \]

    Its state transition may depend on the entire carrier history. Nevertheless, both couplings to the collective tangent vanish, so linear elimination produces an identically zero collective memory kernel there. This is a symmetry result for the selected observation and input, not a general absence of memory or a transverse stability theorem. Freezing K or replacing its trigonometric coefficients by rationals would require a separate model or controlled approximation.

    4. The first nonlinear contribution and its retained information¶

    Orient a C5 edge c -> d and put alpha=Phi_d-Phi_c, d_j=z_dj-z_cj, m_cd=(1/9)sum_j d_j^2. Because sum_j d_j=0, Taylor's theorem gives

    \[ \frac19\sum_j\sin(\alpha+d_j)-\sin\alpha =-\tfrac12\sin\alpha\,m_{cd}+r_{cd},\qquad |r_{cd}|\le\frac1{54}\sum_j|d_j|^3. \tag{7} \]

    Choose incidence B with a -1 at each edge's tail and +1 at its head, so S5=-B sin(B^T Phi). The leading correction and its error satisfy

    \[ \mathcal R_2=\frac\gamma8 B [\sin(B^T\Phi)\mathbin\odot m],\qquad \|\mathcal R-\mathcal R_2\|_1 \le\frac\gamma{108}\sum_{\{c,d\},j}|d_j|^3 \le\frac{2\gamma}{27}Z^3. \tag{8} \]

    The last bound uses max|d_j|<=2Z and sum_edges,j d_j^2=z^T(L5 tensor I9)z<=4Z^2. It is a uniform pointwise error when the actual hidden state satisfies (2); it is not a forecast obtained from a tangent approximation to that state. The quadratic term may vanish when outer sines or the incidence sum cancel. At nonzero outer phase gaps it is generically present even though the carrier tangent kernel is zero. The edge variance contains correlations between constituent slots, rather than a freely fitted response coefficient.

    To see the next information obligation, write xi=(u,z) and C=xi xi^T. Along the hidden tangent, exactly

    \[ C'=\mathcal A_h C+C\mathcal A_h^T. \tag{9} \]

    For the actual nonlinear rows, an additional n xi^T+xi n^T is present, where n=((gamma/4)[G(Phi,z)+K(Phi)z],0)=O(||z||^2) on a bounded regular domain. Its contribution is of third or higher order in the joint hidden amplitude. The phase covariance block alone consumes the form-phase cross blocks: Czz'=(gamma/4)(L_f Cuz+Czu L_f). The same edge variances therefore do not close their own evolution. A covariance approximation needs its own propagated error, initial covariance and collective-history model; (9) alone is not an exact nonlinear moment closure or a dimension reduction.

    There is also an exact obstruction to replacing all hidden state by C. At U=0 and the outer winding-one twist, take u=0 and let child zero's hidden phases be (a,a,-2a,0,0,0,0,0,0), with other children zero and a=2^-40. Replacing z by -z preserves xi xi^T and R_2. However, mean_j sin(z_0j)=[2 sin a-sin(2a)]/9, so

    \[ \mathcal R_0(z)=-\frac\gamma9\cos(2\pi/5)\sin a(1-\cos a)<0, \qquad \mathcal R_0(-z)=-\mathcal R_0(z)>0. \tag{10} \]

    Both preparations lie in the same declared inference class. They have the same full second moment but different instantaneous collective correction. Thus second moments are a controlled approximation target, not an exact state representation for the nonlinear law. The cubic remainder in (8) retains precisely information that a covariance-only description can lose.

    5. Equal current correction, storage and loss, different acceleration¶

    Let kappa=2*pi/5, Phi_c=(c-2)*kappa, U=0 and h=(1,-1,0,0,0,0,0,0,0). Choose epsilon=2^-40, put z_0j=epsilon*h_j with all other children zero, and set u^+=z, u^-=-z. Use the same held zero input. These are algebraic states, not new source responses. They belong to the declared larger inference class, with zero common means, the same winding-one cycles and strict initial collars. No inclusion in the image of the actual acquired source is inferred.

    Both states have identical phases, U, Phi, R, U' and Phi'. Their exact correction is

    \[ \mathcal R=\frac{\gamma\sin\kappa(1-\cos\epsilon)}{18} (0,1,0,0,-1). \tag{11} \]

    Fine form storage is u^T L_f u/2=5epsilon^2 in both; phase storage agrees because the phases are identical. Also ||L_f u||^2/4=14epsilon^2 for both and the supplied work is zero. Thus total storage and instantaneous loss do not remove their ambiguity.

    The full phase row gives opposite hidden velocities, (z')^+ = gamma*L_f*z/4 and (z')^- = -(z')^+, while Phi'=0. For the edge from child 1 to child 0, (L9+3I)h=(6,-6,1,0,0,0,0,0,-1). Differentiating its actual sine average gives 6[cos(kappa-epsilon)-cos(kappa+epsilon)] =12 sin(kappa) sin(epsilon), hence

    \[ (\mathcal R'_1)^\pm =\pm\frac{\gamma^2\epsilon\sin\kappa\sin\epsilon}{12}, \qquad (U''_1)^+-(U''_1)^- =\frac{\gamma^2\epsilon\sin\kappa\sin\epsilon}{6}>0. \tag{12} \]

    Other terms in the differentiated mean row agree. Even the enlarged instantaneous observation (U,Phi,R,H_f,loss_f) therefore has no autonomous law valid for this whole class. The missing form-phase association determines how the same initial phase variance will evolve. This is a strict local derivative obstruction; no finite readout margin, time horizon for a resolved signal, or acquisition of this pair is claimed.

    6. Fixed matrix inheritance and actual work¶

    At any fixed admitted fine state, change the prescribed neutral input by d. The fine row in (1) changes by R d, and the observed mean derivative changes by exactly d. The proposed positive-r law in the same means and clock instead changes it by M_r d, with M_r=(I+r L5^2)^-1. Equality for every neutral d forces M_r=I on that subspace, hence r=0 because L5 is invertible there. A bounded input switch leaves the state continuous and tests this channel without assigning an artificial duration to an event.

    This obstruction holds even at an actual acquired state. It excludes exact all-input inheritance of the positive-r matrix under the fixed observation and input map; it does not exclude accidental equality for one history, a separately justified approximation, another chart, boundary actuation or a changed constitutive law. At r=0, the hidden correction still has to be retained away from the exact carrier.

    With H_f=x^T L_f x/2+sum_edges(1-cos Delta theta) and H_5=U^T L5 U/2+sum_C5(1-cos Delta Phi), the existing storage split and (1) give

    \[ \begin{aligned} H_f'&=-\tfrac94\|L_5U\|^2-\tfrac14\|L_fu\|^2 +9(L_5U)^Tf,\\ H_5'&=-\tfrac14\|L_5U\|^2+(L_5U)^T(f+\mathcal R),\\ R_H'&=-\tfrac14\|L_fu\|^2-9(L_5U)^T\mathcal R. \end{aligned} \tag{13} \]

    The correction exchanges signed storage between retained and hidden state. It supplies no additional external work and need not dissipate the collective storage by itself. R_H remains signed, rather than a nonnegative reserve. The matrix law has a different own storage; comparing them cannot replace this inherited balance or silently count the same interaction twice.

    Verification and remaining boundary¶

    The static controls reconstruct fine edges, projections, fields and chain-rule derivatives. They check the heat-integral normalization, tangent blocks, quadratic correction and cubic remainder, storage/work, input obstruction and the factored nonzero derivative witness. They reuse shared exact matrix and rational interval arithmetic. No new solver or public API is installed; the existing source transport remains the conditional norm owner.

    These results distinguish a derived nonlinear feedback mechanism from a supplied matrix law without identifying a physical force or selecting a unique fundamental dynamics. The finite compression result retains five initial interface variances and propagates their nonlinear forecast error under the same fine law. Its conditional intrinsic improvement does not supply those statistics or initial phases from the coarse source norm bounds, nor reduce measurement uncertainty. The preparation-aware initialization retains the original separate recovery bounds and known attachment means to admit a carried carrier prediction for the actual prepared family. The sole queue owns its numerical realization and prospective forecast.
