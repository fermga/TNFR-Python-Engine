    Certified endpoint observation of a prepared mobile encounter¶

    Scope: evaluate the one reserved cubic-response certificate declared in the frozen protocol. The complete law, source balls, source registration, clock, horizon and identity contract are unchanged. This is a detached calculation of an analytic Duhamel integral with a proved transfer to the complete nonlinear flow, not numerical integration of a nonlinear trajectory. It installs no new native flow or support event. The research program alone owns the next task.

    1. Exact finite Fourier representation¶

    Use the unitary transform hat u_k=N^-1/2 sum_j u_j exp(-2*pi*i*k*j/N). Let c=T/2, k0=N/4, b_m=1-|m|/Q and S2=sum_{|m|<Q} b_m^2. The nominal complex bookkeeping variable of source A has Fourier coefficients

    \[ \widehat z^A_{k_0+m}(0)=\frac{a b_m}{\sqrt{S_2}} e^{-2\pi i(k_0+m)c/N},\qquad |m|<Q, \]

    and zero coefficients elsewhere. The real channels are separately retained: on that positive band hat x=hat z/2, hat theta=hat z/(2i); the opposite coefficients follow by conjugacy of each real channel. Source B is the exact spatial reflection of A, so hat x^B_k=hat x^A_{-k} and likewise for phase. No extra relative phase is selected from the response.

    For lambda_k=1-cos(2*pi*k/N), the actual tangent block is

    \[ B_k=\lambda_k\begin{pmatrix}-\sigma&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \sigma=\pi e/w. \]

    With omega=sqrt(1-sigma^2/4)>0, its exact propagator is

    \[ U_k(s)=e^{-\sigma\lambda_k s/2} \left[\cos(\omega\lambda_k s)\,\mathrm{Id} +\frac{\sin(\omega\lambda_k s)}\omega \begin{pmatrix}-\sigma/2&-1\\1&\sigma/2\end{pmatrix}\right]. \tag{1} \]

    The instrument requires sigma*T<=1, in addition to its positive-loss law admission. This keeps every decay argument within the shared unit-exponential domain and ensures the square root is separated from zero. It is a numerical instrument domain, not a new restriction on TNFR generally. The fixed model satisfies it. Form and phase must not be replaced by a single scalar complex propagator: positive form loss mixes the two bookkeeping carrier directions.

    Take D theta_j=theta_j-theta_{j+1} and d_k=1-exp(2*pi*i*k/N). If alpha and beta are the Fourier coefficients of the two tangent edge-phase differences, the mixed form forcing is the cyclic convolution

    \[ \widehat g_k(s)=\frac{\overline d_k}{4N} [\alpha*\beta*(\alpha+\beta)]_k. \tag{2} \]

    The two unitary Fourier products each contribute N^-1/2; omitting this normalization would give an incorrect signal. The cubic integrand has separate form and phase channels U_k(T-s)*(hat g_k(s),0).

    Both tangent phase histories occupy only the two radius-15 bands. Equation (2) occupies the two radius-45 bands around +/-N/4 after modular folding, 182 modes in total. No cubic output mode is discarded. This finite support statement concerns the cubic predictor, not the nonlinear full flow. The instrument requires 3(Q-1)<N/4, N<=4096 and Q<=32 for this representation.

    Reflection exchanges A and B and preserves their mixed forcing, so g is reflection-even. Its Fourier coefficients are real and equal at opposite modes, as are those of the propagated integrand. The implementation intersects the two independently enclosed equal real coefficients and checks that the imaginary enclosures contain zero before retaining this exact symmetry. It retains both partners when applying Parseval's norm.

    2. Outward arithmetic and the integration error¶

    Elementary functions, square roots and final coefficient boxes use the shared 128-bit outward rational intervals. For polynomial convolution, their dyadic endpoints become exact integers on the same grid. All four endpoint products are enclosed, and integer sums are accumulated before a single outward projection per output coefficient. This is an optimization of a finite polynomial reduction, not an alternative interval arithmetic or a floating FFT. An opaque evaluation closure captures constants rebuilt from fresh primitive source and model inputs; externally supplied derived reports are not consumed. Trigonometric argument reduction subtracts an integer multiple of an outward pi/2 interval, then applies the exact quarter-turn identities and the shared sine/cosine owners to the residual. Choosing that integer from a midpoint is only a representation choice: the identity holds for every integer, including at uncertain quadrant boundaries. The residual interval retains pi uncertainty.

    Let qmax=2*pi*(Q-1)/N. On the input and output Fourier supports, respectively,

    \[ K_{\rm in}=(1+\sigma)(1+q_{\max}),\qquad K_{\rm out}=(1+\sigma)(1+3q_{\max}) \]

    bound the tangent operator norm: its rotational part has norm one and its loss part norm sigma, while 1-cos(pi/2+q)<=1+|q|. Actual tangent propagators are contractive. Each differentiated input has norm at most a*K_in^j. The trilinear forcing has norm at most 8*a^3, using ||D||<=2. Differentiating its three inputs and the final propagator gives, by the multinomial theorem, the whole-window integrand derivative bound

    \[ \|f^{(j)}(s)\|_2\le8a^3\Lambda^j, \qquad \Lambda=K_{\rm out}+3K_{\rm in} =(1+\sigma)(4+6q_{\max}). \tag{3} \]

    This retains the actual damped generator in every derivative. It does not infer an error from the difference of two sampled numerical answers.

    Use an n-point Gauss-Legendre rule on each of P equal panels. Its nodes are certified by exact rational sign bisection of the degree-n Legendre polynomial: n disjoint sign-changing intervals exhaust its roots. Outward weights are 2/[(1-r^2)*P_n'(r)^2]. The positive Peano kernel gives the same remainder constant for the Euclidean vector norm as for a scalar, with no dimension multiplier. Equations (3) and the Gauss remainder imply

    \[ E_G\le 8a^3 T\left(\frac{\Lambda T}{P}\right)^{2n} \frac{(n!)^4}{(2n+1)((2n)!)^3}. \tag{4} \]

    Choose n=12. Starting at one panel, double P until this prior bound is at most one tenth of eta_num=a^3/1000, or the frozen limit 65,536 prevents another doubling. This selection reads no integrand or response. For the fixed inputs, Lambda<23/5 and P=64 suffices; hence 768 integrand evaluations are prescribed before the response is known.

    Node, weight, initial coefficient, propagator and convolution rounding are retained in the accumulated spectral boxes. The Euclidean norm of their component radii bounds the vector arithmetic error. The separate outward Parseval norm interval retains norm-reduction uncertainty; the numerical certificate uses E_G plus the larger of these two arithmetic allowances. All source materialization is included. The final cubic norm interval expands the quadrature norm interval by E_G. If the total error exceeds eta_num, numerical certification is unavailable even if a computed lower bound is positive. There is no automatic higher-precision retry.

    \[ [\max(0,l-K_5),\ u+K_5], \]

    The two lower-bound tests remain separate. Their scientific certificates also require the declared identity bounds and successful numerical certification.
