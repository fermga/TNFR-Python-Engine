    Complete-law admission for slow receiver acquisition¶

    The unchanged two-ramp preparation admits an exact second-order phase law and a uniformly controlled first-order velocity approximation after its initial form layer. At structural time 400, every noncentral receiver phase actually moves in the direction of the donor ramp. Its slow-phase rate has magnitude greater than \(3/20\), with the sign of \(j-4\). This strengthens the earlier mixed-coordinate current result: the receiver's primitive phase, rather than only \(\theta+\gamma x\), has the stated instantaneous motion.

    For every later time, the complete phase velocity differs from the sine descent field by less than \(11\times10^{-6}\) in Euclidean norm. This uniform velocity bound does not identify the full trajectory with a phase-only trajectory. It supplies a source-aware differential inclusion and a precise corridor and terminal-capture contract. These estimates alone do not establish receiver acquisition, continued donor retention or joined capture. The subsequent frozen enclosure certifies loss of both donors while the receiver stays at winding zero throughout the validated interval. Later acquisition or regeneration remains unresolved. This admission evaluates no reference trajectory or reserved response.

    Retained source, law and identity¶

    The complete state, support and preparation are those of the receiver-mechanism owner. There are 27 labelled nodes, three unit C9 supports and the oriented contacts \((0,1),(1,2),(2,0)\). Each contact uses offsets zero and one with weights one half. All support is present at preparation; its weighted Laplacian is \(L\), and every node has weighted degree four. Capacities remain one. The structural clocks are

    \[ \tau=(1023/1024)t,\qquad s=\epsilon\tau,\qquad \gamma=1/(1023\pi),\qquad \epsilon=\gamma^2. \tag{1} \]

    These are fixed structural units, without a laboratory-time identification. Write

    \[ A=L/4,\qquad S_i(\theta)=\sum_jw_{ij}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i), \qquad F(\theta)=S(\theta)/4 . \tag{2} \]

    The actual detached weighted sine law is

    \[ x_\tau=-Ax+\gamma F(\theta),\qquad \theta_\tau=\gamma Ax . \tag{3} \]

    No forcing, state reset, support event or evolving conductance is added. This is neither native unique-neighbor phase pressure nor execution of a registered operator. The nodal identity alone does not select (3).

    Let \(q_j=j-4\), \(\alpha=2\pi/9\), and let \(Q\) contain \(q\) in regions zero and one and zero in region two. Retain

    \[ m=\frac{2046}{9}(355/113)^2,\quad x_0=mQ+\eta_x,\quad \theta_0=\eta_\theta, \quad |\eta_{x,i}|\le r_x=10^{-10},\quad |\eta_{\theta,i}|\le r_\theta=10^{-10}. \tag{4} \]

    Both errors have separate exact zero sums within each original C9. Every allowed combination is included. Only the global means remain conserved after evolution begins. The common phase origin and continuous lifts remain fixed. All 54 fine coordinates, boundary contacts and the full preparation cost are retained. The nominal storage is \(150m^2\), including \(78m^2\) on contacts; its error allowance and the regional boundary-work identity remain in the linked source owner.

    The relevant identity is principal-difference winding around each marked C9. The earlier theorem establishes winding \((1,1,0)\) at \(\tau=200\) and excludes any receiver branch crossing throughout its proved initial delay. Neither that exclusion nor a directed phase velocity specifies an eventual acquired identity. This retained constituent and boundary state follows the NFR ontology.

    Exact second-order phase representation¶

    All vectors below lie on the global mean-zero leaf. The actual connected support makes \(A\) positive definite there, with

    \[ A\succeq gI,\quad g=1/10,\qquad \|A\|_2\le2, \qquad \|F\|_2\le M=\sqrt{27},\quad \|DF\|_2\le K=2 . \tag{5} \]

    The last inequality follows from the symmetric weighted cosine Laplacian: \(DF=-B\operatorname{diag}(w_e\cos\delta_e)B^T/4\), whose absolute quadratic form is bounded by that of \(L/4\). These bounds hold globally in the supplied continuous lifts, including phase-branch passages. They require no all-time acute assumption.

    Put \(v=\theta_s\). Equation (3) gives an invertible representation on this leaf:

    \[ v=Ax/\gamma,\qquad x=\gamma A^{-1}v,\qquad \boxed{\epsilon A^{-1}\theta_{ss}+\theta_s=F(\theta).} \tag{6} \]

    Indeed, differentiating \(\theta_\tau=\gamma Ax\), substituting the form row and changing both derivatives to the same clock yields \(\epsilon\theta_{ss}=A(F-\theta_s)\). Conversely, (6) and the reconstruction of \(x\) give both original rows. The initial velocity is fixed by the original source:

    \[ \theta(0)=\eta_\theta,\qquad \theta_s(0)=\frac{A(mQ+\eta_x)}{\gamma}. \tag{7} \]

    It is not a freely reset slow initial condition. Form memory is retained as this velocity. If a nonzero global form mean were admitted, its conserved value would also have to be retained separately; it is zero under (4).

    Let

    \[ U(\theta)=\sum_{\{i,j\}}w_{ij}[1-\cos(\theta_j-\theta_i)], \qquad H=\tfrac12x^TLx+U(\theta). \]

    Since \(\nabla U=-4F\), the same storage becomes

    \[ \mathcal E:=H/4 =\frac\epsilon2v^TA^{-1}v+\frac14U(\theta),\qquad \boxed{\mathcal E_s=-\|v\|_2^2.} \tag{8} \]

    Multiplication of (6) by \(v\) proves (8); it is the original loss identity in the new coordinates and clock. The matrix \(\epsilon A^{-1}\) is a derived structural coefficient for this model. Its occurrence does not identify an inertial mass, mechanical energy or a physical equation. Dropping the second derivative changes the model and needs an error bound.

    Uniform defect from the phase-descent field¶

    Define the actual velocity defect

    \[ r=v-F(\theta) =\frac{Lx-\gamma S(\theta)}{4\gamma} =-\frac{x_\tau}{\gamma}. \tag{9} \]

    This is a consequence of both declared rows, not a pressure reconstructed from the response. Differentiating in structural time gives the exact law

    \[ r_\tau=-[A+\epsilon DF(\theta)]r -\epsilon DF(\theta)F(\theta). \tag{10} \]

    Both \(r\) and the forcing in (10) are globally mean zero. For \(\lambda=g-K\epsilon>0\), the Euclidean norm therefore satisfies

    \[ D^+\|r\|_2\le-\lambda\|r\|_2+\epsilon KM. \tag{11} \]

    For nonzero \(r\), this follows by pairing (10) with \(r/\|r\|_2\) and using (5); the upper right derivative extends the inequality through zero.

    The source gradient from the actual weighted support satisfies \(\|LQ\|_2^2=2277/2\). Since \(F(0)=0\), a full-source initial bound is

    \[ R_0=\frac{m\sqrt{2277/2}}{4\gamma} +\frac{2\sqrt{27}\,r_x}{\gamma} +2\sqrt{27}\,r_\theta\ \ge\ \|r(0)\|_2 . \tag{12} \]

    It retains the two independent errors and does not replace the original source by a recovered or stationary state. Solving (11) gives

    \[ \|r(\tau)\|_2\le R(\tau):= R_0e^{-\lambda\tau} +\frac{\epsilon KM}{\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda\tau}). \tag{13} \]

    In particular, the simpler monotone upper envelope \(R_0e^{-\lambda\tau}+\epsilon KM/\lambda\) is available without deciding whether \(R_0\) exceeds the asymptotic bound.

    At the analytic release time \(\tau_0=400\), outward rational enclosures give

    \[ R_0e^{-400\lambda}+\frac{\epsilon KM}{\lambda} <\rho:=\frac{11}{10^6}. \tag{14} \]

    The left side is approximately \(0.000010061734672\). It decreases thereafter, so the complete law satisfies, for every \(\tau\ge400\),

    \[ \boxed{\|\theta_s-F(\theta)\|_2<\rho.} \tag{15} \]

    No phase trajectory was evaluated to choose (14). Time 400 is an analytic release for a uniform estimate, not a reserved acquisition deadline. For other nonnegative primitive error allowances, (12)--(14) must be recomputed and may be unavailable. The original errors in (4) supply the frozen conclusion.

    The bound is uniform on velocity defect, not on distance to any chosen first-order trajectory. Small persistent defects can accumulate or act across a basin boundary. It would be invalid to replace (15) by an infinite-time shadowing or common-attractor assertion.

    The actual phase potential nevertheless obeys the useful inequality

    \[ U_s=-4F(\theta)\mathbin{\cdot}[F(\theta)+r] \le-4\|F(\theta)\|_2\,[\|F(\theta)\|_2-\rho]. \]

    Thus it strictly decreases wherever \(\|F\|_2>\rho\) after release. Near the critical set this inequality is inconclusive; the complete storage (8) remains the exact monotone quantity everywhere.

    Actual receiver phase motion at release¶

    The source-controlled transport estimate in the mechanism proof applies at any declared time. Set \(P=\alpha Q\), and define

    \[ \begin{split} A_0&=\gamma(m\sqrt{120}+\sqrt{27}\,r_x),\\ B_0&=e^{-40}A_0+10\epsilon\sqrt{27},\\ b_0&=\sqrt{120}|\gamma m-\alpha| +\sqrt{27}(r_\theta+\gamma r_x) +B_0+400\epsilon\sqrt{27}. \end{split} \tag{16} \]

    Then

    \[ \|\gamma x(400)\|_2\le B_0,\qquad \|\theta(400)-P\|_2\le b_0<21/100000 . \tag{17} \]

    The phase radius is approximately \(0.000207559164\). The rational ramp mismatch remains explicit: \(355/113\) is not set equal to \(\pi\). At release the donor principal internal differences are \(\alpha\) plus at most \(\sqrt2b_0\), while receiver differences have magnitude at most \(\sqrt2b_0\). Their respective windings are therefore still \((1,1,0)\). More strongly, for every \(200\le\tau\le400\), the phase radius is bounded by

    \[ \sqrt{120}|\gamma m-\alpha| +\sqrt{27}(r_\theta+\gamma r_x) +e^{-20}A_0+10\epsilon\sqrt{27}+400\epsilon\sqrt{27}. \]

    This also has positive donor branch margin. Thus neither donor loses its acquired winding between its certified time-200 state and release. The earlier receiver-delay theorem already covers that whole interval.

    The exact reference receiver current is

    \[ F_{2,j}(P)=\tfrac14(1+\cos\alpha)\sin(\alpha(j-4)). \tag{18} \]

    The degree-four support and the sine Lipschitz bound give, nodewise,

    \[ |F_i(\theta(400))-F_i(P)|\le\sqrt2b_0 . \tag{19} \]

    For all eight noncentral receiver nodes, (15), (18) and (19) imply

    \[ \operatorname{sign}(j-4)\,\theta_{s,2,j}(400) >\tfrac14(1+\cos\alpha)\sin(\pi/9)-\sqrt2b_0-\rho >\frac3{20}. \tag{20} \]

    The estimate keeps every permitted source error, so it is not restricted to the exactly symmetric nominal source. No sign is claimed for the central receiver node. In structural time the rate bound is multiplied by \(\epsilon\), and in original time also by \(1023/1024\). Positive instantaneous phase motion does not guarantee a branch crossing, monotone motion at later times or retained donor identity thereafter.

    Source-aware corridor and terminal-capture contract¶

    Use elapsed slow time \(u=\epsilon(\tau-400)\). The actual post-release phase is an absolutely continuous solution of the inclusion

    \[ \theta_u=F(\theta)+r(u),\qquad \|r(u)\|_2<\rho, \qquad \|\theta(0)-P\|_2\le b_0. \tag{21} \]

    The error \(r\) is the dynamically constrained defect (9), not a supplied controller. The inclusion is an outer description: proving a property for every admissible disturbance proves it for the full law, while an unsuccessful inclusion bound does not refute acquisition.

    For example, let a proposed differentiable phase corridor be described by \(h_k(\theta)\le0\). A strict outward-boundary estimate

    \[ \nabla h_k(\theta)\mathbin{\cdot}F(\theta) +\rho\|\nabla h_k(\theta)\|_2<0 \tag{22} \]

    on each active boundary, together with source inclusion, prevents a first exit of the actual phase. A receiver-acquisition argument would also need progress to a declared terminal set; invariance alone supplies no entry or finite passage time. A directed phase or winding observation does not replace this boundary and progress proof.

    A separately admitted finite reference can instead provide a tube. On an interval of slow length \(h\), let \(w(u)\) have global mean zero, continuous declared lifts, and a rigorously bounded residual \(\|w_u-F(w)\|_2\le\eta\). Suppose both the reference and its prospective error ball lie in a convex domain \(D\) within the conserved global mean-zero phase leaf, and freshly bound

    \[ \mu\ge\sup_{z\in D} \lambda_{\max}\bigl(DF(z)|_{\mathbf1^\perp}\bigr). \tag{23} \]

    The symmetric Jacobian preserves \(\mathbf1^\perp\). The restriction retains the actual error directions and permits a negative relative growth bound; the full 27-coordinate Jacobian always has the common-phase zero mode. Every error ball below is taken within the same mean-zero leaf. If the incoming phase error is at most \(e_0\), then a sufficient whole-interval error envelope is

    \[ e(u)=e^{\mu u}e_0+(\rho+\eta)\begin{cases} (e^{\mu u}-1)/\mu,&\mu\ne0,\\ u,&\mu=0. \end{cases} \tag{24} \]

    The proof pairs the error equation with its normalized error, uses the signed logarithmic bound (23), and integrates its scalar inequality. Strict inclusion of \(w(u)+\overline B_{e(u)}\) in \(D\) closes the first-exit argument; a presumed future tube cannot certify its own premise. The global choice \(\mu=K=2\) is always available, but sharper local bounds may be essential. At the first interval take \(w(0)=P\), \(e_0=b_0\); later intervals inherit the complete preceding endpoint error, without resetting it to zero. Any residual, state, lift, Jacobian or source premise must be re-admitted rather than copied from a report's cached verdict.

    Throughout that finite transport, each donor's fixed registered internal edge must satisfy

    \[ |\delta_e(w(u))|+\sqrt2e(u)<\pi . \tag{25} \]

    The seam integers are the winding-one donor integers admitted at release. Equation (25) retains each donor identity continuously. The receiver must be allowed to cross its initial principal branch; an imposed all-time acute receiver cell would exclude the outcome being tested.

    For terminal capture, use the equal-support target \(\Theta=\alpha(q,q,q)\) and the full-state barrier from the capture owner. The full form remains bounded for every \(\tau\ge400\) by \(\|x(\tau)\|_2\le X_0:=B_0/\gamma\). At a proposed terminal reference \(w_*\), with certified phase error \(e_*\), require both

    \[ \max_{\text{fine }e}|\delta_e(w_*)|+\sqrt2e_*<2, \tag{26} \]

    and

    \[ \boxed{ U(w_*)-U(\Theta)+\|\nabla U(w_*)\|_2e_* +4e_*^2+4X_0^2<196/625 .} \tag{27} \]

    All edge integers in (26) are the fixed integers registered to \(\Theta\), including the receiver and contact edges. The potential and gradient in (27) use the actual complete weighted support. The global Hessian bound \(\|\nabla^2U\|_2\le8\) proves the phase remainder \(4e_*^2\); the same Laplacian bound proves the form contribution \(4X_0^2\). The linear gradient term generally does not vanish and must be retained. A phase-only energy or separately passing marginal budgets cannot replace (26)--(27) on the same state.

    If a source-admitted corridor or finite tube proves donor retention and (26)--(27), the actual full state enters the joined trapping sublevel. The existing barrier then proves winding one in every constituent for all future time and convergence to the equal registered organization. No such corridor or endpoint is asserted here. The contract identifies the remaining nonlinear estimate without changing the source, law or clock.

    The implementation rebuilds the primitive source, support, gradient norm, release transport and velocity-defect inequalities. It uses the shared preparation kernel and outward rational arithmetic. Its report certifies the reduction and release motion only; it accepts no trajectory or capture verdict. The execution plan remains the sole queue. A later numerical response must have its horizon, observation, source and budget frozen before evaluation. Supplied donor preparation, support and constitutive law remain premises of every result above.
