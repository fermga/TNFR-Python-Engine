    Composing finite contact responses under a held sine law¶

    Two separately predicted contact responses need not add to the joint response, even when every evolution is linear in its state. This chapter bounds that composition error for the complete positive-capacity D/4 sine law. The error bound is proportional to the product of the two contact conductances on each fixed finite window. Exact controls distinguish shared ports, independent contacts and communication through old support. The result concerns prediction of a supplied interaction, not autonomous attachment or the formation of a new joint organization.

    The single-contact comparison already retains a common full initialization. The repeated-composition theorem already treats registered multi-edge interfaces under its quarter-sine premises. The causal substitution theorem retains feedback under a different degree-normalized law. The new question is the error of adding two separately observed contact responses while holding the complete source and capacity vector fixed across four branches. These precedents supply methods, not transferred coefficients or verdicts.

    1. Four complete branches and one constituent observation¶

    Use one finite node set, a fixed symmetric nonnegative old weighted support, and two fixed undirected contact edges with incidence vectors \(b_1,b_2\). Their endpoints may share a node or be distinct. For held parameters \(p,q\ge0\), write

    \[ L_{p,q}=L_0+pL_1+qL_2,\qquad L_i=b_ib_i^T, \qquad S_{p,q}(\theta)=S_0(\theta)+pS_1(\theta)+qS_2(\theta), \tag{1} \]

    where each sine current uses exactly the same conductances as its Laplacian. Each \(S_i\) is the unit-conductance current on its edge. On every branch prescribe

    \[ D=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_i),\quad \nu_i>0,\qquad x'=D[-L_{p,q}x+\gamma S_{p,q}(\theta)]/4, \quad \theta'=\gamma DL_{p,q}x/4, \quad \nu'=0. \tag{2} \]

    Capacities, \(\gamma\ge0\), old conductances and the structural clock are held. The complete initial signed form and continuous phase lift are the same for every \((p,q)\), including all unobserved coordinates. There is no later input, event or conductance change. The state and its capacity vector can vary between members of an admitted family; matching is within each member, not equality to one fitted nominal preparation.

    The four branches have parameter pairs \((0,0),(w_1,0),(0,w_2),(w_1,w_2)\), abbreviated \(0,1,2,12\). For a fixed nonempty constituent \(A\), define

    \[ m^{\rm a}=\frac1{|A|}\sum_{i\in A}x_i, \qquad \Delta_{\rm a}(h)=m_{12}^{\rm a}(h)-m_1^{\rm a}(h) -m_2^{\rm a}(h)+m_0^{\rm a}(h). \tag{3} \]

    This is the joint matched response minus the two separate matched responses: the common disconnected baseline has coefficient plus one. All initial terms cancel because the four complete preparations match. It is not a comparison of independently selected members of state boxes.

    Supply bounds \(0<m\le\nu_i\le M\) for every node, \(\|x(0)\|_\infty\le B_0\), \(0\le\gamma\le g\), and a degree ceiling \(K\) for the support with both contacts at their upper weights. Nonnegative weights make this a degree ceiling on the whole parameter rectangle. Merely checking \(K\ge\max(W_{1,\rm hi},W_{2,\rm hi})\) is necessary scalar admission, not authentication of the actual degree: shared ports consume the sum of incident weights. Requiring that sum at every node instead would unnecessarily exclude valid distinct-port supports. The actual old support, endpoint association, capacity vector, constituent membership and common initialization remain caller premises.

    Zero contact conductance removes that edge's contribution from both rows of (2). This differs from the native zero-relation boundary, where a bare-neighbor phase channel can retain a zero-conductance relation. No result here transfers to a different support or degree-normalization law.

    2. Full-state sensitivity to either contact¶

    Finite weighted graphs give globally Lipschitz lifted vector fields in (2): the form dependence is linear and sine has bounded derivatives. Complete solutions exist on every finite window and depend smoothly on both parameters. The same initial state is parameter-independent. Thus first and mixed parameter derivatives start at zero, including at the nonnegative rectangle boundary by restriction of the smooth family.

    Put \(k=MK\) and \(\alpha=gk/2\). As in the single-contact proof, \(-DL_{p,q}/4\) generates a maximum-norm contracting Markov semigroup, and every branch has

    \[ \|x(t)\|_\infty\le B(t)=B_0+\frac{kg}{4}t. \tag{4} \]

    At a fixed parameter pair, write \(\xi_j=\partial_jx,\eta_j=\partial_j\theta\), and \(u_j=\|\xi_j\|_\infty,v_j=\|\eta_j\|_\infty\). Differentiation in contact \(j\) gives

    \[ \begin{aligned} \xi_j'&=-DL\xi_j/4+\gamma D\,\mathrm DS(\theta)\eta_j/4 +D[-L_jx+\gamma S_j(\theta)]/4,\\ \eta_j'&=\gamma DL\xi_j/4+\gamma DL_jx/4. \end{aligned} \tag{5} \]

    Here \(L,S\) denote the current complete support, and \(\mathrm D\) denotes differentiation with respect to phase. The first derivative therefore satisfies

    \[ \begin{aligned} u_j(t)&\le\int_0^t[\alpha v_j(s)+M(2B(s)+g)/4]ds,\\ v_j(t)&\le\int_0^t[\alpha u_j(s)+MgB(s)/2]ds. \end{aligned} \tag{6} \]

    Both rows and both direct edge-forcing terms are retained. No form mean, internal mode or phase feedback has been set to zero.

    Define

    \[ b_0=M\left[\frac{(1+g)B_0}{2}+\frac g4\right],\qquad b_1=\frac{M(1+g)kg}{8},\qquad P(t)=b_0t+\frac{b_1t^2}{2}. \]

    The scalar integral comparison for \(u_j+v_j\) gives, uniformly on the parameter rectangle and \(0\le t\le h\),

    \[ u_j(t)+v_j(t)\le e^{\alpha t}P(t)\le G P(t), \qquad G\ge e^{\alpha h}. \tag{7} \]

    These are numerical product norms in the fixed form, phase and clock normalization of (2). Adding the bounds does not identify physical units or define a new structural observable. A unit change must transform both rows and their coefficient bounds before the comparison is reused.

    3. Mixed sensitivity retains contact feedback and sine curvature¶

    Let \(\chi=\partial_1\partial_2x\) and \(\psi=\partial_1\partial_2\theta\). Differentiating (5) again gives

    \[ \begin{aligned} \chi'={}&-DL\chi/4+\gamma D\,\mathrm DS(\theta)\psi/4 +\gamma D\,\mathrm D^2S(\theta)[\eta_1,\eta_2]/4\\ &+D[-L_1\xi_2-L_2\xi_1 +\gamma\mathrm DS_1(\theta)\eta_2 +\gamma\mathrm DS_2(\theta)\eta_1]/4,\\ \psi'={}&\gamma D[L\chi+L_1\xi_2+L_2\xi_1]/4. \end{aligned} \tag{8} \]

    The two edge-Jacobian terms survive when \(\gamma=0\); they describe how one contact changes the state on which the other acts. The sine Hessian is a separate forcing term. Dropping either contribution would change the mixed response being bounded.

    For each old or new edge, phase increments are at most twice the nodal maximum. The complete sine Hessian consequently obeys

    \[ \|\mathrm D^2S(\theta)[\eta_1,\eta_2]\|_\infty \le4K v_1v_2. \]

    After multiplication by \(\gamma D/4\), its form forcing is at most \(gk v_1v_2\). A unit edge has Laplacian norm at most two, and its sine Jacobian has the same bound. Each edge's combined form/phase forcing from the other sensitivity is therefore at most

    \[ \frac M2[(1+g)u_j+g v_j] \le\frac{M(1+g)}2(u_j+v_j). \]

    If \(z=\|\chi\|_\infty+\|\psi\|_\infty\), heat contraction, (7) and the zero initial mixed derivative give

    \[ z(t)\le\int_0^t\left[\alpha z(s) +M(1+g)G P(s)+gkG^2P(s)^2\right]ds. \tag{9} \]

    The two polynomial integrals are

    \[ A_1=\int_0^hP(s)ds=\frac{b_0h^2}{2}+\frac{b_1h^3}{6}, \qquad A_2=\int_0^hP(s)^2ds= \frac{b_0^2h^3}{3}+\frac{b_0b_1h^4}{4}+\frac{b_1^2h^5}{20}. \]

    A second scalar comparison proves

    \[ z(h)\le C(h):=M(1+g)G^2A_1+gkG^3A_2. \tag{10} \]

    The first term retains contact feedback; the second retains the complete sine curvature. This estimate uses no acute chart, small-horizon condition or disjoint-port assumption. It can become conservative on long windows.

    Apply the fundamental theorem of calculus in both parameters to the full state \(y=(x,\theta)\):

    \[ y(w_1,w_2)-y(w_1,0)-y(0,w_2)+y(0,0) =\int_0^{w_1}\int_0^{w_2}\partial_1\partial_2y(p,q)\,dq\,dp. \]

    Hence the complete mixed-state product norm and the arithmetic observation are bounded by

    \[ \|\Delta x\|_\infty+\|\Delta\theta\|_\infty\le E(h):=W_{1,\rm hi}W_{2,\rm hi}C(h),\qquad |\Delta_{\rm a}(h)|\le E(h). \tag{11} \]

    The same formulas with an earlier endpoint bound the whole window. For fixed finite background bounds and horizon, the deterministic allowance is of product order in the two conductances. It is exactly zero if either upper weight is zero, even when the other contact or old support drives substantial motion. This conclusion would not follow by subtracting two independent first-order state boxes.

    4. Separate weighted readings, noise and representation¶

    The actual inverse-capacity mean of the same constituent is

    \[ m^\rho=\sum_{i\in A}\rho_i x_i,\qquad \rho_i=\frac{1/\nu_i}{\sum_{j\in A}1/\nu_j}>0, \qquad\sum_{i\in A}\rho_i=1. \]

    Its weights are fixed across the four branches for each family member. Both the arithmetic and this weighted observation have norm one on the form maximum norm, so \(|\Delta_\rho(h)|\le E(h)\) as well. The observations need not have equal values. Their common universal allowance does not use a donor count, port capacity, internal-capacity contrast or inverse-capacity mass. Those quantities are unnecessary inputs to this norm projection; actual membership and normalization still need association. There is no autonomous equation for either constituent mean.

    For either observation, use its own four final-time absolute reading-error radii \(\epsilon_{12},\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_0\). With \(e=\epsilon_{12}+\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2+\epsilon_0\),

    \[ \Delta\in[-E,E],\qquad\widehat\Delta\in[-E-e,E+e]. \tag{12} \]

    The coefficients are \((+1,-1,-1,+1)\); arbitrary correlations are allowed. Initial-value or baseline errors in a different increment-based instrument require their own association. Algebraic cancellation of the true initial state does not cancel unrelated measurement errors.
