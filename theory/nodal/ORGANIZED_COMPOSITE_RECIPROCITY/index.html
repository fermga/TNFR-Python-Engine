    Carried regional prediction through a reciprocal finite contact¶

    The regional-boundary result initializes a useful finite predictor from one associated regional reading. Its two reference means then evolve under a supplied leak. This chapter asks whether those means and their accumulated errors can be carried into contact with another responding composite, without resetting either actual state or making a new observation at contact entry.

    The result is conditional on the same actual-source associations and complete degree-loss law. A four-mean reference predicts outgoing form transfer within a fixed error. Two actual partner histories show why omitting the partner's regional information loses this prediction. This is approximate task-specific prediction, not an exact reduced NFR, autonomous contact selection or acquisition of a new joint identity.

    External interaction, interface factorization and finite auxiliary exchange already treat reacting partners on other complete models. Preparation-informed initialization and computable prediction already distinguish carried references from replacement of an actual state. The present result supplies this regional predictor's continuation; their coefficients and source certificates are not transferred.

    1. Actual sources and the complete supplied contact¶

    For \(p=1,2\), retain an independently associated connected eighteen-node conditional UM successor. Each has eighty-one held internal edges, degree nine, unit capacities, eighteen old unit conductances and sixty-three actual proposed conductances. Its regions \(A_p,B_p\) have nine nodes each. The winding-one and winding-two constituents, their registration and actual conductance associations remain part of the preparation.

    Write \(H_p=L_{W_p}\), and let \(S_p\) be the unweighted pairwise sine current on that same support. Both copies use the same structural clock:

    \[ x_p'=\frac{-H_px_p+\gamma S_p(\theta_p)}9+f_p,\qquad \theta_p'=\frac{\gamma H_px_p}9,\qquad \gamma=\frac1{1023\pi}<g:=\frac1{3000},\qquad \tau=\frac{1023}{1024}t. \tag{1} \]

    Every time below is a duration in \(\tau\). The pressure is the independently specified first row with capacity one. No laboratory clock, energy, mass or charge identification is supplied.

    Retain the earlier constants

    \[ \Delta=\frac1{1024},\quad \eta=20\,10^{-70},\quad R=\frac1{32}, \quad L=h=T=\frac1{32},\quad a_{\max}=\frac1{16}. \tag{2} \]

    Original form and phase preparation allowances are at most \(10^{-10}\). Each actual source starts at a finite age with its nine marked registration gaps independently bounded by a supplied \(\rho_p\le\Delta/8\). Earlier convergence proves existence of finite ages meeting the fixed positive threshold \(\Delta/8\); it supplies neither an observed numerical age nor finite attainment of a stronger bound such as \(\rho_p=0\). The latter remains an independent readiness premise if supplied.

    Each source first receives balanced charging \(a_p\) on \(A_p\) and \(-a_p\) on \(B_p\), with \(|a_p|\le a_{\max}\), for duration \(L\). Each then receives the earlier leak \(f_p=-P_{A_p}x_p\) for duration \(h\). An associated first-region mean reading is made before this leak, not after it. These two actual histories reach the new contact without a state reset. Their total absolute charging amount is at most \(2\cdot18a_{\max}L=9/128\), and each balanced charge has zero net form.

    At contact, remove both leaks and supply nine reciprocal form channels between matching \(A\)-slots:

    \[ f_{1,j}=x_{2,j}-x_{1,j},\qquad f_{2,j}=x_{1,j}-x_{2,j}\quad(j\in A),\qquad f_{p,j}=0\quad(j\in B). \tag{3} \]

    Their strength is one and they last for duration \(T\). This is a supplied continuous joint input law, not a graph-edge insertion. Neither internal degree, support, conductance, capacity nor phase row changes. The full field consumes thirty-six forms followed by thirty-six phases. Each copy retains its own phase gauge; no cross-copy phase law or common phase-origin identification is used.

    Let \(m_{pA}=\sum_{A_p}x_{p,j}/9\) and similarly for \(m_{pB}\). The observable is signed outgoing structural form from the first copy:

    \[ Q(T)=9\int_0^T(m_{1A}-m_{2A})\,ds =9\left[(m_{1A}+m_{1B})(0)-(m_{1A}+m_{1B})(T)\right]. \tag{4} \]

    The second copy's total form change is \(Q\). Joint total form is conserved; neither individual total is generally constant. Recorded output has independent error \(\epsilon_Q\le10^{-6}\). The finite partner reacts through its complete state, rather than supplying a prescribed waveform. This fact alone gives no error bound for an approximation which freezes it.

    2. Complete-state retention and supplied work¶

    Let \(y_p=\theta_p-\Theta_p-b_p\mathbf1\), where \(b_p\) is that copy's conserved actual phase gauge. The preceding proof bounds the full norm, including common form after leakage:

    \[ N_p^2=\|x_p\|_2^2+\|y_p\|_2^2,\qquad N_p<\frac9{512} \quad\hbox{at contact entry}. \tag{5} \]

    It does not replace the reached state by the stationary template.

    Stationarity gives the exact identity \(S_p(\Theta_p+y_p)=-K_p(y_p)y_p\), with \(K_p\) the integrated edge Hessian, not a linearization. The support and held weights give \(\|H_p\|_2,\|K_p\|_2\le18\), hence \(\|H_p-K_p\|_2\le36\). For \(\mathcal N^2=N_1^2+N_2^2\), both rows of (1) imply

    \[ \begin{split} \tfrac12(\mathcal N^2)'={}& \sum_{p=1}^2\left[-\frac{x_p^TH_px_p}{9} +\frac{\gamma}{9}x_p^T(H_p-K_p)y_p\right]\\ &-\sum_{j\in A}(x_{1,j}-x_{2,j})^2 \ \le 2g\mathcal N^2. \end{split} \tag{6} \]

    The upper norm derivative obeys \(\mathcal N'\le2g\mathcal N\), including at zero by continuity. For \(0\le s\le T\),

    \[ \mathcal N(s) <\sqrt2\,\frac9{512}e^{2gT} <\frac{17}{12}\frac9{512}\frac{48000}{47999} <\frac1{40}<R. \tag{7} \]

    Here \(2gT=1/48000\), and \(e^z\le(1-z)^{-1}\) for \(0\le z<1\). Each copy is bounded by this joint norm. It need not retain its smaller individual entry bound \(9/512\) during reciprocal exchange.

    The source and prefix bounds, followed by (7), retain the original phase charts and all four C9 windings. The marked-gap derivative bound is \(4gR\). Over all three finite stages,

    \[ |\hbox{marked gap change}| \le4gR(L+h+T)=\frac1{256000}<\frac{\Delta}{8}. \tag{8} \]

    Readiness therefore keeps all eighteen original marked gaps below \(\Delta/4\). This retains both original objects; it does not establish an outer phase identity between them.

    Norm dissipation must not be confused with the fine storage balance. For the earlier internal storage \(V_p\) and \(q_p=H_px_p\),

    \[ V_p'=-\frac{\|q_p\|_2^2}{9}+q_p^Tf_p,\qquad (V_1+V_2)'_{\rm added} =-\sum_{j\in A}(q_{1,j}-q_{2,j})(x_{1,j}-x_{2,j}). \tag{9} \]

    The added term is not generally nonpositive. Opposite form inputs do not establish physical energy conservation or passivity of this storage. For example, Cauchy--Schwarz bounds its absolute value by \(36\mathcal N^2\), so its integrated absolute work is bounded by \(36T/40^2\) on this window. There is no support or state jump at switching, but the continuing supplied input and its work remain explicit. Retention uses (6), not a false monotonicity assertion for (9).

    3. Four regional means and the actual projected remainder¶

    Each copy's source-associated nominal cross strength is

    \[ c=\frac{13}{3}-\frac{\Delta}{2\pi},\qquad k=\frac c9=\frac{13}{27}-\frac{\Delta}{18\pi}. \tag{10} \]

    Every actual proposed edge differs from its nominal counterpart by less than \(\delta_w=10^9\eta\). The larger legal weight box alone does not imply that association. The coefficient retains the original preparation origin and is not refreshed from current phases.

    As in the regional owner, enclose \(k\) outward and take its rational midpoint \(k_0\) and radius \(\rho_k\), with \(0<k_0<1/2\). Projection onto each pair of regional means retains a residual \((r_p,-r_p)\). Counting the forty-five cross-region edges bounds the live sine contribution, actual weight deviation and coefficient rounding by

    \[ |r_p|\le \frac{10}{9}N_p(g+\delta_w)+\frac23N_p\rho_k. \tag{11} \]

    The first terms use the edge-gradient and sine Lipschitz bounds; the last uses \(|m_{pA}-m_{pB}|\le2N_p/3\). With (7), require the fresh contact admission

    \[ F_{\rm contact}:=\frac{10}{9}\frac1{40}(g+\delta_w) +\frac23\frac1{40}\rho_k<F:=10^{-5}. \tag{12} \]

    The previous prefix uses the same fixed \(F\), with \(N_p<9/512\). Live phase motion is priced in (11). Its upper bound does not establish that the chosen observation resolves that motion against a linear model.

    Slot matching gives exact regional exchange \(m_{2A}-m_{1A}\); no additional cross-copy covariance is needed. For \(z=(m_{1A},m_{1B},m_{2A},m_{2B})^T\), the actual projection is

    \[ z'=Jz+(r_1,-r_1,r_2,-r_2)^T,\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix} -k_0-1&k_0&1&0\\ k_0&-k_0&0&0\\ 1&0&-k_0-1&k_0\\ 0&0&k_0&-k_0 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{13} \]

    The rational reference sets only the explicit residual to zero. Its positive semigroup contracts the maximum norm: \(J\) is Metzler with zero row sums. The actual projected law remains nonlinear through the bounded residual.

    4. Carrying initialization through the previous leak¶

    Let \(\mu_p\) be copy \(p\)'s original pre-leak regional reading, associated with error \(\epsilon_p\le10^{-6}\).
