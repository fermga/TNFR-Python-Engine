## 2. The declared law retains the full exchange

Fix a finite connected simple unit-support graph with at least two
nodes. Let `L` be its combinatorial Laplacian, `d_i` its degrees,
and let all held capacities `nu_i` be strictly positive. Write

\[
K=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_i/d_i),\qquad
q=Lx,\qquad
S_i(\theta)=\sum_{j\sim i}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i).
\]

Form is real and signed, phase is circular, and the common clock is
declared. For `e>0,w>0,beta>0`, set

\[
a=\frac w\pi,\qquad b=\frac{w}{\beta\pi},\qquad a=\beta b.
\]

The complete unforced sine law is

\[
\dot x=-eKq+aKS(\theta),\qquad
\dot\theta=bKq.
\]

It is distinct from native neighbor-argument pressure and its phase
mobility. Holding capacity and support fixed is a premise, not an
inferred universal law.

Define structural storage

\[
E(x,\theta)=\frac12x^\mathsf TLx+
\beta V(\theta),\qquad
V(\theta)=\sum_{\{i,j\}}\bigl(1-\cos(\theta_j-\theta_i)\bigr).
\]

The [existing exact decomposition](RELATIONAL_PATTERN_MEMORY.md#sine-relative-pattern-state)
uses `grad E=(q,-beta S)` and gives

\[
\binom{\dot x}{\dot\theta}
=
\left[
\begin{pmatrix}0&-bK\\bK&0\end{pmatrix}
-
\begin{pmatrix}eK&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\right]\nabla E,
\qquad
\dot E=-e\,q^\mathsf TKq.
\]

Both consumed rows appear in this identity. The reversible terms
exchange storage between form and phase; they cancel in the total
balance because the first matrix is skew. They have not been deleted
from the dynamics. The constant skew matrix supplies the same-law
Poisson structure discussed in that owner, rather than an auxiliary
Hamiltonian assigned to an unrelated graph.

This balance justifies reciprocal exchange within the supplied model.
It does not derive the sine kernel, its coefficient or the dissipation
strength uniquely from `xdot=nu*p`. Nor does the structural storage
become physical energy without an independent measurement bridge.

The [exact nonlinear representations](SINE_PATTERN_DYNAMICS.md#sine-form-phase-memory-equivalence)

## 3. The full equilibrium derivative

Take an exact critical pattern `x_*=c*1,S(theta_*)=0` and define
its phase Hessian

\[
H=\nabla^2 V(\theta_*)
 =L_{\cos(\theta_{*,j}-\theta_{*,i})}.
\]

Assume `H` is positive definite after removal of common phase
rotation. Strictly acute critical edge gaps are a sufficient condition
on connected support. This hypothesis is essential; not every phase
configuration is a stable pattern.

Let

\[
h=K^{-1/2}\mathbf1,\qquad
\mathcal Q=h^\perp,\qquad
B=K^{1/2}LK^{1/2},\qquad C=K^{1/2}HK^{1/2}.
\]

Both matrices annihilate `h` and are positive definite on
`mathcal Q`. Every inverse and square root below is restricted
to this quotient, rather than inverting a singular full Laplacian.
In transformed perturbations
`xi=K^(-1/2) delta x` and `eta=K^(-1/2) delta theta`,
with their common-origin components removed, the exact tangent is

\[
\dot\xi=-eB\xi-aC\eta,\qquad
\dot\eta=bB\xi.
\]

Consequently

\[
\ddot\eta+eB\dot\eta+abBC\eta=0,
\qquad
B^{-1}\ddot\eta+e\dot\eta+abC\eta=0.
\]

This is derived from the joint nodal rows. It does not add a primitive
oscillator, identify capacity with an angular frequency or reconstruct
pressure from a response.

The quotient tangent is asymptotically stable. For example, testing the
second quadratic pencil on a possibly complex eigenvector `v` gives

\[
m s^2+e n s+ab c=0,\qquad
m=v^*B^{-1}v>0,\quad n=v^*v>0,\quad c=v^*Cv>0.
\]

A real root is negative. For a nonreal root, its imaginary part gives
`Re(s)=-e*n/(2m)<0`. This establishes stability, but does not
classify every root as real or nonreal.

In general `B` and `C` do not commute. An isolated-ring
discriminant cannot classify a different full graph without checking
that reduction. If `H=rho*L`, then `C=rho*B` even
with heterogeneous positive capacity: capacity changes modal rates
and eigenvectors, but not the resulting common pole-class condition.
Otherwise use the whole pencil and actual observations.
The [native collective-mode result](RELATIONAL_PATTERN_MEMORY.md)
and the sine derivative have different coefficients and cannot be
interchanged merely because both contain phase feedback.

## 4. A finite-frequency maximum for every active collocated port

### Declare the probe and the measured response

Introduce, solely for this response question, a supplied form-rate
input `f*u(t)`:

\[
\dot x=-eKq+aKS+f\,u(t),\qquad \dot\theta=bKq.
\]

The fixed real vector `f` and scalar waveform `u` specify the
probe. This does not derive a sinusoidal drive from an autonomous
environment or install an input in the unforced engine.
The exact balance now includes

\[
\dot E=-e\,q^\mathsf TKq+u\,y,\qquad
y=f^\mathsf Tq=f^\mathsf TLx.
\]

Thus `y` is the output conjugate to this input in structural
storage work. It is not necessarily a laboratory power measurement.
Changing the output to a single phase or a different region changes
the response claim.

Let `P_Q` project onto `mathcal Q` and put

\[
F=P_{\mathcal Q}K^{-1/2}f,\qquad
G=B^{1/2}F,\qquad
\zeta=B^{1/2}\xi,\qquad
A=abB^{1/2}CB^{1/2}.
\]

The common-origin input component is tracked separately in the
weighted form mean and contributes no work to `y`.
An **active port** means `F!=0`, equivalently `G!=0`.
A nonzero spatially uniform form-rate input has `F=0` and is
an explicit excluded case: it moves only the common form origin.

At the tangent level the transfer from `u` to `y`, with zero
initial perturbation, is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H(s)=sG^\mathsf T
 (s^2I+esB+A)^{-1}G.}
\]

Indeed, `zeta'= -eB zeta-aB^(1/2)C eta+G*u` and
`eta'=bB^(1/2)zeta`; eliminating `eta` yields the
displayed transfer, and `y=G^T zeta`. This is a tangent
frequency response around the exact pattern. A finite nonlinear
experiment needs its own amplitude, error and preparation admission.

### Conditional theorem and proof

For every active port under the stated hypotheses, the scalar gain
`|mathcal H(i*omega)|` has a positive global maximum at at least
one finite `omega>0`.

First, `A` and `B` are positive definite. For `omega>0`,
the matrix

\[
M(\omega)=A-\omega^2I+ie\omega B
\]

is nonsingular: if `M v=0`, the imaginary part of `v^*M v`
would be `e*omega*v^*Bv=0`, forcing `v=0`.
There is no imaginary-axis pole, in agreement with the stability
argument above.

For `z=M(omega)^(-1)G`, direct multiplication gives

\[
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal H(i\omega)
 =e\omega^2 z^*Bz>0.
\]

The response is therefore nonzero at every positive frequency.
It is continuous there, and its endpoint asymptotics are

\[
\mathcal H(i\omega)
 =i\omega\,G^\mathsf TA^{-1}G+O(\omega^2)
 \quad(\omega\to0),
\]

\[
\mathcal H(i\omega)
 =\frac{G^\mathsf TG}{i\omega}+O(\omega^{-2})
 \quad(\omega\to\infty).
\]

The gain tends to zero at both ends. Choose any positive interior
value; outside a sufficiently large compact interval the gain is
smaller than that value. Continuity on the interval supplies a
positive finite-frequency global maximum.

The gain is also bounded in terms of the admitted input and loss:

\[
|\mathcal H(i\omega)|^2
\le\omega^2(G^\mathsf TB^{-1}G)(z^*Bz)
=\frac{\|F\|^2}{e}\operatorname{Re}\mathcal H(i\omega),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal H(i\omega)|\le\frac{\|F\|^2}{e}.}
\]

This is frequency selectivity of a specified structural-work port
through reciprocal form--phase dynamics. It holds for noncommuting
`B,C` and does not require complex temporal poles. It does not
prove a narrow peak, large gain, a particular maximum frequency,
selective response at every remote node or a maintained autonomous
pulse. The normalization of `f` fixes the numerical gain.

## 5. Modal examples make the output distinction explicit

If `B,C` commute, choose a common normalized mode `psi` with
`B*psi=lambda*psi` and `C*psi=mu*psi`, where
`lambda,mu>0`. With `F=r_in*psi` and modal coordinates
`X=psi^T xi,Theta=psi^T eta`,

\[
\dot X=-e\lambda X-a\mu\Theta+r_{\rm in}u,\qquad
\dot\Theta=b\lambda X.
\]

Write `d=e*lambda` and `k=ab*lambda*mu`. The three
distinct outputs have transfers

\[
\frac{X(s)}{u(s)}=\frac{r_{\rm in}s}{s^2+ds+k},\qquad
\frac{\Theta(s)}{u(s)}=\frac{b\lambda r_{\rm in}}{s^2+ds+k},\qquad
\frac{y(s)}{u(s)}=\frac{r_{\rm in}^2\lambda s}{s^2+ds+k}.
\]

The last identity uses `y=r_in*lambda*X`. An unweighted form
coordinate is not the structural-work output unless the required
factor is retained.

For a nonzero modal input:

- Free perturbations oscillate precisely when
  `d^2<4k`, or `e^2*lambda<4ab*mu`.
- Form and conjugate-work gain peak at
  `omega^2=k` for every `d>0`, including real-pole cases.
  Their peak gains are respectively `|r_in|/d` and `r_in^2/e`.
- Phase gain has a nonzero-frequency maximum only when
  `d^2<2k`. Then
  `omega_peak^2=k-d^2/2`; otherwise its maximum is at zero
  frequency.

These statements follow by differentiating the squared gain with
respect to `omega^2`. They demonstrate why an oscillatory pole,
a form-response maximum and a phase-response maximum are not
interchangeable resonance tests.

At phase consensus, `C=B`. For a uniform cycle twist with
acute edge increment `alpha`, `C=cos(alpha)*B`.
Writing `rho=mu/lambda`, the modal poles satisfy

\[
s^2+e\lambda s+ab\rho\lambda^2=0.
\]

At the default `e=w=1/2,beta=1`, every such mode with
`0<rho<=1` has distinct negative real poles because
`e^2-4ab*rho=1/4-rho/pi^2>0`. Its phase-output gain
has no nonzero-frequency maximum, while its active form and
conjugate-work outputs still do. Thus real poles do not negate
the theorem in Section 4, and that theorem does not imply
underdamped free oscillation.

The same scalar reduction is unavailable for a general attached
network whose `B,C` do not commute. Neither an isolated
cycle nor a frozen intermediary classifies all its modes.

### Unit form-input specialization on a cycle

For `C_n` with `n>=3`, common positive capacity `nu`, degree two,
and an exact acute twist of integer winding `j`, write

\[
\rho=\cos\frac{2\pi j}{n}>0,\qquad 4|j|<n.
\]

Choose a real Euclidean-unit nonconstant Laplacian eigenmode
`psi`, indexed by an integer `1<=m<n`, with

\[
L\psi=\ell\psi,\qquad
\ell=2-2\cos\frac{2\pi m}{n}>0,\qquad
r=\frac{\nu\ell}{2}.
\]

This `r` is the spatial rate; the supplied input is normalized
in the **original** form coordinates as `f=psi`.
Writing `delta x=X*psi,delta theta=Theta*psi` gives

\[
\dot X=-erX-a\rho r\Theta+u,\qquad
\dot\Theta=brX,\qquad y=\ell X.
\]

Consequently, for `D(s)=s^2+er*s+ab*rho*r^2`,

\[
\mathcal H_X(s)=\frac{s}{D(s)},\qquad
\mathcal H_\Theta(s)=\frac{br}{D(s)},\qquad
\mathcal H_y(s)=\frac{\ell s}{D(s)}.
\]

The form and structural-work peaks occur at
`omega^2=ab*rho*r^2`, with gains

\[
\max_\omega|\mathcal H_X(i\omega)|=\frac1{er},\qquad
\max_\omega|\mathcal H_y(i\omega)|
 =\frac{\ell}{er}=\frac2{e\nu}.
\]

Here `||F||^2=2/nu`, so this mode attains the general
collocated bound. The phase-output peak and pole classification
still have their distinct conditions
`e^2<2ab*rho` and `e^2<4ab*rho` respectively.
The constant mode has `ell=0` and is a common-origin input,
not an active-port member of this theorem.

\theta_{h,*}=0

### A remote frequency maximum and an autonomous rebound

Stability on the quotient makes `mathcal T_R(i*omega)` continuous
for all real `omega`. The static calculation gives its zero-frequency
limit, while the exact Markov coefficient gives

\[
\mathcal T_R(s)
 =-\frac{be\,k_{d_0}k_hk_{r_0}}{s^3}+O(s^{-4})
\quad(|s|\to\infty).
\]

It is therefore not identically zero and its gain tends to zero at
both frequency endpoints. A nonzero rational transfer cannot vanish
on the entire positive imaginary axis. Choose a positive interior
gain; outside a sufficiently large compact frequency interval the
gain is smaller. Continuity then proves a positive global gain
maximum at some finite `omega>0`.

This remote, noncollocated theorem requires neither a positive-real
transfer nor complex temporal poles. It imports neither the
collocated gain ceiling nor nonvanishing response at **every**

Now remove the input and prepare a donor-only initial disturbance,

\qquad \epsilon>0.
\]

At the tangent level its autonomous output is

y_{\epsilon}(t)=\epsilon g_R(t),\qquad

\]

This uses an initial condition, not a persistent or secretly supplied

It is strictly negative for sufficiently small positive time. Its

This is a necessary **transient rebound**, not a proof of a complex

The full nonlinear sine law has the same initial second derivative

Smooth dependence on the initial state additionally implies a

## 8. Permanent motion, irreversible loss and the reversible boundary

### A finite closed dissipative system has no nonstationary recurrence

Retain finite connected unit support, fixed nonnegative capacities,
`w,beta>0` and no input, support event, clipping or added phase
velocity. Unlike the stable-response theorems above, this subsection
allows zero capacities. Let `A={i:nu_i>0}` and `Z` be its complement.
The sine law remains globally smooth on real form and circular phase,
and its storage balance is

\[
\dot E=-e\sum_{i\in A}k_iq_i^2,\qquad k_i=\nu_i/d_i.
\]

For `e>0`, a full-state return modulo common form and phase origins
returns the storage. Continuity and nonnegativity therefore force
`q_i=0` at every active node throughout the return interval. Every
phase row then vanishes; inactive form rows also vanish.

There is no loophole through a zero-capacity anchor. If both `A` and
`Z` are nonempty, differentiate the active constraints:

\[
0=\dot q_A=L_{AA}\dot x_A.
\]

The grounded Laplacian `L_AA` is positive definite. Its quadratic
form is the sum of squared differences on active--active edges and
squared active coordinates on edges to the frozen set; connectivity
makes its null space trivial. Thus `xdot_A=0`. If all capacities
are positive, `q=0` instead gives `x=c(t)*1`. The sine law's
weighted form-mean identity,

\[
\mathbf1^\mathsf TK^{-1}\dot x
 =-e\,\mathbf1^\mathsf Tq+a\,\mathbf1^\mathsf TS=0,
\]

forces `c'=0`. If all capacities are zero, both rows are frozen
from the outset. In each case the recurrent trajectory is stationary.
Zero capacity can preserve nonzero pressure or form contrast at an
equilibrium, but cannot create a loss-free moving sector of this law.

The argument also excludes recurrence along a sequence of times
returning to the initial state modulo origins: monotone storage
converging back to its initial value must have been constant at every
earlier time. It does not confuse decreasing-amplitude transients
with recurrent full-state motion.

For the native argument-pressure completion, the same recurrence
conclusion holds while the full solution remains in its admitted
regular domain with positive phase metric. Its loss is the same
`-e*q^T Kq`, and its selected phase row freezes at zero capacity.
With some inactive nodes the grounded-Laplacian proof is unchanged.
With all nodes active, write the native positive phase metric as
`M=diag(m_i)` and its source as `g`. At `q=0` the form row is
`xdot_i=w*nu_i*g_i=c'`, so

\[
c'\sum_i\frac{m_i}{\nu_i}
 =w\sum_i m_i g_i=0.
\]

The final equality follows from `Mg=-grad V` and phase-origin
invariance. This extends the existing
[native recurrence proof](RELATIONAL_EXCHANGE_ADMISSION.md#relational-pulse-scope)
to held zero capacities without substituting the sine phase law for
the native one. It does not continue native trajectories through
their undefined resultant or branch boundary.

### The sine obstruction also controls nonperiodic long-time motion

For the globally smooth sine law with `e>0`, every such finite-system
trajectory approaches the **equilibrium set**. This is stronger than
excluding exact periodic motion, but does not select one limiting
phase pattern or a convergence rate.

Here is a compactness proof retaining the full state. Storage bounds
all form differences on connected support. If every capacity is
positive, the conserved weighted form mean also bounds the common
form origin. If some capacity is zero, the frozen form of any such
node anchors that origin instead. Circular phases lie on a compact
torus. Hence the trajectory stays in a compact set of real form and
circular phase and has global continuation.

Its nonempty compact omega-limit set is invariant and has constant
storage `E_infinity`. Every complete trajectory within that set
therefore satisfies `q_A=0` and `thetadot=0`. Its sine currents
are constant, and its form velocity is the constant vector `aKS`.
A nonzero constant velocity would leave the compact limit set, so
that velocity must vanish. Every state of the omega-limit set is
an equilibrium; equivalently the distance of the original state to
the equilibrium set tends to zero.

This proof permits heterogeneous and zero held capacities. It does
not establish convergence to one equilibrium, a uniform relaxation
time or a theorem for another runtime. In particular, the compact
smooth sine argument cannot be imported into native argument dynamics
without separately controlling that model's regular-domain boundary.

For the specific two-C5/live-intermediary support with **strictly positive**
held capacities, the
[finite critical-geometry classification](RELATIONAL_PATTERN_MEMORY.md#sine-eleven-node-asymptotic-equilibria)
strengthens this result to convergence to one relative equilibrium.
An explicit conserved-lift reconstruction also fixes its limiting common
phase origin. That support-specific theorem does not select which
equilibrium a preparation reaches or supply a convergence deadline;
it does not replace the broader equilibrium-set statement above.

### A finite passive environment is not an unlimited source

Retaining additional environmental nodes does not supply energy
without accounting for it. More generally, if a closed full system
has storage bounded below and obeys

\[
\dot E_{\rm total}=-D_{\rm total},\qquad D_{\rm total}\ge0,
\]

then

\[
\int_0^T D_{\rm total}(t)\,dt
 \le E_{\rm total}(0)-E_{\min},\qquad
\frac1T\int_0^T D_{\rm total}(t)\,dt\longrightarrow0.
\]

A subsystem loss included in `D_total` cannot remain positive on
average forever by receiving work from a finite passive environment.
It may receive a finite transient supply or exchange storage
reversibly. Neither possibility derives a perpetual drive for a
dissipative periodic response. For example, at an active sine node,

\[
e k_i q_i^2=\frac{e}{b^2k_i}\dot\theta_i^2.
\]

A repeated nonconstant phase cycle with a fixed period consumes a
strictly positive loss per cycle. An initially finite closed storage
cannot pay that cost indefinitely.

This obstruction concerns the stated total balance. It does not
assume that an observed subsystem is closed, or that irreversible
loss is fundamental rather than an effective description after
discarding degrees of freedom. The origin and admissibility of that
loss are separate research questions; naming an environment does not
answer them.

### Exact nonlinear periodic exchange on the sine P2 boundary

Now change the declared constitutive premise to `e=0`, retaining
`w,beta>0`, fixed support and held capacity. This boundary is already
admitted by `RelationalExchangeModel`; under its weight-normalization
convention the effective phase weight is then one. No primitive
oscillator or external pump is appended.

On a single unit edge `P2`, write

\[
u=x_0-x_1,\qquad \delta=\theta_1-\theta_0,\qquad
\sigma=\nu_0+\nu_1.
\]

If `sigma>0`, including one frozen endpoint, the complete nonlinear
relative rows reduce exactly to

\[
\dot u=a\sigma\sin\delta,\qquad
\dot\delta=-b\sigma u,\qquad
\ddot\delta+ab\sigma^2\sin\delta=0,
\]

\[
E=\frac12u^2+\beta(1-\cos\delta),\qquad \dot E=0.
\]

Thus the pendulum equation is a consequence of the two consumed
nodal rows in this preparation, not an assumed equation for a new
state variable. The full node coordinates are recovered without
dividing by either capacity:

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_0(t)&=x_0(0)+\frac{\nu_0}{\sigma}[u(t)-u(0)],\\
x_1(t)&=x_1(0)-\frac{\nu_1}{\sigma}[u(t)-u(0)],\\
\theta_0(t)&=\theta_0(0)-\frac{\nu_0}{\sigma}[\delta(t)-\delta(0)],\\
\theta_1(t)&=\theta_1(0)+\frac{\nu_1}{\sigma}[\delta(t)-\delta(0)].
\end{aligned}
\]

For every level `0<E<2*beta`, choose the relative phase lift centered
on its energy well `2*pi*n`. Its deviation from that center librates
between `-A` and `A`, where

\[
A=\arccos(1-E/\beta)<\pi,\qquad
|u|_{\max}=\sqrt{2E},\qquad
m=\frac{E}{2\beta}\in(0,1).
\]

The level curve is compact, regular and contains no equilibrium, so
it is traversed periodically. Both relative coordinates return, and
the reconstruction proves full-state periodicity even when precisely
one capacity is zero. Define the complete elliptic integral using
its **parameter** `m`, not its square-root modulus:

\[
\mathbf K(m)=\int_0^{\pi/2}
 \frac{d\psi}{\sqrt{1-m\sin^2\psi}}.
\]

Separation of the conserved energy, followed by
`sin((delta-2*pi*n)/2)=sqrt(m)*sin(psi)`, gives the exact period

\[
\boxed{
T(E)=\frac{4}{\sigma\sqrt{ab}}\,\mathbf K(m)
 =\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\beta}}{w\sigma}\,\mathbf K(m).}
\]

This is the model-clock period. With

\[
T_0=\frac{2\pi}{\sigma\sqrt{ab}}
 =\frac{2\pi^2\sqrt{\beta}}{w\sigma},
\]

the integrand bounds give

\[
\boxed{T_0<T(E)\le\frac{T_0}{\sqrt{1-m}}}
\qquad(0<m<1).
\]

Also `T(E)->T0` as `E->0` and `T(E)->infinity` as
`E->2*beta` from below. For common capacity `nu`,
`T0=pi^2*sqrt(beta)/(w*nu)`. These analytic bounds permit a
detached period enclosure from independently captured initial storage;
they require no sampled oscillation or fitted frequency.

At `E=0` the state is stationary. At `E=2*beta` the
antipodal equilibrium and nonperiodic separatrix need separate
admission. Above that energy the relative phase rotates; full circular
state return must additionally account for individual phase windings.
Neither the libration period formula nor a native phase-branch claim
is silently extended to those cases. If `sigma=0`, both nodes
are frozen regardless of initial storage.

### Reuse the native result without replacing its clock

The existing [native P2 reversible result](RELATIONAL_EXCHANGE_ADMISSION.md#relational-pulse-scope)
already proves periodic exchange on `0<E<beta` inside its acute
domain. Here `delta` denotes the centered acute gap. Its two relative
rates at `e=0` are the sine rates
multiplied by `1/sinc(delta)`:

\[
\dot u_{\rm native}=a\sigma\delta,\qquad
\dot\delta_{\rm native}
 =-\frac{b\sigma u}{\operatorname{sinc}\delta}.
\]

Thus the two laws share the relative energy curves there, but

\[
dt_{\rm native}=\operatorname{sinc}(\delta)\,dt_{\rm sine}.
\]

Their nonzero-amplitude periods differ. The common small-amplitude
limit does not make them the same constitutive law or the same clock.
The sine law's smooth phase-circle continuation also does not remove
the native law's separate boundary obligations.

### What a permanent-pulse claim has and has not established

These results establish a sharp conditional distinction: finite
closed sine systems with positive form dissipation approach their
equilibrium set, whereas the admitted reversible P2 law has exact
nonlinear periodic states prepared at nonzero storage. The
[replica pulse proof](../TNFR_SCALE_GEOMETRY_AND_BRIDGE.md#sine-replica-internal-pulse)
extends periodic-state existence to a supplied ten-node preparation
with static collective means and moving internal constituents.

Energy conservation prevents one such periodic orbit from attracting
an open neighborhood containing other energy levels. Its amplitude,
phase of motion and period are determined by preparation; no
amplitude-selection mechanism or asymptotically attracting limit cycle
has been derived. An exactly prepared equilibrium remains an
equilibrium by uniqueness. Periodic-state existence is therefore not
spontaneous creation from an equilibrium or from an absent substrate.
It also does not prove robustness: the
[complete replica variation](../TNFR_SCALE_GEOMETRY_AND_BRIDGE.md#sine-replica-pulse-variation)
and its
[small-amplitude splitting](../TNFR_SCALE_GEOMETRY_AND_BRIDGE.md#sine-replica-pulse-splitting)

Nor does the existence proof select `e=0` as the fundamental law.
Choosing that boundary solely to obtain the desired pulse would leave
the selection question unanswered. Whether an irreversible loss term
is independently justified at the fundamental level, derived through
a declared reduction with retained memory and preparation, or restricted
to an effective sector remains a separate closure question. A
finite Euler trajectory, an added drive, a hidden energy reserve or
an auxiliary graph spectrum cannot substitute for that derivation.

## 9. Finite conservative reduction gives memory, not irreversible loss

### Begin with the same nonlinear law and retained environment

Use the already admitted `e=0` sine law on the complete unit path
`left--h--right`. The intermediary is a live TNFR node, not an
external waveform. Hold nonnegative capacities `nu_left,mu,nu_right`, the
positive storage scale `beta` and effective phase weight `w>0`
fixed. Declare the constant clock conversion

\[
\tau=\frac w\pi t.
\]

In this clock the exact nonlinear equations are
`dx/dtau=KS(theta)` and `dtheta/dtau=KLx/beta`. No coefficient
is dropped: the factor `w/pi` is carried by the declared conversion
back to the structural clock `t`.

Define oriented endpoint deviations from the intermediary,

\[
r=x_{\rm left}-x_h,\quad s=x_{\rm right}-x_h,\qquad
\alpha=\theta_h-\theta_{\rm left},\quad
\gamma=\theta_h-\theta_{\rm right},
\]

and their actual nonlinear edge storages

\[
E_{\rm left}=\frac12r^2+\beta(1-\cos\alpha),\qquad
E_{\rm right}=\frac12s^2+\beta(1-\cos\gamma).
\]

Substitution of **all three** nodal rows gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{dE_{\rm left}}{d\tau}
 =\frac\mu2\bigl(r\sin\gamma-s\sin\alpha\bigr)
 =-\frac{dE_{\rm right}}{d\tau}.}
\]

The endpoint capacities cancel from this exchange identity; the
intermediary capacity remains. In the structural clock multiply its
right-hand side by `w/pi`. Thus a decreasing regional storage can
be exact reversible transfer to the retained neighboring region.
It is not by itself evidence of dissipative microscopic loss.
Reversing both phase contrasts preserves the two edge storages and
reverses the transfer. The local storage values alone therefore do
not determine even its sign.

The visible endpoints alone do not close the full nonlinear law for
arbitrary hidden preparation. For example, keep both visible forms
and phases zero and the hidden form zero. Hidden phases `eta` and
`-eta`, with `0<eta<pi`, give the same visible state and the
same edge storages, but opposite visible form rates
`plus/minus nu_i*sin(eta)` in clock `tau`. When at least one
endpoint capacity is positive, these rates differ. A single autonomous
function of that visible state cannot equal both rates. Supplying
the hidden preparation, retaining its state, or proving a separately
restricted invariant family is essential.

### Exact tangent memory with a rational clock representation

For an independently specified reduction question, linearize at

This is the exact derivative of the same nonlinear law, ordered as
`(delta x_all,delta theta_all)`. If `beta,nu` are rational,
every generator entry is rational. The constant clock change thus
permits exact use of
[`derive_coordinate_memory`](../../src/tnfr/mathematics/linear_observation.py)

Then

 +\int_0^\tau B e^{D(\tau-s)}C\,y(s)\,ds.}

In particular `D^2=-omega^2 I`, so

\]

 =-\frac{\omega^2}{2}e^{D\tau},
\qquad i,j\in\{\mathrm{left},\mathrm{right}\}.}
\]

It is a periodic signed matrix, not a decaying friction kernel.

The four visible coordinate rows and their first output-Krylov

\]

matrix. The quotient generator is diagonalizable with purely
imaginary eigenvalues. If common origins are appended, they add
constant modes rather than decaying ones. Every linear observation
is a finite sum of those harmonics and constants.

Suppose an all-state exact linear Markov closure `z=Oz_full`

The [linear-observation owner](../../src/tnfr/mathematics/linear_observation.py)

Likewise, a nonzero observed response of the finite conservative

frequencies, the long-time average of its squared norm is the sum

strictly positive unless the response vanishes identically, whereas
a bounded response converging to zero has average squared norm zero.
Thus even a selected nonzero preparation cannot equal a strictly
decaying Markov response for all future times.

This does not forbid apparent attenuation over a finite observation
window, or a reduced approximate loss law with a declared limiting
procedure, preparation, error and horizon. It proves that finite
conservative elimination alone has not justified permanent positive
memoryless damping. An exact kernel cannot silently become such a
law by dropping its hidden source, deleting its recurrence or calling
the removed degrees of freedom an environment. The general argument
is a **tangent** statement at the specified critical targets; the
nonlinear endpoint counterexample and nonlinear edge balance above
have their separately stated scope.

Nor does repeated exchange or a collection of modes establish

## 10. Almost-everywhere recurrence of the full nonlinear reversible law

### State, law and the measure being used

Fix a finite connected simple unit-support graph with `n>=2`,

`e=0`. There are no inputs, support changes, resets, clipping or

\dot\theta=bKLx,\qquad a=w/\pi,\quad b=w/(\beta\pi),

on the state manifold

\[
\mathcal M=\mathbb R^n\times(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^n.
\]

The form coordinates are real and the phases are circles. Recurrence
below means nearness in this full product state space, rather than
return of separately chosen real phase lifts. Use Lebesgue measure
in form and Haar measure on the phase torus, normalized to total

null sets.

This theorem uses the displayed constant degree/capacity mobility in both
rows. Conservation of the same storage under another reciprocal mobility
does not establish conservation of this measure or the bounded mean slab.
The [alternative-mobility audit](../TNFR_SCALE_GEOMETRY_AND_BRIDGE.md#sine-mobility-relative-geometry)

The two conserved quantities needed here are

\rho_i=\frac{d_i}{\nu_i}>0.
\]

The storage identity gives `Edot=0`, and pairwise cancellation
of the sine currents gives

\[
\dot m
 =\frac{a\sum_i S_i}{\sum_i\rho_i}=0.
\]

No weighted mean of unwrapped phases is needed as a global torus
coordinate. In particular, arbitrary real capacity ratios do not
make such a lifted mean a single-valued observable on the torus.

### Compact invariant slabs and two-sided continuation

Choose an energy ceiling `E_max>0` and an interval
`m_lo<m_hi` of positive width. Define the family

\[
\mathcal S=
\{(x,\theta):E(x,\theta)\le E_{\max},
\quad m_{\rm lo}\le m(x)\le m_{\rm hi}\}.
\]

Let `D_G` be the unweighted graph diameter. A shortest path and
Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
|x_i-x_j|^2
 \le {\rm dist}_G(i,j)\sum_{\text{path}}(x_u-x_v)^2
 \le 2D_G E_{\max}.
\]

Since `m` is a positive weighted average of the forms,

\[
|x_i-m|\le\sqrt{2D_G E_{\max}}
 \le R,\qquad R=\sqrt{2(n-1)E_{\max}}.
\]

Thus every state in the slab satisfies the explicit coordinate bound

\[
\boxed{
m_{\rm lo}-R\le x_i\le m_{\rm hi}+R\quad\text{for every node}.}
\]

The slab is closed inside this bounded form box times a compact
phase torus, so it is compact. Its product volume is finite; with
the stated Haar normalization it is at most
`(m_hi-m_lo+2R)^n`. It also has positive volume: choose uniform
form with mean strictly inside the interval and phase consensus.
There `E=0<E_max`, and a sufficiently small open neighborhood
stays within both strict inequalities.

The smooth vector field preserves the two defining quantities in
both time directions. A local solution starting in the slab cannot
leave it. On this compact set the smooth field is bounded and
locally Lipschitz, excluding finite-time escape and supplying
continuation for all positive and negative times. Hence the flow
`Phi_t` is defined on the whole slab and maps it bijectively
onto itself.

### Exact volume preservation

In local phase charts the full divergence vanishes directly:

\[
\operatorname{div}F
 =\sum_i\frac{\partial\dot x_i}{\partial x_i}
  +\sum_i\frac{\partial\dot\theta_i}{\partial\theta_i}=0.
\]

Each form rate depends on phase rather than form; each phase rate
depends on form rather than phase. The smooth periodic phase
dependence makes this calculation compatible across torus charts.
The flow Jacobian obeys

\[
\frac d{dt}\det D\Phi_t
 =(\operatorname{div}F)\circ\Phi_t\;\det D\Phi_t=0,
\]

with initial determinant one. Thus the full product volume, and
its restriction to the invariant slab, is preserved. This proof
uses the nonlinear sine field itself, not a tangent matrix or a
statistical assumption of random mixing.

### Recurrence from finite measure: an elementary argument

Fix any positive time increment `h` and write `T=Phi_h`.
This is an invertible measure-preserving map of a finite-measure
space. For a measurable subset `A` of the slab, let
`B subset A` consist of the states that never revisit `A` at
any strictly positive integer iterate. The sets
`B,T(B),T^2(B),...` are pairwise disjoint: an intersection would
give one point of `B` a later return to `A`. They all have
the same measure. Finite total measure therefore forces `B`
to have measure zero.

States in `A` that return only finitely often are also a null
set: their last return lies in `B`, so they belong to the
countable union `union_{j>=0} T^(-j)(B)`. Almost every point
of `A` consequently revisits `A` infinitely many times.

Apply this result to a countable basis of neighborhoods in the
compact metric slab, and remove the countable union of exceptional
null sets. For each remaining state, use successively smaller
basis neighborhoods containing that state and choose increasing
return iterates. This gives

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{n_jh}(z)\longrightarrow z,\qquad n_j\longrightarrow\infty,
\quad\text{for almost every }z\in\mathcal S.}
\]

This is recurrence of the full nonlinear state on the phase torus.
The full-measure set may depend on the fixed increment `h`;
the proof does not require one set chosen simultaneously for every
positive real increment. A single declared increment already proves
continuous-time recurrence along a sequence of future times.

Almost all these recurrent states are nonstationary. Indeed,
strict positivity of `K` makes `thetadot=0` imply
`Lx=0`. Connectivity then gives `x=c*1`, a one-dimensional
subspace of `R^n` with zero Lebesgue measure for `n>=2`.
The equilibrium set is contained in that form subspace times
the phase torus, and therefore has zero ambient product measure.
This does not require classification of the additional condition
`S(theta)=0`.

One can exhaust the whole finite-state manifold by the countable
invariant slabs `E_max=j` and `-j<=m<=j`, with positive
integers `j`. Thus the same almost-everywhere conclusion holds
in the full product measure as well. The declared finite-volume
slab remains useful for a bounded, inspectable family admission.

### An exact exceptional preparation prevents a chosen-state claim

Almost everywhere is essential. On the same reversible P2 law,
take `u=x_0-x_1=2*sqrt(beta)` and `delta=theta_1-theta_0=0`
initially, with `sigma=nu_0+nu_1>0`. Its storage is exactly

\]

\[
u(t)=2\sqrt\beta\,\operatorname{sech}(\Omega t),\qquad
\delta(t)=\pi-4\arctan\!\bigl(e^{\Omega t}\bigr).

At `t=0` these are the stated form contrast and equal phases.

tends to zero and relative phase tends to `-pi`. The full

This exception needs no irrational stored phase. For example,
`beta=1,nu_0=nu_1=1,x=(1,-1),theta=(0,0)` is a wholly
rational preparation with `E=2` and weighted form mean zero.
It lies inside the slab `E_max=3,-1<=m<=1` but is

that selected state.

### What this establishes for a pulse investigation

### Persistence does not establish capture from outside

There is a precise formation boundary under the same complete conservative
flow. If a family `U` is invariant in **both** time directions, then

\[
\Phi_T(z)\in\mathcal U\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z\in\mathcal U.
\]

The reverse implication is invariance; the forward implication follows by
applying `Phi_(-T)` to the endpoint. In particular, a certificate that a
trajectory lies in such a protected identity family cannot establish that
it entered that family from outside. This statement requires two-sided
invariance; a merely forward-invariant dissipative capture region need not
have this property.

Recurrence supplies a further obstruction. Let `A` be a closed target in
an admitted conservative slab, and let `z` be a recurrent state outside
`A`. If `dist(Phi_t(z),A)` tended to zero as `t->infinity`, a returning
sequence `Phi_(t_j)(z)->z` and continuity of distance would give
`dist(z,A)=0`, contradicting closedness and `z not in A`. Consequently,
the basin converging to `A` from outside it is confined to the
recurrence-null exceptional set. This uses the ambient measure above,
not a measure on an arbitrarily selected preparation surface.

Neither result excludes temporary organization, entry into a noninvariant
observational region, exceptional separatrix convergence, or exchange
between a region and its retained environment. They do require a formation
study to declare its target, initial set and lifetime separately from a
maintenance certificate. Apparent regional settling must keep the hidden
state and the [finite-memory boundary](#finite-conservative-memory);
eliminating constituents does not turn conservative exchange into exact
irreversible loss.

These conservative obstructions do not invalidate the separate
[validated native-law transit](RELATIONAL_EXCHANGE_ADMISSION.md#relational-validated-transit)
from a winding-zero preparation into a maintained winding-one pattern.
That result uses a different complete dissipative law and a forward capture
region. Its retained continuous proof, frozen finite-executor verdict and
supplied initial preparation keep their separate evidence scopes.

## 11. Counterexamples prevent an unconditional claim

The theorem has concrete boundaries.

- An exact unforced equilibrium remains still. A susceptibility
  to a probe does not supply that probe or start motion.
- A pure common-origin input has `F=0` and zero conjugate
  response. Other noncollocated observations can hide excited
  modes or have identically zero transfer.
- A phase-output response can be low-pass even when the
  conjugate-work response has an interior maximum.
- Zero capacity, an indefinite or additionally degenerate phase
  Hessian, disconnected support or a different pressure/phase
  law fall outside the stated positive-definite quotient theorem.
- At `e=0` the finite stable-gain proof does not apply.
  At vanishing reciprocal coupling the same second-order
  mechanism disappears; pure diffusion is a different closure.

The [permanent-pulse admission above](#permanent-pulse-admission)
strengthens the unforced `e>0` sine boundary to approach toward
the equilibrium set, including held zero capacities. Its native-law
recurrence extension retains the separate regular-domain hypotheses.
The exact `e=0` periodic branch has different conservation and
attraction properties; it does not repair a failed positive-loss claim
without changing the declared constitutive premise.

A periodic input may balance loss in a driven response; the
required input and structural work must be retained. A hidden
TNFR environment can supply a history-dependent influence, but
its state, feedback and initial condition must be carried by the
[memory derivation](RELATIONAL_PATTERN_MEMORY.md). It cannot
silently be replaced by a freely specified periodic signal.

These are conditional mathematical mechanisms compatible with
the repository's research aim. They do not uniquely select the
fundamental pressure law, identify physical matter or establish
that every TNFR state must oscillate. The existence of this
representation or response class alone establishes no novelty
relative to established network dynamics.
