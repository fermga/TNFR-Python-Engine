# Causal composition of acquired sine-class interfaces

<a id="sine-class-interface-composition"></a>

The [collective interface](SINE_CLASS_COLLECTIVE_INTERFACE.md) already
eliminates the 48 noncentral coordinates of the three-C9 system. Its
block-diagonal hidden kernels implicitly contain a component decomposition.
The [earlier reduced-port composition](SINE_REDUCED_PORT_COMPOSITION.md)
also derives degree-aware assembly and storage balances for its different
retained surrogate. Repeating either factorization would not establish a
new composition result.

This owner gives a substitution theorem: component causal maps may be
connected through the actual form **and** phase boundary inputs, with an
explicit error bound after feedback. It also constructs a particular
cubic component hierarchy with a certified complete-state residual,
without evaluating another coefficient or response. The distinction is
between a prescribed disturbance and an input generated by a neighboring
pattern under the same law. The law, support and bridge occurrence remain
supplied premises.

<a id="sine-composition-complete-model"></a>
## Fixed support and exact component boundary rows

Keep nodes \(0,\ldots,26\), three C9 rings, bridges \((4,13),(13,22)\),
unit capacities and class tuples \((1,k,1)\), \(k=1,2\). The central
nodes \(c_r\) have final degrees
\[
 (c_1,c_2,c_3)=(4,13,22),\qquad (d_1,d_2,d_3)=(3,4,3);
\]
all other degrees are two. Let \(D\) be this final degree matrix,
\(L\) the whole graph Laplacian and \(A=D^{-1}L\). With
\(\gamma=1/(1023\pi)<g=1/3000\) and structural clock
\(\tau=(1023/1024)t\), the complete rows are
\[
 x'=-Ax+\gamma D^{-1}S(\theta),\qquad
 \theta'=\gamma Ax,\qquad
 S_i(\theta)=\sum_{j\sim i}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i).
 \tag{1}
\]
Continuous phase lifts are retained. Targets have aligned central origins
and the original ring windings. Actual source deviations retain the
[acquired-family association](SINE_CLASS_NONLINEAR_ORGANIZATION.md#sine-nonlinear-organization-source);
a Cartesian coordinate cover is not a claim of independently preparable
corners. No source is reacquired or reset.

For ring \(r\), let \(L_r,S_r,U_r\) contain only its nine internal edges,
where \(U_r=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in r}[1-\cos(\theta_j-\theta_i)]\).
Its matrix \(D_r\) still uses the **final** degrees, including \(d_r\)
at the center. Write \(X_r=x_{c_r}\), \(\Phi_r=\theta_{c_r}\), and
define unnormalized boundary currents
\[
 j_r^x=\sum_{s\sim r}(X_s-X_r),\qquad
 j_r^s=\sum_{s\sim r}\sin(\Phi_s-\Phi_r).
 \tag{2}
\]
The normalized two-channel input and the open component equations are
\[
 u_r^x=\frac{j_r^x+\gamma j_r^s}{d_r},\qquad
 u_r^\theta=-\frac{\gamma j_r^x}{d_r},
 \tag{3}
\]
\[
 x_r'=-D_r^{-1}L_rx_r+\gamma D_r^{-1}S_r(\theta_r)
                  +e_{c_r}u_r^x,\qquad
 \theta_r'=\gamma D_r^{-1}L_rx_r+e_{c_r}u_r^\theta.
 \tag{4}
\]
The local selector in (4) means the center in the ring's node order.
Substitution of (2)-(3) reconstructs every row of (1) exactly. In
particular, the two inputs in (3) are correlated consequences of the
same neighboring forms and phases. Omitting the phase input changes
the complete law even when the form row appears correct.

An isolated degree-two C9 has a different normalized central row.
Its coefficients cannot replace (4). Here no edge is added or deleted:
the composition is a description of the already admitted support.

<a id="sine-composition-balances"></a>
## Component charges, storage supply and events

Define the component weighted charges
\(m_r^x=\mathbf1^TD_rx_r\) and
\(m_r^\theta=\mathbf1^TD_r\theta_r\). Between events,
\[
 (m_r^x)'=j_r^x+\gamma j_r^s,\qquad
 (m_r^\theta)'=-\gamma j_r^x.
 \tag{5}
\]
Both edge currents reverse sign at the other endpoint. Summing (5)
therefore preserves the two full weighted charges. Component charges
generally change: the neighboring organization supplies a real boundary
input, not an independently chosen forcing value.

For internal storage put
\[
 \mathcal H_r=\tfrac12x_r^TL_rx_r+U_r,\qquad
 a_r=L_rx_r,\qquad b_r=\nabla U_r=-S_r.
\]
Direct differentiation of (4) gives
\[
 \mathcal H_r'
 =-a_r^TD_r^{-1}a_r+
 \frac{(a_{r,c_r}-\gamma b_{r,c_r})j_r^x
                  +\gamma a_{r,c_r}j_r^s}{d_r}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The supply uses internal pressure and phase gradient, which depend on
hidden state. It is not \(X_ru_r^x+\Phi_ru_r^\theta\).

Each existing bridge contributes its storage once:
\[
 \mathcal H_b=
 \sum_{(r,s)\in\{(1,2),(2,3)\}}
 \left[\tfrac12(X_s-X_r)^2+1-\cos(\Phi_s-\Phi_r)\right].
\]
Combining its derivative with (6), or substituting the recomposed rows
in the whole storage, yields
\[
 \mathcal H=\sum_r\mathcal H_r+\mathcal H_b
             =\tfrac12x^TLx+U(\theta),\qquad
 \mathcal H'=-(Lx)^TD^{-1}(Lx).
 \tag{7}
\]
The sum of internal losses alone is not the right side of (7).
Bridge storage and the boundary supplies are required.

For simultaneous central form jumps \(x^+=x^-+B_pq\), phase is unchanged,
and
\[
 \Delta\mathcal H=q^TB_p^TLx^-+\tfrac12q^TW_pq,\qquad
 W_p:=B_p^TLB_p=
 \begin{pmatrix}3&-1&0\\-1&4&-1\\0&-1&3\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The weighted form-charge jump is
\(3q_1+4q_2+3q_3\); the phase charge does not jump. Cross terms in
(8) cannot be discarded. Contact work from the original joining event
retains its original certificate; repartitioning the equations is not
another support event.

These are identities of the exact composed law. Approximate component
substitutes do not acquire exact storage or charge conservation merely
because their outputs are close. Their residuals enter the balances
as additional terms.

<a id="sine-composition-causal-maps"></a>
## Component maps and the closed causal problem

An exact component map \(\mathcal P_r\) takes its complete pre-input
18-coordinate state, its supplied central form jumps, and two locally
integrable input histories \((u_r^x,u_r^\theta)\). It returns its
central form and phase history from (4). It retains all sixteen hidden
coordinates through their causal influence. There is no instantaneous
input-to-output feedthrough; inputs enter time derivatives. Form jumps
are applied separately and carried through subsequent flow.

The internal diffusion is a Markov generator in maximum norm:
\(-D_r^{-1}L_r\) has nonnegative off-diagonals and zero row sums.
The normalized internal sine and phase-Laplacian Lipschitz bounds
are at most two. Thus the open equations are globally Lipschitz in
state for prescribed integrable inputs.

Let \(\mathcal B(v)\) denote the map (2)-(3) from the six central
coordinates to all physical input channels. The exact composed
interface solves
\[
                  v=\mathcal P[\mathcal B(v)].
 \tag{9}
\]
The complete pre-input source is a fixed argument, not determined by
the six current ports. Substitution proves that (9) is equivalent
to (1) with the same initial state and events. The globally Lipschitz
full field therefore gives a unique solution on every finite window;
equivalently one can solve the Volterra equation successively on
short intervals. No contraction on the entire window is assumed.

For identical sources and jumps but different open inputs, heat
contraction and the phase row give the component response estimate
\[
 \|\mathcal P[u](t)-\mathcal P[\widetilde u](t)\|_\infty
 \le\int_0^t e^{2g(t-s)}
              \|u(s)-\widetilde u(s)\|_\infty\,ds.
 \tag{10}
\]
It also bounds the complete local state difference. All input
components in (10) are the normalized physical channels (3).
Since a center has \(d_r-2\) bridge neighbors and
\(\max_r(d_r-2)/d_r=1/2\),
\[
       \|\mathcal B(v)-\mathcal B(\widetilde v)\|_\infty
                \le(1+g)\|v-\widetilde v\|_\infty.
 \tag{11}
\]
The sine estimate is global on real phase lifts.

<a id="sine-composition-output-substitution"></a>
## A portable output-map substitution theorem

Suppose replacement component maps \(\widetilde{\mathcal P}_r\)
have independently certified discrepancy at most \(\eta\), uniformly
over the entire declared two-channel input-history domain, with the
same sources and jumps. Suppose both exact and replacement feedback
solutions exist and their inputs stay in that domain. In particular,
existence of a replacement feedback solution is not implied by its
pointwise output tolerance alone.

Applying (10) to the exact maps and then (11), the closed output
error \(e(t)\) satisfies
\[
 e(t)\le\eta+(1+g)\int_0^t e^{2g(t-s)}e(s)\,ds.
\]
Its scalar resolvent gives, for \(0\le H\le2\),
\[
 \sup_{t\le H}e(t)\le
 \eta\left[1+\frac{1+g}{1+3g}
                    \big(e^{(1+3g)H}-1\big)\right]<8\eta
 \quad(\eta>0).
 \tag{12}
\]
For \(\eta=0\), the error is zero. The rational sufficient estimate
\(e^{(1+3g)H}\le e^{2+1/500}
 <(11/4)^2(500/499)<8\) proves the last constant.

This is a modular substitution result, rather than a fit of one global
output. Its hypothesis is stronger than an error for a single prescribed
impulse. The existing central-impulse \(R_5\) bound does not certify
arbitrary continuous form-and-phase inputs, and is not inserted as
\(\eta\) in (12). A component model that drops hidden initialization
must separately satisfy the uniform input/source hypothesis; linear
invisibility of an initial mode does not prove nonlinear invisibility.

<a id="sine-composition-lifted-residual"></a>
## A second substitution contract using reconstructed state

There is a useful alternative when each replacement supplies a complete
18-coordinate causal lift. After recomposition, suppose the lifted
state has the full-law defects
\[
 \widetilde x'=-A\widetilde x+
          \gamma D^{-1}S(\widetilde\theta)+r_x,\qquad
 \widetilde\theta'=\gamma A\widetilde x+r_\theta.
 \tag{13}
\]
The maximum initial discrepancies are \(\eta_x,\eta_\theta\).
Include cumulative event mismatches in those budgets if the event
maps do not match exactly. Let
\(R_x\ge\int_0^H\|r_x(t)\|_\infty dt\) and
\(R_\theta\ge\int_0^H\|r_\theta(t)\|_\infty dt\).
For example, uniform residual bounds \(\rho_x,\rho_\theta\) give
\(R_x=H\rho_x,R_\theta=H\rho_\theta\).

The exact complete-law solution and the lift then obey
\[
 X\le \eta_x+R_x+2gH Y,\qquad
 Y\le \eta_\theta+R_\theta+2gH X.
\]
Writing \(a_x=\eta_x+R_x\), \(a_\theta=\eta_\theta+R_\theta\)
and \(\alpha_H=2gH<1\), one obtains
\[
 X\le\frac{a_x+\alpha_Ha_\theta}{1-\alpha_H^2},\qquad
 Y\le\frac{a_\theta+\alpha_Ha_x}{1-\alpha_H^2}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Diffusion contraction is retained, rather than replaced by a growing
absolute row-sum estimate. Both phase and form are needed to close
these inequalities.

Since a central Laplacian row has absolute row sum \(2d_r\), the
same bound gives \(|(Lx)_{c_r}-(L\widetilde x)_{c_r}|\le2d_rX\).
For an identical simultaneous jump \(q\), its quadratic work term
in (8) cancels between the two histories, so
\[
       |W(q;x^-)-W(q;\widetilde x^-)|
                   \le X\sum_r2d_r|q_r|.
 \tag{14a}
\]
This is a work enclosure, not an energy identity for the substitute.
Indeed its continuous storage derivative includes the additional
terms \((L\widetilde x)^Tr_x+
\nabla U(\widetilde\theta)^Tr_\theta\). Exact dissipation and
identity remain properties of the complete law under its own premises.

If exact bridge currents are evaluated on the replacement ports,
the local lifted row defects assemble directly into (13). If bridge
currents are themselves approximated or truncated, their defects must
also be included. An open-loop port tolerance alone supplies neither
of these full-row residual budgets. Equations (12) and (14) are
different admission contracts.

<a id="sine-composition-cubic-construction"></a>
## Constructing the local cubic interfaces without a full-state solver

The following witness supplies an explicit bound for (14). It uses
component kernels and the complete amplitude hierarchy, not a numerical
trajectory or a newly evaluated coefficient.

For each ring with cosine \(c=\cos(\kappa_r\,2\pi/9)\), define
\[
 T_c=\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma c\\-\gamma&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad E_r^{\rm int}=-\frac2{d_r}T_c.
 \tag{15}
\]
The sixteen hidden coordinates have the same grounded eight-node
path generator, propagator \(G_r\), port-to-hidden factor and
hidden-to-port factor as the
[explicit kernel owner](SINE_CLASS_COLLECTIVE_INTERFACE.md#sine-collective-interface-kernels).
Only the two-coordinate local central generator in (15) is internal.
Adding the linear bridge feedback reconstructs the old full six-port
generator exactly. In particular, replacing (15) by a degree-two
isolated center fails this recomposition.

Let \(A_b\) be the three-port bridge Laplacian divided rowwise by
\((3,4,3)\). Around the aligned central target, the bridge feedback
has linear part and cubic form part
\[
 \mathcal B_1(v)=
 \begin{pmatrix}-A_bX-\gamma A_bY\\ \gamma A_bX\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 (\mathcal B_3(v))_r^x
   =-\frac{\gamma}{6d_r}\sum_{s\sim r}(Y_s-Y_r)^3,\qquad
 (\mathcal B_3(v))^\theta=0.
 \tag{16}
\]
The bridge quadratic term is zero because its target phase difference
is zero. This does not eliminate the internal ring quadratic term.

Admit external central form impulses with total variation
\[
 A_*=\sum_j\|q_j\|_1\le7/5000,\qquad H\le2.
 \tag{17}
\]
Each component solves its two-coordinate first-level Volterra equation
using \(E_r^{\rm int}\), its own self-memory kernel, and the first-level
input \(\mathcal B_1(v_1)\), besides its external jumps. Its first
hidden level is the explicit convolution of that port history.
The second hidden level is the convolution of the internal quadratic
force. Reflection gives \(v_2=0\), but \(h_2\) is retained. The third
port equation receives \(\mathcal B_1(v_3)+\mathcal B_3(v_1)\) and its
internal forcing
\[
               2\mathbb Q_r(z_1,z_2)
                          +\mathbb T_r(z_1,z_1,z_1).
 \tag{18}
\]
Hidden third levels are reconstructed by the same fixed convolutions.
Only first-level forms jump at an external impulse. All levels carry
between events. The linear initialization source is independently
composed with \(\mathcal B_1\), retaining every ring's complete
pre-input source.

These are three two-coordinate component equations at each retained
visible level. The hidden levels are explicit causal integral
functionals; there is no new simultaneous 54-coordinate integration.
The construction is a triangular succession of linear Volterra
equations: the second and third forcing terms are polynomials in
already determined lower levels. Finite input variation and complete
finite initialization therefore give a unique solution of each level
on every finite horizon. The envelopes below then establish the stated
approximation domain; existence is not inferred from closeness alone.
Adding the component equations and (16) gives exactly the first,
second and third equations of the
[complete amplitude hierarchy](SINE_CLASS_CUBIC_RESPONSE.md#sine-cubic-triangular-variation).
Uniqueness at each successive linear Volterra level proves equality
with that hierarchy. Thus independently assembling the components
does not omit a path through a hidden odd mode or a bridge.

This construction composes the bridge **order by order**. Evaluating
the untruncated sine of the approximate port phases would add higher
orders and would be a different approximation. The old reflection
remainder transfers to the constructed hierarchy by the proved
equivalence; it does not automatically transfer to such a different
feedback algorithm.

<a id="sine-composition-explicit-defect"></a>
## An explicit complete-state defect and finite bound

Let the maximum initial form and phase residuals about the declared
common origins be at most \(\epsilon\ge0\). The uniform numerical
statement below additionally assumes \(\epsilon\le10^{-32}\). The linear source
retains those residuals exactly. Original acquired sources use their
original common origins; any later centered description must retain
its accumulated means separately. No parity is assumed for the actual
initial residuals.

Use the complete lift
\(\widetilde z=z_s+z_1+z_2+z_3\), including hidden \(z_2\).
Let \(D_H=1-2g^2H^2\), \(\ell_H=1-2gH\), and set
\[
 \begin{split}
 Y_1&=\frac{gA_*}{D_H},&
 X_2&=\frac{2gH Y_1^2}{D_H},&
 Y_2&=2gH X_2,\\
 X_3&=\frac{H}{D_H}
           \left(4gY_1Y_2+\frac43gY_1^3\right),&
 Y_3&=2gH X_3,&
 Y_s&=\frac{\epsilon}{\ell_H},\qquad W=Y_2+Y_3+Y_s.
 \end{split}
 \tag{19}
\]
These are envelopes, not measured coefficients. The first bound uses
the heat-phase formula for the signed central input measure. The
normalized quadratic and cubic maps have bounds
\(\|Q(a,b)\|_\infty\le2\|a\|_\infty\|b\|_\infty\) and
\(\|T(a,b,c)\|_\infty\le(4/3)\|a\|_\infty
\|b\|_\infty\|c\|_\infty\). Integrating their form forcings and
the linear phase feedback gives the common denominator \(D_H\)
and the remaining bounds in (19).

Taylor expansion of the real sine about each target edge has a
normalized fourth-order remainder at most
\((2/3)\|y\|_\infty^4\). Put \(w=y_2+y_3+y_s\). The lifted
form equation includes \(Q(y_1,y_1)\),
\(2Q(y_1,y_2)\) and \(T(y_1,y_1,y_1)\). Subtracting these
terms from \(Q(y_1+w,y_1+w)+T(y_1+w,y_1+w,y_1+w)\)
and bounding the fourth-order remainder gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \rho_x=g\bigg[
 &4Y_1(Y_3+Y_s)+2W^2\\
 &+\frac43\left(3Y_1^2W+3Y_1W^2+W^3\right)
          +\frac23(Y_1+W)^4\bigg],\qquad \rho_\theta=0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{20}
\]
All terms are nonnegative rational expressions in the admitted
budgets. This includes internal and bridge truncation, as well as
nonlinear source interactions. In particular \(Y_s\) is not silently
set to zero for a zero-input history.

The phase row of every level is linear and exact, so its defect
vanishes. Sources and jumps of the lift and complete law agree.
Consequently (14) gives
\[
 \sup_{t\le H}\|x(t)-\widetilde x(t)\|_\infty
       \le E_x:=\frac{H\rho_x}{1-4g^2H^2},\qquad
 \sup_{t\le H}\|\theta(t)-\widetilde\theta(t)\|_\infty
       \le2gH E_x.
 \tag{21}
\]
Unlike the sharp central reflection remainder, this is a bound on
**all** reconstructed coordinates.

Every expression in (19)-(21) is nondecreasing in the nonnegative
budgets on (17). At their upper endpoints and \(\epsilon=10^{-32}\), exact rational
substitution gives
\[
 Y_1<5\,10^{-7},\quad Y_2<4\,10^{-19},\quad
 Y_3<2\,10^{-25},\qquad
 \rho_x<1.1\,10^{-29},\qquad E_x<2.2\,10^{-29}.
 \tag{22}
\]
This checks a whole finite input/window domain, not a coefficient
or trajectory sweep. An arbitrary new approximate local map still
needs its own (12) or (13) admission; (22) certifies only the
explicitly constructed hierarchy. The hierarchy here is a mathematical
causal functional. Numerical convolution, coefficient or rounding errors
of any executable approximation require their own additional allowance;
they are not included silently in (21).

<a id="sine-composition-preserved-observation"></a>
## A named interaction distinction survives composition

Use the already declared single mediator jump \(q=7/10000\),
\(H=1\), and read \(x_{13}(H)\) separately for each class.
The [response-free comparison](SINE_CLASS_CHANGED_INPUT_PREDICTION.md#sine-changed-input-prior-memory-gap)
gives nominal full-law minus grounded tangent port output
strictly above \(1/40000\). The comparator retains its own
visible-source uncertainty and has no hidden memory. Each model's
actual-source allowance is at most
\(B_s=\epsilon/(1-2g)\); each reading error is
\(\delta=10^{-8}\).

The constructed component hierarchy has its actual linear source,
and (21) bounds its difference from the actual full response.
Thus its recorded output remains above the separately recorded
grounded comparator by at least
\[
 \frac1{40000}-2B_s-2\delta-E_x
                     >2.49\,10^{-5}.
 \tag{23}
\]
The coarser uniform \(E_x\) in (22) already suffices. No fresh
coefficient is needed to establish this strict inequality. The
[retained independent full-law result](SINE_CLASS_COLLECTIVE_FORWARD_PROTOCOL.md#sine-collective-forward-reserved-result)
is compatible finite evidence for the same supplied law and word;
it is not a new evaluation of component feedback.

The sharper reflection-based \(R_5+E_{\rm init}\) bound also remains
available after the hierarchy equivalence proof. It can preserve
the older delicate class-dependent mixed statistic under its own
word, source and recording budgets. Bound (22) alone is not claimed
to resolve that much smaller contrast.

The structural consequence is that the neighboring patterns can
supply both continuous boundary channels while the component maps
retain their internal dynamics, rather than treating those inputs
as independently prescribed scalars. This does not derive a unique
interaction law. It does not show that nonlinear correction is
necessary for the particular memory-loss comparison in (23).
Formation, identity and work certificates belong to the exact
complete state; output fidelity alone does not transfer them to
every substitute.

The general domain (17) remains an approximation domain. It does
not assert that every allowed impulse schedule satisfies the old
work ceiling or acute trapping condition. The two specific existing
words retain their separately established event and identity
premises. There is no additional support event, response campaign,
source acquisition or physical measurement in this composition proof.

## Implementation and evidence boundary

The [private structural and bound owner](../../src/tnfr/physics/_sine_class_interface_composition.py)
reuses the admitted complete-law geometry and shared scalar admission.
It exposes internal degree-aware partitions, both bridge rows and their
linear/cubic factors, the conditional two-row residual bound, and the
explicit cubic residual certificate. It evaluates no causal kernel,
finite-time coefficient or trajectory. The generic output-map theorem
(12) is an analytic contract; the implementation does not infer its
uniform domain or tolerance from a supplied endpoint report.

The [independent component controls](../../tests/physics/test_sine_class_interface_composition.py)
compare recomposition with complete edge equations, check charges and
storage supply, reject degree-two and form-only substitutions, retain
hidden initialization, and exercise exact residual arithmetic and
representation boundaries. The
[retained forward-evidence audit](../../tests/physics/test_sine_class_port_readout_evidence.py)
reconstructs the existing nominal endpoints and comparison allowances
without regenerating the response. Applying the new bound to those
retained intervals is a compatibility consequence, not another
reserved evaluation or a proof of coefficient generation.
