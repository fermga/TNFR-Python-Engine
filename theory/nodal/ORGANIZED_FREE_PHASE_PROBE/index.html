    A freely reacting organized probe through one full relative turn¶

    Two formed C9 constituents can traverse every relative registration on one freely evolving history while both internal identities remain acute. An initially supplied difference of their common forms drives the turn; neither constituent is held, and no phase reset or continuous input is used after contact. The actual mean-form record determines the relative phase through the already declared kinetic row. An integral identity then reads a current harmonic without differentiating that record. The finite-record construction below bounds the same readout from finitely many uncertain samples, under explicit timing and kinetic calibration premises. Separately admitted primitive phase and form snapshots provide a circular necessary test of that kinetic row, without reconstructing phase from it. The preparation result below distinguishes the large supplied form contrast from what any unforced common-contact schedule can generate.

    This is conditional operational access under the existing supplied constitutive family. It does not select that family, establish physical measurement, or replace source, event-work and observation admission. The research program alone selects further work. The phase-faithfulness classification still distinguishes a complete operational alphabet from one current channel's invisible shifts.

    Preparation by a symmetry, not a reset to a target¶

    An isolated C9 under this law is invariant under a constant common-form translation. Its Laplacian annihilates that constant, both evolution rows are unchanged, and its internal storage and identity are unchanged. Thus an independently admitted own-law formation/recovery history can be prepared from the outset with common forms \(+6/\gamma\) and \(-6/\gamma\), while retaining every reached residual and its original error correlations. Conserved zero phase means give the stated relative lift zero without an attachment-time phase reset.

    This is the same exact symmetry used in own-law outer acquisition. Its use here is conditional on that law's source admission and the inequality (4). It does not transfer an endpoint law's source certificate to another law, authenticate records, or evaluate a source producer. The large common forms are supplied preparation. Their lack of isolated Laplacian-storage cost is an identity of (3), not a claim that physical preparation is free or that a native operator creates this state.

    2. Exact mean rows and a monotone full turn¶

    Put

    \[ r=X_A-X_B,\qquad \delta=\Phi_A-\Phi_B,\qquad v=\gamma r,\qquad s=\frac a2\tau,\qquad \overline J=\frac19\sum_{k=0}^8 j_\lambda(\delta+q_{A,k}-q_{B,k}). \tag{5} \]

    Internal currents cancel in each child mean, and the matching edges retain exactly the same relative mean form. Summing (3) therefore gives the exact full-state equations

    \[ r_\tau=-\frac a2(r+\gamma\overline J),\qquad \delta_\tau=\frac{\gamma a}2r,\qquad \boxed{v_s=-v-\gamma^2\overline J,\quad \delta_s=v.} \tag{6} \]

    Both constituents react. On the invariant carrier this reduces to \(\overline J=j_\lambda(\delta)\); no such substitution is needed for the following transit bound.

    Write \(c_0=\gamma^2J_0\). The canonical constants give \(0<c_0<1/2\), hence also \(c_0\le1\). Variation of constants with \(v(0)=12\) gives, for \(0\le s\le1\),

    \[ 12e^{-s}-c_0(1-e^{-s}) \le v(s)\le 12e^{-s}+c_0(1-e^{-s})\le12. \tag{7} \]

    Using \(e^{-1}>1/3\) and \(c_0<1/2\) in the lower bound yields \(v(s)>11/3\). Integration of the lower bound, with \(e^{-1}<2/5\) and the weaker \(c_0\le1\), gives

    \[ \begin{split} \delta(1)-\delta(0) &\ge12(1-e^{-1})-c_0e^{-1}\\ &>\frac{34}{5}>2\pi. \end{split} \tag{8} \]

    The elementary estimates on \(e\) follow from its positive series: \(5/2<e<3\). The final comparison follows, for example, from \(\pi<22/7\). Thus a unique \(s_*\in(0,1)\) satisfies \(\delta(s_*)=2\pi\). In fact \(s_*>\pi/6\), since \(v\le12\). No trajectory or numerical root is needed to prove this statement. The flow continues freely; \(s_*\) merely ends the readout integral.

    This establishes access to every intermediate relative registration in one actual history, without holding either phase fixed or independently preparing a separate contact for every query.

    3. Fine-state retention through the nonacute transit¶

    Let \(P\) project onto the separate zero-mean subspaces of the two rings. It commutes with \(L\). Write \(L_f=L|_{\operatorname{ran}P}\). The fine projection of the gradient is zero on the carrier for every \(\delta\): each ring's two internal currents cancel and its cross current is constant over slots. Consequently the exact fine rows are

    \[ q_\tau=\frac\gamma4 L_fz,\qquad z_\tau=-\frac14L_fz-\frac\gamma4 g(q,\delta),\qquad g(q,\delta)=A(q,\delta)q. \tag{9} \]

    Here \(A\) is the integrated projected Hessian on the straight fine segment from zero to \(q\), at the current relative mean phase. On the bootstrap tube \(\|q\|<1/100\), every internal gap differs from \(\alpha\) by less than \(1/50\). In particular its magnitude is below \(18/25\). With \(C_6(u)=1-u^2/2+u^4/24-u^6/720\),

    \[ j_\lambda'(u)\ge\cos u-\frac2{243} \ge C_6(18/25)-\frac2{243}>\frac7{10} \quad\hbox{on those internal gaps}. \tag{10} \]

    The cross-edge curvature can be negative during the turn, but is bounded below by \(-J_1\). On the two fine ring spaces the internal Laplacian has lowest eigenvalue \(2-2\cos(2\pi/9)>2/5\) and norm at most four; the matching Laplacian has norm two. Therefore the actual bounds are

    \[ L_f\succeq\frac25I,\qquad \frac14I\preceq A(q,\delta)\preceq5I, \tag{11} \]

    uniformly in the complete relative phase circle. For the lower bound, \((7/10)(2/5)-2aJ_1>1/4\); the upper norm is bounded by \(J_1(4+2a)<5\). These are own-law bounds for every \(0\le\lambda\le1\), not a transfer from the sine tangent. For the internal spectral bound, \(\alpha>2/3\) and cosine decreases on this interval, while \(\cos(2/3)\le1-(2/3)^2/2+(2/3)^4/24=191/243<4/5\).

    Define the positive functional

    \[ F=\frac12\|q+\gamma z\|^2+\frac12\gamma^2\|z\|^2, \qquad \|q\|^2\le4F,\qquad F\le\|q\|^2+\frac32\gamma^2\|z\|^2. \tag{12} \]

    Substitution of both fine rows gives the exact identity

    \[ F_\tau=-\frac{\gamma^2}{4} \left[q^\top g+z^\top L_fz+2\gamma z^\top g\right]. \tag{13} \]

    Since \(\|g\|\le5\|q\|\), Young's inequality gives \(2\gamma|z^\top g|\le\|q\|^2/8+200\gamma^2\|z\|^2\). The canonical \(\gamma<1/3000\) satisfies \(2/5-200\gamma^2>1/8\) and \(3\gamma^2/2<1\). Equations (11)--(13) consequently imply

    \[ \boxed{F_\tau\le-\frac{\gamma^2}{32}F,\qquad F(\tau)\le F_0e^{-\gamma^2\tau/32} =F_0e^{-125s/2}.} \tag{14} \]

    Initially (4) and (12) place \(q\) strictly inside the tube. The same strict bound remains at any proposed first exit, so continuity closes the bootstrap through the whole observation horizon. Both internal cycles stay acute with winding one, including every allowed fine direction. No stationary profile replaces the actual state.

    The full relative turn necessarily passes nonacute matching gaps and their phase cuts. This proves retention of the two constituent identities, not a fixed acute identity of the joined graph or fixed windings on every new mixed cycle. U3 at a support event is a separate admission; it is not reapplied as a filter of the unchanged global smooth current during (3).

    For later observation, put \(d_k=q_{A,k}-q_{B,k}\). Its sum is zero. Taylor expansion and (2) give the quadratic error

    \[ \begin{split} |\overline J-j_\lambda(\delta)| &\le\frac{J_2}{18}\sum_kd_k^2 \le\frac{J_2}{9}\|q\|^2\\ &\le\frac{4J_2F_0}{9}e^{-125s/2}. \end{split} \tag{15} \]

    The first-order correction cancels because the fine means are retained.

    4. Supplied contact work¶

    Immediately before attachment the two rings are disconnected. Their state is unchanged by adding the nine weighted edges. At the declared \(\delta(0)=0\), the exact storage jump is

    \[ \Delta H=a\sum_k\left[ \frac12(r_0+z_{A,k}-z_{B,k})^2+ V_\lambda(q_{A,k}-q_{B,k})\right]\ge0. \tag{16} \]

    The cross term linear in \(r_0\) vanishes by the separate fine zero means. Since \(V_\lambda(0)=j_\lambda(0)=0\), \(0\le V_\lambda(u)\le J_1u^2/2\). Using \(r_0=12/\gamma\) gives the source-inclusive bound

    \[ \boxed{\Delta H\le \frac{81}{125} +a\left(\rho_x^2+J_1\rho_\theta^2\right).} \tag{17} \]

    The ideal carrier's cost is exactly \(81/125\); the residual term is retained for the actual source. The earlier isolated formation/storage history is separate from this positive attachment work. Nothing in the continuous loss supplies a reservoir, an autonomous attachment time or a native event realization. The horizon \(T=2000/\gamma^2\) and weak contact are explicit parts of this operational design, not a change of the constitutive law or a laboratory time estimate.

    5. A derivative-free harmonic observation¶

    The mean-form record gives \(v(s)=\gamma(X_A(s)-X_B(s))\). The unchanged phase row gives exactly

    \[ \delta(s)=\delta(0)+\int_0^s v(u)\,du. \tag{18} \]

    This uses the declared initial relative lift and kinetic law. It does not use an inverse of the current being tested, hidden fine phases, or a held phase reference. It cannot simultaneously validate an unknown kinetic row or recover an unadmitted initial phase.

    For any positive integer \(n\), define from the same continuing record, up to its one-turn readout time \(s_*\),

    \[ B_n=\frac1{\pi\gamma^2} \left\{ \frac n2\int_0^{s_*}\cos(n\delta)\,v^3\,ds -\int_0^{s_*}\sin(n\delta)\,v^2\,ds -\frac12[\sin(n\delta)v^2]_0^{s_*} \right\}. \tag{19} \]

    To verify it, multiply \(v_s+v=-\gamma^2\overline J\) by \(v\sin(n\delta)\), integrate, and use \(\delta_s=v\) to integrate the \(vv_s\) term by parts. The result is exactly

    \[ B_n=\frac1\pi\int_0^{s_*} \overline J(s)\sin(n\delta(s))\,v(s)\,ds. \tag{20} \]

    No derivative of a measured record remains. In the primary preparation \(\delta(0)=0\), \(\delta(s_*)=2\pi\), so the endpoint term in (19) vanishes. It is displayed because it must be retained for an independently admitted nonzero starting phase and a complete turn from that phase.

    Strict monotonicity permits \(d\delta=v\,ds\). If \(b_n\) is the actual sine Fourier coefficient of \(j_\lambda\), orthogonality and (15) imply

    \[ \boxed{|B_n-b_n|\le\frac{8J_2F_0}{9}.} \tag{21} \]

    Indeed the absolute integral of the fine current error over a turn is at most \(2\pi\) times its uniform upper bound. For this supplied family, \(b_1=1\), \(b_9=\lambda/2187\), \(b_{18}=-\lambda/4374\), and the other positive-index coefficients vanish. Formula (21) concerns the actual freely reacting fine state, not a nominal carrier sampled at imposed registrations.

    Exact access is not finite-resolution identification¶

    Let \(A_n\) denote the expression in braces in (19). On the ideal carrier its ninth-harmonic value is \(\pi\gamma^2\lambda/2187\). This raw contrast is small; the normalization in (19) does not increase measurement accuracy. For the fixed declared \(\gamma\) and normalization, if a separately admitted complete record and quadrature error bounds \(|\widehat A_n-A_n|\) by \(E_n\), its total coefficient uncertainty is at most

    6.1 Subtracting the known free relaxation¶

    \[ h(s)=12e^{-s},\qquad w(s)=v(s)-h(s),\qquad R(s)=\frac{v(s)^2-h(s)^2}{2\gamma^2} =\frac{h(s)w(s)+w(s)^2/2}{\gamma^2}. \tag{23} \]

    6.2 Finite sampling, reconstruction and stopping¶

    Use \(N+1\) samples at nominal scaled times \(s_i=i/N\), \(i=0,\ldots,N\), with \(N\) a positive integer and \(H=1/N\). The following are independently supplied observation premises:

The true kinetic law and \(\gamma=1/(1023\pi)\) remain unchanged; \(\widehat\gamma\) is only an observation calibration. An arbitrary unknown clock scale is not admitted unless it satisfies the uniform exposure bound throughout this horizon. The error allowances are deterministic joint uniform bounds and may be correlated. Independent justification means justification apart from the evaluated response; no statistical independence or stochastic averaging is assumed.

    For computable enclosures let \(g_-\le\gamma\le g_+\) be positive rational bounds and let \(\pi_-\le\pi\le\pi_+\). Use the norm-derived sufficient upper bound \(F_0\) from (4), replacing \(\gamma\) by \(g_+\) there. Define

    \[ |\widehat v_i-v(s_i)|\le E,\qquad \widehat v_i=\widehat\gamma\widehat r_i. \tag{29} \]

    The term \(12\kappa\) retains the error in the large form mean; it cannot be dropped merely because the constitutive contrast is small.

    \[ |\widetilde v-v|\le\varepsilon_v,\qquad |\widetilde\delta-\delta|\le E_\delta. \tag{31} \]

    The integral in (30) uses only an exponential and the integral of a linear function on each cell; it requires no derivative of any reading.

    \[ \varepsilon_v< \min\left(\frac{11}{3},\,\frac{34}{5}-2\pi_+\right). \tag{32} \]

    6.3 Calibration, quadrature and endpoint errors¶

    The residual normalization in (30) uses the uncertain kinetic calibration. Its complete error, rather than a numerator-only error, is bounded by

    \[ \varepsilon_R= \frac{(24+\varepsilon_v)\varepsilon_v}{2G_-^2} +R_0\left((1-\kappa)^{-2}-1\right),\qquad |\widetilde R-R|\le\varepsilon_R. \tag{34} \]

    To see this, first compare \(\widetilde v^2\) with \(v^2\), using \(v\le12\); then compare \(\gamma^2/\widehat\gamma^2\) with one in the true residual. Both errors are retained in (34).

    For an explicit finite quadrature, take a positive integer \(K\). Each has length at most \(H/K\), no portion crosses a data knot, and there are at most \(NK\) such evaluations. This splitting matters because \(\widetilde w_s\) can jump at a data knot.

    \[ \begin{split} W&=C+E,\qquad D=2C+2E/H,\qquad V=12+W,\qquad V_1=12+D,\\ \widehat R_0&=(12W+W^2/2)/G_-^2,\\ \widehat R_1&=(12W+VD)/G_-^2,\\ \widehat R_2&=(12W+24D+D^2)/G_-^2,\\ A_0&=nV+2,\\ A_1&=nV_1+n^2V^2+2nV,\\ A_2&=12n+3n^2VV_1+n^3V^3+2nV_1+2n^2V^2,\\ M_{f,2}&=\widehat R_2A_0+ 2\widehat R_1A_1+\widehat R_0A_2,\\ E_Q&=\frac{M_{f,2}}{24(NK)^2}. \end{split} \tag{35} \]

    For example \(|\widetilde w|\le W\), \(|\widetilde w_s|\le D\) and \(\widetilde w_{ss}=0\) inside a cell. Therefore

    \[ \widetilde R_{ss} =\frac{h\widetilde w-2h\widetilde w_s+ (\widetilde w_s)^2}{\widehat\gamma^2}. \tag{36} \]

    This proves the three residual bounds in (35). For \(\widetilde A=n\widetilde v\cos(n\widetilde\delta) -2\sin(n\widetilde\delta)\), the bounds on \(|\widetilde A|,|\widetilde A_s|,|\widetilde A_{ss}|\) are respectively \(A_0,A_1,A_2\), using \(\widetilde\delta_s=\widetilde v\) and \(|\widetilde v_{ss}|=h\le12\).     Let \(Q_{N,K}\) be this quadrature of \(\widetilde R\widetilde A\), and retain the reconstructed endpoint correction:

    \[ \widehat B_n^{\,\rm exact}=\frac1\pi\left\{ Q_{N,K}-[\widetilde R\sin(n\widetilde\delta)] _0^{\widehat s_*}\right\}. \tag{37} \]

    Both sine factors at these endpoints are \(\sin(n\widehat\delta_0)\); they need not vanish. Define the deliberately conservative observation bound

    \[ E_O=\varepsilon_R(nV+4) +R_0\left[n\varepsilon_v+(12n^2+4n)E_\delta\right] +J_0\varepsilon_v. \tag{38} \]

    Here is a direct comparison with the true full-turn integral. For \(A=nv\cos(n\delta)-2\sin(n\delta)\), equations (31) and (34) imply

    \[ |\widetilde R\widetilde A-RA| \le\varepsilon_R(nV+2) +R_0\left[n\varepsilon_v+(12n^2+2n)E_\delta\right]. \tag{39} \]

    Each endpoint difference is at most \(\varepsilon_R+R_0nE_\delta\). Finally the true current integral between \(\widehat s_*\) and \(s_*\) is bounded by \(J_0\varepsilon_v\), using \(d\delta=v\,ds\), \(|\overline J|\le J_0\) and (33). Adding these terms proves (38), including the true and reconstructed endpoints. The last term could be sharpened near a full turn but no such improvement is used here.

    Allow a further certified coefficient error \(\varepsilon_{\rm num}\) in evaluating (37). Combining (21), (35) and (38) gives the finite-record theorem

    \[ \boxed{|\widehat B_n-b_n|\le \mathcal E_n:= \frac{8J_2F_0}{9}+\frac{E_O+E_Q}{\pi_-} +\varepsilon_{\rm num}.} \tag{40} \]

    The terms separately retain fine-state bias, raw readings, clock exposures, calibration, phase readout, interpolation, stopping, endpoints, quadrature and numerical evaluation.
