    Constitutive dependence and source selection under weak contact¶

    The retained harmonic alternative changes the attracting relative registrations of the weakly contacted organization. At the fixed spoke weight eta=1/100000, a declared full-state neighborhood contains twenty stationary organizations modulo common phase rotation: ten strict relative minima and ten index-one saddles. All five origins that attract under the sine law become saddle origins under this alternative.

    An own-law source theorem below also places ten explicit intervals of the original donor preparation inside the alternative's attracting basins, retaining all original errors and the complete observation history. On one declared common preparation interval, the two laws select asymptotic relative origins separated by more than pi/12. The alternatives retain the same support, capacities, clock and constituent profile; their collective registration geometry is nevertheless different. Neither reached sources nor the sine basin certificate are substituted for the alternative's own history and bounds.

    1. Complete weighted law and the full-state domain¶

    Write c=0,...,4 for host children and c=5=D for the donor; j=0,...,8 is the internal slot. The primitive node order is 9*c+j. Put

    \[ \begin{split} \eta&=\frac1{100000},\qquad \gamma=\frac1{1023\pi},\qquad \tau=\frac{1023}{1024}t_{\rm original},\\ V(a)&=1-\cos a+\frac{(1-\cos9a)^2}{6\,9^4},\\ j(a)&=V'(a)=\sin a+\frac{\sin9a-\tfrac12\sin18a}{2187}. \end{split} \tag{1} \]

    Let L_eta be the unnormalized weighted Laplacian, and let \(S_i(\theta)=\sum_{b\sim i}w_{ib}j(\theta_b-\theta_i)\). Write \(U(\theta)=\sum_{\{i,b\}}w_{ib}V(\theta_b-\theta_i)\) for its phase potential. The complete unforced law and its storage are

    \[ \begin{split} x'&=\frac{-L_\eta x+\gamma S(\theta)}4,& \theta'&=\frac\gamma4L_\eta x,\\ H&=\frac12x^TL_\eta x+U(\theta),& H'&=-\frac14\|L_\eta x\|^2. \end{split} \tag{2} \]

    Prime means the declared tau clock in both rows. The pressure in the held-capacity nodal row is supplied by the first complete equation; it is not reconstructed from a response. Odd current and Laplacian row sums preserve total form and total lifted phase. All coefficients retain their structural units; no laboratory clock or storage unit is identified. Support, eta and the absence of later inputs/events remain premises. The balance follows from this alternative potential and current, not from copying a sine certificate.

    Put alpha=2pi/9 and b=2pi/5. With compatible old marked-edge lifts, write

    \[ \begin{split} \theta_{c,j}&=\alpha(j-4)+\Phi_c+q_{c,j},& \sum_jq_{c,j}&=0,\\ t&=\frac15\sum_{c=0}^4\Phi_c-\Phi_D,& b_c&=b(c-2),\\ h_c&=\Phi_c-\Phi_D-t-b_c,& \sum_{c=0}^4h_c&=0. \end{split} \tag{3} \]

    Here t is a relative origin, not the evolution clock. The domain is

    \[ \|h\|_2\le6\eta,\qquad \|q\|_\infty\le\frac1{100}, \qquad t\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z),\qquad \mathbf1^Tx=0. \tag{4} \]

    Forms may be any finite signed values on that mean leaf. Internal closing edges and host-rim closing edges retain their original one-turn conventions. All old compatible gaps remain positive and below pi: internal gaps differ from alpha by at most 1/50; rim gaps differ from b by at most 12*eta+1/50. Thus (4) retains all six internal windings, all nine rim windings and all 32 zero mixed host periods of the complete marked class. Spoke phases have no added acute restriction.

    The domain is a stationary classification neighborhood. It is not asserted to be positively invariant or to contain the initial actual source. Sections 6--8 retain that source in a larger energy trap and classify its limit using the stationary theorem. No finite settling time is supplied.

    2. Own-law host-shape contraction¶

    The real derivative bounds from (1) are

    \[ J_0=\frac{1459}{1458}<\frac{1001}{1000},\quad J_1=\frac{245}{243}<\frac{101}{100},\quad J_2=\frac{10}{9},\quad J_3=\frac83, \qquad |j^{(r)}|\le J_r\quad(0\le r\le3). \tag{5} \]

    Let D be the forward C5 incidence, L5=D^T D and \(P=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/5\). Define

    \[ \begin{split} c_b&=\cos b=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{4},& a&=j'(b)=c_b+\frac{\sqrt5}{486},\\ A&=aL_5,&G&=A^+,& \lambda_1&=a(2-2\cos b),& \lambda_2&=a(2-2\cos2b). \end{split} \tag{6} \]

    G acts on the zero-mean host space. The elementary bounds \(3/10<c_b<1/3\) give \(\lambda_1>2/5\); also \(a<8/25\) and \(2-2c_b<7/5\) give \(\lambda_1<9/20\). The bound a<8/25 follows, for example, from \(c_b<5/16\) and \(\sqrt5/486<1/216\). Consequently \(\|G\|_2<5/2\), and \(\|D\|_2<2\).

    On the internal carrier q=0, ground the donor phase at zero and seek host phases \(t+b_c+u_c\), with zero-mean u. The four projected stationary equations are exactly

    \[ \begin{split} u&=-G\{D^TN(Du)+\eta Pj(t+b+u)\}=:\mathcal K_t(u),\\ N(d)&=j(b+d)-j(b)-a d. \end{split} \tag{7} \]

    The scalar b in N is the rim gap; the b inside the vector current is \((b_c)_{c=0}^4\). All current derivatives below are evaluated on real arguments. Since \(|N(d)|\le J_2d^2/2\), the map satisfies on \(\|u\|_2\le6\eta\)

    \[ \begin{split} \|\mathcal K_t(u)\|_2 &\le\frac52\left[4J_2(6\eta)^2+ \eta\sqrt5\frac{1001}{1000}\right]<6\eta,\\ \|D_u\mathcal K_t\|_2 &\le Q:=\frac52\left[48J_2+\frac{101}{100}\right]\eta =\frac{16303}{12000000}<\frac1{500}. \end{split} \tag{8} \]

    The bounds use \(\|D^TN(Du)\|_2\le4J_2\|u\|_2^2\) and \(\|D^T\operatorname{diag}(N'(Du))D\|_2\le8J_2\|u\|_2\). Thus, for every real t, there is exactly one projected stationary shape u(t) in the declared host ball. It is smooth in t by the invertible projected derivative. This is an own-law real contraction; the complex growth bounds of the sine current are not reused.

    Write \(f(t)=j(t+b)\), \(u_1(t)=-Gf(t)\). Each active frequency 1, 9 and 18 has zero sum on the five host vertices. Direct subtraction of (7) and differentiation in t give

    \[ \begin{split} \|u-\eta u_1\|_2 &\le\frac52\left[4J_2\,36+6\frac{101}{100}\right]\eta^2 <416\eta^2,\\ \|u_t\|_2 &\le\frac{(5/2)\sqrt5(101/100)}{1-Q}\eta<6\eta,\\ \|u_t-\eta u_{1,t}\|_2 &\le\frac52\left[8J_2\,36+6J_2+6\frac{101}{100}\right]\eta^2 <832\eta^2. \end{split} \tag{9} \]

    In the last line, retain separately the rim derivative defect, \(\eta[j'(t+b+u)-j'(t+b)]\), and \(\eta\operatorname{diag}(j'(t+b+u))u_t\). These terms are what bound the derivative of the actual shape, rather than just its values.

    3. Collective torque and its finite C1 remainder¶

    Let W(t) be the alternative wheel potential evaluated at its projected stationary shape. The remaining stationary equation is

    \[ T(t)=W'(t)=\eta\sum_{c=0}^4j(t+b_c+u_c(t))=0. \tag{10} \]

    Cyclic relabeling makes W and T periodic with period 2*pi/5. Reflection and phase inversion make W even and T odd. Both claims use uniqueness of the shape in (8), as well as the actual symmetries of (1). Therefore \(T(m\pi/5)=0\) exactly for every integer m. Symmetry alone does not determine their curvature.

    Put \(\varepsilon=1/2187\). The five-point Fourier identities give

    \[ \begin{split} f^TGf &=\frac5{2\lambda_1} [1+\varepsilon^2-2\varepsilon\cos(10t)] +\frac{5\varepsilon^2}{8\lambda_2},\\ \sum_cj'(t+b_c)u_{1,c} &=-\frac12\frac d{dt}(f^TGf) =-K\sin(10t),\qquad K=\frac{25}{2187\lambda_1}>\frac1{40}. \end{split} \tag{11} \]

    Indeed the ninth harmonic is spatially mode minus one on this rim, whereas the eighteenth is mode minus two. Only frequencies 1 and 9 have a nonconstant cross term in f^TGf; their inner product is \(-5\cos(10t)/2\). The eighteenth-harmonic square contributes the constant last term. Equation (11) is an exact coefficient identity, not yet a finite-eta verdict.

    Taylor's formula, (5) and (9) bound the actual torque and its derivative:

    \[ \begin{split} |T(t)+K\eta^2\sin(10t)|&<960\eta^3,\\ |T'(t)+10K\eta^2\cos(10t)|&<3001\eta^3, \qquad t\in\mathbb R. \end{split} \tag{12} \]

    Here are explicit constants for both remainders:

    \[ \begin{split} C_0&=\sqrt5\frac{101}{100}\,416+ \frac{J_2}{2}\,36<960,\\ C_1&=\sqrt5\left(J_2\,416+\frac{101}{100}\,832\right) +\frac{J_3}{2}\,36+J_2\,36<3001. \end{split} \tag{13} \]

    The rational upper bound \(\sqrt5<161/72\), obtained from \(161^2-5\,72^2=1\), proves the displayed strict inequalities. For C0, subtract the exact linear term and use the actual current's quadratic J2 Taylor remainder. For C1, expand \(j'(t+b+u)(1+u_t)\): the two linear defects use 416 and 832, the second-order j' remainder contributes \(J_3\|u\|^2/2\), and the product \([j'(t+b+u)-j'(t+b)]u_t\) contributes \(J_2\|u\|\|u_t\|\). No unbounded derivative of a remainder is inferred from a value estimate.

    4. Twenty stationary registrations in the declared domain¶

    On the relative-origin circle, let

    \[ I_k=\left[\frac{k\pi}{10}-\frac\pi{60}, \frac{k\pi}{10}+\frac\pi{60}\right], \qquad k=0,\ldots,19. \tag{14} \]

    These intervals are disjoint. At their endpoints and everywhere outside their interiors, \(|\sin(10t)|\ge1/2\). The leading torque magnitude there exceeds \(\eta^2/80\), while its error is at most \(960\eta^3=0.0096\eta^2<\eta^2/80\). It has opposite signs at the two endpoints of every interval and no zeros outside the intervals.

    Within I_k, \(|\cos(10t)|\ge\sqrt3/2>4/5\), and its sign is \((-1)^k\). The leading derivative magnitude exceeds \(\eta^2/5\), while its error is at most \(3001\eta^3=0.03001\eta^2<\eta^2/5\). Thus the actual torque is strictly monotone in each interval and has exactly one simple zero there.

    For even k, the zero is exactly \(t=k\pi/10=m\pi/5\), by the symmetry already proved. Its derivative is negative. For odd k, the unique zero in I_k has positive derivative. The proof does not assign its exact position or assert that it differs from its interval center. There are exactly ten of each type.

    The four host shape directions have positive curvature. In fact the rim weights in the domain satisfy the lower bound proved in section 5, so the host shape block exceeds \(L_5/4-\eta J_1I>I/5\). Eliminating that block gives the scalar Schur complement W''=T'. Consequently the wheel relative Hessian is positive definite at the ten odd-interval zeros, and has four positive directions and one negative direction at the ten exact even-interval zeros.

    5. Full-state stationary exclusion and stability¶

    The full fine directions cannot create additional stationary points inside (4). To prove this for the actual alternative, let \(C_6(v)=1-v^2/2+v^4/24-v^6/720\). Along the straight segment from the carrier phases to any q in (4), compatible internal gaps obey \(|\delta|<18/25\), and rim gaps obey \(0<\delta<13/10\). For example, \(\pi<22/7\) gives \(\alpha+1/50<18/25\) and \(b+12\eta+1/50<13/10\). Since \(j'(\delta)\ge\cos\delta-2/243\), exact rational cosine bounds give

    \[ C_6(18/25)-\frac2{243}>\frac7{10},\qquad C_6(13/10)-\frac2{243}>\frac14. \tag{15} \]

    Spoke curvatures may be negative, but are bounded below by -J1. Let P_f subtract each child's slot mean. On its 48-dimensional range, the internal C9 Laplacian has gap greater than 2/5, while the largest eigenvalue of the nine disjoint spoke stars is six. Every Hessian along that segment therefore satisfies

    \[ P_{\rm f}\nabla^2U P_{\rm f}\big|_{\operatorname{ran}P_{\rm f}} >\left[\frac7{10}\frac25-6\eta\frac{245}{243}\right]I =\frac{226751}{810000}I>\frac14I. \tag{16} \]

    For any collective phases, the carrier has \(P_{\rm f}\nabla U=0\): internal currents cancel and wheel currents are constant in the slot. Integrating (16) consequently gives \(q^TP_{\rm f}\nabla U(\theta)>\|q\|^2/4\) for nonzero q.

    At a full equilibrium, the phase row in (2), connected support and zero total form first force x=0. The form row then forces \(\nabla U=0\), which by (16) forces q=0. The projected host equation and contraction force h=u(t). Thus all full stationary states in (4) are exactly the twenty wheel equilibria already classified; noncarrier directions were retained and excluded by their own-law curvature, rather than discarded before testing stationarity.

    At a carrier, \(j'(\alpha)=\cos\alpha\) exactly. In slot-first order the phase Hessian has the product form

    \[ M_{54}=\cos\alpha\,L_9\otimes I_6+I_9\otimes M_{\rm wheel}. \tag{17} \]

    The 48 fine directions remain positive by (16), while the five relative collective directions have the inertia from section 4. The ten minima therefore have 53 positive relative phase directions. The ten saddles have 52 positive directions and one negative direction, with no extra zero direction besides common phase rotation.

    This phase curvature controls the actual complete two-row dynamics. On the relative space its own linearization has the symmetric pencil

    \[ s^2L_\eta^{-1}+\frac s4I+\frac{\gamma^2}{16}M_{54}. \tag{18} \]

    For a positive phase Hessian, the scalar quadratic identity obtained by testing an eigenvector has all roots with negative real part. For an index-one Hessian, the pencil increases strictly for positive real s from one negative direction to a positive matrix, giving exactly one positive eigenvalue. The imaginary part of the same scalar identity excludes nonreal eigenvalues with nonnegative real part; invertibility of the relative Hessian excludes s=0. All remaining eigenvalues have negative real part. Thus the ten minima are locally exponentially attracting on fixed-mean leaves, and the other ten are hyperbolic index-one saddles of the full law.
