    Collective boundary constraints and the scope of restoration¶

    This owner states a conditional consequence of the supplied two-row constitutive flow: a positively curved boundary removes exactly the old zero-curvature directions that its actual phase constraints distinguish. The full flow, rather than a static stiffness alone, determines relaxation. The result uses ordinary positive-semidefinite linear algebra and local dynamical systems arguments. It is not an additional TNFR axiom or a selection principle for a fundamental constitutive law.

    The form foundation, parameter foundation and NFR participation contract retain their separate responsibilities. The conditional critical parent in the interaction owner provides one concrete control below; its preparation and physical identification are not premises supplied by this theorem.

    Required premise Consequence admitted here Information still needed
    Complete held two-row law, connected support and clock Exact storage loss and full linear pencil Selection of that law, support or clock
    Actual equilibrium and its old positive-semidefinite Hessian Old neutral phase subspace Formation or entry into its local domain
    Actual positive boundary curvatures Kernel intersection and restoration of the distinguished directions Positivity from an independently justified constitutive premise
    Admitted positive normal curvature Exact static compliance Dynamic memory, rates and hidden initialization
    Positive complete relative Hessian Local exponential attraction of the full smooth flow Quantitative basin or finite capture deadline
    A surviving Hessian kernel Semisimple linear zero modes and their linear projector Nonlinear center dynamics and higher-order attraction

    1. Actual model and equilibrium¶

    Use an orthonormal chart of the relative phase space after removing the common phase rotation. Use the supplied nondimensional structural chart and held unit nodal capacities. Both the relative phase displacement and signed form have dimension \(n\). The total form is zero and the common phase origin is fixed. Hold the connected support, its symmetric positive-definite relative conductance Laplacian \(L\), the positive constant \(\gamma\), and the clock \(\tau\). The complete unforced model is

    \[ x'=\frac{-Lx-\gamma\nabla U(\theta)}4,\qquad \theta'=\frac{\gamma Lx}4,\qquad {}'=\frac{d}{d\tau}. \tag{1} \]

    Assume \(U\) is sufficiently smooth on the declared local phase chart. Its storage and continuous loss are

    \[ H(x,\theta)=\frac12x^\top Lx+U(\theta),\qquad H'=-\frac14\|Lx\|^2. \tag{2} \]

    The cancellation in (2) follows directly from both rows of (1); it does not select \(U\). Fix an admitted equilibrium \(x_*=0\), \(\nabla U(\theta_*)=0\). All subsequent Hessians are evaluated at this same equilibrium:

    \[ M=\nabla^2U(\theta_*)=M_0+B^\top K B,\qquad M_0\succeq0,\qquad K\succ0. \tag{3} \]

    For pairwise boundary edges, \(B\) is their oriented phase-difference map restricted to the relative chart, and \(K_{ee}=w_e V_e''(\delta_{e,*})\). Thus \(K\) contains the actual constitutive curvatures at the actual gaps, not merely positive conductances \(w_e\). The old contribution \(M_0\) must also be re-admitted at \(\theta_*\); a displaced equilibrium need not retain an old endpoint's Hessian. More general boundary observations are allowed only when their complete Hessian really has (3). A nonlinear observation with nonzero current can add Hessian terms omitted by an incidence-only formula.

    The theorem does not assume that a support addition is passive. Adding a pairwise edge of weight \(w_e\) in both rows changes the storage by \(w_e[(x_i-x_j)^2/2+V_e(\theta_i-\theta_j)]\), under that edge's declared potential normalization. That event work is separate from (2).

    2. Which collective directions does the boundary restore?¶

    For every relative phase vector \(v\),

    \[ v^\top Mv=v^\top M_0v+\|K^{1/2}Bv\|^2. \tag{4} \]

    Both terms are nonnegative. A zero quadratic value of a symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix is equivalent to membership in its kernel. Consequently

    \[ \boxed{\ker M=\ker M_0\cap\ker(K^{1/2}B).} \tag{5} \]

    Let \(T\) be any full-rank basis of \(\ker M_0\), with \(k_0\) columns. Under \(K\succ0\), the number of surviving relative phase zero modes is

    \[ k=\dim\ker M=k_0-\operatorname{rank}(BT). \tag{6} \]

    The criterion concerns restrictions on the old neutral space. Adding edges, increasing conductance or preserving a graph symmetry does not alone determine this restricted rank or the actual curvature signs.

    If \(K\succeq0\) includes zero-curvature rows, (5) still holds, but (6) uses \(\operatorname{rank}(K^{1/2}BT)\). Such rows impose no quadratic restoring constraint. If \(K\) is signed, neither nonnegativity in (4) nor the kernel-intersection conclusion follows in general.

    3. Full-flow modes, initialization and nonlinear scope¶

    Put \(y=\theta-\theta_*\), and order the full linear state as \((y,x)\). The actual Jacobian and its relative quadratic pencil are

    \[ J=\begin{pmatrix} 0&\gamma L/4\\ -\gamma M/4&-L/4 \end{pmatrix},\qquad Q(s)=s^2L^{-1}+\frac s4I+\frac{\gamma^2}{16}M. \tag{7} \]

    Eliminating \(x\) gives \(L^{-1}y''+y'/4+\gamma^2My/16=0\), so no simultaneous diagonalization of \(L\) and \(M\) has been assumed. For an eigenpair \(Q(s)v=0\), multiplication by \(v^*\) gives \(a s^2+b s+c=0\), with \(a>0,b>0,c\ge0\). Its nonreal roots have negative real part and its nonzero real roots are negative. All nonzero full-flow eigenvalues therefore lie strictly in the left half-plane.

    For \(k>0\), to count the zeros and admit their initialization, let \(W\) be any full-rank basis of \(\ker M\), and set \(G_W=W^\top W\). Define

    \[ R=\begin{pmatrix}W\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad C=G_W^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}W^\top&\gamma W^\top\end{pmatrix}. \tag{8} \]

    Direct substitution yields \(JR=0\), \(CJ=0\), \(CR=I_k\). A generalized zero eigenvector satisfying \(Jz=Ra\) would give \(a=CJz=0\). Hence zero is semisimple. The full \(2n\)-dimensional flow has exactly \(k\) zero modes and \(2n-k\) strictly stable modes. Its zero spectral projector and retained linear amplitude are

    \[ P_0=RC= \begin{pmatrix} WG_W^{-1}W^\top&\gamma WG_W^{-1}W^\top\\ 0&0 \end{pmatrix},\qquad a_0=G_W^{-1}W^\top(y_0+\gamma x_0). \tag{9} \]

    The Gram matrix is essential when \(W\) is not orthonormal. In the linearized system, \(C(y(\tau),x(\tau))\) is constant and the stable component decays. Formula (9) is a linear spectral initialization; it is not a nonlinear phase-only measurement or an exact nonlinear quotient.

    If (5) has trivial kernel, then \(M\succ0\) and \(J\) is Hurwitz. The smooth actual nonlinear flow is locally exponentially attracting. For example, a positive solution of \(J^\top P+PJ=-I\) gives a local quadratic Lyapunov function after bounding the nonlinear remainder. This statement supplies no numerical radius, uniform contact limit or source-capture certificate.

    If a kernel survives, (5)--(9) alone imply no nonlinear attraction. A separately admitted center manifold can retain nonlinear normal lag, and its tracking initialization need not equal (9) beyond first order. The critical-parent initialization proof is a model-specific example of the additional work.

    A sufficient higher-order local admission is a strict local minimum of the full storage, an isolated stationary point in a small compact sublevel component, and containment of that component in the identity chart. Equation (2), \(L\succ0\), and invariance imply that a trajectory with identically zero loss has \(x=0\) and \(\nabla U=0\). LaSalle's argument then gives local asymptotic attraction. Neither strict higher-order storage nor isolation follows from the kernel rank.

    4. Static normal compliance and dynamic elimination¶

    4.1 Exact quadratic static reduction¶

    Complete the old kernel basis \(T\) by a matrix \(S\) such that \(\mathcal R=[T\ S]\) is invertible. Orthogonality is unnecessary. Write \(y=Tq+Sz\), and define

    \[ A=S^\top M_0S\succ0,\qquad D=BT,\qquad E=BS. \tag{10} \]

    Because \(M_0T=0\), the exact quadratic phase storage has block matrix

    \[ \mathcal R^\top M\mathcal R= \begin{pmatrix} D^\top KD&D^\top KE\\ E^\top KD&A+E^\top KE \end{pmatrix}. \tag{11} \]

    Minimizing over the admitted normal coordinate gives

    \[ z_*(q)=-(A+E^\top KE)^{-1}E^\top KDq \tag{12} \]

    and the exact effective static stiffness

    \[ \begin{split} M_{\rm stat} &=D^\top[K-KE(A+E^\top KE)^{-1}E^\top K]D\\ &=D^\top(K^{-1}+EA^{-1}E^\top)^{-1}D. \end{split} \tag{13} \]

    The second form follows by the matrix inversion identity; it applies to strictly positive \(K\). The bracket is positive definite, so

    \[ \ker M_{\rm stat}=\ker D,\qquad 0\preceq M_{\rm stat}\preceq D^\top KD. \tag{14} \]

    The upper value is the result of artificially freezing the normal coordinates. Equality along a given \(q\) holds precisely when \(E^\top KDq=0\). Thus normal compliance can reduce the stiffness without changing which directions are restored under these premises. For diagonal edge \(K\), zero-curvature rows can be deleted before using the inverse expression; more generally restrict to its positive eigenspace. The first Schur expression remains available for \(K\succeq0\).

    Normal invertibility also admits a local nonlinear stationary minimizing graph by the implicit-function theorem. This is static elimination at fixed retained coordinate. It does not establish that this graph is invariant under (1).

    4.2 The actual spectral and initial-state reduction¶

    Transform the full pencil by congruence, \(\widehat Q(s)=\mathcal R^\top Q(s)\mathcal R\). Its normal block has positive mass, damping and stiffness, and is invertible for \(\operatorname{Re}s\ge0\). The retained spectral equation uses

    \[ Q_{\rm dyn}(s)= \widehat Q_{TT}(s) -\widehat Q_{TS}(s)\widehat Q_{SS}(s)^{-1} \widehat Q_{ST}(s),\qquad Q_{\rm dyn}(0)=\frac{\gamma^2}{16}M_{\rm stat}. \tag{15} \]

    Only its zero-frequency value is fixed by (13). For the linear initial-value problem, the transformed Laplace right-hand side is

    \[ \widehat f(s)= \mathcal R^\top\left[ \left(sL^{-1}+\frac14I\right)y_0+\frac{\gamma}{4}x_0 \right], \tag{16} \]

    and elimination retains \(\widehat f_T-\widehat Q_{TS}\widehat Q_{SS}^{-1}\widehat f_S\). The normal initialization is not generally removed. The frequency dependence corresponds to normal dynamics and, in a reduced time-domain description, memory. Any cancellation or exact Markov closure needs its own proof.

    For a small explicit control, take

    \[ L=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\quad M=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad T=e_1,\quad S=e_2. \tag{17} \]

    Here \(M_{\rm stat}=0\) and the static cross block vanishes, but \(\widehat Q_{TS}=-s^2/3\) and \(\widehat Q_{SS}=2s^2/3+s/4+\gamma^2/16\). Dynamic coupling and hidden initialization remain. Even a zero static cross block cannot justify discarding those terms.

    The existing native effective-connection proof already distinguishes static curvature from dynamic recovery on its own admitted acute model. Equations (10)--(17) apply that method to an old critical kernel with actual boundary constraints; they do not transfer its coefficients or verdicts between laws. The shared derive_coordinate_memory implementation already provides the exact block-memory identity for an admitted linear generator and declared retained coordinates. It does not authenticate the graph, equilibrium or nonlinear closure. No additional solver is required for this theorem.

    5. One fixed-total-conductance control on the critical parent¶

    5.1 Matched model and restricted boundary rank¶

    Use the previously admitted 54-node critical parent and one nine-node neighbor from the single-port interaction owner. Hold its actual \(\gamma,\tau\), a fixed \(0<\eta\le10^{-12}\), and its own critical constitutive parameter \(\lambda_c(\eta)\). The parent has hosts \(c=0,\ldots,4\), donor \(c=5\), and the neighbor is \(c=6\); every child has nine slots. The aligned equilibrium is unchanged by the following additions because the new gaps vanish and \(j_{\lambda_c}(0)=0\), \(j_{\lambda_c}'(0)=1\).

    Compare exactly the same total per-slot added conductance \(\epsilon=\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2>0\):

    • A single port places weight \(\epsilon\) on each of the nine same-slot host-2-to-neighbor edges.
    • A split port places weight \(\epsilon_1>0\) on those edges and weight \(\epsilon_2>0\) on each of the nine same-slot donor-to-neighbor edges.

    These are alternative attachments from the same disconnected parent-plus-neighbor support with zero external weights, not a sequential event changing the single attachment into the split one. The comparison reallocates the host-port weight; it does not add a second copy of that port at unchanged weight. Both rows use the same weights. There is no new controller, clock or input.

    Let \(t\) be the parent's physical host-minus-donor phase coordinate and \(d\) the neighbor-minus-parent mean phase. The parent critical tangent has slot-constant entries at the two ports

    \[ h=w_{2,j}=\frac16+u_{2,t}(0)>\frac17,\qquad g=w_{5,j}=-\frac56,\qquad a=h-g=1+u_{2,t}(0)>0. \tag{18} \]

    The old relative phase kernel is two-dimensional: the parent's critical direction and the independent relative mean phase. On this kernel the boundary observations are \(d-ht\) and \(d-gt\). One port has restricted rank one. Positive weights on both ports have rank two because \(h\ne g\). The single port retains one quartic critical direction; the split port removes both old relative phase zero directions.

    The 63-node relative phase space has dimension 62. Its 56 fine phase directions remain positive: the parent's 48 and the neighbor's eight, with positive-semidefinite boundary additions. The remaining old normal directions are also positive. Thus the split model has \(M_{63}\succ0\), and all 124 full relative flow eigenvalues lie strictly in the left half-plane for every fixed positive pair \((\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)\). Its smooth full flow is locally exponentially attracting. The single-port model instead has one zero and 123 stable modes; its quartic attraction needs the separate proof already given in the interaction owner.

    5.2 Exact normal compliance at the aligned equilibrium¶

    The distinction survives relaxation of the internal host shape. Let \(P=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/5\), \(\ell=Pe_2\), \(u_0=u(0)\), and let \(D_5\) be the forward rim-difference map. Write \(b=2\pi/5\) and \(b_c=b(c-2)\). In the mean-zero host space define the actual own-law Hessian

    \[ \begin{split} A_H=P\{& D_5^\top\operatorname{diag} [j_{\lambda_c}'(b+D_5u_0)]D_5\\ &+\eta\,\operatorname{diag} [j_{\lambda_c}'(b_c+u_{0,c})]\}P\big|_{\mathbf1^\perp} \succ\frac15I,\qquad \chi=\ell^\top A_H^{-1}\ell\in(0,4). \end{split} \tag{19} \]

    These are the admitted parent normal bounds, not a fit to the new response. The last inequality uses \(\|\ell\|^2=4/5\). At fixed physical \(t\), let \(y\) denote host normal displacement from the stationary parent tangent. The exact quadratic phase storage is

    \[ U_2=\frac92\left[ y^\top A_Hy+ \epsilon_1(d-ht-\ell^\top y)^2+ \epsilon_2(d-gt)^2 \right]. \tag{20} \]

    The factor nine retains all slots. Fine coordinates minimize at zero: their gradient vanishes on the carrier and their actual quadratic form is positive. Thus (20) is the full quadratic static reduction, not an assumption that the fine state can be omitted dynamically.

    Put \(k=\epsilon_1\epsilon_2/(\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2)\). Minimization first over \(d\) and then over \(y\) gives

    \[ d_*=\frac{\epsilon_1(ht+\ell^\top y)+\epsilon_2gt}{\epsilon}, \qquad y_*=-\frac{ka}{1+k\chi}A_H^{-1}\ell\,t, \tag{21} \]

    and

    \[ \boxed{K_{\rm eff}=\frac{9ka^2}{1+k\chi}},\qquad \frac{9ka^2}{1+4k}<K_{\rm eff}<9ka^2. \tag{22} \]

    The upper numerator alone freezes the internal host normal state. The denominator is its exact compliance correction. The local nonlinear static minimizing potential satisfies \(U_{\rm red}(t)=U_*+K_{\rm eff}t^2/2+O(t^4)\). Normal invertibility gives this graph and the inherited reflection makes the potential even. In the single-port limit \(k=0\), the quadratic stiffness vanishes irrespective of the positive total conductance. Equation (22) is a static stiffness, not a decay rate.

    5.3 Coupled dynamic control¶

    The released means provide a direct reason to retain that distinction. Let \(r\) be neighbor-minus-parent mean form and retain \(d\) as above. Define

    \[ c=\frac7{24},\quad m=\frac{9\gamma^2}{4\|w\|^2},\quad S_0=\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2,\quad S_1=\epsilon_1h+\epsilon_2g,\quad S_2=\epsilon_1h^2+\epsilon_2g^2. \tag{23} \]

    Projection of the actual two-row Jacobian onto the three zero modes at zero added contact gives the leading small-contact matrix on \((t,r,d)\):

    \[ \begin{pmatrix} -mS_2&0&mS_1\\ c\gamma S_1&-cS_0&-c\gamma S_0\\ 0&c\gamma S_0&0 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{24} \]

    It follows by summing the two marked boundary contributions, with the same parent critical projection and \(6:1\) mean weights as in the interaction owner. The two-row cancellation in the critical left projection removes a direct retained mean-form term from its first row; it does not remove the other two equations.     6. Counterexamples identify the missing premises¶

    A graph and its loss do not select curvature or a relaxation rate. For one relative coordinate take \(L=l>0\) and \(U(q)=a(1-\cos q)\). The origin is stationary and its polynomial is

    \[ \frac{s^2}{l}+\frac s4+\frac{\gamma^2a}{16}=0. \tag{25} \]

    Positive, zero and negative \(a\) give, respectively, stable modes, a zero mode, and a positive mode; distinct positive values give different rates at the same \(L\). Alternatively, a positive conductance sine edge has opposite curvature signs at its stationary gaps zero and \(\pi\). Its graph and continuous loss identity are the same. The equilibrium and actual law are therefore indispensable inputs to (3).

    Old semidefiniteness and positive boundary curvature have separate roles. With \(M_0=\operatorname{diag}(-1,0)\), \(B=(0,1)\), \(K=1\), the boundary distinguishes the old kernel but the total Hessian remains indefinite. With \(M_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\), \(B=(1,0)\), \(K=-1\), the total Hessian is zero: signed curvature has created an additional cancellation kernel. With \(K=0\), any nonzero \(B\) imposes no quadratic constraint. These controls prevent extending (6) to unsigned topological rank without its hypotheses.

    A positive Hessian at a nonstationary state is insufficient. For \(U(q)=1-\cos(q-a)\) with small nonzero \(a\), the Hessian at zero is positive but the gradient is nonzero. The point is not an equilibrium of (1) and has no equilibrium-attraction verdict.

    A surviving kernel does not decide higher-order attraction. Consider the smooth periodic nonnegative potentials

    \[ U_4(q)=(1-\cos q)^2=\frac{q^4}{4}+O(q^6),\qquad U_6(q)=(1-\cos q)^3=\frac{q^6}{8}+O(q^8). \tag{26} \]

    They have the same zero Hessian at zero, while their first restoring currents are \(q^3\) and \(3q^5/4\). Both have isolated local storage minima; their higher-order laws are different. The equally nonnegative periodic potential \(U_-(q)=4-(1-\cos q)^2\) instead has a quartic local maximum at zero and the same zero Hessian. Nearby zero-form states have strictly lower storage than that equilibrium, so (2) prevents them from converging to it. Nonnegative storage and a positive-semidefinite tangent thus do not prove attraction. The additive constant in \(U_-\) changes no current or loss; this control does not also impose the incompatible normalization \(U_-(0)=0\). These are fixed-support local constitutive controls; they do not claim equal support-event work, which depends on the declared potential normalization.

    Static elimination does not discard dynamical state. Equation (17) has zero static cross curvature and nonzero dynamic coupling. Formula (16) retains the hidden initial state. The actual critical-parent state witnesses likewise distinguish instantaneous full-state rates from a slaved center law. Neither matched coarse means nor a static minimizer is a complete state of the arbitrary nearby flow.
