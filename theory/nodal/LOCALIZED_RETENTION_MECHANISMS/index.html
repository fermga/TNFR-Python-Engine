    Retention mechanisms and the complete-law envelope boundary¶

    Scope: mechanism admission and one local finite-input variational evaluation under the existing positive-loss cycle law. Exact balances and cubic coefficient identities are proved below; envelope profiles solve an auxiliary modulation equation. A full-state residual theorem and a relative-storage comparison specify how to validate such a reference against the actual law, retaining near resonances. The first correction panel is evaluated in section 17; the full horizon, nonlinear envelope maintenance and interaction-generated composites remain unproved. The original source and strict-passage result remain unchanged. The research program alone selects further work and owns reactivation of the deferred variational dependency.

    1. Retained law and internal relations¶

    Keep the complete two-row model: signed real form x, a continuous real phase lift theta, unit cycle conductances and lengths, held unit capacities, beta one, no input or events, and the clock tau=w*t/pi. For e,w>0, sigma=pi*e/w, A=L/2,

    \[ x'=-\sigma Ax-\tfrac12D^T\sin(D\theta),\qquad \theta'=Ax. \tag{1} \]

    Writing z=x+i*theta retains two real channels. It gives

    \[ z'=F_\sigma(z)=iAz-\sigma A\operatorname{Re}z+R(\operatorname{Im}z), \quad R(\theta)=-\tfrac12D^T[\sin(D\theta)-D\theta]. \tag{2} \]

    This is neither complex EPI nor an identification with a quantum state. The actual positive-loss tangent J_sigma has the existing contractive Euclidean semigroup. A proposed envelope is an approximate full-state history, not an invariant subspace or a replacement for either row.

    Supplied symmetry does not establish acquisition¶

    Reflection Jz(j)=z(-j) commutes with (1). The original nominal joint preparation is reflection symmetric, so that relation is preserved exactly. The same-source linear control also preserves it. The relation is supplied initially and does not discriminate a newly formed organization.

    For primitive errors of norm at most epsilon each, the initial antisymmetric part has norm at most 2*epsilon. Within the original joint norm bootstrap ||z||,||Jz||<2a, the shared derivative estimate ||DR||<=4S^2 with S=2a gives

    \[ \left\|\frac{z(\tau)-Jz(\tau)}2\right\| \le 2\epsilon\exp(16a^2\tau). \tag{3} \]

    This robust retention is still retention of a prepared symmetry. It cannot replace initial absence and discriminating acquisition in the formation contract.

    The exact period-four family reduces to independent damped pendula u and v. With E_u=u'^2/2+1-cos(u) and similarly for v,

    \[ H=\frac N2(E_u+E_v),\qquad E_u'=-\sigma u'^2,\quad E_v'=-\sigma v'^2. \tag{4} \]

    There is no transfer term between these two modal energies. For any pendulum solution q and nonnegative time shift delta, u(tau)=q(tau) and v(tau)=q(tau+delta) give an exact pair. Such a relative registration can be supplied; these equations do not select it through mutual exchange. Decay toward zero also destroys an oscillation's phase observability. Localized modulation and its boundary cannot be removed by invoking this reduction.

    2. What can accumulate locally under this law?¶

    The regional balance owner requires boundary exchange. Here an exact cycle-specific expression makes the consumed phase information explicit. Orient cycle edges consistently, put y=Dx, u=Dtheta, and let edge indices j and k be neighbors on the edge cycle. Their rows are

    \[ y'=-\tfrac\sigma2 L_e y-\tfrac12L_e\sin u,\qquad u'=\tfrac12L_e y,\qquad L_e=DD^T. \tag{5} \]

    For h_j=y_j^2/2+1-cos(u_j), define

    \[ F_{jk}=\tfrac12(\sin u_j\,y_k-y_j\sin u_k) +\tfrac\sigma4(y_j^2-y_k^2)=-F_{kj}. \]
    \[ h_j'=-\sum_{k\sim j}F_{jk} -\tfrac\sigma4\sum_{k\sim j}(y_j-y_k)^2. \tag{6} \]

    Direct differentiation of h and substitution of (5) prove (6). Summing over all edges recovers H'=-(sigma/2)||Lx||^2. On a fixed subset, internal currents cancel; an increase requires inward boundary current to exceed local dissipation. Form gaps and sine phase gaps determine that current, so intensities or centers alone cannot infer it. Positive total loss does not exclude transient regional accumulation. Conversely, positive inflow does not prove retention or select a boundary. Moving membership requires its separate transport accounting; (6) is for fixed sets.

    3. The cubic balance selects a different candidate regime¶

    Let an on-grid carrier satisfy kN in 2*pi*Z, and write

    \[ \lambda=1-\cos k,\quad v=\sin k,\quad d=\tfrac12\cos k, \quad g=\lambda^2/8,\quad h_3=\frac{\sin^3(k/2)\sin(3k/2)}6,\quad \lambda_3=1-\cos(3k). \tag{7} \]

    Use a positive bookkeeping scale varepsilon, distinct from the original source error epsilon, and slow variables X=varepsilon*(j+v*tau), S=varepsilon^2*tau, phi=kj+lambda*tau. The candidate carrier travels with site velocity -v.

    Exact cubic harmonics and a two-channel reconstruction¶

    The cubic part of R is R_3(theta)=D^T(Dtheta)^3/12. At locally constant B, substituting theta=Im(B*exp(i*phi)) gives exactly

    \[ R_3=-ig|B|^2B e^{i\phi}+ig|B|^2\overline B e^{-i\phi} -ih_3B^3e^{3i\phi}+ih_3\overline B^3e^{-3i\phi}. \tag{8} \]

    For example, a forward phase gap has coefficient (exp(ik)-1)B/(2i); cubing and applying the backward incidence row gives all four terms. Keeping only the resonant first term would discard an actual part of the form row.

    Cancellation through cubic order uses the auxiliary equation

    \[ iB_S=d B_{XX}+g|B|^2B \tag{9} \]

    and the full reconstruction

    \[ z_{\rm app}=\varepsilon B e^{i\phi}+\varepsilon^3 C, \quad C=a_{-1}|B|^2\overline B e^{-i\phi} +a_3B^3e^{3i\phi}+a_{-3}\overline B^3e^{-3i\phi}, \tag{10} \]
    \[ a_{-1}=-\lambda/16,\qquad a_3=-\frac{h_3}{3\lambda-\lambda_3},\qquad a_{-3}=-\frac{h_3}{3\lambda+\lambda_3}. \tag{11} \]

    Require lambda>0 and nonzero displayed denominators. The operator partial_tau-iA multiplies a constant harmonic exp(i*l*phi) by i*(l*lambda-lambda(l*k)); (11) cancels the three nonresonant terms of (8). Envelope derivative errors remain and are bounded in section 5. Both real coordinates are reconstructed as x_app=Re(z_app), theta_app=Im(z_app); neither row is declared exact by (9).

    The carrier k=pi/4 is already mode 128 of the unchanged C_1024 support. It has positive curvature and no third-harmonic resonance:

    \[ \lambda=1-\frac{\sqrt2}2,\quad d=\frac{\sqrt2}4,\quad g=\frac{3-2\sqrt2}{16},\quad h_3=\frac{\sqrt2-1}{48}, \]
    \[ (a_{-1},a_3,a_{-3})= \left(-\frac{2-\sqrt2}{32},\frac1{96}, -\frac{3\sqrt2-2}{672}\right). \tag{12} \]

    This is an analytical candidate, not a searched optimum. For general k, third-harmonic resonance and spatial aliases must be checked; spatially aliased terms can still have distinct temporal frequencies.

    The actual damping does not disappear¶

    Equation (9) is a conservative comparison equation. All actual damping can be retained in the residual of (10), as in section 5. Alternatively, writing gamma=sigma/varepsilon^2 yields the leading damped modulation

    \[ iB_S=dB_{XX}+g|B|^2B-i\frac{\gamma\lambda}{2}B. \tag{13} \]

    Its counterrotating damping corrector is -i*gamma*conj(B)*exp(-i*phi)/4. This follows by multiplying its coefficient by -2i*lambda, giving the omitted damping coefficient -gamma*lambda*conj(B)/2. For a periodic envelope, d/dS integral |B|^2=-gamma*lambda*integral |B|^2 under (13). Fixed positive sigma therefore cannot be suppressed in a varepsilon -> 0 limit or replaced with zero to certify a desired profile. The residual bound below applies to the conservative reference (9)--(11); using (13) and its additional corrector requires redoing that bound.

    4. Which relation can the auxiliary balance maintain?¶

    For d,g>0, (9) admits the exact line profile

    \[ B(X,S)=\rho\operatorname{sech}(X/L)e^{-i\Omega S},\qquad \rho^2L^2=\frac{2d}{g}=\frac{8\cos k}{\lambda^2},\qquad \Omega=\frac d{L^2}=\frac{g\rho^2}{2}. \tag{14} \]

    This follows from sech''=sech-2*sech^3. The relation locks the envelope shape, height, width and slow internal phase in the comparison equation; the corresponding carrier frequency is lambda-varepsilon^2*Omega. It is not a freely chosen stationary shape of the original nodal law.

    The sign restriction is substantive. For a smooth real decaying profile f, d*f''+g*f^3=Omega*f has first integral d*f'^2/2=Omega*f^2/2-g*f^4/4. Exponential decay requires Omega/d>0, and a nonzero turning amplitude requires Omega>0. Thus this bright-profile balance requires d>0. At the original k=pi/2, d vanishes and this quadratic-dispersion mechanism is unavailable. This does not exclude every higher-order modulation or nonperturbative mechanism at that carrier.

    Periodic references and the source scale¶

    The line sech cannot be sampled across a cycle seam and treated as a smooth periodic solution. An exact periodic alternative of (9), with kappa and rho strictly positive, is defined by

    \[ B=f(X)e^{-i\Omega S},\quad f''=\kappa^2(2-m)f-\frac{2\kappa^2}{\rho^2}f^3,\quad f(0)=\rho,\quad f'(0)=0, \]
    \[ 0<m<1,\quad \rho^2=2d\kappa^2/g,\quad \Omega=d\kappa^2(2-m),\quad (f')^2=\frac{\kappa^2}{\rho^2} (\rho^2-f^2)(f^2-\rho^2(1-m)). \tag{15} \]

    The solution oscillates smoothly between rho*sqrt(1-m) and rho. Separation of variables gives period 2K(m)/kappa, where K(m)=integral_0^(pi/2) (1-m*sin(u)^2)^(-1/2) du. Choose that period to equal varepsilon*N; this supplies a genuinely periodic reference with one maximum per cycle. Its trough, concentration, derivative bounds and error allowance still need a quantitative admission. No value of m, width, horizon or source tolerance has been evaluated here. For fixed N and m, that period condition fixes the site-scale height varepsilon*rho=2*K(m)*sqrt(2*d/g)/N and inverse width varepsilon*kappa=2*K(m)/N. Changing varepsilon alone only reparametrizes this reference; the derivative bounds change with it. It is not a free parameter for reducing the physical residual on fixed support.

    The line-profile scale already explains why the original small preparation cannot simply be renamed. Write physical site width ell=L/varepsilon and height b=varepsilon*rho; (14) implies

    \[ b\ell=K_*:=\frac{\sqrt{8\cos k}}{\lambda},\qquad K_*>8 \quad\hbox{at }k=\pi/4. \tag{16} \]

    For the literal sampled sech with integer center and 1<=ell<=N/4, at least ell sites have distance at most ell. Since sech(1)^2>1/4, its norm squared exceeds b^2*ell/4>=K_*^2/N. On C_1024 its norm therefore exceeds 1/4, whereas the entire original joint tube has norm below 1/2048. This is a scale incompatibility of this particular reference; it is not a theorem excluding all small nonlinear localized states. The full corrected periodic reference must have its own source admission.

    A single retained envelope is not capture of two incoming patterns¶

    For patterns to remain within a common width O(varepsilon^-1) over a slow interval O(varepsilon^-2), their leading group velocities must differ by at most O(varepsilon), unless a different, nonperturbative mechanism changes them. Near pi/4, nonzero cosine makes this require carrier separation O(varepsilon). Replacing the original carriers with +/-pi/4 still leaves velocity difference sqrt(2): focusing alone does not capture that counterpropagating pair. Distant carriers can share a velocity; they require a separately derived multiple-envelope system. The equal-velocity carrier control derives the local cubic channels for pi/4 and 3*pi/4, retaining temporal labels within spatial aliases. Its modulation restrictions do not provide a full-law formation or maintenance certificate.

    A prepared profile satisfying (14) or (15) already contains its phase/shape relation. Even successful full-law maintenance would not establish its acquisition from two initially independent patterns. That claim additionally needs initial absence, a compatible relative preparation, retained boundary state, entry and a discriminating continued response under the same law.

    5. An explicit conditional bridge to the complete law¶

    Here is a sufficient finite-time validation method, rather than an appeal to a formal asymptotic symbol. Let B solve (9), be periodic in X with period varepsilon*N, and have continuous bounded derivatives through order three in X and order one in S on the full proposed slow interval. Define uniform bounds b_j>=sup|partial_X^j B|, j=0,...,3, and b_S=|d|*b_2+g*b_0^3>=sup|B_S|. All bounds include intervening X values, not just graph samples. Put

    \[ A_0=|a_{-1}|+|a_3|+|a_{-3}|,\qquad A_1=\sum_{l\in\{-1,3,-3\}}|a_l|\,|v-\sin(lk)|, \quad D_k=|1-e^{ik}|, \]
    \[ G=\varepsilon(D_kb_0+\varepsilon b_1) +2\varepsilon^3 A_0b_0^3. \tag{17} \]

    Every phase gap of (10) has magnitude at most G. For r_app=z_app'-F_sigma(z_app), a uniform per-site bound is

    \[ \begin{aligned} r_*={}&\varepsilon^4\left[ \frac{b_3}{6}+3A_1b_0^2b_1 +\frac{b_1}{2}(D_kb_0+\varepsilon b_1)^2\right]\\ &+\varepsilon^5 A_0\left[3b_0^2b_S+3b_0b_1^2 +\frac32b_0^2b_2\right]\\ &+\varepsilon^3 A_0b_0^3G^2+\frac{G^5}{120} +2\sigma(\varepsilon b_0+\varepsilon^3 A_0b_0^3), \qquad \|r_{\rm app}\|_2\le\sqrt N\,r_* . \tag{18} \end{aligned} \]

    The terms have separate derivations:

    • Taylor expansion of the leading nearest-neighbor row leaves varepsilon^4*b_3/6 after (9). Corrector transport differs from the leading group velocity and is bounded using |partial_X F_l(B)|<=3*b_0^2*b_1.
    • Freezing B in the cubic phase gaps changes each gap by at most varepsilon^2*b_1. The cubic difference inequality supplies the last term in the first bracket.
    • The corrector's slow derivative and second spatial remainder use |partial_S F_l|<=3*b_0^2*b_S and |partial_X^2 F_l|<=6*b_0*b_1^2+3*b_0^2*b_2.
    • Its addition changes each phase gap by at most 2*varepsilon^3*A_0*b_0^3, giving the cubic correction term. The global Taylor inequality |sin(u)-u+u^3/6|<=|u|^5/120 gives the fifth-order term.
    • The final term bounds the entire actual damping row, using the nearest-neighbor bound |A Re(z_app)|<=2*sup|z_app|.

    The Euclidean sqrt(N) factor is retained; localization or cancellations cannot silently replace it. The residual is a pair of real-row errors, including the phase row, not a fitted effective pressure.

    Let the initial full-state error be at most delta>=0 and propose a strictly positive error radius r and horizon T over 0<=tau<=T. In that radius every actual and intervening phase gap is bounded by G+2r. Since

    \[ DR(\theta)=\tfrac12D^T\operatorname{diag}(1-\cos D\theta)D, \qquad \|DR\|\le(G+2r)^2=:L_*, \tag{19} \]

    variation of constants under the actual contractive J_sigma and Gronwall give the sufficient bootstrap condition

    \[ E_*:=e^{L_*T}\bigl(\delta+T\sqrt N\,r_*\bigr)<r. \tag{20} \]

    A first exit at norm r contradicts this estimate, so the full-state error stays at most E_* throughout the interval. A required acute phase chart can be admitted separately by G+2r<pi/2. Reference norm and concentration floors must exceed their error allowances before any retained identity is certified. If (20) fails, this sufficient method is inconclusive; no nonexistence or dynamical instability follows.

    This is a conditional theorem with explicit finite constants. It has not been instantiated for a prepared envelope. In particular, at T=S_*/varepsilon^2, the fixed positive sigma contribution must still be evaluated. A formal small residual or a profile of (9) alone cannot certify that window. Using localization-aware norms or a damped reference may improve the method, but each needs its own proved bound.

    A scoped obstruction to this coarse certificate¶

    For the proposed periodic family (15), one can decide a limitation without selecting a profile or evaluating a response. Fix the actual original law coefficients e=2^-20, w=1-e, so sigma=pi/(2^20-1), retain N=1024, and take k=pi/4. Put b=varepsilon*rho and define one slow internal-phase time by

    \[ T_\phi=\frac1{\varepsilon^2\Omega} =\frac2{g b^2(2-m)}. \tag{21} \]

    Consider the particular certificate requiring both (20) and the acute-chart condition G+2r<pi/2, with r>0. Since 1/4<lambda<1/3, g<1/72, D_k>1/2 and pi<4, this condition forces r<1 and b<4. Consequently

    \[ T_\phi b>\frac1{g b}>18,\qquad T_\phi\sqrt N\,r_*>1152\sigma >\frac{3456}{2^{20}}>\frac1{400}. \tag{22} \]

    Here the second inequality uses the actual positive damping term r_*>=2*sigma*b; b_0 is at least the profile maximum rho. Also

    \[ L_*T_\phi\ge G^2T_\phi \ge\frac{32}{\lambda(2-m)}>48. \tag{23} \]

    Even at zero initial error, the published sufficient majorant therefore obeys

    \[ E_*>\frac{1+48+48^2/2}{400} =\frac{1201}{400}>1>r, \tag{24} \]

    using exp(u)>=1+u+u^2/2 for nonnegative u. The contradiction persists for any T>=T_phi. Thus this coarse acute-chart certificate cannot validate the periodic conservative reference for a full nonlinear phase time. Changing width, refining source error or increasing arithmetic precision cannot repair this bound within the stated class.

    This is a theorem about a sufficient method, not an obstruction to actual nonlinear maintenance. It does not exclude (20) without the additional acute condition, shorter intervals, other reference histories or other error estimates. A structure-sensitive stability argument or an explicitly damped reference with its own residual would change the method's premises. The present calculation does not establish that either alternative succeeds.

    The existing moving-reference relative-storage identity is a reusable method for retaining reactive cancellations. Its earlier conservative graph, exact reference and quantitative constants do not transfer here. Positive loss, two-row reference residuals, moving phase curvature and the uncontrolled common means must all be re-admitted before using such storage to bound this full-state error. Relative storage is not automatically decreasing along a moving reference.

    6. Relative storage retains the complete error balance¶

    Let (x_*,theta_*) be a differentiable, independently specified reference on the same cycle and clock, with residuals in both actual rows:

    \[ r_x=x_*'+\sigma Ax_*+\tfrac12\nabla V(\theta_*),\qquad r_\theta=\theta_*'-Ax_*,\qquad V(\theta)=\sum_e[1-\cos(D\theta)_e]. \tag{25} \]

    Use matched continuous phase lifts, u=x-x_*, eta=theta-theta_*, a=Dtheta_*, b_e=(Deta)_e, and define

    \[ \mathcal E=\tfrac12u^TLu+V(\theta_*+\eta)-V(\theta_*) -\nabla V(\theta_*)^T\eta. \]

    Direct differentiation and substitution of both rows give the exact identity

    \[ \begin{split} \mathcal E'={}&-\tfrac\sigma2\|Lu\|^2 +\sum_e[\sin(a_e+b_e)-\sin a_e-\cos a_e\,b_e]a_e'\\ &-u^TLr_x-[\nabla V(\theta_*+\eta)-\nabla V(\theta_*)]^T r_\theta. \tag{26} \end{split} \]

    The form/phase exchange cancels. The moving-curvature term does not cancel and has no fixed sign. This extends the earlier moving-pattern identity to the actual positive loss and an approximate reference; it does not transfer that owner's graph, exact reference or constants.

    Means, coercivity and the retained coordinates¶

    Let P project off the constant nodal vector. All inverse powers of L below act only on its mean-zero subspace. Put

    \[ y=(L^{1/2}u,L^{1/2}\eta),\quad U=DL^{-1/2},\quad U^TU=I, \quad \ell_1=4\sin^2(\pi/N). \]

    Suppose ||y||<=rho and ||a||_infinity+rho<=chi<pi/2. With kappa=cos(chi)>0, Taylor's integral formula gives

    \[ \tfrac\kappa2\|y\|^2\le\mathcal E\le\tfrac12\|y\|^2, \qquad \|(u,\eta)\|^2\le\ell_1^{-1}\|y\|^2 +N(\bar u^2+\bar\eta^2). \tag{27} \]

    The missing means have exact independent equations bar(u)'=-bar(r_x), bar(eta)'=-bar(r_theta). They need their initial values and residual integrals. Constant form or phase errors have zero relative storage; neither a full-state estimate nor a zero-mean residual may be inferred from storage alone. The factor ell_1^-1 is the actual finite-cycle coercivity cost, not a carrier-band approximation.

    Taylor expansion of the moving term in (26) gives

    \[ \mathcal E'=-\tfrac\sigma2\|Lu\|^2+y^TM(\tau)y +\mathcal R_3+\mathcal W_r,\qquad M=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&-\tfrac12U^T\operatorname{diag}(\sin a\,a')U\end{pmatrix}, \tag{28} \]
    \[ |\mathcal R_3|\le\frac{\|a'\|_\infty\rho}{6}\|y\|^2, \qquad |\mathcal W_r|\le\|y\|\mathfrak r,\qquad \mathfrak r=\|(L^{1/2}r_x,L^{1/2}r_\theta)\|. \]

    For the second bound, write the sine difference as t_e b_e, where t_e=int_0^1 cos(a_e+s*b_e) ds and |t_e|<=1, and apply Cauchy--Schwarz to the two residual rows together. No pressure is reconstructed from a response and no error mode is discarded.

    7. A conditional comparison that retains near resonances¶

    There is a precise alternative to taking the absolute value of all of M before integration. Define the skew principal tangent on the mean-zero coordinates by

    \[ J_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&-A\\A&0\end{pmatrix}. \]

    This is a comparison operator inside the proof. Actual damping stays in (26) and in the error equation; the physical law is still (1).

    For the reconstruction (10), its leading edge wave can be written a_0=Im(c(tau)*exp(i*lambda*tau)), where c is the complex edge amplitude obtained by applying D to varepsilon*B(X,S)*exp(i*k*j). Its coefficients vary with the envelope. With omega=2*lambda, set

    \[ H=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&\frac{i\lambda}{4} U^T\operatorname{diag}(c^2)U\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_{\rm fast}=\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\omega\tau}H). \tag{29} \]

    Indeed -(a_0*a'_{0,fast})/2=Re(i*lambda*c^2*exp(2i*lambda*tau)/4). All envelope derivatives, generated harmonics and the difference between sine and its argument remain in M-M_fast. This is an exact decomposition of (28), not a declaration that its remainder vanishes.

    The finite-dimensional homological equation¶

    Choose an orthonormal complex eigenbasis J_0 v_alpha=i*mu_alpha*v_alpha, with all nonconstant cycle modes and both frequency signs. For a complex symmetric matrix H use the congruence coefficients Hhat_alpha,beta=v_alpha^T H v_beta. The transpose is intentional: these are quadratic forms, not Hermitian expectation values. The congruence preserves the Frobenius norm. On these coefficients the operator i*omega*C+J_0^T*C+C*J_0 has divisor i*(omega+mu_alpha+mu_beta).

    Fix a positive threshold Delta before any bound evaluation. Retain every entry with |omega+mu_alpha+mu_beta|<=Delta in H_near, including zero divisors. Let H_far=H-H_near, and define the symmetric matrix C by

    \[ \widehat C_{\alpha\beta}= \begin{cases} -2\widehat H_{\alpha\beta}/[i(\omega+\mu_\alpha+\mu_\beta)], &|\omega+\mu_\alpha+\mu_\beta|>\Delta,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \tag{30} \]

    Reconstruct C by inverse congruence and set K=Re(exp(i*omega*tau)*C). The matrices K and Re(exp(i*omega*tau)*C') are real symmetric. Direct differentiation, with the same fixed spectral split, proves

    \[ K'+J_0^TK+KJ_0=-2\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\omega\tau}H_{\rm far}) +\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\omega\tau}C'). \tag{31} \]

    In particular, conservative sufficient bounds are

    \[ \|K\|_2\le 2\|H_{\rm far}\|_F/\Delta,\qquad \|\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\omega\tau}C')\|_2 \le2\|H'_{\rm far}\|_F/\Delta. \tag{32} \]

    Actual operator-norm bounds may be sharper. A changing cutoff would add terms not covered by (31). The split does not delete perturbations: near entries remain in M_ret=M-M_fast+Re(exp(i*omega*tau)*H_near).

    Positive metric, actual loss and a full-state bootstrap¶

    On a prospective interval admit uniform bounds

    \[ \|K\|_2\le k_*<\kappa,\quad \|M_{\rm ret}\|_2\le m_*,\quad \|\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\omega\tau}C')\|_2\le d_*,\quad \|a'\|_\infty\le a_*. \]

    Define E_c=E+y^T K y/2. Equations (27) and (31) imply

    \[ \frac{\kappa-k_*}{2}\|y\|^2\le E_c \le\frac{1+k_*}{2}\|y\|^2. \tag{33} \]

    For completeness, the exact error equation in these coordinates is y'=J_0*y+f_sigma+f_nl-rhat, with ||f_sigma||<=2*sigma*||y|| and ||f_nl||<=chi^2*||y||. The latter follows from sin(a+b)-sin(a)-b=(int_0^1 cos(a+s*b) ds-1)*b, ||L^(1/2)D^T||=4, and 1-cos(q)<=chi^2/2 in the admitted chart. Here rhat=(L^(1/2)r_x,L^(1/2)r_theta); it is not an additional forcing law.

    Substitution in the derivative of E_c proves

    \[ E_c'\le-\tfrac\sigma2\|Lu\|^2+c_*\|y\|^2 +(1+k_*)\|y\|\mathfrak r,\qquad c_*=m_*+\tfrac12d_*+k_*(2\sigma+\chi^2)+a_*\rho/6. \tag{34} \]

    Thus q=sqrt(2*E_c) has the comparison majorant

    \[ q(\tau)\le Q(\tau):=e^{\alpha\tau} \left[\sqrt{1+k_*}\|y(0)\| +\frac{1+k_*}{\sqrt{\kappa-k_*}} \int_0^\tau e^{-\alpha s}\mathfrak r(s)\,ds\right], \qquad \alpha=\frac{c_*}{\kappa-k_*}. \tag{35} \]

    At zeros of E_c use a regularized square root and pass to the limit. If the initial chart holds and Q(T)<rho*sqrt(kappa-k_*), a first exit from ||y||<rho contradicts (33)--(35). Equations (27) and the independently integrated means then control the complete nodal error. This is a conditional theorem; every bound is uniform over the declared interval and source ball. Small residuals alone do not supply k_*<kappa or that bootstrap.

    Actual positive damping has not been removed: (34) retains its negative storage production and bounds its interaction with K explicitly. A better use of the negative term requires an additional proved estimate. A damped envelope reference also needs its own residual under (25). Neither is supplied by selecting a cutoff. The bound improves on (20) only when its admitted constants and full-state margins demonstrate that improvement.

    8. Which curvature terms cannot be averaged away?¶

    Two exact coefficient calculations delimit the preceding construction. They concern the frozen leading carrier in the homological equation, not a solved localized reference. They are not trajectory observations.

    The carrier block has a nonzero resonant source¶

    Temporarily write the leading reference as theta_*=b*sin(phi_j), phi_j=k*j+lambda*tau, with real amplitude b, and take a same-carrier error z-z_*=(alpha+i*beta)*exp(i*phi_j). This complex notation still represents two real errors. Exact spatial summation on C1024 at k=pi/4 gives

    \[ \mathcal E_0=N\lambda(\alpha^2+\beta^2),\qquad \left\langle-\tfrac12\sum_e a_ea_e' b_e^2\right\rangle =-\tfrac N2\lambda^3 b^2\alpha\beta. \tag{36} \]

    The brackets denote the principal fast average, with frozen error quadratures, not averaging an evaluated nonlinear response. The term can have either sign. Its homological divisor is zero: 2*lambda-lambda-lambda=0. A bounded periodic quadratic correction cannot have a nonzero constant derivative along this periodic principal motion. Therefore this source cannot be removed by such a correction.

    Its maximum magnitude relative to E_0 is 2*g*b^2. In the conservative auxiliary envelope's carrier-rotating frame, linearization about constant amplitude gives alpha'=0, beta'=-2*g*b^2*alpha in the fast clock at this order: amplitude error changes the phase rate. The resulting shear has linear-in-time entries, not an exponential-instability proof. Over the formal phase time (21), the Euclidean logarithmic-norm bound for this auxiliary shear has exponent g*b^2*T_phi=2/(2-m), of order one. This coefficient calculation does not bound all other modes or certify the localized envelope. In particular, the actual loss, spatial modulation and residual remain in (34)--(35).

    Adjacent modes invalidate an exact-resonances-only shortcut¶

    For distinct modes q=k+h, r=k-h, the principal divisor is exactly

    \[ \delta_h=\lambda(q)+\lambda(r)-2\lambda =2\cos k(1-\cos h),\qquad h=2\pi/N. \tag{37} \]

    It is nonzero but small. Use unitary Fourier coefficients z_q,z_r, d_q the incidence symbol with |d_q|^2=2*lambda(q), and A_b=|d_k|*b. Put P_qr=d_q*d_r*z_q*z_r*exp(-2i*(lambda*tau+arg(d_k))). The principal moving production on this pair is -A_b^2*lambda*Im(P_qr)/8; along J_0, P_qr'=i*delta_h*P_qr. Consequently its compulsory zero-homogeneous correction is

    \[ \mathcal Q_{qr}=-\frac{A_b^2\lambda}{8\delta_h} \operatorname{Re}P_{qr},\qquad \sup_{(z_q,z_r)\ne(0,0)} \frac{|\mathcal Q_{qr}|}{\lambda(q)|z_q|^2+\lambda(r)|z_r|^2} =\frac{2g b^2}{|\delta_h|}. \tag{38} \]

    The supremum is over nonzero pair errors; equality uses equal weighted modal magnitudes and the appropriate relative phase. For the amplitude scale imposed by periodic family (15), this ratio is

    \[ R_h=\frac{2K(m)^2}{N^2\sin^2(\pi/N)}. \tag{39} \]

    It exceeds one half even as m tends to zero. Shrinking the bookkeeping varepsilon at fixed physical profile cannot make it vanish. The continuous elliptic integral K increases from pi/2 to infinity, so the class contains an amplitude with K(m)=pi. For that analytic witness R_h>2, while b=2*pi*sqrt(2d/g)/1024<1/16 because sqrt(2d/g)<9 and pi<7/2. Its leading phase gaps are small. Yet the canonical principal correction has a direction below -2*E_0. The relative-storage Hessian itself is at most E_0 since cos(a)<=1; adding this correction loses positivity.

    This is a scoped obstruction to eliminating all nonzero divisors around J_0 while retaining only exact resonances with a small correction over that frozen-carrier amplitude class. It does not exclude (30), which can retain these adjacent modes, or all positive time-dependent metrics. Adding a large homogeneous metric changes the normalization and its error bounds. Including the slow phase rate or loss in a different inverse changes the divisors and requires a separate calculation. The actual localized envelope has spatially varying coefficients and additional couplings; (39) is not a nonexistence or instability theorem for it.

    9. The spatially varying periodic reference has explicit finite bounds¶

    The following calculation applies to the whole periodic family (15), not its frozen carrier. Fix the analytical rule Delta=lambda/2 for (30). Retain the original support and loss, N=1024, sigma=pi/(N^2-1), and k=pi/4. No profile parameter, source tolerance or response is selected or evaluated. The formulas below are uniform in the continuous translation of the profile and in time.

    Physical profile and derivative budgets¶

    Write the site-scale profile as F(s)=varepsilon*f(varepsilon*s) and put

    \[ \zeta=\frac{2K(m)}N,\quad b=\zeta\sqrt{2d/g},\quad \omega_s=d\zeta^2(2-m)=\frac{g b^2(2-m)}2,\quad \psi_j=kj+(\lambda-\omega_s)\tau. \]

    The full reconstruction (10) is exactly z_app,j=F(j+v*tau)*exp(i*psi_j)+F(j+v*tau)^3*sum_l a_l*exp(i*l*psi_j). The bookkeeping varepsilon has disappeared. The first integral and its derivative give the uniform bounds

    \[ \|F\|_\infty\le b,\quad B_1=\frac{\zeta b m}{2}\ge\|F'\|_\infty,\quad B_2=\zeta^2 b m\ge\|F''\|_\infty,\quad B_3=\frac{\zeta^3 b m(4+m)}2\ge\|F'''\|_\infty. \tag{40} \]

    For example, set s=1-(F/b)^2 in [0,m]. Then F'^2=zeta^2*b^2*s*(m-s), whose upper bound follows from s*(m-s)<=m^2/4. Also F''=zeta^2*F*(2s-m) and F'''=zeta^2*(-4-m+6s)*F', proving the other two inequalities. The exact slow derivative of the profile factor is -i*omega_s*F. It must not be omitted when differentiating the moving metric.

    Let A_h=sum_l |l*a_l|, with the three harmonic coefficients from (11). For the leading edge amplitude c of (29) and the harmonic contribution to the phase gap, define

    \[ \begin{split} C_0&=D_k b+B_1,\qquad C_1=|v|(D_k B_1+B_2)+\omega_s C_0,\\ G_h&=2A_0b^3,\qquad V_h=6|v|A_0b^2B_1+2|\lambda-\omega_s|A_h b^3,\\ G&=C_0+G_h,\qquad a_*=\lambda C_0+C_1+V_h. \end{split} \tag{41} \]

    The fundamental theorem of calculus across one site proves ||c||_infinity<=C_0, ||c'||_infinity<=C_1. Differentiating every harmonic, including its spatial transport, gives the bounds G_h and V_h. Consequently ||a||_infinity<=G and ||a'||_infinity<=a_* for the complete reference.

    To specify exactly the inherited scalar residual budget under audit, put S_0=d*B_2+g*b^3 and rewrite (18) without varepsilon:

    \[ \begin{split} r_*={}&B_3/6+3A_1b^2B_1+(B_1/2)C_0^2\\ &+A_0(3b^2S_0+3bB_1^2+\tfrac32b^2B_2) +A_0b^3G^2+G^5/120+2\sigma(b+A_0b^3). \tag{42} \end{split} \]

    Here S_0>=omega_s*b; it deliberately retains the already proved scalar budget. No lower bound on the actual residual is asserted by any positive term of (42). Sharper use of the exact slow derivative, harmonic phases or damping row would define a different budget needing its own proof.

    All-mode bounds without a dimension factor from the Frobenius norm¶

    For a smooth N-periodic function use its continuous Fourier coefficients with normalization 1/N, and let W be their absolute sum. Parseval and Cauchy--Schwarz, with sum_(n!=0) 1/n^2=pi^2/3, give

    \[ W(F)\le b+\frac{N B_1}{2\sqrt3}=:W_0,\qquad W(F')\le\frac{N B_2}{2\sqrt3}=:W_1. \tag{43} \]

    The second bound uses the zero mean of a periodic derivative. Absolute convergence makes sampling legitimate: discrete Fourier coefficients sum aliased continuous coefficients, so their absolute sum cannot increase. Translation changes the phases of the continuous coefficients, so the same alias bound holds uniformly over shifts; discrete aliased coefficients need not acquire one common phase. Multiplication obeys W(f*g)<=W(f)*W(g) and the one-site difference has norm at most two. Thus the sampled edge amplitudes satisfy

    \[ W(c)\le Z_0:=D_kW_0+W_1,\qquad W(c')\le Z_1:=2(|v|W_1+\omega_s W_0). \]

    This is a norm of Fourier coefficients, not a restriction to selected carrier modes. In the normalized eigenbasis of J_0, each congruence entry of H has magnitude lambda*|hat(c^2)(-q-r)|/8. The factors from U have modulus one on every nonconstant mode. Summing over both frequency signs bounds every absolute row and column sum by lambda*W(c^2)/4. The symmetric near/far mask cannot increase these sums. The Schur bound, unitary invariance of matrix norms and (30) therefore prove, for the fixed Delta=lambda/2,

    \[ \begin{split} k_*&=Z_0^2\ \ge\|K\|_2,\qquad d_*=2Z_0Z_1\ \ge\|\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\omega\tau}C')\|_2,\\ m_*&=\tfrac\lambda4 Z_0^2 +\tfrac12\left[(G_h+G^3/6)a_*+C_0(C_1+V_h)\right] \ \ge\|M_{\rm ret}\|_2. \end{split} \tag{44} \]

    For the last remainder, write sin(a)*a'-a_0*a'_(0,fast)=(sin(a)-a_0)*a'+a_0*(a'-a'_(0,fast)). The harmonic gap is at most G_h and |sin(a)-a|<=G^3/6, giving the second term of m_*. Thus (44) includes spatial transport, slow phase, generated harmonics and sine curvature, with no frozen-envelope assumption. Metric positivity still requires k_*<cos(chi) separately. These are explicit sufficient constants, not a claim that this cutoff is optimal.

    The inherited scalar defect cannot close this bootstrap¶

    Use the constant residual allowance mathfrak r_*=2*sqrt(N)*r_* in (35), justified by ||L^(1/2)||=2, and the natural derivative budget a_* from (41). Even with zero initial error, the resulting majorant cannot close its edge-error ball over T>=T_phi=1/omega_s, for any 0<m<1 and positive radius rho satisfying the chart premises. This conclusion does not depend on the size of the retained-block bound or on the cutoff.

    To prove it, set c_5=D_k^5/120. The published allowances obey

    \[ a_*\ge\lambda D_k b,\quad r_*\ge2\sigma b+c_5b^5,\quad T_\phi>\frac1{g b^2},\quad \delta_\kappa:=\cos(\chi)-k_*\le1. \tag{45} \]

    If metric positivity fails, there is already no certificate. Otherwise alpha>=a_*rho/(6*delta_kappa)>0. The constant-residual term in (35) and exp(x)-1>=x^2/2 imply

    \[ \frac{Q(T_\phi)}\rho \ge\frac{(1+k_*)a_*\mathfrak r_*T_\phi^2} {12\delta_\kappa^{3/2}} \ge\frac{a_*\mathfrak r_*T_\phi^2}{12}. \tag{46} \]

    For the fixed carrier, lambda>1/4, D_k>3/4, g<1/72 and c_5>1/512; the original loss satisfies sigma>3/N^2. Using u+v>=2*sqrt(u*v) for positive u,v gives

    \[ \begin{split} \frac{a_*\mathfrak r_*T_\phi^2}{12} &>\frac{\sqrt N\lambda D_k}{6g^2} \left(\frac{2\sigma}{b^2}+c_5b^2\right)\\ &\ge\frac{\sqrt N\lambda D_k}{3g^2}\sqrt{2\sigma c_5} >\frac{81\sqrt3}{128}>1\qquad(N=1024). \tag{47} \end{split} \]

    Thus Q(T_phi)>rho, whereas (35) requires Q(T_phi)<rho*sqrt(delta_kappa)<=rho. Larger horizons or initial errors only increase this particular majorant. Neither choosing another width nor eliminating every other growth allowance repairs it.

    There is a separate obstruction if one attempts only to improve the cubic error term while retaining the same scalar forcing allowance and global edge-to-node conversion. Even granting zero growth, zero initial error and zero mean error, the resulting full-state allowance U_T obeys

    \[ \frac{U_{T_\phi}}{\sqrt N\,b(1+A_0b^2)} \ge\frac4{g(2-m)\sqrt{\ell_1}(1+A_0b^2)} \left(\frac{2\sigma}{b^2}+c_5b^2\right) >\frac{9\sqrt3}{8}>1. \tag{48} \]

    Here G+rho<pi/2 implies b<4; A_0<1/16 gives 1+A_0b^2<2, and sqrt(ell_1)<8/N. The same arithmetic-geometric-mean bound proves the final inequality. The denominator in (48) is already an upper bound on the reference norm. This allowance therefore cannot certify an error smaller than the signal itself, even before resonance growth. It is not a lower bound on actual error or a failure of the exact flow.

    Method decision. Equations (40)--(44) supply the previously missing spatially varying constants, but the inherited scalar defect treatment fails before their optimization could matter. Equations (47)--(48) exclude that specified combination of residual, nonlinear remainder and norm conversion, not all relative-storage methods or all conservative references. A damped reference has not been proved necessary. Retaining the defect's phase, spatial support and two-row correlations in its transport, or a different justified residual/observation estimate, changes the premises. Merely bounding the retained block more tightly does not.

    10. Signed defect transport retains phase, aliases and both rows¶

    The next comparison changes the failed allowances, rather than optimizing (42). Let h_0=2*pi/N, omega= lambda-omega_s, and expand the continuous periodic profiles in Fourier coefficients normalized by 1/N. The real coordinates of the complete reference in section 9 are

    \[ x_*=X_1\cos\psi+X_3\cos3\psi,\qquad \theta_*=\Theta_1\sin\psi+\Theta_3\sin3\psi, \]
