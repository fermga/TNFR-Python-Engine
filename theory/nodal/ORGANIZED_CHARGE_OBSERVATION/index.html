    Conserved charge observations under the organized sine law¶

    Model, observation class and scientific scope¶

    This owner classifies restricted conserved observations of the complete organized sine law. It distinguishes a local contribution from a general collective property: additivity across regions does not imply that each contribution reads only one node. The classification below imposes that additional information restriction explicitly. It is not a theorem excluding physical charge or all collective charge maps.

    The physical comparison below closes with an exact obstruction to a specified density-only electroquasistatic realization. A separate modal proof also excludes one homogeneous local edge redistribution as a smooth complete density state, including near an acute C9 twist. These conclusions leave descriptions with retained internal material state open. They do not admit a physical dataset or infer an electromagnetic origin for TNFR.

    Keep a fixed finite connected simple unit graph \(G=(V,E)\), with \(n=|V|\ge2\), its unnormalized Laplacian \(L\), held unit capacities, and the complete quarter law

    \[ x'=\frac{-Lx+\gamma S(\theta)}4,\qquad \theta'=\frac{\gamma Lx}4,\qquad S_i(\theta)=\sum_{j\sim i}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i),\qquad \gamma=\frac1{1023\pi}>0. \tag{1} \]

    Prime denotes the declared tau clock of the organized contact. All \(x_i\in\mathbb R\) are signed form, and all phases lie on \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\). Both rows use the same quarter factor, including on graphs with unequal degrees. There is no forcing, coordinate reset, support event, variable capacity or additional phase velocity. This is a detached theorem for the smooth-sine comparison; it neither selects the law nor executes a registered operator. The actual 144-edge contact graphs are members of this support class.

    The complete field preserves \(\sum_i x_i\), by pairwise cancellation. It also preserves \(\sum_i\widetilde\theta_i\) on any consistently transported set of continuous phase lifts. The latter is not a single-valued real observation of the circular state. Common form translation is an exact symmetry of (1), but calling that symmetry unobservable is a separate observation premise. These distinctions retain the form foundation, parameter foundation, common-origin owner and collective-property ontology.

    Identical local contributions: an exhaustive classification¶

    Let \(f:\mathbb R\times\mathbb T\to\mathbb R\) be \(C^2\), the same function at every node, and define

    \[ Q_f(x,\theta)=\sum_{i\in V}f(x_i,\theta_i). \tag{2} \]

    Each contribution consumes its own form and circular phase only. It does not consume neighbor states, incident edges, a region label, a hidden reference, history, a phase lift or the state of a measuring apparatus. Require (2) to be conserved for every solution and initial state in the full domain of (1), not only along a selected acquired family or inside one winding chamber.

    Conditional classification. Under these hypotheses,

    \[ \boxed{f(x,\theta)=a x+b,\qquad Q_f=a\sum_i x_i+bn,} \tag{3} \]

    for constants \(a,b\in\mathbb R\). Conversely every expression (3) is conserved on the fixed support. The proof only needs \(C^1\) regularity; the stated \(C^2\) class is a sufficient convenient domain.

    Proof¶

    Conservation for every initial state implies the pointwise identity

    \[ 0=4Q_f' =\sum_i f_x(x_i,\theta_i) [- (Lx)_i+\gamma S_i(\theta)] +\gamma\sum_i f_\theta(x_i,\theta_i)(Lx)_i. \tag{4} \]

    First take \(x_i=s\) for every node. Give one vertex \(k\) phase \(\beta\), and every other vertex phase \(\alpha\). Its degree \(d_k>0\) because the graph is connected and has at least two nodes. The form Laplacian and entire phase row vanish. Only edges incident on \(k\) contribute to (4), giving

    \[ 0=\gamma d_k\sin(\alpha-\beta) [f_x(s,\beta)-f_x(s,\alpha)]. \tag{5} \]

    Thus \(f_x(s,\alpha)=f_x(s,\beta)\) whenever the sine is nonzero. Continuity supplies the remaining pairs of angles. Since \(s\) was arbitrary, integration in form gives

    \[ f(x,\theta)=A(x)+B(\theta), \tag{6} \]

    where \(B\) is a single-valued periodic function.

    Next take all phases equal, so \(S=0\). The last term of (4) is \(\gamma B'(\alpha)\sum_i(Lx)_i=0\). Give vertex \(k\) form \(r\) and every other vertex form \(s\). The remaining identity is

    \[ 0=-d_k(r-s)[A'(r)-A'(s)]. \tag{7} \]

    For any unequal \(r,s\), their derivatives agree. Hence \(A'(x)=a\) is constant. Finally (4) reduces, for every form vector and phase state, to

    \[ 0=\gamma B'(\theta)^T Lx. \tag{8} \]

    Because \(L\) is symmetric and its kernel on a connected graph is \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\), (8) implies \(B'(\theta_i)=B'(\theta_j)\) for all nodes. Phase assignments are arbitrary, so \(B'\) is constant on the circle. Periodicity gives \(\int_0^{2\pi}B'(u)\,du=0\), making that constant zero. This proves (3). The converse follows from the conserved form sum and fixed node count.

    Common-origin invariance is an additional restriction¶

    If, in addition, the observation must satisfy

    \[ Q_f(x+c\mathbf1,\theta)=Q_f(x,\theta) \quad\text{for every real }c, \tag{9} \]

    then (3) changes by \(anc\), forcing \(a=0\). Only the constant \(bn\) remains. Declaring every reference node to carry zero contribution fixes \(b=0\). Therefore there is no nonzero, state-dependent, origin-invariant conserved observation in the identical local class (2) on this full domain.

    This result does not require the actual form sum to vanish. Conversely, the zero form totals in the acquired contact preparations are facts about those preparations, not a derivation of universal neutrality. A symmetry-invariant interpretation of charge cannot be imposed merely because the underlying differential equations have that symmetry.

    A retained form reference permits a nonzero inventory¶

    Extend the observation data by one independently declared scalar reference \(r\), held with \(r'=0\). Its constancy is an explicit premise; it is not a new law inferred from an evaluated response. For each fixed \(r\), the same proof gives

    \[ f(x,\theta;r)=a(r)x+b(r). \tag{10} \]

    Require covariance of the observation under a common change of the form chart and its retained reference,

    \[ f(x+c,\theta;r+c)=f(x,\theta;r). \tag{11} \]

    Comparison of the coefficients of \(x\) in (11) gives \(a(r+c)=a(r)=\kappa\). Its constant term then gives \(b(r)=b_0-\kappa r\). Normalization \(f(r,\theta;r)=0\) fixes \(b_0=0\), leaving exactly

    \[ q_i=\kappa(x_i-r),\qquad Q_A=\kappa\sum_{i\in A}(x_i-r). \tag{12} \]

    The scale \(\kappa\) is not supplied by TNFR. If \(q_i\) is proposed in coulombs and form in a declared unit X, then \([\kappa]=\mathrm C/\mathrm X\). A physical clock would separately need a conversion from tau to seconds. Neither unit assignment nor (11) establishes such a measurement bridge.

    For a fixed region \(A\subseteq V\), edge cancellation in (1) gives

    \[ Q_A'= \frac\kappa4\sum_{\substack{i\in A,\ j\notin A\\i\sim j}} [x_j-x_i+\gamma\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)]. \tag{13} \]

    The complement receives the opposite boundary current, and \(Q_V'=0\). Thus (12) is additive, signed and transported. This is the uniform-quarter counterpart of the existing degree-normalized regional inventory; its weights and clock cannot be copied from that different law. Storage loss does not destroy (12), and the boundary phases remain consumed state.

    If \(r\) instead evolves, the regional row has the additional term \(-\kappa |A|r'\). If node membership, scale, support or form changes in an event, its actual inventory jump needs separate accounting. Conserved continuous totals do not establish event conservation, admission, descent or occurrence. The constant \(bn\) in (3) similarly changes when the node count changes; it is not a conserved physical particle number without an applicable support-event law.

    A self-centered reference has a different meaning¶

    Choosing \(r=\bar x_V=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i\) makes (12) invariant under a common form shift and keeps \(r'=0\) under (1). This is allowed, but it is a collective reference rather than a contribution with only its own node as input. It enforces \(Q_V=0\) by definition. Regional excesses can be nonzero and still satisfy (13).

    The reference set must remain part of the observation. If it is enlarged from \(V\) to a disjoint set \(V\cup W\), with \(m=|W|>0\), the same unchanged region \(A\subseteq V\) acquires

    \[ Q_A^{V\cup W}-Q_A^V =\kappa\frac{|A|m}{n+m} (\bar x_V-\bar x_W). \tag{14} \]

    Equation (14) is a change of the declared observation reference, not a current or an instantaneous physical influence from \(W\). It proves that arbitrary re-centering against a new environment cannot be treated as the same localized charge measurement. A fixed external reference or a fixed whole support avoids that reassignment, while retaining its additional reference premise. No charge quantization follows from either choice.

    Boundaries and collective possibilities beyond the site classification¶

    The full-state and information hypotheses are essential:

    • If \(\gamma=0\), phase is frozen and every periodic \(\sum_i B(\theta_i)\) is conserved. The phase-elimination step (5) fails, as it must. Equation (1) has strictly positive gamma.
    • On a declared continuously lifted phase chart, a linear lifted-phase sum is conserved. Changing one representative by \(2\pi\) changes that sum, so it is not a globally single-valued real charge on the circular state. This distinction does not forbid periodic collective functions of the total lifted phase, which are outside (2).
    • For a single isolated node, both rows vanish and every function of its state is conserved. The connected support with at least two nodes is a premise, not an incidental convenience.
    • Conservation only within a selected winding chamber or acquired source family is weaker than conservation on the full model domain. The proof uses uniform-phase and arbitrary-form configurations; it cannot classify a map defined only near a formed organization. Winding may be locally conserved until a branch crossing, without being a smooth global single-node sum.
    • Different node functions, retained references, boundary state, spatial geometry, nonlocal correlations, history and detector interaction are outside the identical local information restriction. Neither this theorem nor the earlier multiplicative-factorization obstruction excludes those collective descriptions.

    For example, an antisymmetric edge allocation \(p_{ij}=-p_{ji}\), supplied as a regular circular function of the actual boundary state, permits

    \[ \widetilde q_i=\kappa(x_i-r)+\sum_{j\sim i}p_{ij}. \tag{15} \]

    Its full sum equals that of (12), while its regional sum gains a boundary term. Regional additivity still holds. Equation (15) is an algebraic possibility, not a selected charge law: its evolution consumes \(p_{ij}'\), its spatial/source interpretation needs independent justification, and support events need their own admission. It explicitly shows why the absence of a phase term in (3) is not an absence of all phase-dependent collective densities. Calling the added term polarization would require the corresponding physical observation and field relations. The local edge obstruction below additionally restricts autonomous closure for one such allocation class; it does not remove its algebraic conservation.

    The execution plan alone decides whether a particular collective extension has sufficient independent justification for investigation. No extra state, interaction law, source acquisition or empirical evaluation is installed by these conditional identities.

    Independent electroquasistatic reference and observation map¶

    The physical reference is a specified macroscopic regime, not a proposed microscopic origin of electric charge. Use fixed isotropic linear permittivity epsilon>0, a prescribed nonnegative smooth conductivity sigma(z) of compact support, no impressed current, and potential tending to zero at spatial infinity. Position z and physical time t_lab are independently defined, and rho_e is the free-charge density; the fixed linear dielectric response has no independent polarization state. In the electroquasistatic approximation the conventional equations are

    \[ -\epsilon\nabla^2\phi=\rho_e,\quad E=-\nabla\phi,\quad J=\sigma E,\quad \partial_{t_{lab}}\rho_e=-\nabla\cdot J =\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla\phi). \tag{16} \]

    The separate premises are documented in the authors' MIT edition of Electromagnetic Fields and Energy: charge conservation, section 2.3, quasistatic approximation and its error, section 3.3, Poisson's equation, section 4.2, and Ohmic constitutive law, section 7.1. These source sections were reviewed on 2026-10-10. Fixed conductivity and instantaneous linear polarization exclude independent material memory, chemical drive and magnetic induction at the retained order. They are restrictions of this reference, not universal electromagnetic assumptions. A physical application would have to bound the neglected fields and material-law error; a structural clock or small numerical step does not do so.

    For a localized smooth charge distribution the specified boundary condition gives the potential

    \[ \phi(z)=\frac1{4\pi\epsilon} \int\frac{\rho_e(z')}{|z-z'|}\,d^3z'. \tag{17} \]

    This is the reference model's superposition integral, section 4.5. It makes the full spatial density sufficient to determine the electric field in this setting. It does not make the net charge sufficient. For example, ideal equal-and-opposite point charges of magnitude Q_*>0 at +a e_x,-a e_x, or at +a e_y,-a e_y, both have zero total. Potential probes at R e_x,R e_y, with R>a>0, give the response matrix

    \[ \frac{Q_*}{4\pi\epsilon} \left(\frac1{R-a}-\frac1{R+a}\right) \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. \tag{18} \]

    Its rank is two. This is an algebraic static extended-source control with specified sources and ideal noninvasive probes, not an acquisition or transport experiment. It establishes why the earlier TNFR response-rank obstruction is not by itself a discriminator against conventional electric charge. It does not require the two reference sources to arise under (1).

    A candidate complete density observation, before calibration¶

    Supply fixed disjoint spatial cells with normalized smooth shapes w_i(z), each supported in its cell, so integral w_i=1. Their locations, sizes and orientations are input geometry, not deductions from slot labels or graph conductances. Consider the candidate

    \[ q_i=\kappa(x_i-r),\quad \rho_e(z)=\sum_i q_i w_i(z),\quad \tau=b(t_{lab}-t_0),\quad b>0,\quad\kappa\ne0. \tag{19} \]

    Here r is fixed as in (12), kappa has units C/X, w_i has units m^-3, and b converts seconds to the declared structural clock. The ideal cell observation is integral_cell rho_e=q_i; a potential observation would instead consume (17) and the sensor's specified spatial weighting. Every charge derivative transforms as dq_i/dt_lab=b*kappa*x_i'. Neither x_i nor theta_i has been called voltage or physical position.

    This is a fully stated candidate map class, not independently calibrated apparatus. A real comparison would need source preparation, fixed r, spatial shapes, clock, scales, sensor loading and error bounds established before reserved evaluation. None are estimated here. The argument below fails already for exact, noiseless observations for every nonzero kappa and positive b, so fitting those missing calibrations cannot repair it. An approximate restricted bridge would need its own error and domain proof.

    Full-density closure obstruction under the retained law¶

    With fixed sigma, epsilon, geometry and boundary data, (16)--(17) assign one density derivative to each complete density. In particular rho_e=0 everywhere implies phi=E=J=0 and rho_e_dot=0. This is a consequence of Poisson plus the stated current law and boundary condition. Electrostatics alone does not specify charge transport. Zero net charge, or zero charge only in a region with externally imposed field, would not give this conclusion.

    Reuse the actual same-slot 54-node support of organized external interaction. Let alpha=2*pi/9. At every host node (c,j) set x_H=r, theta_H=alpha*j; at every donor node set x_D=r, theta_D=alpha*j+beta. Compare beta=0 and beta=pi/2. These are two allowed mathematical initial states of the complete quarter law on exactly the same support. They are not asserted to be reached members of the frozen acquisition box. Their old internal and outer phase profiles are well defined; no finite maintenance certificate is inferred.

    The complete density (19) is zero for both. All form Laplacians and all phase rates vanish initially. The two internal cycle currents cancel at each node, and host outer currents vanish. The remaining contact terms are

    \[ x'_{H,c,j}=\frac\gamma4\sin\beta,\qquad x'_{D,j}=-\frac{5\gamma}4\sin\beta,\qquad \theta'=0. \tag{20} \]

    Thus all derivatives vanish at beta=0, whereas at beta=pi/2 each host cell gains b*kappa*gamma/4 and each donor cell loses 5*b*kappa*gamma/4 in physical-time charge units. The total remains zero: 45 host contributions cancel the nine donor contributions. Disjoint shapes make the density derivative in the beta=pi/2 preparation nonzero as a spatial function.

    Consequently no deterministic density-only evolution, including (16), can represent both states through (19). This is stronger than two equal net inventories having different currents: the entire mapped spatial density and all reference inputs agree. The site classification explains why adding a circular phase term to an identical local conserved density does not fix this full-domain map. It does not prohibit retaining phase as additional material state, a collective density, or a justified smaller invariant family.

    The phase-sensitive transfer mechanism was already established by the regional interaction owner. Equation (20) specializes its cancellation argument to the actual uniform quarter law and support. The new conclusion is the specified physical-map obstruction, not a newly discovered phase response. The execution controls check all 54 rates and reciprocal balance through the shared contact observer; they are neither a physical experiment nor proof of the general classification.

    The gate therefore closes with a scoped exact map/law incompatibility. Installing (17) as a TNFR readout alone would merely supply the conventional electrostatic law. Matching that static readout would give no independent discriminator; omitting phase from the full transport still fails (20). Physical charge, non-Ohmic materials, polarization state, memory, other laws and prepared subdomains remain open. The earlier P2 and Volts decisions retain their distinct pure-EPI laws and failed source admissions. No archived response was replayed and no empirical dataset was admitted here.

    Homogeneous local edge redistribution does not close one density field¶

    The edge allocation in (15) preserves the total inventory algebraically. That property does not establish an autonomous law for its spatial density. The following obstruction covers one explicitly specified extension and uses the unchanged complete quarter law, without a numerical trajectory.

    Give each edge an arbitrary orientation. Let the incidence matrix B have -1 at its tail and +1 at its head, so that L=BB^T and S(theta)=-B sin(B^T theta). Retain one held reference r and a nonzero scale kappa. Consider the observation

    \[ q(x,\theta)=\kappa(x-r\mathbf1) +B\,p(B^Tx,B^T\theta),\qquad \kappa\ne0. \tag{E1} \]

    Here the same scalar C1 function p(u,v) is applied to every edge. It is 2pi-periodic in v and satisfies p(-u,-v)=-p(u,v), so reversing a supplied edge orientation leaves q unchanged. Dependence on other edges, independent material state, unequal edge laws and history is excluded from this class. The observation is defined on a full-state neighborhood of the equilibrium under consideration; a selected lower-dimensional preparation set is a different domain. Its total remains kappasum_i(x_i-r).

    By a smooth density closure mean a C1 field G with

    \[ Dq(x,\theta)F(x,\theta)=G(q(x,\theta)) \tag{E2} \]

    throughout that neighborhood, where F is both full rows of (1). The claim allows an arbitrary G, including spatially nonlocal linearizations. No specific electrostatic kernel or conductivity is needed for the obstruction.

    Necessary mode condition¶

    First take a uniform equilibrium x=r1, theta=theta_01. Put a=p_u(0,0) and b=p_v(0,0). Since p(0,0)=0, q=0 there, and

    \[ Dq_*=[\kappa I+aL,\ bL],\qquad DF_*={1\over4} \begin{pmatrix}-L&-\gamma L\\ \gamma L&0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{E3} \]

    At an equilibrium the derivative of (E2) gives Dq_* DF_=DG(0)Dq_. For an eigenvector of L with positive eigenvalue lambda, define c_lambda=(kappa+alambda,blambda). A perturbation on that mode which belongs to ker(c_lambda) must have zero observed derivative. Consequently c_lambda times [[-1,-gamma],[gamma,0]] must be proportional to c_lambda when that row is nonzero. Equivalently,

    \[ P(\lambda)= \gamma(\kappa+a\lambda)^2 -b\lambda(\kappa+a\lambda) +\gamma b^2\lambda^2=0. \tag{E4} \]

    If c_lambda=0, (E4) also holds automatically. Thus a mode erased by the observation does not evade this necessary condition. The argument does not assume that DG(0) is a function of L or initially diagonal in its eigenbasis: kernel preservation alone implies (E4).

    The polynomial P has degree at most two and constant term gamma*kappa^2, which is nonzero. A graph with at least three distinct positive Laplacian eigenvalues therefore cannot admit (E2) for any a,b, and hence for any p in the declared class. This is a necessary linearization obstruction to exact nonlinear closure; satisfying it on some smaller spectrum would not prove nonlinear closure.

    The obstruction also holds near an acute C9 twist¶

    This conclusion need not rely on a uniform-phase neighborhood. Orient a C9 cycle consecutively and take x=r1 and theta_j=theta_0+2pij/9 modulo 2pi. Every oriented edge has the same circular difference delta_=2pi/9. The closing edge differs by a full turn, so periodicity gives the same p and derivatives there. A constant edge allocation has zero incidence divergence on this oriented cycle, hence q_*=0. Put

    \[ c=\cos(2\pi/9)>0,\qquad a=p_u(0,\delta_*),\quad b=p_v(0,\delta_*). \]

    The true tangent at this equilibrium replaces the upper-right block in (E3) by -gammacL; the lower-left block remains gamma*L. The required polynomial is therefore

    \[ P_c(\lambda)= \gamma c(\kappa+a\lambda)^2 -b\lambda(\kappa+a\lambda) +\gamma b^2\lambda^2=0. \tag{E5} \]

    Its constant term is gammackappa^2, again nonzero. The cycle has the four distinct positive eigenvalues

    \[ \lambda_k=2-2\cos(2\pi k/9),\qquad k=1,2,3,4. \]

    They are strictly increasing because their arguments lie in (0,pi). Four distinct roots are impossible for the nonzero polynomial (E5). Thus no observation (E1) has a smooth autonomous density law on any full-state neighborhood of this acute winding-one equilibrium. This uses the actual twisted tangent, not a transferred consensus certificate. It does not show that the retained finite acquisition history fills such a neighborhood, nor exclude a map defined only on an admitted smaller invariant family.

    The conclusion strengthens the conservation-only example (15): adding one homogeneous local edge allocation cannot make the mapped density a complete state for the unchanged quarter dynamics on these domains. It does not exclude a density accompanied by independent internal material variables, unequal or nonlocal observations, a memory law, or a different complete model. Nor does it identify the mathematical edge allocation with physical polarization. A fixed nonzero clock conversion rescales the tangent and leaves its kernel condition unchanged.
