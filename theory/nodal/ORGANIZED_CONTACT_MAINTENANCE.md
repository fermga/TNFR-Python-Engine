## 2. Two full-state stationary controls and their complete tangents

### Aligned constituent origins

For a common origin Phi and common form m, the aligned state

\[
 x^*_{c,j}=m,\qquad \theta^*_{c,j}=\psi_j+\Phi
 \tag{2}
\]

is stationary: opposite internal currents cancel and every wheel current
vanishes. It retains six internal windings one and nine outer windings zero.
The global means choose m and Phi; these are not independently damped modes.

Let `L9` denote the C9 Laplacian and K the W6 Laplacian. In the chosen node
order, the unweighted form Laplacian and phase-storage Hessian at (2) are

\[
 L=I_6\otimes L_9+K\otimes I_9,\qquad
 M_+=\cos\alpha\,I_6\otimes L_9+K\otimes I_9.
 \tag{3}
\]

Both are positive on the common-mode complement. This is a full-fine
statement, including perturbations that leave the twelve-coordinate carrier.

### Antipodal external constituent

The spoke currents still vanish, so this state is also stationary. Its

\[
 M_-=\cos\alpha\,I_6\otimes L_9+K_-\otimes I_9.

The stationary label and the same marked windings do not imply the same

 \lambda_9(k)=2-2\cos(2\pi k/9),\qquad k=0,\ldots,8.

## 3. Triangles obstruct reuse of a wholly acute winding-one cell

At any fixed slot j, let d_c be the principal host ring gap from c to c+1
and sigma_c the principal spoke gap from host c to the external node.
The oriented triangle periods are integers

\[
 n_c=\frac{d_c+\sigma_{c+1}-\sigma_c}{2\pi},\qquad
 \sum_{c=0}^4n_c=\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{c=0}^4d_c.
 \tag{6}
\]

These are topological identities for the actual fine state, with principal
branches away from their cuts. Outer winding one makes their sum one, so
some triangle has a nonzero period. Its three absolute principal gaps
cannot all be strictly below `2*pi/3`. In particular a wholly acute joined
chart cannot retain outer winding one. If all five old ring gaps remain
acute, that triangle additionally forces at least one spoke gap to have
absolute value at least `3*pi/4`: its two spoke magnitudes must sum to at
least `2*pi-|d_c|`, which exceeds `3*pi/2` in the strict chart.

This holds independently at each of the nine slots and requires no carrier
assumption. It explains why the earlier positive-edge-cosine storage trap
cannot simply be reused on the enlarged support. It does **not** establish
that the complete phase Hessian is indefinite: negative edge weights can
be compensated by the rest of a graph. Nor does it prohibit equilibria
with some nonacute edges or prove loss of an actual source winding.

### Closed acute-cell obstruction

Suppose a carrier stationary state has outer winding one and five directed
principal ring gaps `d_j` in `[-pi/2,pi/2]`. Their sum is `2*pi`; consequently
none can be negative, since the other four have sum at most `2*pi`.
Set the hub phase to zero, and denote the ring phases modulo `2*pi` by
phi_j. The stationary ring equations are

\[
 \sin\phi_j=\sin d_j-\sin d_{j-1}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Choose a maximal gap `d_m`. Since sine is increasing on `[0,pi/2]`, its
first endpoint has nonnegative sine and its second endpoint nonpositive
sine. The positive oriented gap is at most `pi/2`, so these endpoints
straddle the hub's antipodal phase. Choose representatives

\[
 \phi_m=\pi-u,\qquad \phi_{m+1}=\pi+v,
 \qquad u,v\ge0,\quad u+v=d_m.
\]

Adding the two adjacent relations in (9) gives

\[
 \sin d_{m-1}+\sin d_{m+1}
 =2\sin d_m-\sin u-\sin v
 \le\sin d_m\le1,
 \tag{10}
\]

where `sin(u)+sin(v)>=sin(u+v)` for these angles. For two angles a,b in
`[0,pi/2]`, `sin(a)+sin(b)<=1` implies `a+b<=pi/2`. For completeness,
at fixed sum `s>=pi/2` the concave sine sum has minimum
`1+sin(s-pi/2)` at an endpoint; equality at sum `pi/2` requires one angle
zero and the other `pi/2`. Equation (10) therefore bounds the sum of the
two gaps adjacent to d_m by `pi/2`.

The maximal gap and the other two gaps are each at most `pi/2`. Since the
five gaps sum to `2*pi`, every bound must be an equality. Thus four gaps
are `pi/2` and one is zero. At the two coincident endpoints of that zero
gap, (9) would require the same phase to have sine minus one and plus one,
respectively. This is impossible. Hence **no carrier stationary state lies
in the closed acute outer-winding-one cell**. Reflection of all phases
proves the corresponding winding-minus-one result.

The argument uses the actual unit ring and spoke weights. It neither
changes the interface nor identifies a constitutive coefficient from a
desired retained organization.

### Dynamical consequence and its precise boundary

Every carrier trajectory starting in this cell leaves its closure after
some finite time. To prove this, suppose instead it remained there for all
future times. Conserved mean form and (8) bound X. The phase torus is
compact, and the closed acute winding cell stays away from principal
branch cuts, so the trajectory has a nonempty compact omega-limit set.
Storage is nonincreasing. Its largest invariant zero-loss subset requires
`KX=0`; the phase row then vanishes, and invariance of the form row requires
`S_W6(Phi)=0`. The closed-cell obstruction excludes that set, contradicting
indefinite retention. The same reasoning applies for every supplied hub
origin and every finite initial carrier form.

This supplies no uniform exit deadline. Leaving an acute cell at absolute
gap `pi/2` is not changing winding at a branch cut of absolute gap pi.
Nor does carrier exit establish exit of every actual fine-state source:
off the carrier, internal C9 torques enter the stationary equations and
cannot be discarded from (9). Broader nonacute winding-one equilibria and
their possible attraction require their own classification.

## 5. A full-fine obstruction to stable retention near the carrier

The previous section's stationary equations are restricted to the carrier.
A different argument applies to the entire fine phase Hessian, even when
internal currents do not cancel. Let

\[
 M(\theta)=B\,\operatorname{diag}(\cos\delta_e)B^T,
 \qquad
 D(\theta)=\sum_{54\ \mathrm{internal\ edges}}
                  [1-\cos(\delta_e-\alpha)].
 \tag{11}
\]

Here B is any consistently oriented incidence matrix. Orient each internal
cycle in increasing slot order; its closing gap retains the declared
`+2*pi` winding representative. The cosine in D makes any equivalent
representative harmless. D measures departure of the internal edge gaps
from the carrier twist. It is not a distance between complete states and
does not replace their form or phase coordinates.

Suppose all nine old outer cycles have strictly acute winding one. With
an arbitrary common reference beta, put

\[
 \chi_{c,j}=\theta_{c,j}-\psi_j-\beta,
 \qquad v_{c,j}=\sin\chi_{c,j},\quad
 w_{c,j}=\cos\chi_{c,j}.
\]

For every edge, the squared differences of these two vectors sum to
`2*(1-cos(chi_b-chi_a))`. Their two phase quadratic forms therefore obey

\[
 v^TMv+w^TMw
 =2\sum_e\cos\delta_e\,[1-\cos(\chi_b-\chi_a)].
 \tag{12}
\]

For same-slot wheel edges, the demodulated difference is the actual gap.
For internal edges it is the actual gap minus alpha, modulo `2*pi`.
Since `cos(delta_e)<=1` and every bracket is nonnegative, the internal
contribution to the sum on the right before its factor two is at most D.
This inequality does not assume positive internal cosines.

To bound a wheel, let its five acute ring gaps be d_i. Winding one makes
them positive and their sum `2*pi`. Put `c5=cos(2*pi/5)` and
`C=sum_i cos(d_i)`. Concavity of cosine and the quadratic-mean inequality
give

\[
 0<C\le5c_5<5/2,\qquad
 \sum_i[\cos d_i-\cos^2d_i]
 \le C-C^2/5\le5c_5(1-c_5).
 \tag{13}
\]

The hub's antipodal point is bracketed by two consecutive ring vertices.
Their hub-relative phases are `pi-a` and `pi+b`, with nonnegative a,b
and `a+b<pi/2`. The two corresponding spoke contributions
`cos(sigma)-cos(sigma)^2` sum to less than minus two, since
`cos(a)+cos(b)>1` and `cos(a)^2+cos(b)^2>1`. Each of the remaining three
spoke contributions is at most `1/4`. Thus every wheel contributes less
than minus d, where

\[
 d=\frac54-5c_5(1-c_5)
   =\frac{5(7-3\sqrt5)}8>0.
 \tag{14}
\]

Combining the nine wheels and the internal edges yields the strict bound

\[
 v^TMv+w^TMw<2[D(\theta)-9d].
 \tag{15}
\]

Consequently **D<=9d excludes a positive-semidefinite phase Hessian** in
this acute outer-winding-one domain. At least one of v,w has negative
quadratic form. Subtracting its global mean preserves that form because
`M*1=0`, so the negative direction belongs to the conserved-phase-mean
tangent space. All fine coordinates are included; no carrier substitution
or assumed cancellation of internal torques enters this test.

At a stationary state, negative phase curvature supplies a positive growth
root for the complete two-row dynamics. On the zero-mean subspace L is
invertible. Eliminating the form perturbation gives

\[
 L^{-1}\delta\theta''+\tfrac14\delta\theta'
       +\frac{\gamma^2}{16}M\delta\theta=0.
 \tag{16}
\]

The symmetric matrix polynomial
`P(s)=s^2*L^{-1}+s*I/4+gamma^2*M/16` has a negative eigenvalue at s=0
and is positive definite for sufficiently large positive s. Continuity
therefore supplies a positive s with singular P(s), and its kernel gives
an exponentially growing complete tangent mode. Such a stationary state
is linearly unstable on the fixed-mean leaf.

This is a local stationary obstruction. A negative Hessian at a moving
source is not a positive Lyapunov exponent for that trajectory, nor proof
of future identity loss. A stable stationary retained outer-winding-one
organization must either have **D>9d**, involving changed internal gaps,
or lie outside the old strictly acute outer chart. Existence or acquisition
of either alternative remains unproved. Instability on the full relative
state space also does not prove that the 96-dimensional prepared family
intersects every growing direction.

For the actual outer-one source at attachment, let Z be its freshly rebuilt
full-phase error about the carrier and m_o the continuous per-node phase
drift bound carried through the entire observation word. The commands are
constant within each child and their final sum is zero; no commanded
internal edge difference is retained. Host internal edge deviations from
alpha are bounded by `2*(Z+m_o)`, while the external constituent's deviations
are bounded by `2*q`, with `q=1e-70`. Thus all original source errors and
all relative origins satisfy the initial bound

\[
 D(\theta(0))\le90(Z+m_o)^2+18q^2.
 \tag{17}
\]

This checks that the actual initial outer-one family lies inside the
negative-curvature domain when the right side is below 9d. It is neither
a stationary-state substitution nor a future bound on D along its motion.

### 5.1. Winding triangles and full-domain necessary curvature tests

The analytical mechanism admission declared at `0aec0fe4` asks whether
phase-Hessian witnesses can cover the complete source-compatible marked
class. The following argument removes the acute outer-gap hypothesis from
a necessary defect bound and excludes every common internal profile. It
does not exclude all nonseparable stationary states. All results use (1),
the original 144 unit edges and the complete phase Hessian; no source,
trajectory, optimizer or numerical certificate is evaluated.

Put `g(a)=cos(a)-cos(a)^2`. For one wheel, define

\[
 F_W=\sum_{e\in W_6}g(\delta_e).
 \tag{17a}
\]

**Winding-triangle bound.** If the five rim edges avoid their principal cuts
and have winding one, then `F_W<=-1/4`, regardless of the hub origin or any
acute-gap assumption.

First avoid spoke cuts as well. Orient each spoke triangle consistently
with its rim edge. Their principal windings sum to the rim winding, because
oppositely oriented spokes cancel. Each triangle winding is `-1`, `0` or
`1`, so at least one is `1`. Its three principal increments `a,b,c` are
strictly between zero and pi and sum to `2*pi`: a nonpositive increment
could not complete that sum with the other two strictly below pi.

For such a triangle, `g(a)+g(b)+g(c)<=-2`. Here is a direct proof that also
admits boundary limits. Relabel so `c=max(a,b,c)>=2*pi/3`, and put
`p=cos(c/2)` and `z=cos((a-b)/2)`. Then `0<=p<=1/2` and `p<=z<=1`.
The sum is

\[
 2(1-2p^2)z^2-2pz-4(1-p^2)^2.
 \tag{17b}
\]

It is convex in z, so its maximum is at an endpoint. At `z=p` it equals
`-2-8(p^2-1/2)^2`; at `z=1` it equals
`-2+2p[2p(1-p^2)-1]`. Both are at most `-2`, since `2p(1-p^2)<=1`.
Each of the seven remaining wheel edges has `g<=1/4`. Thus
`F_W<=-2+7/4=-1/4`. If a spoke is at a cut, perturb only the hub origin
to avoid all spoke cuts and pass to the limit. The marked rim winding is
unchanged and the cosine expression is continuous.

For the complete fine state, orient internal gaps in increasing slot order
and define

\[
 \begin{split}
 W&=\sum_{90\ \mathrm{wheel\ edges}}g(\delta_e),&
 A&=\sum_{54\ \mathrm{internal\ edges}}\cos\delta_e,\\
 B_2&=\sum_{54\ \mathrm{internal\ edges}}\cos^2\delta_e,&
 C_2&=\sum_{54\ \mathrm{internal\ edges}}\cos\delta_e\sin\delta_e.
 \end{split}
 \tag{17c}
\]

For `m=0,...,8`, take the real vectors
`v_m(c,j)=sin(theta_(c,j)-m*alpha*j)` and
`w_m(c,j)=cos(theta_(c,j)-m*alpha*j)`, with `alpha=2*pi/9`.
The phase reference is periodic on C9, including its closing edge.
The squared-difference identity used in (12) gives exactly

\[
 \frac{v_m^TMv_m+w_m^TMw_m}{2}
 =W+A-B_2\cos(m\alpha)-C_2\sin(m\alpha).
 \tag{17d}
\]

There is no assumption of stationarity or a separable phase profile here.
For nine winding-one rims, (17a) implies `W<=-9/4`. Positive semidefiniteness
of M consequently requires

\[
 A-\max_{m=0,\ldots,8}
       [B_2\cos(m\alpha)+C_2\sin(m\alpha)]\ \ge\ \frac94.
 \tag{17e}
\]

For `m=1`, the internal contribution is
`sum_internal cos(delta_e)[1-cos(delta_e-alpha)]<=D`, with D from (11).
Thus **`D<9/4` excludes a positive-semidefinite full phase Hessian**, even
for nonacute outer gaps and reflection-breaking states. The non-strict
boundary `D=9/4` is not excluded by this bound. At a stationary point the
negative direction gives complete-law instability by (16). At a moving
state it remains an instantaneous curvature statement, not a future
identity-loss verdict. The original acute-domain theorem and frozen source
assessment retain their own stated bounds.

### 5.2. No stable stationary contact with a common internal phase profile

Suppose the full phase state has the form
`theta_(c,j)=psi_j+Phi_c` modulo a turn for all six children, and its rim
origins have winding one. The shared profile psi need not be uniform,
acute or a stationary internal cycle. Choose vectors constant within each
child: `v_(c,j)=sin(Phi_c)` and `w_(c,j)=cos(Phi_c)`. Internal edges
contribute exactly zero. The nine wheels have identical gaps, hence

\[
 v^TMv+w^TMw=18F_W\ \le\ -\frac92.
 \tag{17f}
\]

At least one vector has negative quadratic form. Subtracting its global
mean preserves that form and places it on the full relative phase leaf.
Consequently **no stationary state with a shared internal phase profile
and retained outer winding one can be stable under (1)**. This excludes
arbitrary common-profile distortions and arbitrary rim origins, without a
reflection or acute-gap premise. It is broader than the helical carrier
and does not require the constituent internal torques to vanish separately.

A possible stable stationary target must therefore be genuinely
nonseparable: some relative constituent phase difference must depend on
the internal slot. Such dependence is a necessary property, not a supplied
new law or evidence that a stable target exists. It does not establish
formation, capture, a bound state or a physical constituent.

\[

 \tag{17g}
\]

Their sum is `2*pi`; all lie strictly inside the acute interval. For hosts
put `Phi_c=(c-2)*pi/3`, `c=0,...,4`, and put `Phi_D=0` for the donor.
Use `theta_(c,j)=psi_j+Phi_c` and zero forms, with a common phase translation
if needed to retain the declared lifted mean. The five rim gaps are four
copies of `pi/3` and the closing `2*pi/3`. The internal closing branch is
`-1`, as is the rim closing branch; all other marked branches are zero.
Every mixed marked square telescopes to zero. Thus this is in the
[original complete class](ORGANIZED_CONTACT_STATIONARY_SEARCH.md#marked-cycle-information-and-connected-chambers),
not merely a configuration sharing fifteen winding labels.

Per wheel the rim contributes `1/4` to `F_W`, and the spokes contribute
`-1`, giving `W=-27/4` over all nine wheels. Per internal child the sums in
(17c) are

\[
 A_0=\frac72+\sqrt3,\qquad B_0=\frac{13}4,\qquad
 C_0=\frac{5\sqrt3}4,\qquad
 \sqrt{B_0^2+C_0^2}=\frac{\sqrt{61}}2.
 \tag{17h}
\]

For every one of the nine m values, the half pair sum in (17d) is at least
`-27/4+6*(A_0-sqrt(61)/2)>1/4`. The strict inequality follows exactly from
`sqrt(3)>5/3` and `sqrt(61)<8`, which give
`A_0-sqrt(61)/2>7/6`. No floating-point trigonometric evaluation is needed.

Nevertheless let u equal one on host child zero and zero elsewhere, then
subtract its global mean. Internal edges again contribute zero. At that
host the two rim cosines are `1/2` and `-1/2`, and the spoke cosine is
`-1/2`, so `u^T M u=-9/2`. The full Hessian is not positive semidefinite.
The state is explicitly nonstationary: at slot `j=1` the equal adjacent
internal sine currents cancel, while `S_(0,1)=sqrt(3)/2`.

This example defeats the **nine pair-sum tests**, not every individual sine
or cosine vector, their linear combinations or the full quadratic form on
their span. A positive pair sum can hide a negative individual direction.
It also does not refute a stronger theorem restricted to stationary states.
The new common-profile test (17f) correctly rejects this same configuration.

The pair tests and common-profile obstruction alone do not admit a
chamber-wide stationary certificate. The following stationarity arguments
further restrict the nonseparable sector without discarding full relative
curvature or replacing it by more evaluations of these nine sums.

### 5.4. Every stationary PSD candidate has a nonzero aligned resultant

 \tag{17i}
\]

Every node has odd degree, five on a host and seven on the donor. For odd
d, the maximum of `sum a_k^2` on `[-1,1]^d` with `sum a_k=0` is `d-1`.
Indeed a vertex of this polytope has at most one nonsaturated coordinate.
The other `d-1` coordinates sum to an even integer, so the remaining
coordinate must be zero; the saturated coordinates balance. Convexity
puts a maximum at a vertex. Apply this to the stationary sine currents:
`sum sin(delta_ib)=0` implies `sum cos(delta_ib)^2>=1` at every node.

Suppose the full phase Hessian M is positive semidefinite. Edgewise
`cos(delta)<=1` gives `M<=L`. The C9 Laplacian is bounded by `4I`, while
W6 is a subgraph of K6 and its Laplacian is bounded by `6I`. Thus
`0<=M<=10I`, and spectral calculus gives `M^2<=10M`. Since `M_ii=r_i`,

\[
 r_i^2+1\ \le\ (M^2)_{ii}\ \le\ 10r_i,
 \qquad r_i\ge5-2\sqrt6>\frac1{10}.
 \tag{17j}
\]

Stationarity also makes `conj(z_i)*R_i` real. Consequently
`R_i=r_i*z_i` with the strict bound (17j), and every such candidate obeys
the regular fixed-point equation `z_i=R_i/|R_i|`. This excludes zero
resultants and antipodal alignment, without a separable, acute or reflection
premise. It is a necessary condition, not a proof of existence, uniqueness
or full Hessian positivity. The angular fixed-point derivative still has
signed weights `cos(delta_ib)/r_i`; their row sum is one, but negative
entries prevent inference of a maximum principle or contraction.

Nor can unrestricted stress positivity replace tangent-Hessian positivity.
With adjacency matrix A and `T=diag(r)-A`, stationarity gives `T*z=0`,
but `1^T*T*1=-2*V(theta)`. Requiring T to be PSD would force zero potential
and synchronization. The stable aligned-twist control (2) already has
`T*1=(2*cos(alpha)-2)*1<0`, although its M is positive on the full relative
leaf by (3). This rejects that stronger certificate, not the control.

### 5.5. Stationary comparison with a cyclically shifted profile

Use lifts in the original complete product marked chamber: internal and
rim closing branches are `-1`, and all other marked branches are zero.
Let `kappa_(c,j)` be the forward principal internal gap, and define

\[
 \theta^+_{c,j}=\begin{cases}
 \theta_{c,j+1}-\alpha,&j<8,\\
 \theta_{c,0}+2\pi-\alpha,&j=8,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad d_{c,j}=\theta^+_{c,j}-\theta_{c,j}
              =\kappa_{c,j}-\alpha.
 \tag{17k}
\]

This is the slot automorphism followed by a common phase rotation, with
the compatible lift retained. It preserves the complete law, every child
lifted mean and the marked branch vector. Internal and rim principal gaps
are cyclically shifted. Thus a stationary theta gives a stationary theta+
with a permutation-conjugate Hessian M+. The straight lifted segment
`theta+t*d`, `0<=t<=1`, stays in the original marked chamber: each marked
gap interpolates between two values strictly inside the same cut bounds.
This segment is a comparison of configurations, not a solution trajectory.

For each oriented edge, let a and b be its gaps at theta and theta+ using
the same lift/branch convention at both endpoints. Set
`h=(b-a)/2` and `mu=(a+b)/2`. Include all 144 edges. On unmarked spokes
retain these compatible lifted gaps; independently wrapping the two
endpoints would change the secant. On every edge
`2h=d_right-d_left=kappa_right-kappa_left`, hence `|h|<pi`.
Stationarity at both endpoints and the edge quadratic form give

\[
 \begin{split}
 0&=\sum_e(\sin b_e-\sin a_e)(b_e-a_e)
     =4\sum_e\cos\mu_e\,h_e\sin h_e,\\
 \frac{d^TMd+d^TM^+d}{2}
   &=4\sum_e\cos\mu_e\,h_e^2\cos h_e\ \ge\ 0
       \quad\text{if }M\succeq0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{17l}
\]

Subtracting these identities gives the necessary inequality

\[
 \sum_e\cos\mu_e\,h_e(\sin h_e-h_e\cos h_e)\ \le\ 0.
 \tag{17m}
\]

The factor `h*(sin h-h*cos h)` is strictly positive for `0<|h|<pi`:
it is even, and the derivative of `sin h-h*cos h` is `h*sin h>0` for
positive h in that interval. Therefore a PSD stationary candidate must
either have a changing edge with `cos(mu)<0`, or satisfy `cos(mu)=0`
on every changing edge. In the latter case (17l) has zero quadratic form
at both PSD endpoints, so `Md=0`. It also gives `sin a=sin b` separately
on every edge. The next argument excludes this entire flat alternative,
including Hessians with additional zero eigenvalues.

### 5.6. The flat alternative requires nine exceptional slots in six children

Suppose the flat alternative holds. Equality of each edge's sine current
before and after the shift means its current is independent of the slot.
Each internal C9 then has a common positive current: zero current would
force all its cut-free gaps to zero, and negative current would force all
gaps negative, both contradicting winding one. Write its acute solution
as `a` in `(0,pi/2]`; each gap is either a or `pi-a`.
If there are m obtuse gaps, their sum is `9a+m*(pi-2a)=2*pi`.
For `m>=2` this sum is at least `2*pi+5a>2*pi`. Thus the only profiles are

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{uniform:}&\kappa_j=2\pi/9\quad\text{for every }j,\\
 \text{one obtuse gap:}&\kappa_{k_c}=6\pi/7,\qquad
                  \kappa_j=\pi/7\quad(j\ne k_c).
 \end{array}
 \tag{17n}
\]

Adjacent children cannot have different types. On a wheel edge connecting
such children, at the eight ordinary internal slots the successive
compatible wheel gaps differ by `+/-5*pi/63`. There are two consecutive
ordinary slots, so three consecutive wheel gaps would have equal sine
and nonzero constant spacing `epsilon=+/-5*pi/63`. This is impossible:
`sin t=sin(t+epsilon)=sin(t+2epsilon)` first implies
`sin(t+epsilon)*(1-cos epsilon)=0` and then `sin epsilon=0`.
The wheel is connected, so all six children have the same type.

If all are uniform, they share an internal profile and section 5.2 gives
a negative relative Hessian direction. In the other case, d from (17k)
has the same two values in every child: its high value occurs only at
slot `k_c`, and the difference between the two values is `q=5*pi/7`.
Use `Md=0` at node `(c,k_c-1)`. Its only internal neighbor with high d is
the forward neighbor, across an edge of weight `cos(pi/7)>0`; its internal
contribution to Md is `-q*cos(pi/7)`, which is nonzero. Cancellation
requires a wheel neighbor whose exceptional slot is `k_c-1`; otherwise
all wheel differences of d vanish. At `(c,k_c+1)`, the unique internal
high neighbor is backward across the obtuse edge. Its contribution is
`+q*cos(pi/7)`, so a wheel neighbor with exceptional slot `k_c+1` is also
required. These arguments need no sign assumption on the wheel weights.

The nonempty set of occupied exceptional slots is therefore closed under
both neighboring slots modulo nine. It must contain all nine slots, but
six children can occupy at most six. This contradiction excludes the
flat PSD alternative exactly, without a strict-positivity assumption.

Consequently **every stationary candidate with a PSD full phase Hessian in
the original complete marked class has a changing edge with strictly
negative midpoint cosine in (17k)**. This is negative curvature of one
changing edge potential at the comparison midpoint. Neither negative total
segment curvature nor a negative candidate-Hessian direction has been
proved; other edges can contribute positively. It is not an assertion
about time evolution. Symmetry and stationarity alone have not forced a
common profile.

For an independent nonstationary control, the phase-pair blind point
(17g) has exactly two changing internal gaps per child under (17k), while
its wheel gaps are unchanged. Its stationarity secant is

\[
 \sum_e(\sin b_e-\sin a_e)(b_e-a_e)
       =3\pi(\sqrt3+1)>0.
 \tag{17o}
\]

It therefore fails the first identity in (17l), although all nine pair
sums pass. The stationary conditions add information missing from those

The remaining sector is regular, genuinely nonseparable and has negative
midpoint cosine on a changing edge. Neither that necessary property nor

### 5.7. The donor block is strictly positive at every PSD candidate

Partition the full phase Hessian into 45 host and nine donor coordinates:

\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\C^T&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C_{(c,j),k}=-\cos(\sigma_{c,j})\,\mathbf1_{j=k},\qquad
 C^TC=\operatorname{diag}(s_j),\quad
 s_j=\sum_{c=0}^4\cos^2(\sigma_{c,j}),
 \tag{17p}
\]

where `sigma_(c,j)=theta_(D,j)-theta_(c,j)`. A is the principal block of
the **joined** Hessian, including spoke diagonal terms; it is not the
Hessian of the isolated host. D similarly includes donor internal edges
and spoke diagonal terms. No internal edge cosine is assumed positive.

host phases at that slot would equal the donor phase plus or minus pi/2.
Opposite choices at adjacent rim nodes would place their marked edge at
a forbidden cut. Equal choices around the connected rim give winding zero.
Both alternatives contradict the admitted cut-free winding-one rim.
Thus C has full column rank, even though individual spoke cosines may vanish.

If M is PSD, D is PSD. A nonzero `y` in `ker D` would give
`(0,y)^T*M*(0,y)=0`, hence `M*(0,y)=0` and `Cy=0`, a contradiction.

\[
 D^2+\operatorname{diag}(s_j)\preceq10D,
 \qquad D\succeq\frac1{10}\operatorname{diag}(s_j)\succ0.
 \tag{17q}
\]

These are curvature statements on the actual phase state and require no
stationarity assumption. If D has a negative direction, extending it by
zero host coordinates gives a negative full direction. If D is PSD but
singular, take `Dy=0`, `y!=0`. Since `A<=10I` and `Cy!=0`, the explicit
vector `v=(-Cy/10,y)` has `v^T*M*v<=-||Cy||^2/10<0`. Thus failure of
strict donor positivity excludes full PSD in this marked class; it is not
merely a failed sufficient test. Subtract the common mean to obtain a

 Q\ \ge\ \frac1{10}\sum_j(t_j^2+s_j)>0.
 \tag{17r}

At stationarity, summing the donor equations additionally gives

strictly positive real aggregate host-donor phase overlap. This is a
derived phase balance, not an identified physical observable or a new
interaction law. Its quantitative bound depends on the actual phase state.

### 5.8. Exact donor relaxation and the remaining 44 relative directions

For any admitted phase state with `D>0`, define its Schur complement

\[
 K=A-CD^{-1}C^T.
 \tag{17s}
\]

For arbitrary host variation h and donor variation b, completing the square
gives the exact identity

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}h\\b\end{pmatrix}^{\!T}
 M\begin{pmatrix}h\\b\end{pmatrix}
 =h^TKh+(b+D^{-1}C^Th)^TD(b+D^{-1}C^Th).
 \tag{17t}
\]

Thus `M>=0` if and only if `K>=0`. The nine donor directions contribute
strictly positive curvature; all negative and additional zero directions
are preserved by K. Since `M*1=0`,
`D*1_D=-C^T*1_H` and `K*1_H=0`. Strict positivity of the full Hessian on
53 relative phase directions is therefore equivalent to positivity of K
on all **44 host-relative directions**. An exact negative K witness h
lifts to `(h,-D^-1*C^T*h)` with the same quadratic form; global recentering
does not change it. This is a congruence and inertia identity, not equality
of eigenvalues, dynamical modes or decay times.

Together with section 5.7 this is a complete pointwise curvature criterion
on the original marked class: test strict donor positivity, then retain
the full K. Checking only host means would discard 40 host internal
directions. The inequality `K<=A` shows that allowing the donor to respond
can only lower the quadratic cost compared with holding it fixed. D need
not have nonpositive off-diagonal entries, and K need not be a Laplacian
with positive conductances. Neither matrix defines a supplied pressure law.

There is also an exact **local stationary** interpretation. At any point
with `S_D=0` and `D>0`, the implicit function theorem gives a unique local
donor branch `b=phi(a)` solving `S_D(a,b)=0`, with
`phi'(a)=-D^-1*C^T`. Define the reduced potential
`W(a)=V(a,phi(a))`. On this branch,

\[
 \nabla W=-S_H(a,\phi(a)),\qquad \nabla^2W=K.
 \tag{17u}
\]

Full stationary points correspond exactly to critical points of W in this

The donor coordinates remain represented by `phi(a)` and their nine

stationary graph need not be invariant under that flow. In particular,
`S_D=0` neither removes donor form diffusion nor freezes its phase row.
The reduction supplies no autonomous 44-coordinate TNFR evolution,

### 5.9. Collective compensation must include the donor response

 h^TKh=\sum_{e\in\mathrm{host\ cut}(U)}\cos\delta_e

 \tag{17v}

Every PSD candidate must make this nonnegative for every U. The last term

### 5.10. Host balance uniquely reconstructs a stationary donor

The local branch in (17u) solves only the nine donor equations. Different
such branches can exist at the same fixed host state. Full stationarity
also requires all 45 host equations, which determine **at most one circular
donor state** in the original outer-one class, without any Hessian premise.

Write the host phases as `h_(c,j)`, the donor phases as `d_j`, and let
`y_j=(cos d_j,sin d_j)^T`. Compute the host-only torque T on the 45 internal
host edges and 45 rim edges, excluding all donor edges. At each slot define

\[
 T_{c,j}=\sum_{b\sim_H(c,j)}\sin(h_b-h_{c,j}),\qquad
 E_j(c,:)=(\sin h_{c,j},-\cos h_{c,j}).
 \tag{17x}
\]

The host equation is `T_(c,j)+sin(d_j-h_(c,j))=0`, hence `E_j y_j=T_j`.
The five-row matrix E has rank two. Otherwise all five host phases would
belong to one antipodal pair: an adjacent change between the two members
would put a marked rim edge at a forbidden cut, while no change would
give winding zero. Both contradict the cut-free winding-one rim. By the
two-column Cauchy--Binet identity,

\[
 G_j=E_j^TE_j\succ0,\qquad
 \det G_j=\sum_{c<e}\sin^2(h_{c,j}-h_{e,j})>0,\qquad
 y_j=G_j^{-1}E_j^TT_j.
 \tag{17y}
\]

Thus every full stationary completion has exactly this donor phasor.
The formula is globally defined on the admitted host class and uses no
argument of a host resultant, acute-gap condition, reflection symmetry
or donor positivity. It does not provide a uniform conditioning bound:
the Gram determinant can approach zero near excluded rim cuts.

This construction is covariant under a common phase rotation. For its
two-dimensional rotation matrix R, `E_j` becomes `E_j R^T`, T is unchanged,
and reconstructed y becomes `R y`. Integer changes of phase representatives
leave the circular reconstruction unchanged. Uniqueness is circular, not
uniqueness of unwrapped lifts. Any subsequent use of lifted means still
requires the declared marked branches and global phase mean.

### 5.12. Donor deformation alone cannot stabilize a common host profile

 (I_j-I_k)+(\sin\beta_j-\sin\beta_k)\cos\Phi_c
           -(\cos\beta_j-\cos\beta_k)\sin\Phi_c=0.
 \tag{17aa}

constant across slots and beta to be constant modulo a turn. Summing I
over the internal cycle gives zero, hence every `I_j=0`.

Consequently the donor shares the same circular internal profile as the
hosts. Section 5.2 then supplies a negative full phase-Hessian direction.
**No stationary state with a common five-host profile can have PSD full
curvature**, even if the donor was allowed to deform independently. This

### 5.19. Every complete unforced contact trajectory converges to one equilibrium

The same fixed sine law gives a global convergence statement without a
marked-class, acute-gap or positive-Hessian premise. Every finite initial
state with zero form mean has a global forward solution, finite total
phase variation and a single limiting equilibrium. The limit can depend on
initialization; this is not uniqueness of the stationary configuration.
It does not decide which equilibrium is reached or whether a stable
stationary target exists in the original marked class.

Write `mathcal H=1^perp`, and restrict L to this relative space. Set
`lambda=lambda_min(L|mathcal H)>0` and `mu=||L||<=10`. At the conserved
lifted phase mean, phase can be represented on the compact flat torus
`mathcal H/(2*pi*(Z^54 intersect mathcal H))`. This quotient retains the
given continuous lift locally; no boundedness of that lift is assumed.
Let q denote this circular relative phase, let V be the 144-edge phase
potential in (1), and put

\[
 k=\frac{\gamma^2}{16}<\frac1{16},\qquad
 U(q)=kV(q),\quad g=\nabla U,\quad P=L^{-1}|_{\mathcal H},\qquad
 v=q'=\frac\gamma4Lx.
 \tag{17ar}
\]

Since `grad V=-S`, differentiation of the actual phase row gives the
equivalent damped mechanical system and its energy identity

\[
 Pv'+\frac14v+g=0,\qquad
 E=\frac12v^TPv+U=kH,\qquad E'=-\frac14\|v\|^2.
 \tag{17as}
\]

Here P is a mathematical metric on the declared coordinates; it is not
an identification of physical mass. The nonnegative energy bounds x and
v on the fixed form-mean leaf. The smooth vector field has bounded phase
currents and only linear form dependence, so solutions exist for all
forward time and their form/circular-phase states are precompact. The
invariance argument used in section 5.17 gives a nonempty compact
omega-limit set consisting entirely of equilibria: zero loss requires
`v=0`, and invariance of that condition in (17as) requires `g=0`.
In particular `v->0`, `g->0` and `E->E_infinity`.

To prove convergence to one point rather than just to this stationary
set, use the auxiliary real-analytic proof function

\[
 F(q,v)=E(q,v)+\frac18g(q)^Tv.
 \tag{17at}
\]

F is not a new model law, physical storage or installed diagnostic. The
declared H and its loss identity remain unchanged.

The phase Hessian satisfies `||Hess U||<=k*mu`: the signed-cosine
Laplacian M obeys `-L<=M<=L`. Along the complete law,

\[
 \begin{split}
 F'&=-\frac14\|v\|^2+
       \frac18\left(v^T(\nabla^2U)v-rac14g^TLv-g^TLg\right),\\
 \frac14|g^TLv|&\le\frac12g^TLg+\frac1{32}v^TLv,\\
 F'&\le-\frac{17}{128}\|v\|^2-\frac1{16}g^TLg
      \le-c\big(\|v\|^2+\|g\|^2\big),\qquad
 c=\min\left\{\frac{17}{128},\frac\lambda{16}\right\}>0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{17au}
\]

The constant uses only `k<1/16` and `mu<=10`, including nonacute states
and marked-cut crossings. F is bounded below on the orbit, decreases,
and has limit `E_infinity`, since its extra term tends to zero. Every
point of the omega-limit set is a critical point of F at that same value.

The local analytic gradient inequality therefore applies to F in a
coordinate chart around each such point. This is the analytic method
for damped second-order systems developed by
[Haraux and Jendoubi (1998), DOI 10.1006/jdeq.1997.3393](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022039697933933);
the local gradient-inequality and finite-length argument is also stated
in [Begout, Bolte and Jendoubi, *On damped second-order gradient systems*, arXiv version 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.8005v5).
Their bounded Euclidean-trajectory hypothesis is not assumed for a
phase lift here. Compactness of the circular omega-limit set supplies a
finite chart cover. Taking the minimum of its positive exponents and
enlarging its constant gives, for every sufficiently late time,

\[
 (F-E_\infty)^{1-\rho}
 \le C\|\nabla F\|
 \le C_1(\|v\|+\|g\|),\qquad 0<\rho\le\tfrac12.
 \tag{17av}
\]

Indeed the orbit eventually stays in every neighborhood of its compact
omega-limit set. In combining the finitely many inequalities one can
also require `0<=F-E_infinity<1`. The last bound follows directly from
`grad_q F=g+(Hess U)*v/8` and `grad_v F=P*v+g/8`.

If F reaches its limiting value at a finite time, (17au) makes the state
stationary thereafter. Otherwise (17au)--(17av) imply

\[
 -\frac{d}{d\tau}(F-E_\infty)^\rho
 \ge c_1(\|v\|+\|g\|),\qquad
 \int_0^\infty(\|v\|+\|g\|)\,d\tau<\infty
 \tag{17aw}
\]

for a positive constant c1 at sufficiently late times; the finite initial
interval contributes a finite amount. Thus the phase has finite total
variation. Its original continuous lift is Cauchy and converges in the
fixed-mean affine space. This proves boundedness of the lift as a
consequence, rather than using it as a premise. Equation (17as) also
gives integrable v', hence integrable `x'=(4/gamma)*P*v'`. The full state
converges to a single point with `x=0` and `S(theta)=0`.

The limit may be a saddle, a degenerate equilibrium or a marked-cut
state. The theorem excludes persistent nonstationary limiting motion
under (1), while allowing transient motion, connecting orbits and damped
oscillations. Even finite phase variation can coexist with infinitely
many marked-cut crossings if the limit lies on a cut. If the limit is
cut-free, all marked windings are eventually constant; their values are
not selected by this argument. No convergence rate, finite settling time,
source-to-target capture or physical identification is supplied.

There is a stronger departure consequence for the already admitted
outer-one source. Define its distance from the fixed uniform reference

`d_uniform=min_p max_(c,j)|z_(c,j)-p_j*q_c|` as in section 5.17.
The current floor (17an) forces every stationary state to satisfy

\[
 d_{\rm uniform}(\theta_\infty)\ge\frac1{9\sqrt2}.
 \tag{17ax}
\]

In particular the entire original source family, at every donor origin,

 \mathcal J=hR_0+[-E(h),E(h)],\qquad
 \widehat{\mathcal J}
 =\mathcal J+[-\epsilon_\Sigma,\epsilon_\Sigma],
 \quad\epsilon_\Sigma=\sum_{k=1}^4\epsilon_k . \tag{49}
\]
Here the four readings are the donor means at the start and end of each
branch, each within its supplied radius of the corresponding actual mean.
Their signed difference lies in \(\widehat{\mathcal J}\), irrespective
of correlations among the four errors. Correlations among state, weight
and coefficient intervals likewise require no independence assumption:
ordinary interval products are outer enclosures, not probability laws.

A strictly negative upper endpoint of \(\widehat{\mathcal J}\) certifies
a negative observed contrast; a strictly positive lower endpoint certifies
a positive one. An interval touching or containing zero is indeterminate.
This includes an exactly zero increment: it does not have a strict sign.
Invalid primitive inputs are rejected before this decision and are not
reported as an unresolved physical response.

For inspection, the same calculation may also return the whole-window
port boxes \(A+[-2X(h),2X(h)]\) and \(D_0+[-2T(h),2T(h)]\).
Substitution into (47) yields uniform donor and host total-rate bounds,
and donor mean-rate bounds after division by \(n_D\). These fields
describe every time in the window. They are distinct from the initial
rate \(R_0\) in (49); the implemented primary increment enclosure is
exactly the initial-rate expression plus its integrated remainder.
There is no inferred autonomous equation for two constituent means.

### 12.4 Requested old-chart retention is independent

\[

## 13. Capacity-dependent finite exchange and the chosen reading

Positive held capacities extend the complete continuation already owned by
the [Silence handoff](ORGANIZED_SOURCE_PORT.md#organized-source-silence-handoff)
and [post-word means](ORGANIZED_SOURCE_CHILD_ORBIT.md#organized-source-word-phase-memory).
The new issue is the finite bridge contrast. An arithmetic constituent
mean can move on the disconnected control, whereas an inverse-capacity
weighted total has exact internal cancellation. Both observations have
conditional finite bounds, but they require distinct normalizations and
reading-error premises.

### 13.1 Shared initial state, complete law and capacity data

Keep section 12's two finite constituents, held symmetric nonnegative
conductances, single positive bridge, declared structural clock and
matched full initial state. The capacities are also identical between
the two branches and held throughout the comparison. On each branch's
own support prescribe
\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_i),\quad \nu_i>0,\qquad
 p=\frac{-Lx+\gamma S(\theta)}4,\quad
 x'=Dp,\quad \theta'=\frac{\gamma DLx}4,\quad \nu'=0.
 \tag{51}
\]
The same conductances occur in \(L\) and \(S\); \(\gamma\ge0\) is held.
Pressure is specified independently, not reconstructed from an evaluated
form increment. These rows are the selected shared-capacity law.
An operator name or the nodal identity does not determine the phase row.

A capacity reset and any accompanying form or phase reset belong before
the common initial state. Their actual endpoint, admission and work must
be established separately. No event occurs during this window. Reuse the
existing storage balance
\(H'=-(Lx)^TD(Lx)/4\), with the same weighted form and phase storage as
section 11. Capacity does not occur explicitly in that storage, but its
change affects both future rows and the conserved weighted means.
Neither the old mean leaf nor a zero reset work implies unchanged motion.

In addition to section 12's raw bounds, supply a global range
\(0<m\le\nu_i\le M\) for **every node**, and a donor-port capacity interval
\(N_u\subset[m,M]\). This range bounds heterogeneous capacities; it does
not assert a common unknown capacity. Let
\[
 \Lambda_D=\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset D}w_{ij}|\nu_i-\nu_j|,
 \qquad
 R_D=\sum_{i\in D}\frac1{\nu_i}.
\]
An associated upper bound \(\overline\Lambda\ge\Lambda_D\) may be
supplied. Otherwise the global degree bound gives the sufficient fallback
\((M-m)n_DK/2\), because the sum of internal donor conductances is at most
\(n_DK/2\). If both bounds are supplied, their minimum remains valid.
Preserve the supplied and effective values; a small supplied bound is
still an external full-state association, not a fact inferred from its sign.

The inverse-capacity mass has the automatic positive enclosure
\(\mathcal R=[n_D/M,n_D/m]\). A supplied positive mass interval may be
intersected with it, preserving the original interval. A disjoint
intersection is invalid. This is a consistency test, not reconstruction
of the capacity vector. The actual mass, port capacity, hidden coordinates
and graph can be correlated throughout the admitted family.

### 13.2 Complete motion and the two balance identities

The generator \(-DL/4\) has nonnegative off-diagonal entries and zero
row sums. Its semigroup preserves constants and contracts the maximum
norm even though \(DL\) need not be symmetric. Put \(k=MK\) and
\(g=\Gamma_{\rm hi}\). On both branches use (46) with \(K\) replaced by
\(k\), obtaining
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B(s)&=B_0+\frac{kg}{4}s,\\
 X(s)&=\left(\frac{kB_0}{2}+\frac{kg}{4}\right)s
                           +\frac{k^2g}{16}s^2,\\
 T(s)&=\frac{gkB_0}{2}s+\frac{k^2g^2}{16}s^2,\\
 F(h)&=\int_0^h[2X(s)+2gT(s)]\,ds\\
 &=\frac{k[B_0(1+g^2)+g/2]}2h^2
                       +\frac{k^2g(1+g^2)}{24}h^3 .
 \end{aligned} \tag{52}
\]
Here \(|x_i(s)-x_i(0)|\le X(s)\) and
\(|\theta_i(s)-\theta_i(0)|\le T(s)\).
These estimates retain both rows and every hidden node. They are valid
without an acute assumption or a small-horizon restriction; loose bounds
can leave the requested sign unresolved.

Orient every internal donor edge \(i\to j\), and define
\(c_{ij}=x_j-x_i-\gamma\sin(\theta_i-\theta_j)\).
For the port retain \(a=x_v-x_u\), \(d=\theta_u-\theta_v\).
The arithmetic donor mean and inverse-capacity form total obey
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (m_D^{\rm a})'
 &=\frac1{4n_D}
   \left[\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset D}w_{ij}(\nu_i-\nu_j)c_{ij}
                     +\nu_u w(a-\gamma\sin d)\right],\\
 Q_D&=\sum_{i\in D}\frac{x_i}{\nu_i},&
 Q_D'&=\frac w4(a-\gamma\sin d),&
 Q_H'&=-Q_D',\\
 P_D&=\sum_{i\in D}\frac{\theta_i}{\nu_i},&
 P_D'&=-\frac{\gamma wa}{4},&
 P_H'&=-P_D'.
 \end{aligned} \tag{53}
\]
The bridge terms are omitted on the disconnected branch. Reversing an
internal orientation reverses both factors in its product and changes
nothing. The internal arithmetic sum generally remains nonzero on the
control. In contrast, \(Q_D\) and \(P_D\) are constant there. The weighted
means are \(m_D^\rho=Q_D/R_D\) and \(P_D/R_D\), using fixed weights within
each member and its declared continuous phase lift.

Ordinary host and donor totals are not generally reciprocal. Even their
bridge contributions have factors \(\nu_v\) and \(\nu_u\); internal
capacity variation adds further terms. Reciprocity holds for the actual
inverse-capacity totals in (53). A host weighted mean additionally
requires its own inverse-capacity mass. None of these identities supplies
an error budget for a different measured quantity.

### 13.3 A finite arithmetic contrast that retains the control

Define \(J_{\rm a}\) as the connected-minus-disconnected change of
\(m_D^{\rm a}\), with four endpoint readings as in section 12.
At the common initial state the internal terms in (53) cancel between
branches. The initial matched slope is therefore
\(\nu_u w[a(0)-\gamma\sin d(0)]/(4n_D)\).
At later times those internal terms need not cancel.

The joined port current changes from its initial value by at most
\(2X(s)+2gT(s)\). For an internal edge, each branch starts at the same
edge state and each endpoint moves by the bounds (52). Hence
\[
 |c_{ij}^{w}(s)-c_{ij}^{0}(s)|\le4X(s)+4gT(s).
\]
This is where the control's full motion enters the proof. Let
\(A=A_0\cap[-2B_0,2B_0]\) as before. Outward interval arithmetic gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_{{\rm a},0}
  &=\frac{N_uW(A-\Gamma\sin D_0)}{4n_D},\\
 E_{\rm a}(h)
  &=\frac{N_{u,\rm hi}W_{\rm hi}+2\overline\Lambda}{4n_D}\,F(h),\\
 J_{\rm a}&\in hR_{{\rm a},0}+[-E_{\rm a}(h),E_{\rm a}(h)] .
 \end{aligned} \tag{54}
\]
The factor two on \(\overline\Lambda\) retains both evolving branches.
Dropping the disconnected arithmetic increment would change the
observable, rather than simplify this proof.

For a whole-window matched derivative bound set
\(A_h=A+[-2X(h),2X(h)]\) and
\(D_h=D_0+[-2T(h),2T(h)]\). Then
\[
 (m_D^{{\rm a},w}-m_D^{{\rm a},0})'
 \in\frac{N_uW(A_h-\Gamma\sin D_h)}{4n_D}
 +\left[-\frac{\overline\Lambda[X(h)+gT(h)]}{n_D},
         \frac{\overline\Lambda[X(h)+gT(h)]}{n_D}\right].
\]
This is a bound for the difference of two derivatives, not the derivative
of the joined arithmetic mean alone. The primary finite enclosure remains
(54); no endpoint box is substituted for an integrated remainder.

### 13.4 Separate weighted readings, retention and reductions

For the weighted donor-mean contrast \(J_\rho\), the disconnected
increment is zero under (51). Its initial slope and finite enclosure are
\[

 J_\rho\in hR_{\rho,0}+[-E_\rho(h),E_\rho(h)].
 \tag{55}
\]
The weighted whole-window derivative is bounded by replacing \(A,D_0\)
with \(A_h,D_h\) in the first expression. It needs no internal-variation
term because the appropriate internal contributions cancel exactly.

Each observed contrast adds the sum of **its own four** absolute
reading-error radii. For a weighted reading, those radii bound values of
the actual member's \(Q_D/R_D\). A reading computed with nominal
capacities requires an additional discrepancy allowance; ordinary mean
errors cannot be reused without justification. Arbitrary correlations
remain allowed. When no weighted-reading errors are supplied, the
conditional true weighted interval remains available, but its observed
interval and sign are unrequested. Missing errors are not zero errors.

For either observation, strict interval sign decides the corresponding
claim; touching zero is indeterminate. Independently, every requested
old-chart margin must satisfy \(\mu_e-2T(h)>0\) for retention throughout
both windows. Association with actual edges, charts and initial winding

\(N_u=\{c\}\), \(\Lambda_D=0\), \(R_D=n_D/c\), and the two means coincide.

\[
 X_c(h)=X_1(ch),\qquad T_c(h)=T_1(ch),\qquad
 F_c(h)=\frac{F_1(ch)}c,\qquad
 hR_{{\rm a},0}=hR_{\rho,0}=(ch)R_{1,0}.
 \tag{56}
\]
Both finite bounds consequently recover section 12 at elapsed structural

unchanged unit-capacity theorem. A loose global capacity ceiling can
weaken a certificate even when the actual capacities are equal.
Local capacity differences are not removed by this common-clock identity.

## 14. Matched interaction bounds at the disconnected limit

Section 13 bounds the internal motion of the two branches separately.
Its arithmetic remainder is valid, but its internal allowance need not
vanish with the bridge conductance. The common full initialization gives
a stronger comparison: only the added bridge drives the difference of
the two complete states. This section retains that difference through
both rows before projecting it onto either constituent observation.

Continuous dependence on coefficients is an established proof method.
The existing [conductance comparison](SINE_WEIGHTED_STAR_CANONICAL_CONTACT.md#sine-state-conductance-perturbation-bound)
uses it under a different normalized conservative law. The result here
concerns the specific held-capacity law (51), its arithmetic mean and
its separately normalized inverse-capacity mean. No coefficients or
verdicts are imported from that different law.

### 14.1 Common preparation and the zero-weight boundary

Keep all of section 13's full-state, capacity, internal-support, clock
and observation premises, extending the bridge domain to \(w\ge0\).
Let \((x^w,\theta^w)\) and \((x^0,\theta^0)\) be the connected and
disconnected solutions of (51). Their complete initial forms, lifted
phases and held capacities are identical, member by member. The initial
state can vary throughout an admitted family; subtracting independent
initial boxes would discard this matching premise.

The old weighted support has Laplacian \(L_0\). If
\(b=e_u-e_v\), then \(L_w=L_0+wbb^T\); the added sine current is
also proportional to \(w\). At \(w=0\) these two complete vector fields
coincide. Uniqueness therefore gives identical full solutions throughout
any finite window. Finite weighted support, bounded sine and the linear
form/phase terms give global Lipschitz fields in the supplied lifts.
This conclusion requires no zero constituent mean or synchronized state.

Here a zero-weight relation is dynamically absent from **both** selected
rows. It does not disappear from every possible graph consumer. In the
[native bare-neighbor law](RELATION_FOUNDATIONS.md#zero-relation-boundary),
phase support or degree can survive zero form conductance. Deleting such
a relation cannot inherit this weighted-law limit. Event admission,
occurrence and structural identity also remain separate questions.

The following norms are numerical coordinate norms in the fixed form,
phase and structural-clock normalization of (51). Adding the two norm
bounds is a comparison device, not a new observable or an identification
of their physical units. A change of units must transform both complete
rows and all corresponding coefficients before this calculation is reused.

### 14.2 Both full-state differences are driven by the bridge

Write \(k=MK\), \(g=\Gamma_{\rm hi}\),
\(B(s)=B_0+kgs/4\), and
\[
 u(s)=\|x^w(s)-x^0(s)\|_\infty,\qquad
 v(s)=\|\theta^w(s)-\theta^0(s)\|_\infty,\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{gk}{2}.
\]
Subtract the form rows using the old generator \(-DL_0/4\), which
contracts the maximum norm as in section 13. The old sine difference
has norm at most \(2K v\). The added bridge has form magnitude at most
\(Mw(2B+g)/4\) at either port. Subtracting the phase rows retains both
\(\gamma DL_0(x^w-x^0)/4\) and the bridge forcing
\(\gamma Dwbb^Tx^w/4\). Consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 u(t)&\le\int_0^t\left[\alpha v(s)
                      +\frac{Mw}{4}(2B(s)+g)\right]ds,\\
 v(t)&\le\int_0^t\left[\alpha u(s)
                      +\frac{Mwg}{2}B(s)\right]ds.
 \end{aligned} \tag{58}
\]
Both differences start at zero. The bridge forcing is evaluated on the
joined branch, whose full form bound is already justified by (52).
No hidden state, internal donor term or phase feedback has been removed.

Define the nonnegative coefficients
\[
 b_0=M\left[\frac{(1+g)B_0}{2}+\frac g4\right],\qquad
 b_1=\frac{M(1+g)kg}{8}.
\]
For \(z=u+v\), (58) gives
\(z(t)\le\int_0^t[\alpha z(s)+w(b_0+b_1s)]ds\).
The scalar integral comparison therefore yields
\[
 z(t)\le w\int_0^t e^{\alpha(t-s)}(b_0+b_1s)\,ds
       \le we^{\alpha t}\left(b_0t+\frac{b_1t^2}{2}\right).
\]
There is no division by \(\alpha\); the same expression includes
\(g=0\). Choose any certified \(G_h\ge e^{\alpha h}\). Uniformly
for \(0\le t\le h\), a family with \(0\le w\le W_{\rm hi}\) has
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Z_h&=W_{\rm hi}G_h\left(b_0h+\frac{b_1h^2}{2}\right),&
 u(t),v(t)&\le Z_h,\\
 H_h&=W_{\rm hi}G_h\left(\frac{b_0h^2}{2}
                              +\frac{b_1h^3}{6}\right),&
 \int_0^h[u(s)+v(s)]\,ds&\le H_h.
 \end{aligned} \tag{59}
\]
The integral estimate uses the polynomial at each time, not an endpoint
rate times the full window. For fixed finite background bounds and horizon,
both quantities are proportional to \(W_{\rm hi}\). In particular they
are exactly zero when \(W_{\rm hi}=0\), even if either individual branch
has substantial internal motion.

### 14.3 Projection onto the original arithmetic observation

For an internal donor edge the unit-Lipschitz sine gives
\[
 |c_{ij}^w(s)-c_{ij}^0(s)|\le2u(s)+2g v(s)
                   \le2q[u(s)+v(s)],\qquad q=\max(1,g).
\]
Thus the arithmetic internal derivative contrast is bounded in absolute
value by \(\overline\Lambda q Z_h/(2n_D)\) throughout the window.
Its integrated magnitude is at most
\(\overline\Lambda q H_h/(2n_D)\). The joined port current still
moves relative to its initial value by at most \(2X(s)+2gT(s)\), with
the same integral \(F(h)\) from (52). The initial matched slope remains
exactly the bridge term in (54). Hence define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{\rm match}(h)&=
 \frac{N_{u,\rm hi}W_{\rm hi}}{4n_D}F(h)
                  +\frac{\overline\Lambda q}{2n_D}H_h,\\
 E_*(h)&=\min\{E_{\rm a}(h),E_{\rm match}(h)\},\\
 J_{\rm a}&\in hR_{{\rm a},0}+[-E_*(h),E_*(h)].
 \end{aligned} \tag{60}
\]
The minimum retains the earlier valid allowance when a long-time
exponential estimate is looser. It cannot increase the remainder, and
\(E_*\le E_{\rm match}\) still tends to zero with the bridge ceiling.
The same minimum can be taken between the two whole-window internal
rate allowances. Neither minimum changes the observed arithmetic mean.

The weighted observation retains (55), because its internal currents
already cancel exactly. Its deterministic remainder is already
proportional to \(W_{\rm hi}\). Both normalized mean contrasts also
satisfy the useful full-state consequence
\[
 |J_{\rm a}|\le u(h)\le Z_h,\qquad
 |J_\rho|\le u(h)\le Z_h. \tag{61}
\]
For the second inequality the weights \((1/\nu_i)/R_D\) are positive
and sum to one. These are bounds for projections of the complete
solutions; they do not supply an autonomous equation for two means.

### 14.4 Exact shrinking bounds, reading errors and chart retention

An outward enclosure for the initial slope coefficient **per unit bridge
weight** can be computed from \(A,\Gamma,D_0,N_u,\mathcal R\), without
first materializing the bridge interval. Let its rational endpoints be
\(C=[c_-,c_+]\). Multiply the exact conductance endpoints last:
\[
 \operatorname{prod}_{\mathbb Q}(W,C)=
 \left[\min_{i,j\in\{-,+\}}w_i c_j,
       \max_{i,j\in\{-,+\}}w_i c_j\right]. \tag{62}
\]
These endpoints enclose the appropriate initial slope. Add the exact
rational remainder endpoints from (60), or from (55) for the weighted
reading, after multiplying by \(h\). The resulting rational endpoint
pairs are certified deterministic enclosures. Their coefficients can
include fixed numerical overestimation of the sine, but that uncertainty
is also multiplied by the exact bridge weight. Their widths and absolute
endpoints therefore tend to zero with \(W_{\rm hi}\). At an exactly
zero bridge both true pairs are exactly \((0,0)\).

The shared dyadic128 interval type is a separate outward representation.
It has a fixed grid, so an arbitrarily small positive rational interval
need not have a display width tending to zero. Retain the exact endpoint
pairs alongside that representation and decide strict signs from the
certified exact endpoints. A positive subgrid weight remains positive;
a zero-width represented display cannot replace primitive admission.
Neither a grid floor nor a display interval is an observation-error model.

For each reading, add the sum of **its own four** absolute error radii to
its true pair. At zero bridge conductance the resulting observed interval
is the corresponding symmetric error interval, not generally \((0,0)\).
Arbitrary correlations remain allowed; no cancellation between readings
is presumed. Missing weighted-error data still leave the weighted observed
interval and sign unrequested. A family containing \(w=0\) cannot have
a uniform strict true or observed sign. With a fixed positive error sum,
sufficiently weak bridges are unresolved even when the underlying true
contrast has a definite sign. No arbitrary weak-contact detectability
follows from deterministic continuity.

Retain the independent old-chart test \(\mu_e-2T(h)>0\) from section 13.
The new difference bound does not replace the absolute phase motion of
either branch: two almost identical trajectories can both leave an old
chart. A resolved sign, a small response and chart retention remain
separate properties. Equality in a sufficient bound is indeterminate,
not a witnessed loss of identity or reversal of the true interaction.

### 14.5 Reductions and an independent analytical discriminator

For tight common capacity \(c>0\),
\(\alpha_c=c\alpha_1\), \(b_{0,c}=c b_{0,1}\), and
\(b_{1,c}=c^2b_{1,1}\). With the same growth rule this gives
\[
 Z_{h,c}=Z_{ch,1},\qquad H_{h,c}=H_{ch,1}/c. \tag{63}
\]
The actual internal capacity variation is zero. The finite arithmetic
and weighted remainders therefore recover the common-clock reduction
(56) to section 12; their normalized observations agree. This is a reduction
of the exact conditional expressions, not a promise that different
orders of outward display arithmetic have identical endpoints.
Heterogeneous capacity variation cannot be absorbed into a single clock.
Zero capacity remains outside the inverse-capacity domain.

For section 13.5's exact three-node example, keep \(w=1\),
\(B_0=1,K=2,M=2,N_u=\{2\},\overline\Lambda=1,n_D=2\),
\(R_D=3/2\) and \(g=0\). Then \(\alpha=0,G_h=1,b_0=1,b_1=0\),
so \(Z_h=h,H_h=h^2/2,F(h)=2h^2\). The new arithmetic allowance is
\[
 E_{\rm match}(h)=\frac{5h^2}{8}<E_{\rm a}(h)=h^2
 \quad(h>0),\qquad
 J_{\rm a}\in\frac h4+
               \left[-\frac{5h^2}{8},\frac{5h^2}{8}\right]. \tag{64}
\]
At \(h=1/4\), this interval is \([3/128,13/128]\), strictly positive
with zero reading errors. The old interval \([0,1/8]\) was indeterminate.
The exact solution (57) and the observable are unchanged; preserving the
common initialization improves what the sufficient bound can decide.
At \(h=1\), the new interval remains indeterminate although the exact
contrast is positive. The refinement supplies no general sign guarantee.
This analytical comparison uses no numerical trajectory.
