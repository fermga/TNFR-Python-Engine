## 2. Finite nonlinear increment under the complete rows

Fix either actual source. Let \((x,\theta)\) denote its unprobed
continuation and \((x^+,\theta^+)\) its probed continuation. Define

\[
 y=x^+-x,\qquad v=\theta^+-\theta,\qquad
 y(0)=0,\quad v(0)=k.
\]

Their exact difference equations are

\[
 y'=\{-Ly+\gamma\Delta S\}/4,\qquad
 v'=\gamma Ly/4,\qquad
 \Delta S=S(\theta+v)-S(\theta).
 \tag{4}
\]

Use \(g\ge\gamma\), the shared outward rational upper bound, and
let \(X\) bound the actual initial form maximum norm. Heat contraction
in the unprobed first row gives, for \(0\le t\le h\),

\[
 \|x(t)\|_\infty\le X+gt,\qquad
 |d(t)-d(0)|\le4g(X+gh)t
 \tag{5}
\]

for every lifted edge gap \(d\). The second bound follows from the
complete phase row, \(\|\theta'\|_\infty\le2g\|x\|_\infty\).
It retains the background's own motion even when its initial form is small.

Put \(A(t)=\|y(t)\|_\infty\) and
\(B(t)=\|v(t)-k\|_\infty\). Every initial edge jump has
magnitude at most \(\epsilon\). Sine is globally one-Lipschitz,
so each edge's current increment is at most
\(\epsilon+2B(t)\) in magnitude. The heat representation of the
first row and direct integration of the second give

\[
 A(t)\le g\epsilon t+2g\int_0^t B(s)\,ds,
 \qquad B(t)\le2g\int_0^t A(s)\,ds.
 \tag{6}
\]

For every \(T\le h\), the supremum \(A_T\) on \([0,T]\)
satisfies \(A_T\le g\epsilon T+2g^2T^2A_T\). The checked
condition \(2g^2h^2<1/2\) therefore implies the pointwise estimates

\[
 A(t)\le2g\epsilon t,\qquad
 B(t)\le2g^2\epsilon t^2.
 \tag{7}
\]

To retain the finite nonlinear remainder, write an edge increment as
\(F(d,u)=\sin(d+u)-\sin d\). The exact inequalities
\(|\partial_dF(d,u)|\le|u|\) and
\(|\partial_uF(d,u)|\le1\) imply

\[
 |F(d(t),u(t))-F(d(0),u(0))|
 \le\epsilon|d(t)-d(0)|+|u(t)-u(0)|.
\]

The initial edge jump \(u(0)\) is held fixed in the first difference;
no small-angle replacement has been made. Summing the four incident edges,
using (5) and \(|u(t)-u(0)|\le2B(t)\), yields

\[
 \|\Delta S(t)-\Delta S(0)\|_\infty
 \le16\epsilon g(X+gh)t+8B(t).
 \tag{8}
\]

Finally integrate the complete first difference row (4), retaining its
heat term \(\|Ly\|_\infty/4\le2A\). Equations (7)-(8) prove

\[
 \left\|y(h)-\frac{h\gamma}{4}\Delta S(0)\right\|_\infty
 \le E(X):=
 2g\epsilon h^2+2g^2\epsilon(X+gh)h^2
 +\frac43g^3\epsilon h^3.
 \tag{9}
\]

An arithmetic child mean has norm at most one as a functional on these
coordinates, so the same error bounds the receiver readout. This is a
finite-time complete nonlinear estimate, including all fine coordinates,
background drift, phase feedback and form diffusion. It is not an assertion
that the initial derivative is the measured response.

\[

\]
