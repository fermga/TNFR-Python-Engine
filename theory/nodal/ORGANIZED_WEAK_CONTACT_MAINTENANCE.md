stationary stability in the original complete marked class unresolved.
This result changes one supplied model parameter: all 45 external spokes
have the same positive weight eta. It proves stationary existence and
all-future identity retention for a declared weak-contact range, including
the original acquired and observed source family. It is not a solution
of the unit-weight stationary problem. An exact stationary continuation

that source interval, the full fine residuals decay exponentially and

explicitly bounded remainder. The original all-origin source family
cannot, however, consist entirely of preparations converging to locally
asymptotically stable equilibria: its retained relative-phase circle
forces at least one exceptional origin for every fixed source member.
At the fixed strength eta=1/100000, a separate transient estimate
certifies five explicit donor-origin intervals selecting the five
attractive relative registrations, while retaining every original
source error and the complete observation history.

## 2. The old marked cell retains its complete boundary barrier

Use the original lifted branches: internal closing edges and host rim
closing edges receive their fixed 2*pi addition. Every old edge gap
lies in (-pi/2,pi/2). The resulting cell preserves the complete marked
class, including the zero mixed host periods, not just fifteen winding
labels. Spokes are unrestricted.

Put

\[
 \begin{split}
 V_n&=n[1-\cos(2\pi/n)],\\
 b_n&=1+(n-1)[1-\cos(3\pi/(2(n-1)))],\\
 V_*&=6V_9+9V_5,\qquad
 B=\min\{b_9-V_9,\ b_5-V_5\}>\frac7{500}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{3}
\]

The strict rational lower bound is the existing
[cycle-face inequality](ORGANIZED_OUTER_WINDING.md#5-every-product-cell-boundary-and-all-future-capture).
Its method applies here because the 99 old unit edges are partitioned
into six C9 and nine C5 cycles. Convexity of 1-cos on the closed acute
interval bounds their total phase cost below by \(V_*\).

On a positive face of an n-cycle, one angle equals pi/2 and the remaining
angles sum to 3*pi/2, giving \(b_n\) by Jensen. A negative internal face
instead has remaining mean 5*pi/16 and a larger cost. A negative rim
face is empty: one angle -pi/2 and four at most pi/2 cannot sum to 2*pi.
These cases cover all 198 signed old-edge faces: 54 positive and 54
negative internal faces, 45 positive rim faces and 45 empty negative rim
faces. No joint attainability of separate cycle minima is assumed.

The weighted spoke potential and the full form storage are nonnegative.
Consequently every actual boundary state satisfies

\[
 H_\eta\ge V_*+B.
 \tag{4}
\]

The old graph has two components. After fixing an origin in each, its
closed acute cell is a bounded polytope of relative lifts. Restoring
the relative host-donor origin on the circle makes its closure compact
modulo common rotation. A fixed lifted mean does not require pretending
that this relative origin is fixed.

**Entry and retention theorem.** If the initial old phases belong to
this cell and \(H_\eta(0)<V_*+B\), no future old edge reaches an acute
face under (1). A first exit would contradict (2)--(4). The strict
energy margin separates the retained phase sublevel from all old faces.
All six internal and nine rim windings and all original mixed host
periods therefore persist for every future time.

For every fixed eta>0 the complete graph is connected. Thus the form
part of \(H_\eta\) bounds all forms on the zero-mean leaf. Together with
the compact circular phase description this makes the retained sublevel
precompact. This is an all-future trapping argument for the full state;
no carrier restriction, acute-spoke premise or phase reset is used.

## 3. Positive contact admits stationary minima and a stable retained set

Consider the reference phase circle

\[
 x=0,\qquad
 \theta_{c,j}=\psi_j+\frac{2\pi(c-2)}5\quad(c<5),\qquad
 \theta_{5,j}=\psi_j+\beta,\qquad

 \tag{5}
\]

It lies strictly inside the old cell for every circular beta. The fifth
roots of unity sum to zero, so its unweighted spoke potential is exactly

\[
 H_\eta\big|_{\text{reference circle}}=V_*+45\eta.
 \tag{6}
\]

For every

\[
 0<\eta<B/45,
 \tag{7}
\]

the reference circle lies below the boundary barrier. Minimizing \(V_\eta\)
on the compact closed old cell, including its relative-origin circle,
then gives an interior minimum: its value is at most (6), whereas every
boundary value is at least \(V_*+B\). At that minimum all full phase
derivatives vanish and the full relative phase Hessian is positive
semidefinite. With x=0 it is a stationary point of both rows in (1).

This proves existence in the original complete marked class. It does
not make the reference circle itself stationary when eta>0, select an
origin, or prove a strictly positive Hessian. The minimum may be
degenerate.

Let \(\mathcal M_\eta\) be the compact set of phase minima just obtained,
modulo common rotation. The equilibrium set with x=0 and phase in
\(\mathcal M_\eta\) is Lyapunov stable in the relative circular phase
space with mean-zero forms. Equivalently one may use its full periodic
preimage on a fixed-mean lift leaf; a single compact choice of lifted
representatives is not asserted to be invariant.
Subtract its minimum storage value from \(H_\eta\). Within the trapping
cell this difference is nonnegative and vanishes exactly on that set:
connectedness makes its form contribution zero only at x=0. Compactness
gives a positive storage separation from the complement of any
neighborhood of the set inside a bounded retained sublevel. Continuity
provides a smaller initial neighborhood below this separation, and
(2) prevents exit. This is set stability, without an assumed isolated
or strictly positive stationary point.

There is a nonempty open set of initial full states below the barrier,
since (6) is strict. Every such state admitted inside the cell retains
the original identity. The actual acquired preparation requires its own
inclusion, supplied in section 5.

### 5.1. The source sublevel makes all fine residuals decay exponentially

Retain \(0<\eta\le1/100000\), the original old acute cell and
\(H_\eta-V_*<3/250\). These premises hold throughout every trajectory
in section 5 by the actual storage identity. The result below covers
any admitted state satisfying those same premises.

Put \(\alpha=2\pi/9\). The premises give a stronger bound on each
internal gap. With one C9 gap fixed at s, Jensen on the other eight
gaps gives the constrained cycle cost

\[
 F_9(s)=1-\cos s+
      8\left[1-\cos\left(\frac{2\pi-s}{8}\right)\right],
 \qquad
 F_9(\pi/3)-V_9>\frac1{25}>\frac3{250}.
 \tag{17a}
\]

Here is an exact certificate for the strict constant. Put
\(C_4(y)=1-y^2/2+y^4/24\) and \(C_6(y)=C_4(y)-y^6/720\).
The alternating cosine bounds on [0,1] and
\(157/50<\pi<22/7\) give
\[
 F_9(\pi/3)-V_9
 >-\frac12+9C_6(44/63)-8C_4(157/240)>\frac1{25};
\]
the last inequality is rational arithmetic. On the feasible acute
interval, \(F_9\) is strictly convex with minimum at alpha, and
\(F_9(-\pi/3)>F_9(\pi/3)\). All other old cycles contribute at least
their own \(V_n\), while form storage and spoke potential are
nonnegative. A gap with absolute value at least pi/3 would therefore
contradict the full storage sublevel. Every actual internal gap satisfies
\(|\kappa_{c,j}|<\pi/3\), throughout the future trajectory and at its
limit. The rim gaps retain their previously proved acute admission.

Define the exact child means and fine residuals using the original
compatible lifts. With \(\psi_j=\alpha(j-4)\), write

\[
 \begin{split}
 a_c&=\frac19\sum_jx_{c,j},&
 x_{c,j}&=a_c+z_{c,j},\\
 \Phi_c&=\frac19\sum_j\theta_{c,j},&
 \theta_{c,j}&=\psi_j+\Phi_c+q_{c,j},\\
 \sum_jz_{c,j}&=\sum_jq_{c,j}=0,&
 L_{\mathrm f}&=L_\eta|_{\operatorname{ran}P},\qquad
 \frac25 I<L_{\mathrm f}\le10I .
 \end{split}
 \tag{17b}
\]

P subtracts each child's mean, so its range has 48 coordinates. It
commutes with \(L_\eta\), because the same weighted wheel is used in
every slot. The lower bound in (17b) follows from the internal C9
factor: \(\lambda_2(L_9)=2-2\cos(2\pi/9)>2/5\).
For example, \(\pi>3\) and the cosine upper polynomial at 2/3 already
prove this rational bound. The upper bound follows from (8).

Compare the actual phases with \(\theta^0_{c,j}=\psi_j+\Phi_c\) along
the straight compatible lifted segment \(\theta^0+s q\), 0<=s<=1.
Internal gaps interpolate between alpha and the actual gaps, remaining
strictly inside (-pi/3,pi/3). Each carrier rim gap is the average of
its nine actual rim gaps on the same closing-edge convention. Every
rim gap along the segment is therefore acute. These facts do not
require the segment's storage to lie below the source sublevel.

On a fine direction, the internal Hessian contributes more than
\(\lambda_2(L_9)/2\), and the rim Hessian is nonnegative. The spoke
Hessian is bounded below by \(-\eta L_{\rm sp}\); its largest possible
negative contribution is \(6\eta\), since the spoke graph is nine
disjoint five-leaf stars. Thus the projected Hessian along this whole
segment is bounded below by \(1/5-6\eta>19/100\) and above by 10.
Moreover \(P\nabla V_\eta(\theta^0)=0\) for every set of child origins,
because its internal currents cancel and its wheel currents are
constant in j. Consequently the actual fine gradient obeys

\[
 \begin{split}
 g&:=P\nabla V_\eta(\theta)
       =\mathcal A q,\qquad
 \mathcal A=\int_0^1
       P\nabla^2V_\eta(\theta^0+s q)P\,ds\big|_{\operatorname{ran}P},\\
 mI&\le\mathcal A\le10I,\qquad m=\frac{19}{100},\qquad
 q^Tg\ge m\|q\|^2,\qquad \|g\|\le10\|q\|.
 \end{split}
 \tag{17c}
\]

Projecting both actual evolution rows gives the exact fine equations

\[
 q'=\frac{\gamma}{4}L_{\mathrm f}z,\qquad
 z'=-\frac14L_{\mathrm f}z-\frac{\gamma}{4}g.
 \tag{17d}
\]

The matrix \(\mathcal A\) can vary with time and with the collective
state. No derivative bound on it is assumed. Instead use the fixed
positive quadratic function

\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal F&=\frac12\|q+\gamma z\|^2
                       +\frac{\gamma^2}{2}\|z\|^2,\\
 \mathcal F'
 &=-\frac{\gamma^2}{4}
       \left(q^Tg+z^TL_{\mathrm f}z+2\gamma z^Tg\right).
 \end{split}
 \tag{17e}
\]

This is an auxiliary proof function, not a replacement storage or a
measurement map. Young's inequality bounds the absolute cross term by
\(m\|q\|^2/2+(200\gamma^2/m)\|z\|^2\).
Since \(\gamma<1/3000\), one has
\(2/5-200\gamma^2/m>m/2\). Also
\(\mathcal F\le\|q\|^2+\|z\|^2\),
\(\|q\|^2\le4\mathcal F\), and
\(\gamma^2\|z\|^2\le2\mathcal F\). It follows that

\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal F'&\le-\rho\mathcal F,\qquad
 \rho=\frac{19\gamma^2}{800},\qquad
 \mathcal F(\tau)\le\mathcal F(0)e^{-\rho\tau},\\

 \end{split}
 \tag{17f}
\]

The rate is in the declared tau clock and is uniform over the supplied

At each fixed positive eta, section 4 still gives one limiting full

### 5.2. The averaged wheel law has an exponentially bounded remainder

Let \(L_{\mathrm W,\eta}\) and \(S_{\mathrm W,\eta}\) be the Laplacian
and sine current of the six-node weighted wheel, with rim weight one
and spoke weight eta. The actual child means in (17b) obey

\[
 a'=\frac{-L_{\mathrm W,\eta}a+
              \gamma[S_{\mathrm W,\eta}(\Phi)+r]}4,\qquad
 \Phi'=\frac{\gamma}{4}L_{\mathrm W,\eta}a.
 \tag{17g}
\]

These rows follow by averaging the complete law: internal form and
phase currents cancel over each cycle. Their residual is exactly

\[
 \begin{split}
 r_c&=\sum_{b\sim c}w_{cb}
  \left[\frac19\sum_j
       \sin(\Phi_b-\Phi_c+q_{b,j}-q_{c,j})
                    -\sin(\Phi_b-\Phi_c)\right],\\
 \|r\|_1
 &\le\frac19\sum_{\{c,b\}\in E_{\mathrm W}}\sum_j
                     w_{cb}(q_{b,j}-q_{c,j})^2\\
 &\le\frac23\|q\|^2
 \le\frac83\mathcal F(0)e^{-\rho\tau}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{17h}
\]

The mean-zero fine residuals cancel the linear Taylor term on every
wheel edge; the sine remainder is at most one half the squared gap.
Summing both incident nodal contributions gives the first bound in
(17h). The second uses
\(L_{\mathrm W,\eta}\le L_{\mathrm W,1}\le6I\).
This reuses the
[constituent-memory distinction](ORGANIZED_CONSTITUENT_MEMORY.md):
hidden fine state can affect the collective rows quadratically, while
here its decay and the resulting error are controlled for the actual
contacted preparation.

The set q=z=0 is an invariant full dynamical carrier with arbitrary
child-constant forms a. On it, (17g) is exactly the weighted wheel law.
It is broader than the stationary x=0 carrier defined in section 6.1.
For the whole original source family the fine residuals approach this
set exponentially, and the exact collective equations acquire a
vanishing remainder. This is an asymptotic reduction with an explicit
error bound, not exact closure of the transient mean state.

In particular (17h) does not prove that the actual means shadow one
autonomous wheel trajectory for all future times, or that they select
the strictly stable branch of section 6. The initial fine state remains
in \(\mathcal F(0)\), and a collective unstable direction can still
amplify a small accumulated error. No coarse event law, controller,
autonomous source selector or physical observation follows.

### 6.1 Full fine-coordinate rigidity near a stable carrier

Put \(\alpha=2\pi/9\). A carrier is a state

\[
 x=0,\qquad \theta_{c,j}=\alpha(j-4)+\Phi_c
 \pmod {2\pi}.
 \tag{18}
\]

stationary precisely when the six origins are stationary for the

\[
 V_{\mathrm w,\eta}(\Phi)
 =\sum_{c=0}^4[1-\cos(\Phi_{c+1}-\Phi_c)]
   +\eta\sum_{c=0}^4[1-\cos(\Phi_D-\Phi_c)].
 \tag{19}
\]

Here rim indices are modulo five. The full potential on the carrier is
\(6V_9+9V_{\mathrm w,\eta}\). Let \(M_{\mathrm w,\eta}\) be the six-node
Hessian of (19). In slot-first tensor order the full phase Hessian is

\[
 M_\eta
 =\cos\alpha\,L_9\otimes I_6
       +I_9\otimes M_{\mathrm w,\eta}.
 \tag{20}
\]

Thus full positive semidefiniteness is equivalent to wheel positive
semidefiniteness. Strict positivity modulo the one common rotation is
equivalent in the same way. If the wheel Hessian is positive semidefinite,

\[
 h^TM_\eta h
 \ge \cos\alpha\,\lambda_2(L_9)\|h\|^2>0
 \quad(h\ne0).
 \tag{21}
\]

This gives a local statement about the full stationary equations, not

in j. At a positive-semidefinite stationary carrier, (21) makes the
derivative of these projected equations with respect to the residuals
invertible. The implicit function theorem makes the zero residual their
unique nearby solution. Every nearby full stationary state is therefore
also a carrier, including when the wheel Hessian itself is degenerate.
The form is zero at every equilibrium on the zero-form-mean leaf because
\(L_\eta\) is connected for eta>0.

In particular, the carrier subset is both relatively open and closed
inside the stationary positive-semidefinite locus. Closedness is the
closed circular identity (18); openness is the preceding normal
uniqueness. Any connected subset of that locus meeting a carrier remains
carrier. This does not exclude other disconnected, nonseparable
stationary sets.

A marked rim cut cannot provide a positive-semidefinite escape from this
carrier locus. If rim neighbors a and b differ by pi, use the wheel
vector \(e_a-e_b\), replicated in every slot. Its internal differences
vanish, while its two spoke terms cancel because the spoke cosines are
opposite. The rim edge a--b contributes -4; the other two incident rim
edges contribute at most 2 together. Hence

\[
 (e_a-e_b)^TM_{\mathrm w,\eta}(e_a-e_b)\le-2,
 \qquad h^TM_\eta h\le-18.
 \tag{22}
\]

This bound holds for every eta>=0 and does not require stationarity.
The carrier's internal gaps remain alpha. Thus neither a marked cut nor
a fine-coordinate bifurcation permits stationary positive-semidefinite
continuation from the carrier to another organization.

### 6.2 The reflected stationary equations have an exact lower branch

Ground the donor origin at zero and take the rim origins, in their
original cyclic order, to be

\[
 (\Phi_0,\Phi_1,\Phi_2,\Phi_3,\Phi_4)
       =(0,u,v,-v,-u),\qquad
 0<u<\pi/2<v<\pi.
 \tag{23}
\]

Set \(a=1+\eta\), \(c=\cos v\), and
\(r=|a+e^{iv}|\). The potential restricted to (23) is

\[
 \mathcal V_\eta(u,v)
 =2a(1-\cos u)+2[1-\cos(v-u)]
       +1-\cos(2v)+2(a-1)(1-\cos v).
 \tag{24}
\]

The u equation is \(\sin(v-u)=a\sin u\). Its locally minimizing
solution is \(u=\arg(a+e^{iv})\), so

\[
 A:=\cos u=\frac{a+c}{r},\qquad
 \sin u=\frac{\sin v}{r},\qquad
 B_1:=\cos(v-u)=\frac{1+ac}{r}.
 \tag{25}
\]

The remaining stationary equation and the defining identity for r give

\[
 \frac a r+2c+a-1=0,\qquad
 c=-\frac{a/r+a-1}{2},\qquad
 f(r,a):=r^3-(a+1)r+a^2=0.
 \tag{26}
\]

Reflection makes the fixed rim and donor equations vanish; the two
remaining equations occur in opposite pairs. These are consequently
the full wheel stationary equations, not merely a constrained critical
point condition.

Let \(a_*>1\) be the unique root

\[
 27a_*^4=4(a_*+1)^3,\qquad
 \frac{107}{100}<a_*<\frac{27}{25},\qquad
 \eta_*=a_*-1\in\left(\frac7{100},\frac2{25}\right).
 \tag{27}
\]

Uniqueness follows since \(a^4/(a+1)^3\) is strictly increasing for
a>0. Substitution of the two rational endpoints gives opposite signs.
For \(1<a<a_*\), f has two positive roots. Use the smaller one,
denoted \(r_-(a)\). It continues from
\(r_-(1)=(\sqrt5-1)/2\), where (23) is the uniform outer twist.
At the endpoint the two roots meet at
\(r_*=\sqrt{(a_*+1)/3}\). On the lower branch

\[
 \frac35<r_-(a)<\sqrt{\frac{a+1}{3}}<1,\qquad
 \frac{dr_-}{da}=\frac{2a-r_-}{a+1-3r_-^2}>0.
 \tag{28}
\]

The cubic's positive critical point is a strict local minimum; its value
is negative exactly before (27), which proves the two-root assertion.
The formulas above also give \(-29/30<c<0\). Thus (25) has
\(0<u<\pi/2<v<\pi\), with u<v. The five positive principal rim gaps are
\(u,v-u,2\pi-2v,v-u,u\). They stay strictly below pi and sum to
2*pi, including at the fold. All six internal windings, all nine rim
windings and all 32 mixed host periods remain those of the original
marked class. For the original closing-edge branch convention, use
the compatible rim lifts \((0,u,v,2\pi-v,2\pi-u)\), with the fixed
2*pi addition on the edge from child four to child zero. The large
\(2\pi-2v\) gap is between the two reflected v origins. A common
rotation matches any declared conserved phase mean.

### 6.3 Full relative stability up to the endpoint

Grounding the donor removes the common phase rotation. The wheel
Hessian splits into ordinary reflection-odd and reflection-even
directions. For the odd vector
\((0,p,q,-q,-p;0)\), write its quadratic form as
\(2(q,p)O(q,p)^T\). For the even vector
\((t,p,q,q,p;0)\), write it as
\(2(q,p,t)E(q,p,t)^T\). On the stationary branch,

\[
 \begin{split}
 O&=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 B_1+2(2c^2-1)+(a-1)c&-B_1\\
 -B_1&r
 \end{pmatrix},\\
 E&=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 B_1+(a-1)c&-B_1&0\\
 -B_1&r&-A\\
 0&-A&A+(a-1)/2
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{29}
\]

The identity \(B_1+aA=r\) supplies the middle diagonal. Substitution
of (25)--(26), retaining the cubic rather than an approximate root,
gives

\[
 \det O=\frac{(1-c^2)(a+1-3r^2)}r,\qquad
 \det E=\frac{(a-1)(r^2+r-1)^4}{8r^4}.
 \tag{30}
\]

At a=1, E is the positive multiple \(\cos(2\pi/5)\) of the
three-vertex path Laplacian. It has two positive eigenvalues and one
zero eigenvalue. Its determinant is strictly positive for every
1<a<=a_* because r increases from the positive zero of
\(r^2+r-1\). Continuity and absence of a zero eigenvalue make E
positive definite throughout this interval. The lower diagonal of O
is r>0. Its determinant is positive before the fold, and zero at the
fold, where O has exactly one zero eigenvalue and one positive one.

Equations (20)--(21) therefore prove strict positivity of all 53
relative full phase directions for \(0<\eta<\eta_*\). This gives a
locally asymptotically stable equilibrium of the actual two-row law:
the strict local storage minimum traps a sufficiently small
neighborhood, and section 4 supplies convergence to the unique local
equilibrium. All forms and all fine phase directions are included.
No source convergence to this particular equilibrium or uniform
attraction rate is inferred.

At eta=eta_* the full relative Hessian has exactly one zero direction,
which is collective and reflection-odd. Its other 52 eigenvalues are
positive; the 48 fine directions retain (21).

### 6.4 The fold is unstable and has no nearby stationary bypass

Eliminate u using (25), and call the resulting potential
\(\widehat{\mathcal V}_a(v)\). Its first derivative is

\[
 \widehat{\mathcal V}_a'(v)
   =2\sin v\left(\frac a r+2\cos v+a-1\right).
 \tag{31}
\]

At (27), its first and second derivatives vanish, whereas

\[
 \widehat{\mathcal V}_{a_*}'''(v_*)
       =\frac{6a_*^3\sin^3v_*}{r_*^5}>0.
 \tag{32}
\]

For example, differentiating the bracket in (31) gives
\(\sin v(a^2/r^3-2)\); at the double root \(a^2=2r^3\).
One further derivative gives (32). The full potential is nine times
this wheel potential plus a constant. The nonzero cubic term supplies
arbitrarily close zero-form states of strictly lower storage, so the
fold is not a local minimum.

It is also Lyapunov unstable, without a center-manifold assumption.
The positive fine block in (21) first makes every nearby equilibrium
a carrier. The positive E block in (29) then forces every nearby wheel
equilibrium into (23): phase inversion combined with rim reflection
preserves the potential, and uniqueness in the transverse implicit
equations sets the reflection-breaking coordinates to zero. The first
balance has u derivative \(a\cos u+\cos(v-u)=r>0\), so its minimizing
elimination is locally unique. Also
\(dr/dv=-a\sin v/r\ne0\), and the bracket in (31) is exactly
\(f(r,a)/(ar)\). Thus (26) is locally equivalent to the full remaining
stationary equation. At the fold
\(f_r=0\), \(f_{rr}=6r_*>0\), and \(f_a=2a_*-r_*>0\).
Thus at fixed eta_* the equilibrium is locally isolated modulo
rotation; on the eta>eta_* side there is no nearby full equilibrium.
If a trajectory from one of the nearby lower-storage states remained
forever in a sufficiently small closed neighborhood, section 4 would
force convergence to this isolated equilibrium, contradicting
nonincrease of storage. Arbitrarily small perturbations therefore
leave that neighborhood.

These same implicit equations give the complete local stationary fold,
including all 54 nodes: two reflected carrier equilibria meet, the
lower-root branch has positive relative Hessian, and the other local
branch has one negative collective direction. No nearby fine or
reflection-breaking stationary branch continues stability through the
endpoint. This is a local classification of this branch, not a global
classification of the remaining nonseparable sector.

The endpoint occurs without a marked winding cut. It also lies beyond
the original acute rim chart. Indeed \(r_*>83/100\) and \(a_*<27/25\)
give
\[
 \frac{a_*}{r_*}+a_*-1
 <\frac{108}{83}+\frac2{25}<\frac75<\sqrt2.
 \tag{33}
\]
Hence \(c_*>-1/\sqrt2\), so the rim gap between v and -v is
\(2\pi-2v_*>\pi/2\), while remaining less than pi. Stability persists
through some nonacute rim configurations and then fails collectively;
leaving the acute energy chart is not itself loss of marked identity
or loss of stability.

### 6.5 The source energy range has five minima and five saddles

The source limit can now be classified without selecting its destination.
Fix \(0<\eta\le1/100000\), and consider a wheel equilibrium with the
original acute winding-one rim and

\[
 V_{\rm rim}-V_5<\frac1{750}.
 \tag{33a}
\]

Every actual limit from section 5 satisfies (33a): its internal profile
is the exact carrier, its forms vanish, and its full excess storage is
less than 3/250. Its nine identical rim contributions therefore give
\(9(V_{\rm rim}-V_5)<3/250\), since the spoke storage is nonnegative.

**Finite-interval classification.** Modulo common phase rotation there
are exactly ten labeled wheel equilibria satisfying these premises. Five are
strict relative minima and five have exactly one negative relative
phase direction. Their full 54-node carriers have, respectively, 53
positive relative phase directions, or 52 positive directions and one
negative direction. The former are locally asymptotically stable for
the actual complete law; the latter are hyperbolic saddles with one
unstable direction. No exceptional zero eigenvalue occurs in this
positive eta interval.

The proof needs the small collective restoring term rather than an
uncontrolled first-order approximation. Ground the donor at zero and
write compatible rim lifts as

\[
 \Phi_c=t+\frac{2\pi(c-2)}5+u_c,
 \qquad \sum_cu_c=0.
 \tag{33b}

Here t is the mean host origin relative to the donor; the centered
uniform rim profile has mean zero. Put \(b=2\pi/5\), \(c_b=\cos b\),
\(s_b=\sin b\), let D be the forward rim incidence, and set
\(A=c_bD^TD\), \(G=A^+\). The projected host stationary equations are

\[
 u=-G\{D^TN(Du)+\eta\sin(t+b(c-2)+u_c)_{c=0}^4\},
 \quad N(d)=\sin(b+d)-\sin b-c_bd.
 \tag{33c}
\]

The sine on the right is a five-vector. G already removes its mean.
A row of G is the cyclic permutation of
\((2/5,0,-1/5,-1/5,0)/c_b\). Consequently

\[
 \begin{split}
 \|G\|_\infty&=\frac4{5c_b}<\frac{259}{100},&
 \|GD^T\|_\infty&=\frac6{5c_b}<\frac{389}{100},\\
 \lambda_1^{-1}&<\frac{47}{20},&

 \qquad \lambda_1=c_b(2-2c_b).
 \end{split}
 \tag{33d}
\]

These bounds use the exact \(c_b=(\sqrt5-1)/4\). They give a uniform
complex-eta construction with real t. Take
\(R=3/200\), \(r_0=1/100\), and center (33c) at
\(u^{(1)}_c=-\eta\sin(t+b(c-2))/\lambda_1\). On \(|\eta|\le R\),
\(\|u^{(1)}\|_\infty\le U_1=141/4000\) and
\(\|Du^{(1)}\|_\infty\le D_1=207/5000\). The complex ball
\(\|u-u^{(1)}\|_\infty\le r_0\) therefore satisfies
\(\|u\|_\infty<U_0=23/500\), \(\|Du\|_\infty<D_0=31/500\).
The fixed point is constrained to the complex zero-mean subspace.

Here are explicit rational bounds for contraction and existence. Define

\[
 C_h(z)=\frac{z^2}{2(1-z^2/12)},\quad
 S_h(z)=\frac z{1-z^2/6},\quad
 S_r(z)=\frac{z^3}{6(1-z^2/20)}.
\]

On the positive arguments used here these bound, respectively,
\(\cosh z-1\), \(\sinh z\), and \(\sinh z-z\), by their convergent
power series. Also \(e^z\le1/(1-z)\). With
\(g_0=259/100\), \(g_1=389/100\), \(c_0=31/100>c_b\), and
\(s_0=24/25>s_b\), direct rational arithmetic gives

\[
 \begin{split}
 Q_0&=2g_1[s_0S_h(D_0)+c_0C_h(D_0)]
                         +\frac{g_0R}{1-U_0}<\frac{13}{25},\\
 B_0&=g_1[s_0C_h(D_1)+c_0S_r(D_1)]
                         +\frac{g_0RU_1}{1-U_1}<\frac{47}{10000},\\
 B_0+\frac{13}{25}r_0&<r_0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{33e}
\]

The first line bounds the derivative of the map in (33c):
\(|\cos(b+d)-c_b|\le s_b\sinh|d|+c_b(\cosh|d|-1)\), and
\(\|D\|_\infty=2\). The second bounds its displacement at
\(u^{(1)}\), using the exact linearized equation for that center.
Thus the map contracts this ball into itself. Its unique solution
\(u(t,\eta)\) is holomorphic in eta on the disk, real for real eta,
and smooth in real t. It is a projected stationary shape, not yet a
stationary wheel: the remaining mean equation is

\[
 T(t,\eta)=\eta\sum_{c=0}^4
           \sin(t+b(c-2)+u_c(t,\eta))=0.
 \tag{33f}
\]

If \(W(t,\eta)=V_{\mathrm w,\eta}(t+b(c-2)+u_c;0)\), the projected
equations give \(\partial_tW=T\). The uniform complex bound is
\(|T|\le5R/(1-U_0)<2/25=:M_0\). Cyclic relabeling makes T periodic
with period 2*pi/5; phase inversion combined with rim reflection makes
it odd in t. Changing eta's sign is equivalent to replacing t by
t+pi. Finally, the coefficient of eta^n in T is a trigonometric
polynomial of degree at most n. This follows inductively from (33c):
the linear forcing has degree one, and multiplication in every Taylor
coefficient adds its harmonic degrees and perturbation orders together.
Only sine harmonics \(\sin(5kt)\) with \(5k\le n\) and parity
\(5k\equiv n\pmod2\) can occur. Orders below five and order six
therefore vanish exactly.

The coefficient at order five follows from the existing exact branch,
without assigning a numerical root. Put \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), and
\(\ell=(2,2,1)^T\) in the reflection-even coordinates (q,p,t) of

\[
 W_{tt}(0,\eta)=\frac{50\det E}{\ell^T\operatorname{adj}(E)\ell}.
 \tag{33g}
\]

After a cyclic rim relabeling, the reflected solution of section 6.2

\(\varphi^{-1}\), \(dr/d\eta=\varphi\), and

\(\det E=(25\varphi^8/8)\eta^5+O(\eta^6)\).

\(W_{tt}(0,\eta)=25\varphi^{10}\eta^5+O(\eta^6)\), which fixes the
only possible fifth-order torque coefficient:

\[
 T(t,\eta)=5\varphi^{10}\eta^5\sin(5t)+\text{higher orders}.
 \tag{33h}
\]

The remainder can be bounded relative to \(\sin(5t)\), including
arbitrarily near its zeros. Cauchy's coefficient estimate gives
\(\|T_n\|_\infty\le M_0/R^n\). Each sine Fourier coefficient of
\(T_n\) has magnitude at most \(2M_0/R^n\). Since
\(|\sin(5kt)|\le k|\sin(5t)|\), the quotient of the nth coefficient
is bounded by \(M_0m(m+1)/R^n\), where \(m=\lfloor n/5\rfloor\).
Use \(m(m+1)\le n(n+5)/25\) and put \(h=\eta/R\). Summing the
absolutely convergent series from order seven gives

\[
 \begin{split}
 \left|\frac{T(t,\eta)}{\eta^5\sin(5t)}-5\varphi^{10}\right|
 &\le \frac{M_0\eta^2}{25R^7}
             \frac{84-148h+66h^2}{(1-h)^3}<200,\\
 5\varphi^{10}&>600.
 \end{split}
 \tag{33i}
\]

The last strict bound is rational arithmetic at eta=1/100000; the
positive-coefficient series increases with eta. The quotient extends
continuously through every zero of sin(5t), by the same uniformly
convergent sine-polynomial quotients. Hence T has exactly the ten zeros
\(t=k\pi/5\), k=0,...,9, on the relative-origin circle. Its derivative
is strictly positive at even k and strictly negative at odd k. This is
a finite-interval conclusion, not just a leading-order prediction.

ball. Every acute winding-one C5 gap is positive: its other four gaps
are strictly below pi/2 and their sum is less than 2*pi. With one gap
fixed at s, Jensen bounds the rim cost below by
\(F_5(s)=1-\cos s+4[1-\cos((2\pi-s)/4)]\). This function increases
above b. Put \(C_8(y)=1-y^2/2+y^4/24-y^6/720+y^8/40320\).
The cosine upper bound on [0,3/2], \(\pi>157/50\), and

\[
 F_5(29/20)-V_5
 >\frac{1545}{1000}-C_8(29/20)
       -4C_8\!\left(\frac{2(157/50)-29/20}{4}\right)
 >\frac1{750}.
 \tag{33j}
\]

Thus every gap of a candidate satisfying (33a) is less than 29/20,
where \(\cos(29/20)>1/10\) follows from the sixth-order cosine lower
polynomial. The constructed shape also has all gaps in (0,29/20),
by its bound \(\|Du\|_\infty<D_0\). The straight compatible segment
from any candidate to the constructed shape with the same mean t stays
in that interval. On its zero-mean host directions the full wheel
potential has Hessian at least
\((1/10)L_5-\eta I>0\), since \(\lambda_2(L_5)>1\).
Two projected stationary shapes cannot occur on such a strictly convex
segment. Every admitted candidate must therefore be the constructed

\(V_{\rm rim}-V_5\le10\|u\|_\infty^2<100\eta^2<1/750\).
The classification covers exactly the stated energy sector.

The four host shape directions are strictly positive. Their Schur
complement is \(W_{tt}\), so (33i) proves the asserted wheel inertia.

This identifies the possible destinations, including their stability

## 7. The all-origin source family cannot select only attractive equilibria

Fix any \(0<\eta\le1/100000\). Fix an actual admissible host and
donor source member: all twelve allowances, every primitive form and

donor recovery and sequential observation history remain fixed. Vary

### 7.1 The actual prepared circle persists at every finite time

Use the original [contact phase representation](ORGANIZED_EXTERNAL_REGISTRATION.md#1-prepared-family-sufficient-state-and-symmetry),
whose equation (2) reads

\[
 \theta_{H,c,j}(0;\beta)=\psi_j+H_{c,j},\qquad
 \theta_{D,j}(0;\beta)=\psi_j+\beta+z_{D,j}.
 \tag{34}
\]

Here \(H\) includes the reached host state and all observation backaction;
\(z_D\) is the actual recovered donor residual. Neither is replaced by
an ideal profile. Isolated donor rotation leaves its complete relative
preparation unchanged, so all forms and both residual arrays are
independent of beta. In particular the two endpoints beta=0 and beta=2*pi
have identical circular states. Their continuous lifts differ by
2*pi on the nine donor vertices and by zero on the host vertices.

The complete field (1) is unchanged by this integer vertex phase shift:
every sine is unchanged, and the phase row depends only on forms.
Uniqueness therefore preserves that same endpoint difference at every
finite time. Continuous dependence on prepared initial data makes

\[
 \begin{split}
 f_\tau(\beta)
  &=\theta_{D,0}(\tau;\beta)-\theta_{H,0,0}(\tau;\beta),\\
 f_\tau(2\pi)-f_\tau(0)&=2\pi
 \end{split}
 \tag{35}
\]

a continuous lifted family for each finite \(\tau\ge0\). The circular
relative phase \(e^{if_\tau(\beta)}\) consequently covers the entire
unit circle, with degree one. This follows directly from the endpoint
identity and the intermediate value theorem; no origin scan or limiting
interchange is used. In particular there is no common finite time at
which the entire family lies in a neighborhood restricting this relative
phase to a proper circular arc.

This statement is about a circle of separately prepared trajectories.
It does not say that any one trajectory keeps rotating: each continuous
phase lift converges by section 4. The joined lifted mean is beta/6 and
is conserved for each trajectory. To compare equilibria across beta,
work on the zero-total-form space with circular phases modulo a common
rotation. The projection in (35) is well-defined on that quotient. A
fixed lifted-mean chart describes the same local relative stability,
but there is no single fixed-mean lift convention turning (34) into an
ordinary closed loop of real coordinates.

### 7.2 Open attractive basins cannot cover this source circle

Suppose every beta in this prepared circle converged to a locally
asymptotically stable relative equilibrium. Each such equilibrium has
an open basin of attraction. Basins of distinct equilibria are disjoint,
because section 4 gives a unique limit. Their inverse images under the
continuous preparation map would form disjoint open sets covering the
connected beta circle. There can therefore be only one nonempty such
set: every preparation would converge to the same relative equilibrium
\(e_*\).

Choose a neighborhood U of \(e_*\) in which the relative phase used
in (35) lies in one proper circular arc. Local asymptotic stability
supplies a smaller neighborhood V whose forward trajectories remain
