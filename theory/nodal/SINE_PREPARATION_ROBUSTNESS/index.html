    Finite preparation families for the held normalized-sine law¶

    This chapter derives sufficient nonlinear error bounds for two preparations under the same complete held law. It connects the established connected/cut point comparison to an explicitly specified computational preparation family. The single registered global comparison is insufficient to certify that family; its actual robustness remains unresolved. The research program owns the current task and execution admission.

    1. Preparation origin and the finite family¶

    A saved binary64 coordinate denotes its exact represented value. Hexadecimal serialization preserves that value; it does not establish an unknown real predecessor, a measurement error distribution or a preparation tolerance. A neighborhood must therefore enter as an additional, explicit policy.

    For every represented form and registered real-phase coordinate f_j, let f_j^- and f_j^+ be its immediately preceding and following finite binary64 numbers. Canonicalize signed zero and require both neighbors to be finite. Define the adjacent-representable family and its closed real hull by

    \[ \mathcal F_{\rm adj}=\prod_{j=1}^{42}\{f_j^-,f_j,f_j^+\}, \qquad \mathcal H=\prod_{j=1}^{42}[f_j^-,f_j^+]. \]

    The family has exactly 3^42 distinct supplied initial vectors. Its full coordinate spacings are fixed by the representation before evaluating the response. This asks whether one representable adjacency can change the prediction. It supplies a computational-resolution robustness class, not a physical uncertainty model or a macroscopic preparation tolerance.

    The larger continuous hull is a sufficient proof domain. Its nonrepresentable interior points are mathematical preparations, not additional stored binary64 vectors. Exact real initial errors must remain present in the argument; replacing the hull by the nominal point would erase them. The full adjacent hull differs from the narrower nearest-even rounding cell. Neither replacing it by that cell nor selecting a smaller radius after a failed bound answers this fixed question.

    All 21 forms and 21 registered phase lifts vary independently. The identical family member initializes both complete branches. Hold capacities, conductances, both support choices, their actual degrees, the original clock, elapsed [0,1/2] and positive marked observation scale w fixed. Do not wrap phase endpoints or independently re-register connected components. Each member has its own conserved means under each branch's weights; no nominal mean constraint is imposed. In the cut branch the child's coordinates remain constant at that member's initial values, not at the nominal child's values.

    Fresh source admission re-admits the nominal center and its association with the retained records under existing evidence premises. It does not show that every perturbed member is produced by the same event sequence. These are supplied post-event states; uniform event admission, acquisition, formation and autonomous identity remain separate questions. Earlier hypothetical Coupling (UM) error budgets do not set these widths.

    For any coordinate, retain the exact asymmetric errors

    \[ e_j^- = f_j-f_j^-,\qquad e_j^+=f_j^+-f_j, \qquad r_j=\max(e_j^-,e_j^+). \]

    At binade boundaries these spacings need not agree. Zero has the two distinct neighbors of smallest positive and negative subnormal magnitude. They are valid nonzero represented coordinates; this is not the rejected admission of a nonzero real that underflows to zero. Finite extreme values lacking two finite neighbors are outside this declared family construction.

    2. A global nonlinear two-block comparison¶

    On a dynamic component with positive bare degrees, the complete law is

    \[ \dot x_i=\frac{\nu_i}{\pi d_i}\sum_{j\sim i}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i), \qquad \dot\theta_i=\frac{\nu_i}{\pi d_i}\sum_{j\sim i}W_{ij}(x_i-x_j). \]

    All coefficients are held, capacities are nonnegative, conductances are nonnegative, and the real lifts have fixed registration. The vector field is globally Lipschitz in these finite coordinates, so solutions exist uniquely on the whole finite window. Isolated constant rows are specified separately; no zero-degree division supplies their law.

    Compare any nominal and perturbed solutions of this same law. Set u(t)=max_i|tilde x_i-x_i| and v(t)=max_i|tilde theta_i-theta_i| over the dynamic component, and define

    \[ a=\frac{2}{\pi}\max_i\nu_i,\qquad b=\frac{2}{\pi}\max_i\frac{\nu_i\sum_{j\sim i}W_{ij}}{d_i}, \qquad M=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\b&0\end{pmatrix}. \]

    The global inequality |sin(alpha+eta)-sin(alpha)| <= |eta| gives |delta x_dot_i| <= 2 nu_i v/pi. The exactly linear phase row gives |delta theta_dot_i| <= 2 nu_i (sum_j W_ij) u/(pi d_i). Taking upper Dini derivatives of the finite maxima therefore yields

    \[ D^+u\leq av,\qquad D^+v\leq bu,\qquad \binom{u(t)}{v(t)}\leq e^{tM}\binom{u_0}{v_0}. \]

    Here u_0 and v_0 are the maxima of the exact coordinate radii in their respective blocks. The last inequality follows from the cooperative linear comparison theorem, or by integrating the two inequalities and iterating their nonnegative kernel. It applies uniformly to every member of the hull. It is a nonlinear difference bound: no small-angle substitution, tangent trajectory or assumed closeness domain enters its proof.

    Both comparison coordinates are nondecreasing because M and the initial allowances are nonnegative. Thus any upper endpoint pair (U,V) for the comparison at T also bounds the errors at every earlier time. This monotonic majorant supplies whole-window control; endpoint proximity of the actual trajectories alone would not do so.

    Use all connected nodes for that branch. For the cut branch use the complete 20-node host, with cut degrees and retained host conductances. Keep separate constant child allowances c_x and c_theta from the original family. They do not enter the isolated host's evolution, but they enter the marked reading. The two block maxima lose correlations and cannot exploit cancellation of fast oscillations; their conservatism is a property of this sufficient bound.

    3. Whole-window portrait and signed-angle gates¶

    In the original oriented plane use y=(theta_child-theta_parent, sqrt(w)*(x_child-x_parent)). If (U_c,V_c) and (U_0,V_0) are the connected and cut-host comparison endpoints, respectively, then uniform squared portrait errors are bounded by

    \[ E_c^2=4(V_c^2+wU_c^2),\qquad E_0^2=(V_0+c_\theta)^2+w(U_0+c_x)^2. \]

    These combine the two coordinate blocks only through the declared observation metric; they do not identify physical units. In particular, freezing the cut child does not remove its preparation error.

    Let q_c,q_0 be positive lower bounds on the nominal squared marked radii over the entire original window. The sufficient admission is

    \[ E_c^2<q_c,\qquad E_0^2<q_0. \]

    Every perturbed portrait then stays nonzero, with radius at least sqrt(q_b)-E_b > 0. The straight homotopy from each nominal portrait stays nonzero as well. The common-preparation identity cancels the two initial angular errors and bounds the error in the difference of clockwise increments by

    \[ \arcsin(E_c/\sqrt{q_c})+\arcsin(E_0/\sqrt{q_0})<\pi. \]

    Consequently a nominal directional gap strictly larger than pi preserves its ordering for the whole family whenever both squared-radius gates pass. The retained point gap has that property. A consumer must still rebuild and admit that gap and both radius minima from associated evidence. Checking gap_lower > pi_upper permits this sufficient test without adding another inverse-trigonometric numerical kernel. It proves a strict ordering, not the sharpest perturbation margin or constant local rotation direction.

    Failure of either sufficient inequality leaves robustness unresolved. It does not produce a counterexample, an actual radius crossing or loss of identity. Arithmetic evidence for a bound and evidence that its premises match the intended source remain distinct obligations.

    4. Numerical method, alternatives and evidence boundary¶

    The pure preparation arithmetic owner uses the existing binary64 neighbor geometry and exact scalar admission. It retains asymmetric widths as rational values before invoking the shared comparison-flow kernel. Constructed controls exercise representation boundaries, exact limiting flows, comparison bounds and the isolated child's observation contribution. These helpers establish conditional arithmetic, not a historical source or a family verdict.

    For each branch, rational upper coefficients a_bar,b_bar give a nonnegative comparison matrix. The shared kernel encloses its exponential action using a series through degree 32, a norm-tail bound and outward dyadic128 arithmetic. A fixed 64-step composition with h=1/128 covers T=1/2 and uses a two-dimensional matrix, within the existing dimension policy. Monotonicity permits a rounded upper endpoint to initialize the next comparison step. Its applicability condition is h max(a_bar,b_bar) <= 1; increasing an exact coefficient to its outward upper endpoint preserves the differential comparison.

    The static preflight reconstructed capacities, weight sums and actual degrees from the already known, receipt-matched nominal result. Both branches satisfy that kernel condition on this fixed grid. This entails 128 comparison calls, without sampling the 3^42 preparations or integrating another trajectory. No family widths, propagated errors or sufficient-gate verdict were evaluated by that coefficient check. It was not a fresh admission of the source history.

    The prior coefficient scales make substantial amplification possible in this majorant. Cheap arithmetic therefore does not promise an informative bound. The main alternatives have different additional premises:

    Method Required extra information and limitation
    One scalar global Lipschitz bound Simpler, but loses the distinct form/phase rates and cannot improve this block comparison without a justified norm change.
    Global two-block comparison above No trajectory-dependent domain or Taylor remainder; may overestimate growth by discarding oscillatory cancellation and graph structure.
    Graph-resolved component comparison Retains neighbor structure; requires an admitted dimension/work policy or a separately justified block reduction. It must not silently bypass the shared 24-coordinate policy with a 42-coordinate matrix.
    Signed Jacobian or energy bounds on retained cells Can retain useful cancellation only with a proved norm/energy estimate and a domain containing the straight segments between all nominal and perturbed states. Conserved energy alone does not control their separation.
    Validated variational flow with nonlinear remainder A tangent calculation needs a uniform remainder and a self-consistent inflated tube for the full family; the old nominal tubes do not supply those premises automatically.

    The global bound is the first inexpensive, falsifiable sufficiency test. A failure may motivate a separately admitted sharper method for the unchanged family; it authorizes neither a width search nor an automatic solver campaign. The old connected derivative and remainder premises remain conditional on their frozen producer. A new comparison must verify both saved results and their retained byte associations, rebuild their observation arithmetic and match fresh nominal primitives; reading cached success flags is insufficient. Host and child uncertainty must not pass through an adapter that reinstates the exact nominal child.

    6. Exact relative state and conserved preparation offsets¶

    The failed global comparison motivates a different error description, not a change of preparation. The following results apply to each complete dynamic component separately. Require positive held capacities and connected positive conductance support, in addition to connected bare support. Zero conductances are allowed only if the remaining positive conductances connect the component. Bare connectivity alone would not make the form quadratic coercive. The isolated cut child is outside this dynamic component and retains both constant coordinates. No inverse mobility is assigned to it.

    Choose an oriented incidence matrix B with edge column b_e=e_i-e_j. Define

    \[ L_w=B\operatorname{diag}(W_e)B^T,\quad L_1=BB^T,\quad D_\nu=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_i/d_i),\quad \rho_i=d_i/\nu_i,\quad \alpha=\rho/(\mathbf1^T\rho). \]

    In the original structural clock, the supplied law is exactly

    \[ \dot x=-\pi^{-1}D_\nu B\sin(B^T\theta),\qquad \dot\theta=\pi^{-1}D_\nu L_wx. \]

    Since alpha^T D_nu is a constant row, both alpha-weighted means are conserved. For two preparations evolving under this same law, let u=tilde x-x and v=tilde theta-theta. Their mean errors m_x=alpha^T u and m_theta=alpha^T v are constant. The projection P=Id-1 alpha^T removes only these origins: u=P u+1 m_x and v=P v+1 m_theta. This is an exact coordinate decomposition, not a restriction that the perturbed preparation has the nominal means. The relative coordinates have dimension 2(n-1); retaining both means recovers the full error. The connected component therefore still has 40 relative coordinates, and the cut host 38. This does not put either into a 24-dimensional full-state validator or establish a smaller autonomous parent-child state.

    For phi=B^T theta and eta=B^T v, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives

    \[ \sin(\phi_e+\eta_e)-\sin\phi_e=c_e\eta_e,\qquad c_e=\int_0^1\cos(\phi_e+s\eta_e)\,ds. \]

    Consequently, with C=B diag(c_e) B^T, the exact finite-error rows are

    \[ \dot u=-\pi^{-1}D_\nu C v,\qquad \dot v=\pi^{-1}D_\nu L_wu. \tag{6.1} \]

    The same equations hold for P u and P v. C depends on both complete solutions through every intervening phase segment; it is not merely the Jacobian of the nominal trajectory. A tangent bound cannot replace that segment premise.

    7. A positive graph metric with signed restoring cancellation¶

    Let C_0 be a fixed symmetric positive quadratic on relative phase coordinates, with kernel exactly span{1}. The concrete candidate below chooses C_0=L_1, independently of the retained phase values. Define the squared relative norm

    \[ \mathcal E=u^TL_wu+v^TC_0v=X+Y,\qquad R=\sqrt{\mathcal E}. \]

    It controls all relative form and phase coordinates; the two conserved origins are retained separately. It is an auxiliary error metric under this supplied law, not a new structural field or a conserved physical energy. Different declared form/phase scales would change the metric and require their own bounds.

    Differentiating with (6.1) cancels the large reciprocal terms exactly:

    \[ \dot{\mathcal E} =\frac2\pi u^TL_wD_\nu(C_0-C)v. \tag{7.1} \]

    This extends the directional metric cancellation to finite preparation errors on the full held component. That earlier example's coefficients, domain and reduction do not transfer. If

    \[ |u^TL_wD_\nu(C_0-C)v|\le k\sqrt{XY}, \]

    then 2 sqrt(XY)<=X+Y gives

    \[ |\dot{\mathcal E}|\le(k/\pi)\mathcal E,\qquad D^+R\le\gamma R,\quad \gamma=k/(2\pi). \tag{7.2} \]

    No positivity of the actual signed phase Hessian is needed for this metric. Negative secant cosines enter C with their signs. They can increase the growth bound, but do not invalidate positivity of the chosen error norm. At zero error, uniqueness preserves zero; the norm inequality extends by continuity.

    A certificate using component-sized matrices¶

    For any positive relative quadratic A define its invertible completion

    \[ Q_A=A+\rho\rho^T/(\mathbf1^T\rho). \]

    This is positive definite, and z^T Q_A z=z^T A z on rho^T z=0. The added term has zero derivative along either complete error row. Let Q_theta=Q_C0 and F=D_nu L_w D_nu. Suppose each secant coefficient has an admitted interval [c_e^-,c_e^+], with midpoint m_e and radius r_e. Set

    \[ D_m=C_0-B\operatorname{diag}(m_e)B^T,\qquad s^2=\operatorname{tr}(Q_\theta^{-1}D_m F D_m), \]
    \[ a_e=b_e^TFb_e,\qquad q_e=b_e^TQ_\theta^{-1}b_e,\qquad k_{\rm trace}=\sqrt{s^2}+\sum_e r_e\sqrt{a_eq_e}. \tag{7.3} \]

    The trace is nonnegative: it is the squared Frobenius norm of L_w^(1/2) D_nu D_m Q_theta^(-1/2). Its operator norm is at most that Frobenius norm. Each interval residual has the rank-one form epsilon_e L_w^(1/2) D_nu b_e b_e^T Q_theta^(-1/2), whose norm is at most r_e sqrt(a_e q_e). The triangle inequality proves (7.2) with k_trace. These square roots are proof notation; the numerical recipe uses the shared outward square-root owner. No irrational matrix square root is computed. Apply the norm estimate to P u and P v: the difference matrices annihilate the common origins and Q_theta equals C_0 on that centered slice. The entire midpoint matrix is retained before the norm estimate. Correlations lost by interval radii and the Frobenius bound weaken, rather than strengthen, this sufficient certificate.

    One could instead prove D_m F D_m <= s^2 Q_theta by a supplied exact matrix inequality. The trace recipe fixes s^2 without a rate search or fitted metric. Both recipes use n-by-n component matrices, not a 2n-dimensional LMI. This algebra does not change the state dimension or bypass the full-state validator's policy.

    For the concrete choice C_0=L_1, a cheap second bound is available. Put

    \[ a_0=2\max_i\nu_i,\quad b_0=2\max_i\frac{\nu_i\sum_{j\sim i}W_{ij}}{d_i},\quad \Delta=\max_e\sup_{c\in[c_e^-,c_e^+]}|1-c|. \]

    For intervals admitted within [-1,1], 0<=Delta<=2. The degree bounds imply ||D_nu^(1/2)L_1 D_nu^(1/2)||<=a_0 and the corresponding weighted bound <=b_0. Also 0<=L_1-C<=Delta L_1. Factoring on the relative subspace proves

    \[ k_{\rm degree}=\Delta\sqrt{a_0b_0},\qquad k=\min(k_{\rm trace},k_{\rm degree}). \tag{7.4} \]

    Taking the minimum intersects two proved upper bounds; it is not a source parameter search. At the global Delta=2 it has the same exponential rate sqrt(a b) as section 2's two-block comparison. An informative improvement therefore needs admitted phase information or useful graph cancellation; merely renaming the norm does not guarantee one.

    8. Relative storage: stronger premises and a useful distinction¶

    The moving-pattern storage identity has a general complete-law form. Its Bregman remainder is

    \[ \mathcal D=\frac12u^TL_wu+ \sum_e[\cos\phi_e-\cos(\phi_e+\eta_e)-\sin\phi_e\,\eta_e]. \]

    Differentiating along both solutions cancels the form/phase exchange and yields

    \[ \dot{\mathcal D}=\sum_e\dot\phi_e [\sin(\phi_e+\eta_e)-\sin\phi_e-\cos\phi_e\,\eta_e]. \tag{8.1} \]

    This is generally neither nonnegative nor a distance metric. Suppose a whole-segment domain supplies a fixed K, positive on relative coordinates with kernel span{1}, such that

    \[ B\operatorname{diag}\cos(\phi+s\eta)B^T\succeq K \quad(0\le s\le1). \]

    Taylor's integral remainder then gives 2D>=u^T L_w u+v^T K v. If the nominal edge rates additionally satisfy B diag(|phi_dot|) B^T <= lambda K, the sine remainder bounded by eta_e^2/2 gives |D_dot|<=lambda D. Thus D(t)<=D(0) exp(integral lambda) while the domain holds. The nominal rates and that domain require whole-time evidence; energy conservation alone supplies neither.

    Graph coercivity is weaker than requiring every edge to be acute. A constructed triangle with phases (a,0,-a), cos(a)=2/3, has cosine weights (2/3,2/3,-1/9). Its two nonzero Hessian eigenvalues are 2 and 4/9, so L_cos>=(4/27)L_1 despite one negative edge. If every segment edge differs from this reference by at most 2/27 radians, cosine's Lipschitz bound gives L_segment>=(2/27)L_1. This is a compatible mathematical domain example, not an admission of the retained preparation.

    A bridge cannot receive this cycle compensation. If its actual cosine is nonpositive, a vector constant on either side makes the signed Hessian quadratic nonpositive. No positive relative lower bound K can then hold at that state. This excludes that coercive-storage recipe, not the positive metric of section 7 or the actual robustness of a prepared system.

    The two-node pi-gap equilibrium gives a separate global obstruction. With equal mobility m=nu/(pi d) and conductance w>0, the relative linearization has eigenvalues +/-2m sqrt(w). Every fixed positive quadratic norm has a logarithmic growth bound at least 2m sqrt(w) at that equilibrium. Its phase Bregman remainder is cos(eta)-1<0 near zero. A global nonexpansion claim is therefore false for this law. At a zero nominal gap, a registered 2pi edge-lift error even has zero phase remainder; small D without a chart cannot control real lifts.

    None of these witnesses describes the retained source. A full clockwise turn of the original portrait also does not imply a nonacute bridge: the ordinary two-node conservative oval with energy between zero and one turns while remaining inside |delta|<pi/2. A turn count cannot replace a phase-domain check.

    15.2 A uniform bound in the existing graph metric¶

    Use the unchanged quadratic E=u^T L_w u+v^T L_1 v and R=sqrt(E). Its exact derivative is

    \[ \dot E=\frac2\pi u^TL_w\widetilde D(L_1-C)v +2u^TL_w a+2v^TL_1b. \tag{15.3} \]

    The nominal inverse completion Q_theta from section 7 can still bound instantaneous relative coordinates: subtract their current nominal-weighted mean before applying the quadratic inequality.     \[ \delta_i=\max\{\nu_i^0-\nu_i^-,\nu_i^+-\nu_i^0\}/d_i,\qquad \eta=\max_i\frac{d_i\delta_i}{\nu_i^0}. \tag{15.4} \]

    \[ k_* = k_{\rm nom}+\eta k_{\rm deg},\qquad |u^TL_w\widetilde D Mv| \le k_*\sqrt{u^TL_wu}\sqrt{v^TL_1v}. \tag{15.5} \]

    To prove the additional term, factor DeltaD as D_0^(1/2) H D_0^(1/2), where H is diagonal with norm at most eta. The norm of L_w^(1/2) D_0^(1/2) is at most sqrt(b_0).     \[ J^2=\sum_{\{i,j\}}W_{ij}(\delta_iS_i+\delta_jS_j)^2 +\sum_{\{i,j\}}(\delta_iQ_i+\delta_jQ_j)^2. \tag{15.6} \]
