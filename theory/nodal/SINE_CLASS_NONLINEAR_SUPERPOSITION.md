\tag{1}
\]

\(\mathcal R\Theta_k=-\Theta_k\). The complete law in deviations

\[
\mathcal T(x,y)=(-\mathcal R x,-\mathcal R y).
\tag{3}
\]
The ideal target and the nominal formation source are fixed by this
transformation; the latter has form proportional to \(j-4\) and flat

This does not make the complete state reflection even. Quadratic

\tag{4}
\]

## F4: a finite symmetry-aware bound

Use a tight outward bound \(\gamma\le g<1/3000\), and set
\[
D_T=1-2g^2T^2,\qquad \ell_T=1-3T,\qquad
C_T=\frac{8g^4T^4}{3D_T^3\ell_T}
 \left(1+\frac{2g^2T^2}{3\ell_T}\right).
\tag{6}
\]
These denominators are positive on the admitted time domain. Consider an
ideal-target history with absolute pulse budget \(A_*=|a|+|b|\),
or the corresponding smaller single-input budget. Diffusion contraction
and the global sine bound give
\[
\|x(t)\|_\infty\le A_*/D_T,\qquad
Y(t):=\|y(t)\|_\infty\le2gA_*t/D_T.
\tag{7}
\]
The full tangent generator \(J_k\) commutes with \(\mathcal R\)
and has maximum row norm less than three. Its exact nonlinear form
residual \(n(y)\) obeys \(\|n(y)\|_\infty\le2gY^2\).
All jumps are reflection even, so integration of its odd part gives
\[
\|x_{\rm odd}(t)\|_\infty
 \le\frac{8g^3A_*^2t^3}{3D_T^2\ell_T},\qquad
\|y_{\rm odd}(t)\|_\infty
 \le\frac{4g^4A_*^2t^4}{3D_T^2\ell_T}.
\tag{8}
\]
At reflection-even phase deviation, the even nonlinear residual contains
only the cosine-weighted \(\sin d-d\) remainder: the base-sine
quadratic part is odd and cancels in that projection. Thus
\[
\|n_{\rm even}(y)\|_\infty
 \le\frac43gY^3+4gY\|y_{\rm odd}\|_\infty.
\tag{9}
\]
The second term follows from \(\|Dn\|_\infty\le4gY\) between
the even projection and the full phase. Integrating (9), using (7)-(8)
and the tangent bound \(1/\ell_T\), proves
\[
|R_{\rm ideal,nonlinear}(T)-R_{\rm ideal,tangent}(T)|
 \le C_T A_*^3.
\tag{10}
\]
This derivation retains the odd internal modes rather than incorrectly
declaring an even nonlinear invariant subspace.

The full nonlinear and tangent fields each have maximum-norm Lipschitz
bound three in the original \((x,y)\) coordinates. Their respective
actual-to-ideal comparisons are at most \(\epsilon/\ell_T\),
with the same jumps; no phase error is divided by \(\gamma\).
Two consequences are useful:
\[
|M_{\rm actual}|\le U_M:=
C_T[(|a|+|b|)^3+|a|^3+|b|^3]
 +\frac{4\epsilon}{\ell_T},
\tag{11}
\]
and, separately for each history \(j\),
\[
|R_{j,\rm actual,nonlinear}-R_{j,\rm actual,tangent}|
 \le U_j:=C_T A_j^3+\frac{2\epsilon}{\ell_T},
\quad (A_{00},A_{10},A_{01},A_{11})=(0,|a|,|b|,|a|+|b|).
\tag{12}
\]
The tangent in (12) starts from the same actual full source and retains
its hidden initialization. The ideal references are proof comparisons,

equation (12) instead compares each of the four individual endpoints.

## Scalar cancellation and four-endpoint record overlap

If \(U_M\le4\delta\), every admitted true mixed value can be
recorded as zero. In the order \((11,10,01,00)\), choose errors
\((-M/4,M/4,M/4,-M/4)\). Each obeys the stated scalar budget,

cannot guarantee a nonzero recorded mixed statistic under every allowed
error. Favorable realized errors can still reveal a nonzero value.

The stronger sufficient condition
\[
\max_j U_j\le\delta
\tag{13}
\]
proves intersection of the complete four-endpoint record sets. Choose the
common record vector to be the four same-source tangent endpoints. The
tangent model uses zero recording errors, while the nonlinear model uses
the endpoint differences bounded in (12). This is an existential common
record for every admitted actual source. It does not assert that every
allowed record is shared, or that the continuous trajectories, intermediate
measurements, phase observations or another protocol are indistinguishable.

Failure of either sufficient test alone is not a proof of discrimination.
A useful positive protocol needs a finite lower bound exceeding its
complete preparation and observation errors. The model's nonlinear
coefficient (5), the scalar cancellation test and the stronger endpoint
overlap test have separate logical roles.
