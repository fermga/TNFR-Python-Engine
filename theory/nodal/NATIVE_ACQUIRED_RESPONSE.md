## 3. Exact finite-kick jets at the critical references

[critical-point identity](RELATIONAL_RECOVERY_AND_INTERACTION.md#quotient-linearization-and-the-restoring-mechanism)

Define `b=e_0`, `c=e_5^T-e_7^T` and

\[
A=-D^{-1}B/2,\qquad C=-H^{-1}K/2,\qquad Q=H^{-1}B/2.
\tag{AR6}
\]

At a reference state after the **finite** kick, phase remains critical and
capacity remains uniformly one. Consequently `nudot=0`, `xdot=epsilon*A*b`
and `thetadot=epsilon*Q*b`. The form row is affine in form and depends on

\[
R(0)=0,\qquad R'(0)=\epsilon cAb,\qquad
R''(0)=\epsilon c(A^2+CQ)b. \tag{AR7}
\]

This establishes the finite-kick identity separately from the tangent
matrix product. It makes no assertion that higher derivatives or finite-time
nonlinear flow equal a tangent solution. At reached states, capacity and
`D(H^-1)` contributions remain in the full flow used below.

For a direct check, `Bb` has entries `3,-1,-1,-1` at nodes `0,1,4,5` and
zero elsewhere. Hence `cAb=1/6` and `cA^2b=-1/6`. Put `v=H^-1*Bb`.
Only `v_0=3/(pi*h)` and `v_5=-1/(pi*h)` contribute to row five of `Kv`;
rows six and nine of `v` vanish. Thus `(Kv)_5=-(h+3)/(pi*h)`, while
`(Kv)_7=0`. It follows that

\[
cCQb=\frac{h+3}{4\pi^2h^2}. \tag{AR8}
\]

The complete reference results are

| Reference | `R'(0)/epsilon` | `R''(0)/epsilon` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consensus | `1/6` | `-1/6+1/(6*pi^2)` |

In particular,

\[
R''_{\rm tw}(0)-R''_{\rm con}(0)=\epsilon a,\qquad
a=\frac{10-3\sqrt5}{12\pi^2}>0. \tag{AR9}
\]

The exact symbolic evaluator independently returns

### A regular full-state tube and derivative bounds

reference, with `N_max=1+r`. Write `C_i+i S_i=sum_j exp(i(theta_j-theta_i))`.

\[
C_i>\min(3/5-4r,8/5-6r)>1/2. \tag{AR16}
\]

Reuse the smooth positive-real representation in
[RB7](NATIVE_FOUNDATIONAL_ACCOUNT.md#native-selection-robustness):

\[
g_i=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{Im}\log(C_i+iS_i),\qquad
k_i=H_i^{-1}=\pi^{-1}\int_0^1\operatorname{Re}(C_i+i tS_i)^{-1}\,dt.
\tag{AR17}
\]

All derivative norms below are induced by the coordinate sup norm, with
independent unit directions for a bilinear derivative. For the resultant,
the first and second phase derivatives have magnitude at most `2*d_i<=6`
and `4*d_i<=12`. The real-linear map `z -> Re(z)+i*t*Im(z)` contracts complex
magnitude for `0<=t<=1`; its real part remains greater than `1/2`.
Differentiating the logarithm and inverse, using `pi>3`, gives

\[
\|Dg_i\|<4,\quad \|D^2g_i\|<56,\quad
0<k_i<2/3,\quad\|Dk_i\|<8,\quad\|D^2k_i\|<208. \tag{AR18}
\]

For example the second inverse bound is
`[2*6^2/(1/2)^3+12/(1/2)^2]/3=208`; the logarithm uses
`[12/(1/2)+6^2/(1/2)^2]/3=56`. These smooth formulas also cover `S_i=0`.

for `q=Bx`. The complete rows are
`F_x=nu*(-q/(2d)+g/2)`, `F_theta=nu*k*q/2`, `F_nu=-B*nu/2`.

\[

J_\theta\le2N_{\max}+24N_{\max}r+2r<4,\qquad J_\nu\le3.
\tag{AR19}

The full second derivative bounds, including mixed capacity directions, are

\[

For `M_theta`, these four terms are respectively the phase/phase,
