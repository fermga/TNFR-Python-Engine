## The retained component state and one-contact normalization

Use local reflection \(R:i\mapsto8-i\), fixing the central port
\(p=4\). For each channel, let \(S\) take the values or averages
on the five layers
\[
(4),\quad(3,5),\quad(2,6),\quad(1,7),\quad(0,8).
\]
Let \(T\) lift a five-vector by assigning its value to each node
in its layer. Then \(ST=I_5\) and \(TS=(I+R)/2\).
Both maps are contractions in the maximum norm. The reduced state
is \(z_k=(\bar x_k,\bar y_k)\in\mathbb R^{10}\), where
\(\bar x_k=Sx_k\) and \(\bar y_k=S(\theta_k-\Theta_k-o_k\mathbf1)\).
Here \(\Theta_{k,i}=k\alpha(i-4)\), \(\alpha=2\pi/9\), and
the declared common origin \(o_k\) is zero for the donor and
\(\varphi\) for the receiver.
This projection defines retained coordinates of a fine state.
Evolving equation (2) instead defines a surrogate trajectory,
which generally differs from the projection of the evolving
nonlinear fine state.

The phase deviations are real retained lifts. Wrapping them before
applying the internal linear operator changes this model. The
internal reference twist and known origins must be restored when
forming a circular bridge difference.

The component has one admitted unit contact. Its internal mobility
is \(K_*=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,\ldots,1/3\text{ at }p,\ldots,1/2)\),
even though \(L_{\mathrm{C9}}\) contains only its internal edges.
Its reduced internal operator is
\[
\bar A=SK_*L_{\mathrm{C9}}T=
\begin{pmatrix}
2/3&-2/3&0&0&0\\
-1/2&1&-1/2&0&0\\
0&-1/2&1&-1/2&0\\
0&0&-1/2&1&-1/2\\
0&0&0&-1/2&1/2
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1}
\]
The edge joining nodes zero and eight lies inside the outermost
layer and contributes zero on reflection-even vectors.
The layer weights are \((3,4,4,4,4)\), of total nineteen.
This is not the isolated-cycle normalization \(L_{\mathrm{C9}}/2\).
A different number or arrangement of contacts would require its
own mobility and reduction admission.

Every internal edge of the class-\(k\) twist has cosine
\(c_k=\cos(k\alpha)>0\). Linearizing only those internal sine
currents gives the following component model:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar x_k'&=-\bar A\bar x_k-\gamma c_k\bar A\bar y_k
 +\frac{e_0}{3}(u_x+\gamma u_\theta),\\
\bar y_k'&=\gamma\bar A\bar x_k-\frac{\gamma e_0}{3}u_x.
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
The two input channels have the definite convention
\[
u_x=x_{\mathrm{other},p}-x_{\mathrm{own},p},\qquad
u_\theta=\sin(\theta_{\mathrm{other},p}-\theta_{\mathrm{own},p}).
\]
They are not two unspecified forces. In the actual composition they
are determined by the other component's retained port outputs.

For \(z_k=(\bar x_k,\bar y_k)\), define
\[
J_k=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\bar A&-\gamma c_k\bar A\\
\gamma\bar A&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\frac13
\begin{pmatrix}
e_0&\gamma e_0\\
-\gamma e_0&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Cz_k=(\bar x_{k,0},\bar y_{k,0}).
\]
The exact input/output identity for this component model is
\[
Cz_k(t)=Ce^{J_kt}z_k(0)
 +\int_0^t Ce^{J_k(t-s)}B\,u_k(s)\,ds. \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(G_k(t)=Ce^{J_kt}B\) is its derived two-channel port kernel.
It depends on class, supplied law and contact normalization, not
on a fitted receiver response. The initial-state term is essential:
the kernel does not determine the initial internal shape.

The twenty-coordinate realization retains both component means.
Writing \(m_*=(3,4,4,4,4)^{\mathsf T}\), equation (1) gives
\[
(m_*^{\mathsf T}\bar x_k)'=u_x+\gamma u_\theta,\qquad
(m_*^{\mathsf T}\bar y_k)'=-\gamma u_x.
\]
Opposite inputs on the two components preserve the combined
degree-weighted charges, while the separate component means can
change. Holding them independently fixed after contact would
introduce a different law.

This is a strict reduction from thirty-six fine coordinates to
twenty retained coordinates, or an equivalent port history with
its initial source. No minimality theorem is asserted. In
particular, writing a kernel is not evidence that all hidden
initialization can be discarded or that one scalar state per
formed class closes the dynamics.

## Autonomous composition keeps the bridge nonlinear

Compose the two component models using
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_{\mathrm D}
 &=\left(\bar x_{\mathrm R,0}-\bar x_{\mathrm D,0},\
\sin(\varphi+\bar y_{\mathrm R,0}-\bar y_{\mathrm D,0})\right),\\
u_{\mathrm R}&=-u_{\mathrm D}.
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
The resulting twenty-coordinate system is autonomous after the
supplied support event. No observed response is installed as an
input, and no port is held to an externally selected future history.
Only the choice \(\ell=2\) of the receiver's already defined
component matrix changes in the held-out experiment.

Put
\[
\bar D=\operatorname{diag}(\bar A,0),\quad
\bar R=\operatorname{diag}(0,\bar A),\quad
q=e_0-e_5,\quad
\bar C_b=qq^{\mathsf T}/3,\quad
\bar A^+=\bar D+\bar R+\bar C_b.

In ten-component vectors \(\bar x,\bar y\), (2)--(4) become
\[
\begin{aligned}

+\frac{\gamma q}{3}
 \sin(\varphi+\bar y_5-\bar y_0),\\
\bar y'&=\gamma\bar A^+\bar x.

The first row is linear in the internal deviations but not in

## Why the discarded nonlinear modes remain controlled

The exact fine sine law does not preserve this even subspace in
general. Around a winding-\(k\) twist, an internal edge contributes
\[
\sin(k\alpha+\delta)
=\sin(k\alpha)+c_k\delta+O(\delta^2).
\]
The terms proportional to \(\sin(k\alpha)\delta^2\) can generate
reflection-odd deviations under an even drive. Linear reflection
symmetry alone cannot prove an exact nonlinear quotient.

For the error proof, extend (5) to all nine coordinates of each
component: use the linearized internal \(c_kK_*L_{\mathrm{C9}}\)
and the exact bridge sine, without projecting either channel.
This thirty-six-coordinate surrogate is a proof device.
Its matrices commute with each component reflection and its bridge
depends only on the fixed ports. Its ideal initial deviations are
zero, so its ideal flow stays even and is exactly the lift of (5).
The actual predictive realization remains twenty-dimensional.

Compare this lifted ideal surrogate to the ideal full sine flow
with the same zero form and target twists plus receiver origin
\(\varphi\). Let \(y_{\mathrm{fine}}(t)\) be phase displacement
from those initial comparison phases and put \(s=|\sin\varphi|\).
The complete joined field has initial normalized sine forcing
\((\sin\varphi/3)(e_a-e_b)\), irrespective of \(k,\ell\).
The contraction/Volterra argument in the
[contact remainder proof](SINE_FORMED_CLASS_CONTACT.md#a-correlated-finite-horizon-remainder)
uses only this forcing, the normalized row bounds and
\(\eta=\gamma^2\). It therefore applies with receiver class two:
\[
\|y_{\mathrm{fine}}(t)\|_\infty
\le\frac{2\gamma^2s}{3}t^2
\quad(0\le t\le h\le1,\ \gamma^2h^2\le3/4). \tag{6}
\]

At the actual fine phase state, the omitted internal Taylor terms
have normalized magnitude at most \(2\|y_{\mathrm{fine}}\|_\infty^2\).
They enter the physical form row multiplied by \(\gamma\) and
do not directly enter the phase row. Both the full field and
the extended surrogate have maximum-norm Lipschitz constant at
most \(2+2\gamma<3\). Thus for \(0<h<1/3\), their full-state
discrepancy obeys
\[
E_{\mathrm{sur}}(h)
\le\frac{2\gamma}{1-3h}
\int_0^h\|y_{\mathrm{fine}}(t)\|_\infty^2\,dt
\le\frac{8\gamma^5s^2h^5}{45(1-3h)}. \tag{7}
\]
Taking the supremum in the integral difference inequality proves
the denominator: \(E_h\le3hE_h+\int_0^h\|\mathrm{defect}\|\,dt\).
The same argument applies to every prefix of the window.
Generated odd modes and their subsequent feedback are included
through the full fine trajectory in (6), not set to zero.

For each actual formation image, arbitrary initial even and odd
errors remain within the endpoint bound \(\epsilon\).
Comparison of the actual full flow to the ideal full flow gives
the separate maximum-norm bound
\[
E_{\mathrm{prep}}^{\mathrm{one}}(h)
\le\frac{\epsilon}{1-3h}. \tag{8}
\]
This is precisely the complete-field argument in the
[actual-state contact handoff](SINE_FORMED_CLASS_CONTACT.md#actual-prepared-states-observation-errors-and-the-disconnected-control).
Combining (7) and (8) bounds the actual receiver form against
the nominal reduced flow without resetting any actual coordinate.
Unknown initial shape is handled by a proved family enclosure,
not inferred from winding, means or port values alone.

## A finite prediction computed from the reduced matrices

The analytic evaluator of (5) starts with \(\bar x=\bar y=0\).
Put
\[
f=\frac{\sin\varphi}{3}q,\qquad
\bar B_{k,\ell}=c_k\bar D+c_\ell\bar R+\cos\varphi\,\bar C_b.
\]
Direct differentiation of the reduced rows gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar x'&=\gamma f,\\
\bar x''&=-\gamma\bar A^+f,\\
\bar x'''&=\gamma(\bar A^+)^2f
-\gamma^3\bar B_{k,\ell}\bar A^+f,\\
\bar x''''&=-\gamma(\bar A^+)^3f
+\gamma^3\left(
\bar A^+\bar B_{k,\ell}\bar A^+
+\bar B_{k,\ell}(\bar A^+)^2\right)f.
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
The receiver is reduced coordinate five.
Because its row of \(\bar D\) vanishes, the two donor cases
have identical receiver derivatives through order three.
Their fourth derivative is obtained from the rational reduced
matrices through
\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi_4&=-e_5^{\mathsf T}
\left(\bar A^+\bar D\bar A^+
+\bar D(\bar A^+)^2\right)(q/3),\\
\Delta\bar Y''''(0)&=\chi_4\gamma^3\sin\varphi\,(c_1-c_2).
\end{aligned} \tag{10}
\]
For example, (1) gives
\((\bar A^+q)_0=4/3\),
\((\bar A^+q)_1=-1/2\), and
\((\bar D\bar A^+q)_0=11/9\).
The receiver bridge entry is \(-1/3\), so \(\chi_4=11/81\).
This value is derived by composing the reduced operators.
It is not a copied parameter from the earlier contact report.
The changed receiver class also changes the individual derivative
rows in (9), even though it cancels from this donor contrast.

Let \(d_c=c_1-c_2>0\). The exact reduced leading expression is
\[
P=\frac{\chi_4}{24}\gamma^3\sin\varphi\,d_c\,h^4. \tag{11}
\]
Its finite response is not identified with this derivative alone.
The normalized joined matrix \(\bar A^+\) has a contracting
nonnegative semigroup, and
\(\|\bar A^+\|_\infty,\|\bar D\|_\infty\le2\).
The correlated first-\(\gamma^2\) semigroup expansion has the
receiver terms
\[
e_5^{\mathsf T}\sum_{p,q\ge0}
\frac{(-1)^{p+q}(\bar A^+)^p\bar D(\bar A^+)^{q+1}f}
{(p+q+3)!}\,h^{p+q+3}.
\]
All \(p=0\) terms vanish. Bounding the \(n=p+q\ge2\) tail
uses \(n\) terms and successive ratio at most \(h/2\).
Restoring the physical factors gives
\[
E_{\mathrm{tail}}
=\frac{4s\,d_c\gamma^3h^5}{45(1-h/2)}. \tag{12}
\]
This derives the tail from the reduced rows and their shared
forcing, rather than subtracting two unrelated marginal estimates.

The reduced bridge remainder has normalized Taylor bound at most
\(2\|\bar y\|_\infty^2\); this deliberately conservative bound
also covers the sharper bridge-only constant. The same Volterra
bootstrap used for (6), now on the reduced rows, bounds the
paired nonlinear evaluation remainder by
\[
E_{\mathrm{nl}}=\frac{8s\gamma^5h^5}{15}. \tag{13}
\]
Equations (12)--(13) bound the finite reduced-flow contrast around
(11). Equation (7) has a different responsibility: it bounds
the reduced-flow approximation to the original nonlinear fine law.
Neither error can stand in for the other.

## Interpretation and limits of the component law

The new claim is conditional predictive reuse: the same independently
derived class matrices and exact bridge convention are composed
with a different receiver, with a uniform finite error for actual
formed families. The model does not discard memory by definition.
Equation (3) retains its initial-state source and the dynamics
of its internal even coordinates; equations (7)--(8) retain the
effects of omitted odd and uncertain initial coordinates.

The finite derivative expansion is one certified evaluation of
this reduced dynamical model. It is not a substitute for defining
the model's retained state, port feedback and initialization.
An insufficient error bound would not itself disprove that a
useful reduction exists; an actual response outside a claimed
enclosure would instead challenge its premises or proof.

The positive-loss microscopic law, source preparation, contact
normalization and work remain supplied. The very long structural
warmup and small readout budget are still mathematical premises.
No minimal realization, universal constituent law, autonomous
contact selection or physical sensor interpretation follows.
