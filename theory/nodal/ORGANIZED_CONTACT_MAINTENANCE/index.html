    1. Complete state, identities and stationary equations¶

    Use node order (c,j), with five host constituents c=0,...,4, external constituent c=5, and internal slots j=0,...,8. The support is the product of C9 and the wheel W6: six internal cycles, five outer ring edges per slot, and five spokes per slot. Its 144 unit edges are those already owned by the external-contact geometry. No edge, capacity, phase row or clock is changed here. Write

    \[ \alpha=2\pi/9,\qquad \psi_j=\alpha(j-4),\qquad \gamma=1/(1023\pi),\qquad S_i(\theta)=\sum_{b\sim i}\sin(\theta_b-\theta_i). \]

    With the unnormalized Laplacian L, the complete law and storage are

    \[ \begin{split} x'&=(-Lx+\gamma S(\theta))/4,& \theta'&=\gamma Lx/4,\\ H(x,\theta)&=\tfrac12x^TLx+ \sum_{\{i,b\}}[1-\cos(\theta_b-\theta_i)],& H'&=-\tfrac14\|Lx\|_2^2. \end{split}\tag{1} \]

    Prime denotes the declared tau clock. Both global form and lifted phase means are conserved. Connectedness implies that a stationary state has constant form and satisfies S(theta)=0. On the declared zero-form-mean leaf its form is zero. A stationary relative phase cannot acquire a nonzero common angular velocity: its conserved lifted mean forbids it.

    The six marked internal winding observations and nine old outer winding observations use oriented principal phase gaps on their fixed cycles. An acute chart requires its declared gaps to lie in (-pi/2,pi/2); crossing its boundary does not by itself change a winding number. Changing a principal winding requires reaching a branch cut at an absolute gap pi. New spoke triangles are part of the support but carry no newly prescribed identity. The complete state retains every fine deviation and both means.

    2. Two full-state stationary controls and their complete tangents¶

    Aligned constituent origins¶

    For a common origin Phi and common form m, the aligned state

    \[ x^*_{c,j}=m,\qquad \theta^*_{c,j}=\psi_j+\Phi \tag{2} \]

    is stationary: opposite internal currents cancel and every wheel current vanishes. It retains six internal windings one and nine outer windings zero. The global means choose m and Phi; these are not independently damped modes.

    Let L9 denote the C9 Laplacian and K the W6 Laplacian. In the chosen node order, the unweighted form Laplacian and phase-storage Hessian at (2) are

    \[ L=I_6\otimes L_9+K\otimes I_9,\qquad M_+=\cos\alpha\,I_6\otimes L_9+K\otimes I_9. \tag{3} \]

    Both are positive on the common-mode complement. This is a full-fine statement, including perturbations that leave the twelve-coordinate carrier.

    Antipodal external constituent¶

    Keep all five host origins at Phi and the external origin at Phi+pi. The spoke currents still vanish, so this state is also stationary. Its internal and old outer windings agree with (2), but its spoke cosines are negative. Write K_minus for the wheel Laplacian with ring weights one and spoke weights minus one. Its phase Hessian is

    \[ M_-=\cos\alpha\,I_6\otimes L_9+K_-\otimes I_9. \tag{4} \]

    The stationary label and the same marked windings do not imply the same stability. If a particular conserved lifted mean is required, subtract the appropriate common phase from all 54 coordinates; this changes neither current nor tangent.

    Exact joint mode classification¶

    The internal Fourier values are

    \[ \lambda_9(k)=2-2\cos(2\pi k/9),\qquad k=0,\ldots,8. \]

    The wheel has a common mode, a host-versus-external mode proportional to (1,1,1,1,1,-5), and four ring Fourier modes with external entry zero. Their simultaneous eigenvalues are:

    Wheel mode Form value mu Aligned phase value kappa Antipodal phase value kappa
    Common 0 0 0
    Host versus external 6 6 -6
    Ring Fourier r=1,...,4 3-2cos(2pi*r/5) 3-2cos(2pi*r/5) 1-2cos(2pi*r/5)

    Each pair r,5-r contributes two real ring directions. For any product mode, define lambda=lambda9(k)+mu and h=cos(alpha)*lambda9(k)+kappa. The complete two-row tangent is

    \[ \begin{pmatrix}\delta x\\\delta\theta\end{pmatrix}' =\frac14 \begin{pmatrix}-\lambda&-\gamma h\\\gamma\lambda&0\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}\delta x\\\delta\theta\end{pmatrix}, \qquad s^2+\frac\lambda4s+\frac{\gamma^2\lambda h}{16}=0. \tag{5} \]

    For the aligned target, all 53 noncommon product modes have positive lambda and h. Their 106 temporal eigenvalues have negative real parts. The remaining two neutral directions are exactly the conserved form and phase means. Thus (2) is locally asymptotically stable on each fixed-mean leaf; the acute storage argument below supplies a nonlinear version.

    For the antipodal target, all nine host-versus-external product modes have h=cos(alpha)*lambda9(k)-6<0, since lambda9(k)<=4 and cos(alpha)<1. Each has one positive and one negative temporal root. The four ring modes have positive antipodal phase values (3-sqrt(5))/2 and (3+sqrt(5))/2, each twice. The other 44 relative product modes therefore contribute 88 stable directions. Altogether there are nine unstable, 97 stable and two neutral directions. This full-state stationary control is a saddle, not a retained-source capture certificate.

    3. Triangles obstruct reuse of a wholly acute winding-one cell¶

    At any fixed slot j, let d_c be the principal host ring gap from c to c+1 and sigma_c the principal spoke gap from host c to the external node. The oriented triangle periods are integers

    \[ n_c=\frac{d_c+\sigma_{c+1}-\sigma_c}{2\pi},\qquad \sum_{c=0}^4n_c=\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{c=0}^4d_c. \tag{6} \]

    These are topological identities for the actual fine state, with principal branches away from their cuts. Outer winding one makes their sum one, so some triangle has a nonzero period. Its three absolute principal gaps cannot all be strictly below 2*pi/3. In particular a wholly acute joined chart cannot retain outer winding one. If all five old ring gaps remain acute, that triangle additionally forces at least one spoke gap to have absolute value at least 3*pi/4: its two spoke magnitudes must sum to at least 2*pi-|d_c|, which exceeds 3*pi/2 in the strict chart.

    This holds independently at each of the nine slots and requires no carrier assumption. It explains why the earlier positive-edge-cosine storage trap cannot simply be reused on the enlarged support. It does not establish that the complete phase Hessian is indefinite: negative edge weights can be compensated by the rest of a graph. Nor does it prohibit equilibria with some nonacute edges or prove loss of an actual source winding.

    4. No stationary acute outer winding on the exact carrier¶

    The invariant carrier retains all twelve coordinates,

    \[ x_{c,j}=X_c,\qquad \theta_{c,j}=\psi_j+\Phi_c, \qquad X'=(-KX+\gamma S_{W_6}(\Phi))/4,\quad \Phi'=\gamma KX/4. \tag{7} \]

    It is a justified restriction of (1), not a replacement for the independently perturbed source. Its exact storage and loss are

    \[ H_{12}=6V_9+\tfrac92X^TKX+ 9\sum_{\{c,b\}\in W_6}[1-\cos(\Phi_b-\Phi_c)], \qquad H_{12}'=-\tfrac94\|KX\|_2^2, \tag{8} \]

    where V9=9*(1-cos(alpha)).

    Closed acute-cell obstruction¶

    Suppose a carrier stationary state has outer winding one and five directed principal ring gaps d_j in [-pi/2,pi/2]. Their sum is 2*pi; consequently none can be negative, since the other four have sum at most 2*pi. Set the hub phase to zero, and denote the ring phases modulo 2*pi by phi_j. The stationary ring equations are

    \[ \sin\phi_j=\sin d_j-\sin d_{j-1}. \tag{9} \]

    Choose a maximal gap d_m. Since sine is increasing on [0,pi/2], its first endpoint has nonnegative sine and its second endpoint nonpositive sine. The positive oriented gap is at most pi/2, so these endpoints straddle the hub's antipodal phase. Choose representatives

    \[ \phi_m=\pi-u,\qquad \phi_{m+1}=\pi+v, \qquad u,v\ge0,\quad u+v=d_m. \]

    Adding the two adjacent relations in (9) gives

    \[ \sin d_{m-1}+\sin d_{m+1} =2\sin d_m-\sin u-\sin v \le\sin d_m\le1, \tag{10} \]

    where sin(u)+sin(v)>=sin(u+v) for these angles. For two angles a,b in [0,pi/2], sin(a)+sin(b)<=1 implies a+b<=pi/2. For completeness, at fixed sum s>=pi/2 the concave sine sum has minimum 1+sin(s-pi/2) at an endpoint; equality at sum pi/2 requires one angle zero and the other pi/2. Equation (10) therefore bounds the sum of the two gaps adjacent to d_m by pi/2.

    The maximal gap and the other two gaps are each at most pi/2. Since the five gaps sum to 2*pi, every bound must be an equality. Thus four gaps are pi/2 and one is zero. At the two coincident endpoints of that zero gap, (9) would require the same phase to have sine minus one and plus one, respectively. This is impossible. Hence no carrier stationary state lies in the closed acute outer-winding-one cell. Reflection of all phases proves the corresponding winding-minus-one result.

    The argument uses the actual unit ring and spoke weights. It neither changes the interface nor identifies a constitutive coefficient from a desired retained organization.

    Dynamical consequence and its precise boundary¶

    Every carrier trajectory starting in this cell leaves its closure after some finite time. To prove this, suppose instead it remained there for all future times. Conserved mean form and (8) bound X. The phase torus is compact, and the closed acute winding cell stays away from principal branch cuts, so the trajectory has a nonempty compact omega-limit set. Storage is nonincreasing. Its largest invariant zero-loss subset requires KX=0; the phase row then vanishes, and invariance of the form row requires S_W6(Phi)=0. The closed-cell obstruction excludes that set, contradicting indefinite retention. The same reasoning applies for every supplied hub origin and every finite initial carrier form.

    This supplies no uniform exit deadline. Leaving an acute cell at absolute gap pi/2 is not changing winding at a branch cut of absolute gap pi. Nor does carrier exit establish exit of every actual fine-state source: off the carrier, internal C9 torques enter the stationary equations and cannot be discarded from (9). Broader nonacute winding-one equilibria and their possible attraction require their own classification.

    5. A full-fine obstruction to stable retention near the carrier¶

    The previous section's stationary equations are restricted to the carrier. A different argument applies to the entire fine phase Hessian, even when internal currents do not cancel. Let

    \[ M(\theta)=B\,\operatorname{diag}(\cos\delta_e)B^T, \qquad D(\theta)=\sum_{54\ \mathrm{internal\ edges}} [1-\cos(\delta_e-\alpha)]. \tag{11} \]

    Here B is any consistently oriented incidence matrix. Orient each internal cycle in increasing slot order; its closing gap retains the declared +2*pi winding representative. The cosine in D makes any equivalent representative harmless. D measures departure of the internal edge gaps from the carrier twist. It is not a distance between complete states and does not replace their form or phase coordinates.

    Suppose all nine old outer cycles have strictly acute winding one. With an arbitrary common reference beta, put

    \[ \chi_{c,j}=\theta_{c,j}-\psi_j-\beta, \qquad v_{c,j}=\sin\chi_{c,j},\quad w_{c,j}=\cos\chi_{c,j}. \]

    For every edge, the squared differences of these two vectors sum to 2*(1-cos(chi_b-chi_a)). Their two phase quadratic forms therefore obey

    \[ v^TMv+w^TMw =2\sum_e\cos\delta_e\,[1-\cos(\chi_b-\chi_a)]. \tag{12} \]

    For same-slot wheel edges, the demodulated difference is the actual gap. For internal edges it is the actual gap minus alpha, modulo 2*pi. Since cos(delta_e)<=1 and every bracket is nonnegative, the internal contribution to the sum on the right before its factor two is at most D. This inequality does not assume positive internal cosines.

    To bound a wheel, let its five acute ring gaps be d_i. Winding one makes them positive and their sum 2*pi. Put c5=cos(2*pi/5) and C=sum_i cos(d_i). Concavity of cosine and the quadratic-mean inequality give
