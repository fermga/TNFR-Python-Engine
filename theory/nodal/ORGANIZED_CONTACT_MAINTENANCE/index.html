    1. Complete state, identities and stationary equations¶

    Use node order (c,j), with five host constituents c=0,...,4, external constituent c=5, and internal slots j=0,...,8. The support is the product of C9 and the wheel W6: six internal cycles, five outer ring edges per slot, and five spokes per slot. Its 144 unit edges are those already owned by the external-contact geometry. No edge, capacity, phase row or clock is changed here. Write

    \[ \alpha=2\pi/9,\qquad \psi_j=\alpha(j-4),\qquad \gamma=1/(1023\pi),\qquad S_i(\theta)=\sum_{b\sim i}\sin(\theta_b-\theta_i). \]

    With the unnormalized Laplacian L, the complete law and storage are

    \[ \begin{split} x'&=(-Lx+\gamma S(\theta))/4,& \theta'&=\gamma Lx/4,\\ H(x,\theta)&=\tfrac12x^TLx+ \sum_{\{i,b\}}[1-\cos(\theta_b-\theta_i)],& H'&=-\tfrac14\|Lx\|_2^2. \end{split}\tag{1} \]

    Prime denotes the declared tau clock. Both global form and lifted phase means are conserved. Connectedness implies that a stationary state has constant form and satisfies S(theta)=0. The complete state retains every fine deviation and both means.

    2. Two full-state stationary controls and their complete tangents¶

    Aligned constituent origins¶

    For a common origin Phi and common form m, the aligned state

    \[ x^*_{c,j}=m,\qquad \theta^*_{c,j}=\psi_j+\Phi \tag{2} \]

    is stationary: opposite internal currents cancel and every wheel current vanishes. It retains six internal windings one and nine outer windings zero. The global means choose m and Phi; these are not independently damped modes.

    Let L9 denote the C9 Laplacian and K the W6 Laplacian. In the chosen node order, the unweighted form Laplacian and phase-storage Hessian at (2) are

    \[ L=I_6\otimes L_9+K\otimes I_9,\qquad M_+=\cos\alpha\,I_6\otimes L_9+K\otimes I_9. \tag{3} \]

    Both are positive on the common-mode complement. This is a full-fine statement, including perturbations that leave the twelve-coordinate carrier.

    Antipodal external constituent¶

    Keep all five host origins at Phi and the external origin at Phi+pi. The spoke currents still vanish, so this state is also stationary. Its internal and old outer windings agree with (2), but its spoke cosines are negative. Write K_minus for the wheel Laplacian with ring weights one and spoke weights minus one. Its phase Hessian is

    \[ M_-=\cos\alpha\,I_6\otimes L_9+K_-\otimes I_9. \tag{4} \]

    The stationary label and the same marked windings do not imply the same stability. If a particular conserved lifted mean is required, subtract the appropriate common phase from all 54 coordinates; this changes neither current nor tangent.

    3. Triangles obstruct reuse of a wholly acute winding-one cell¶

    At any fixed slot j, let d_c be the principal host ring gap from c to c+1 and sigma_c the principal spoke gap from host c to the external node. The oriented triangle periods are integers

    \[ n_c=\frac{d_c+\sigma_{c+1}-\sigma_c}{2\pi},\qquad \sum_{c=0}^4n_c=\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{c=0}^4d_c. \tag{6} \]

    These are topological identities for the actual fine state, with principal branches away from their cuts. Outer winding one makes their sum one, so some triangle has a nonzero period. Its three absolute principal gaps cannot all be strictly below 2*pi/3. In particular a wholly acute joined chart cannot retain outer winding one. If all five old ring gaps remain acute, that triangle additionally forces at least one spoke gap to have absolute value at least 3*pi/4: its two spoke magnitudes must sum to at least 2*pi-|d_c|, which exceeds 3*pi/2 in the strict chart.

    This holds independently at each of the nine slots and requires no carrier assumption. It explains why the earlier positive-edge-cosine storage trap cannot simply be reused on the enlarged support. It does not establish that the complete phase Hessian is indefinite: negative edge weights can be compensated by the rest of a graph. Nor does it prohibit equilibria with some nonacute edges or prove loss of an actual source winding.

    Closed acute-cell obstruction¶

    Suppose a carrier stationary state has outer winding one and five directed principal ring gaps d_j in [-pi/2,pi/2]. Their sum is 2*pi; consequently none can be negative, since the other four have sum at most 2*pi. Set the hub phase to zero, and denote the ring phases modulo 2*pi by phi_j. The stationary ring equations are

    \[ \sin\phi_j=\sin d_j-\sin d_{j-1}. \tag{9} \]

    Choose a maximal gap d_m. Since sine is increasing on [0,pi/2], its first endpoint has nonnegative sine and its second endpoint nonpositive sine. The positive oriented gap is at most pi/2, so these endpoints straddle the hub's antipodal phase. Choose representatives

    \[ \phi_m=\pi-u,\qquad \phi_{m+1}=\pi+v, \qquad u,v\ge0,\quad u+v=d_m. \]

    Adding the two adjacent relations in (9) gives

    \[ \sin d_{m-1}+\sin d_{m+1} =2\sin d_m-\sin u-\sin v \le\sin d_m\le1, \tag{10} \]

    where sin(u)+sin(v)>=sin(u+v) for these angles. For two angles a,b in [0,pi/2], sin(a)+sin(b)<=1 implies a+b<=pi/2. For completeness, at fixed sum s>=pi/2 the concave sine sum has minimum 1+sin(s-pi/2) at an endpoint; equality at sum pi/2 requires one angle zero and the other pi/2. Equation (10) therefore bounds the sum of the two gaps adjacent to d_m by pi/2.

    The maximal gap and the other two gaps are each at most pi/2. Since the five gaps sum to 2*pi, every bound must be an equality. Thus four gaps are pi/2 and one is zero. At the two coincident endpoints of that zero gap, (9) would require the same phase to have sine minus one and plus one, respectively. This is impossible. Hence no carrier stationary state lies in the closed acute outer-winding-one cell. Reflection of all phases proves the corresponding winding-minus-one result.

    The argument uses the actual unit ring and spoke weights. It neither changes the interface nor identifies a constitutive coefficient from a desired retained organization.

Conserved mean form and (8) bound X. The phase torus is compact, and the closed acute winding cell stays away from principal branch cuts, so the trajectory has a nonempty compact omega-limit set.     \[ z(t)\le w\int_0^t e^{\alpha(t-s)}(b_0+b_1s)\,ds \le we^{\alpha t}\left(b_0t+\frac{b_1t^2}{2}\right). \]

    \[ \begin{aligned} Z_h&=W_{\rm hi}G_h\left(b_0h+\frac{b_1h^2}{2}\right),& u(t),v(t)&\le Z_h,\\ H_h&=W_{\rm hi}G_h\left(\frac{b_0h^2}{2} +\frac{b_1h^3}{6}\right),& \int_0^h[u(s)+v(s)]\,ds&\le H_h. \end{aligned} \tag{59} \]

    The integral estimate uses the polynomial at each time, not an endpoint rate times the full window. For fixed finite background bounds and horizon, both quantities are proportional to \(W_{\rm hi}\). In particular they are exactly zero when \(W_{\rm hi}=0\), even if either individual branch has substantial internal motion.

    14.3 Projection onto the original arithmetic observation¶

    For an internal donor edge the unit-Lipschitz sine gives

    \[ |c_{ij}^w(s)-c_{ij}^0(s)|\le2u(s)+2g v(s) \le2q[u(s)+v(s)],\qquad q=\max(1,g). \]

    Thus the arithmetic internal derivative contrast is bounded in absolute value by \(\overline\Lambda q Z_h/(2n_D)\) throughout the window. Its integrated magnitude is at most \(\overline\Lambda q H_h/(2n_D)\). The joined port current still moves relative to its initial value by at most \(2X(s)+2gT(s)\), with the same integral \(F(h)\) from (52). The initial matched slope remains exactly the bridge term in (54). Hence define

    \[ \begin{aligned} E_{\rm match}(h)&= \frac{N_{u,\rm hi}W_{\rm hi}}{4n_D}F(h) +\frac{\overline\Lambda q}{2n_D}H_h,\\ E_*(h)&=\min\{E_{\rm a}(h),E_{\rm match}(h)\},\\ J_{\rm a}&\in hR_{{\rm a},0}+[-E_*(h),E_*(h)]. \end{aligned} \tag{60} \]

    The minimum retains the earlier valid allowance when a long-time exponential estimate is looser. It cannot increase the remainder, and \(E_*\le E_{\rm match}\) still tends to zero with the bridge ceiling. The same minimum can be taken between the two whole-window internal rate allowances. Neither minimum changes the observed arithmetic mean.

    The weighted observation retains (55), because its internal currents already cancel exactly. Its deterministic remainder is already proportional to \(W_{\rm hi}\). Both normalized mean contrasts also satisfy the useful full-state consequence

    \[ |J_{\rm a}|\le u(h)\le Z_h,\qquad |J_\rho|\le u(h)\le Z_h. \tag{61} \]

    For the second inequality the weights \((1/\nu_i)/R_D\) are positive and sum to one. These are bounds for projections of the complete solutions; they do not supply an autonomous equation for two means.

    14.4 Exact shrinking bounds, reading errors and chart retention¶

    An outward enclosure for the initial slope coefficient per unit bridge weight can be computed from \(A,\Gamma,D_0,N_u,\mathcal R\), without first materializing the bridge interval. Let its rational endpoints be \(C=[c_-,c_+]\). Multiply the exact conductance endpoints last:

    \[ \operatorname{prod}_{\mathbb Q}(W,C)= \left[\min_{i,j\in\{-,+\}}w_i c_j, \max_{i,j\in\{-,+\}}w_i c_j\right]. \tag{62} \]

    These endpoints enclose the appropriate initial slope. Add the exact rational remainder endpoints from (60), or from (55) for the weighted reading, after multiplying by \(h\). The resulting rational endpoint pairs are certified deterministic enclosures. Their coefficients can include fixed numerical overestimation of the sine, but that uncertainty is also multiplied by the exact bridge weight. Their widths and absolute endpoints therefore tend to zero with \(W_{\rm hi}\). At an exactly zero bridge both true pairs are exactly \((0,0)\).

    The shared dyadic128 interval type is a separate outward representation. It has a fixed grid, so an arbitrarily small positive rational interval need not have a display width tending to zero. Retain the exact endpoint pairs alongside that representation and decide strict signs from the certified exact endpoints. A positive subgrid weight remains positive; a zero-width represented display cannot replace primitive admission. Neither a grid floor nor a display interval is an observation-error model.

    For each reading, add the sum of its own four absolute error radii to its true pair. At zero bridge conductance the resulting observed interval is the corresponding symmetric error interval, not generally \((0,0)\). Arbitrary correlations remain allowed; no cancellation between readings is presumed. Missing weighted-error data still leave the weighted observed interval and sign unrequested. A family containing \(w=0\) cannot have a uniform strict true or observed sign. With a fixed positive error sum, sufficiently weak bridges are unresolved even when the underlying true contrast has a definite sign. No arbitrary weak-contact detectability follows from deterministic continuity.

    Retain the independent old-chart test \(\mu_e-2T(h)>0\) from section 13. The new difference bound does not replace the absolute phase motion of either branch: two almost identical trajectories can both leave an old chart. A resolved sign, a small response and chart retention remain separate properties. Equality in a sufficient bound is indeterminate, not a witnessed loss of identity or reversal of the true interaction.

    14.5 Reductions and an independent analytical discriminator¶

    For tight common capacity \(c>0\), \(\alpha_c=c\alpha_1\), \(b_{0,c}=c b_{0,1}\), and \(b_{1,c}=c^2b_{1,1}\). With the same growth rule this gives

    \[ Z_{h,c}=Z_{ch,1},\qquad H_{h,c}=H_{ch,1}/c. \tag{63} \]

    The actual internal capacity variation is zero. The finite arithmetic and weighted remainders therefore recover the common-clock reduction (56) to section 12; their normalized observations agree. This is a reduction of the exact conditional expressions, not a promise that different orders of outward display arithmetic have identical endpoints. Heterogeneous capacity variation cannot be absorbed into a single clock. Zero capacity remains outside the inverse-capacity domain.

    For section 13.5's exact three-node example, keep \(w=1\), \(B_0=1,K=2,M=2,N_u=\{2\},\overline\Lambda=1,n_D=2\), \(R_D=3/2\) and \(g=0\). Then \(\alpha=0,G_h=1,b_0=1,b_1=0\), so \(Z_h=h,H_h=h^2/2,F(h)=2h^2\). The new arithmetic allowance is

    \[ E_{\rm match}(h)=\frac{5h^2}{8}<E_{\rm a}(h)=h^2 \quad(h>0),\qquad J_{\rm a}\in\frac h4+ \left[-\frac{5h^2}{8},\frac{5h^2}{8}\right]. \tag{64} \]

    At \(h=1/4\), this interval is \([3/128,13/128]\), strictly positive with zero reading errors. The old interval \([0,1/8]\) was indeterminate. The exact solution (57) and the observable are unchanged; preserving the common initialization improves what the sufficient bound can decide. At \(h=1\), the new interval remains indeterminate although the exact contrast is positive. The refinement supplies no general sign guarantee. This analytical comparison uses no numerical trajectory.
