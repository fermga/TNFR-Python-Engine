### 11.6 Global closure obstruction and a normalized-sine comparison

<a id="global-closure-pressure-comparison"></a>

The [finite-time boundary audit](RELATIONAL_DOMAIN_AND_CAPTURE.md#regular-domain-continuation-and-boundary-access)
requires distinguishing a limited-domain law from a globally defined one.
The [pressure owner](PRESSURE_CONSTITUTIVE_SCOPE.md#43-phase-domain-orientation-and-a-discriminating-structural-response)
already proves that principal-argument pressure has no continuous extension
through either an antipodal branch or a zero neighbor resultant. With
`w*nu_i>0` the form row inherits that discontinuity. Changing only phase
evolution, storage or locality cannot repair it while leaving that form row
unchanged. The [reflected boundary](RELATIONAL_PATTERN_COMPOSITION.md#reflected-boundary-exit)
additionally has path-dependent phase-rate limits from positive resultants.
A regular invertible chart change removes neither obstruction.

#### The incompatibility is stronger than the local-completion theorem

Take P2 with `r=x_0-x_1!=0`, `delta=theta_1-theta_0` in `(-pi,pi)`,
`c=nu_0+nu_1>0` and arbitrary phase velocities `F_0,F_1`. The native form
row and current storage give, before any locality assumption,

\[
E=r^2/2+\beta(1-\cos\delta),\qquad
\dot E=c[-er^2+wr\delta/\pi]+\beta\sin\delta(F_1-F_0).
\]

Preserving the prescribed exact loss forces

\[
F_0-F_1=\frac{wcr\delta}{\beta\pi\sin\delta}.
\]

This relative rate diverges as `delta->pi` at fixed nonzero form contrast.
It is unaffected by a common phase clock. Dropping capacity separability or
allowing nonlocal phase rows therefore cannot supply a bounded completion
with the same pressure, storage and loss.

Even weakening exact loss to total passivity is insufficient. If `F` were
bounded near this antipodal state, its phase-work term would tend to zero,
leaving `lim Edot=c*(-e*r^2+w*r)`. For `e>0` choose `0<r<w/e`; for `e=0`
any `r>0` suffices. The limit is positive, contradicting passivity.
This argument concerns all-state work identities, not an assertion that
these fixed-form paths are trajectories. Actual finite-time collisions have
their separate proof. The bounded `rho*Ng` and fixed-form nonlinear phase
corrections above retain their regular-domain results but cannot cure this
global obstruction.

| Retained requirement | What it forces or leaves open |
| --- | --- |
| Native principal-argument form pressure on the same circular full state | No globally continuous extension; phase-only changes cannot resolve it |
| That pressure, cosine storage and exact global loss | Unbounded relative phase response near antipodal P2, without needing locality |
| That pressure, cosine storage and only total passivity | Still incompatible with bounded phase completion on all finite form states |
| Restricted invariant preparations | Remain legitimate limited-domain models; the sufficient storage theorem and protected basins apply |
| Changed pressure, enlarged state, event or generalized solution convention | Separate model obligations; none is supplied by assigning a numerical value at zero |

#### Reuse an existing phase reading with an explicit changed premise

The repository already distinguishes `J_phi/pi` from argument pressure.
Write `S_i=sum_j sin(theta_j-theta_i)`, `q=Bx` and `D=diag(d_i)` on
fixed connected simple unit support. The following **comparison law** uses
the same signed form, circular phase, held nonnegative capacity and clock:

\[
s_i=\frac{S_i}{\pi d_i},\qquad
\boxed{\dot x=N(-eD^{-1}q+ws),\quad
       \dot\theta=\frac{w}{\beta\pi}ND^{-1}q.}
\]

Its phase-to-form pressure is `s=J_phi/pi` and its response metric is
`H_s=pi*D`. This replaces both native phase-pressure response and its
matching exchange row. It is not the native law with a different domain
setting, and not the separately supplied Kuramoto/U3 sine phase clock:
here sine enters form pressure, while form contrast drives phase.
Existing current telemetry does not select its use as a dynamical law.

On the native regular domain,

\[
s_i=\frac{|z_i|}{d_i}\operatorname{sinc}(\operatorname{Arg}z_i)\,g_i.
\]

Thus the existing geometric relation directly relates the two responses.
The comparison retains cancellation strength as well as direction, and has
no division by a resultant or its argument. It remains defined when phases
cancel. No new primitive variable, cutoff, fitted gain or boundary event is
introduced. Choosing this pressure is nevertheless an additional premise.

#### Complete balance, continuation and inherited geometry

With the unchanged storage `E=E_D+beta*V_phi`, `grad V_phi=-S` gives

\[
\dot E_D=-e q^TND^{-1}q+\frac w\pi q^TND^{-1}S,\qquad
\beta\dot V_\phi=-\frac w\pi S^TND^{-1}q.
\]

The exchange cancels exactly, leaving the same loss `-e q^TND^{-1}q`.
On any fixed finite graph the complete vector field is globally Lipschitz
on the form/phase lift: its form dependence is linear with constant
coefficients, and sine has bounded derivative. Hence every finite initial
state has a unique global solution, descending to circular phases.
This mathematical theorem concerns the comparison, not the native executor.

For strictly positive held capacities, reciprocity supplies two identities:

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\sum_i\frac{d_i}{\nu_i}x_i=0,\qquad
\frac{d}{dt}\sum_i\frac{d_i}{\nu_i}\theta_i=0.
\]

The second uses a chosen continuous phase lift; it is not in general a
single-valued scalar observation on the phase torus. At zero capacity these
weighted expressions are unavailable, although the smooth law and work
balance remain valid. The native law generally conserves neither mean.

With all capacities positive, equilibria are exactly `q=0,S=0`. All native
regular equilibria therefore survive, but the complete set grows: zero and
negative-real resultants with `S=0` are now allowed. The seven-equilibrium
classification must not be transferred as exhaustive.
At each equilibrium the existing
[full-network stiffness proof](RELATIONAL_RECOVERY_AND_INTERACTION.md#regular-equilibrium-stiffness) applies with
`H=pi*D`: positive quotient cosine Hessian gives local exponential recovery
for `e,w,beta>0`, and negative stiffness gives growing real modes.
Consensus has exactly the native linearization. Away from consensus, equal
geometry and stability signs do not give equal rates, memory or basins.

For `e>0` and positive capacities, storage and conserved weighted form mean
give precompactness on form times the phase torus. On the largest invariant
subset with zero loss, `q=0` and
`BND^-1 S=0`, so `ND^-1 S=c*1`. Reciprocity `sum_i S_i=0` forces `c=0`.
LaSalle therefore gives approach to the equilibrium **set**. This does not
prove convergence to consensus, to one equilibrium, or to a physical entity.

#### Conditional selection and remaining freedom

There is a more structural selection route than assuming a constant metric.
Keep cosine storage, the same diffusion channel and exact loss on the full
phase torus for every finite connected simple unit graph and every
nonnegative capacity vector. Require zero-capacity phase freezing, own-capacity
separability and a finite continuous phase law. The **additional pressure
premise** is normalized pairwise superposition,

\[
g_i(\theta)=d_i^{-1}\sum_{j\sim i}f(\theta_j-\theta_i),
\]

with one continuous, periodic, odd kernel `f` across supports. On a star with
only the root active and arbitrary `q_i!=0`, exact loss gives
`beta*S_i*F_i=w*nu_i*q_i*g_i`. Thus every finite family with
`sum_j sin(delta_j)=0` must also have `sum_j f(delta_j)=0`.
All phases, including cancellations and antipodal leaves, are admitted by
the proposed global completion; no regular-domain padding is required.

Reuse the [balanced-star algebra](../TNFR_VARIATIONAL_PRINCIPLE.md#native-phase-storage-classification)
with a different object being classified. The two gaps
`delta,-arcsin(sin(delta))` give
`f(delta)=f(arcsin(sin(delta)))`. Put `h(s)=f(arcsin(s))`. Three gaps with
sines `a,b,-(a+b)` give `h(a+b)=h(a)+h(b)` whenever all arguments lie in
`[-1,1]`. Continuity implies `h(s)=k*s` and therefore `f(delta)=k*sin(delta)`.
Matching the native consensus tangent fixes `k=1/pi`.
Capacity isolation now forces
`F_i=w*nu_i*q_i/(beta*pi*d_i)` whenever `S_i!=0`; own-capacity separability
transfers this row to general capacities. Nonzero `S_i` states are dense
by perturbing a neighbor phase, including near zero resultants, so continuity
extends the row everywhere. Varying only the root phase would not justify
that density at a zero neighbor resultant.

This derives the metric and phase independence instead of assuming them.
It does not derive pairwise superposition, which the native collective Arg
source does not obey. A separate sufficient selection premise is to require
the phase row itself to be phase-independent while leaving the phase-only
pressure initially unrestricted: the same isolated-capacity identity and
consensus tangent then also force the displayed comparison.

Neither selection premise follows from smoothness, storage or circular symmetry.
For example, the comparison family

\[
m_i(\theta)=\frac{1+\epsilon(S_i/d_i)^2}{\pi d_i},\quad
g_i=m_iS_i,\quad
F_i=(w/\beta)\nu_i m_i q_i,\qquad \epsilon\ge0
\]

retains smoothness, positive mobility, exact loss, the same equilibria and
the same consensus Jacobian. It supplies a counterexample to unique selection,
not another production setting. The
[frozen pairing comparison](SINE_PAIR_MOBILITY.md#sine-pairing-constitutive-scope)
reuses its zero-loss members to separate a robust local grouping direction
from different relative response rates. The family generally violates the
common pairwise-superposition premise. The original storage
classification assumed native pressure; changing that premise means cosine
storage is retained here explicitly, not rederived by that old theorem.

#### An exact discriminator before any trajectory

On a three-leaf star, take center phase zero, leaf phases `(0,0,pi/2)`,
uniform form and positive held capacities. All native resultants are regular.
Put `W=sum_i d_i/nu_i` and let `M` be the degree/inverse-capacity weighted
form mean. At this same initial state,

\[
\dot M_{\rm native}
=\frac wW\left[\frac{3\arctan(1/2)}{\pi}-\frac12\right]<0,\qquad
\dot M_{\rm sine}=0.
\]

The sign follows from `atan(1/2)<1/2<pi/6`. Both initial phase rates are
zero, and both laws dissipate zero storage initially; the mean response
still distinguishes them. This is an exact prospective initial-rate
comparison with identical preparation, coefficients and clock, not a fitted
response or physical validation. The older same-mean/different-spread
pressure test remains useful; no frozen acquisition is rerun.

There is also a purely spectral distinction at the same maintained C5
geometry. For a uniform acute cycle twist with `c=cos(kappa)>0`, common
capacity `nu` and nonzero combinatorial Laplacian eigenvalue `ell`, write
`z=2*s/(nu*ell)` for the scaled temporal exponent. The two characteristic
polynomials are

\[
z^2+ez+\frac{w^2}{\beta\pi^2c}=0\quad\text{(native)},\qquad
z^2+ez+\frac{w^2c}{\beta\pi^2}=0\quad\text{(comparison)}.
\]

For winding-one C5, `e=w=1/2,beta=1` and `c=cos(2*pi/5)<1/3`.
The native discriminant `1/4-1/(pi^2*c)` is negative, whereas the comparison
discriminant `1/4-c/pi^2` is positive. Both recover locally, but one has
damped oscillatory linear modes and the other distinct negative real modes.
Local pulse character therefore depends on the constitutive response, even
when the stable geometry and storage agree. This is a tangent theorem,
not a computed nonlinear response or a transfer of earlier memory records.

[`bound_relational_sine_exchange`](../../src/tnfr/physics/relational_sine_comparison.py)
is a detached static comparison with explicit law identity and exact
represented-state trigonometric bounds. It reuses shared graph/scalar

rejection. Its computed interval work residuals are distinct from the
analytic cancellation identity. It installs no dynamics, boundary extension

### 11.7 Common exchange geometry, composition and a controlled common regime

<a id="common-exchange-geometry-and-composition"></a>

The two complete laws above belong to one mathematical family. This is an
identity between declared models, not a derivation of a uniquely selected
microscopic law. Keep the same support, clock, held capacities, coefficients
and storage. Write `M=diag(m_i)` with finite positive entries on its stated
domain. Then

\[
\dot x=N(-eD^{-1}q+wMS),\qquad
\dot\theta=(w/\beta)NMq
\]

gives `Edot=-e*q^T*N*D^-1*q` for every such mobility. In fact
`grad_x E=q` and `grad_theta E=-beta*S`, so the two exchange work terms
cancel by symmetry of `NM`. No derivative of `M` enters this first storage
derivative, even if `M` depends on the state. Existence, regularity, symmetries
and higher derivatives still require their own hypotheses. Equal loss
functions do not imply equal storage histories along different trajectories.

The native choice is `m_i=1/H_i`, while the normalized-sine comparison uses
`m_i=1/(pi*d_i)`. The common skew work structure therefore leaves precisely
the constitutive mobility being investigated. It does not by itself give a
Poisson bracket: the earlier [Jacobi audit](../TNFR_VARIATIONAL_PRINCIPLE.md#1319-structural-closure-tests-exchange-jacobi-and-the-remaining-potential)
distinguishes that extra requirement from work cancellation.

#### Geometric attenuation is not generally a change of clock

On the native regular domain put `z_i=R_i*exp(i*alpha_i)`. The exact ratio is

\[
a_i=\frac{m_i^{\rm sine}}{m_i^{\rm native}}
 =\frac{R_i}{d_i}\operatorname{sinc}\alpha_i
 =\frac{H_i}{\pi d_i},\qquad 0<a_i\le1.
\]

Here `R_i<=d_i` and `0<sinc(alpha_i)<=1`. Equality requires all neighbors
to have the center's phase modulo a turn. The sine law scales both nodal
exchange terms by `a_i` and leaves form diffusion unchanged. Different
nodes generally have different attenuations.

Even P2 with positive diffusion rules out a common clock interpretation.
Set `r=x_0-x_1`, `delta=theta_1-theta_0` in `(-pi,pi)` and
`c=nu_0+nu_1>0`. In these two relative coordinates the native and sine
velocity vectors are

\[
F_n=\left(c[-er+w\delta/\pi],
 -\frac{cw r\delta}{\beta\pi\sin\delta}\right),\qquad
F_s=\left(c[-er+w\sin\delta/\pi],
 -\frac{cw r}{\beta\pi}\right).
\]

Thus

\[
\det(F_n,F_s)=\frac{c^2ewr^2}{\beta\pi}
 \left(1-\frac{\delta}{\sin\delta}\right)\ne0
\]

when `e>0,r!=0,0<|delta|<pi`. A regular common clock change multiplies the
whole vector field by one positive scalar, so it cannot identify these
fields, even after removing common offsets. For `e=0` on P2 only,
`F_s=sinc(delta)*F_n`: there is a restricted orbit equivalence. It is not
general on larger graphs; the preceding star's weighted-mean discriminator
also applies at `e=0` and forbids a positive common multiplier there.

#### A controlled near-alignment regime

Suppose all wrapped incident gaps satisfy `|delta_ij|<=epsilon<pi/2`.
Then `C_i>=d_i*cos(epsilon)` and `|alpha_i|<=epsilon`. Since
`H_i/pi=C_i*tan(alpha_i)/alpha_i>=C_i` with the removable value at zero,

\[
\cos\epsilon\le a_i\le1,\qquad
|g_i-s_i|\le\frac{(1-\cos\epsilon)\epsilon}{\pi},
\]
\[
|\dot\theta_i^{\,n}-\dot\theta_i^{\,s}|
 \le\frac{w\nu_i|q_i|}{\beta\pi d_i}(\sec\epsilon-1),\qquad
|\dot x_i^{\,n}-\dot x_i^{\,s}|
 \le\frac{w\nu_i}{\pi}(1-\cos\epsilon)\epsilon.
\]

These are instantaneous bounds at a shared state, not trajectory enclosures.
The phase discrepancy is `O(epsilon^2*|q_i|)`; the complete discrepancy
is cubic only when form contrasts are also `O(epsilon)`. A future error
estimate must additionally bound the field Lipschitz constant and preserve
the neighborhood throughout the comparison horizon. The common small-signal
regime is derived; the ratio does not supply an autonomous switching rule.

#### Composition exposes an independent premise

At a fixed root state let `P(A)` be the phase source of a nonempty finite
multiset of incident gaps `A`; equal entries mean different neighbors with
the same phase, not parallel edges. **Independent normalized composition**
would require, for every partition into disjoint incident groups,

\[
(|A|+|B|)P(A\sqcup B)=|A|P(A)+|B|P(B).
\]

Induction over singleton groups makes this condition equivalent to
`P(A)=sum_{delta in A} f(delta)/|A|` with `f(delta)=P({delta})`.
It is exactly the added superposition premise used in the preceding
classification, rather than a consequence of that classification.

Both candidates pass equal replication: duplicating every incident
coordinate multiplies `d,S,H,q` by the same factor and preserves their
complete nodal rates. That test is strictly weaker than independent
composition. For example, take `A=(0,0)`, `B=(t)` with `0<t<pi/2`.
Native source composition would require
`Arg(2+exp(i*t))=t/3`. Instead

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\left[\operatorname{Arg}(2+e^{it})-\frac t3\right]
 =\frac{2(\cos t-1)}{3(5+4\cos t)}<0.
\]

The merger defect is nonzero although all groups are regular. Sine sources
obey the merger identity exactly. The smooth collective counterfamily in
section 11.6 also preserves replication while generally violating merger.
Neither nodal locality nor replication establishes independent composition.

The [stationary mediator reduction](SINE_ENVIRONMENTAL_MEMORY.md#sine-mediator-common-geometry)
answers the associated scale question constructively: even a primitive
pairwise sine law induces collective visible interaction after eliminating
one node. It shares the native conditional minimum and inherited storage,
but transmits a different pressure. An emergent `Arg` coordinate is therefore
not a derivation of native `Arg` pressure. Finite hidden capacity retains a
tracking defect and memory; its controlled fast limit has explicit premises.

The [composition controls](../../tests/physics/test_relational_pressure_composition.py)
exercise merger, replication, the local discrepancy bounds and clock
non-equivalence using existing field owners. The
[detached mediation report](../../src/tnfr/physics/relational_sine_mediation.py)
integrates the justified reduction without selecting a new primitive law.
Together these results identify a common exchange geometry and a common
local regime, while leaving the deeper mobility-selection hypothesis open.

### 11.8 Closed form balance can select primitive pressure composition

<a id="closed-form-balance-and-source-selection"></a>

The composition premise has an equivalent whole-graph interpretation within
an explicit information class. This supplies another conditional selection
route; neither conservation nor that information class follows from the
nodal identity. Here a closed graph means that the entire fixed support is
included, with no external source or event. Degree-one vertices are allowed.

Keep finite connected simple unit support with at least two nodes, held
positive capacities, the support Laplacian `B=D-A` and `q=Bx` from section 1,
and the form row

\[
\dot x=N(-eD^{-1}Bx+wg),\qquad e\ge0,\quad w>0.
\]

Require one common phase-source family across supports,
`g_i=P_{d_i}(A_i)`, where `A_i` is the multiset of circular incident gaps
`theta_j-theta_i`. The source consumes only these gaps and their count. It
does not consume form, capacity, neighbor degrees, connections between
neighbors, graph identity, or hidden state. Equal gaps can represent distinct
neighbors. This is stronger than unspecified one-hop locality: replacing
the root's environment by a star must preserve its source. No phase row or
storage identity is needed for the next equivalence.

#### Diffusion fixes the weight of a possible linear form invariant

For `e>0`, suppose a nonzero state-independent vector `a`, allowed to depend
on the held graph and capacities, makes `a^T*x` invariant at every form and
phase state. Varying form at fixed phase removes the phase-only source and
gives

\[
a^TND^{-1}B=0,\qquad BD^{-1}Na=0.
\]

Connectedness gives `ker(B)=span{1}`, hence
`a_i=c*d_i/nu_i` for one nonzero constant `c`. Thus the only possible
nontrivial linear form invariant has, up to scale, the weights

\[
\mathcal Q=\sum_i\frac{d_i}{\nu_i}x_i,\qquad
W=\sum_i\frac{d_i}{\nu_i},\qquad M=\mathcal Q/W.
\]

This identifies the weight if such an invariant is required; it does not
establish its existence. For `e=0` this uniqueness argument is unavailable.
For the displayed weight and any `e>=0`, the exact accounting identity is

\[
\dot{\mathcal Q}=w\sum_i d_i g_i.
\]

The positive-capacity premise matters: these inverse-capacity quantities
are unavailable at a zero-capacity node.

#### Whole-graph balance is equivalent to an odd pairwise source

In the gap-only source class, require `sum_i d_i*g_i=0` for every phase
state on every finite connected support. It suffices to require this on
every star. Put `f(delta)=P_1({delta})`. On the single-edge graph the two
root gaps are `delta` and `-delta`, so balance gives
`f(delta)+f(-delta)=0`. On a star with root phase zero and arbitrary leaf
phases `delta_1,...,delta_d`, the root has source `P_d(A)` and each leaf
has source `f(-delta_j)`. Therefore

\[
0=dP_d(A)+\sum_{j=1}^d f(-\delta_j),\qquad
\boxed{P_d(A)=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^d f(\delta_j),\quad
       f(-\delta)=-f(\delta).}
\]

Conversely, summing this formula over any reciprocal graph cancels the
two orientations of each edge. Whole-graph balance is therefore equivalent
to normalized pairwise superposition with one odd circular kernel, and to
the preceding independent normalized-composition identity together with
singleton oddness. No continuity or differentiability is used. Requiring
the balance at one positive capacity assignment per star is enough because
the source is capacity-independent and the capacities cancel from the
accounting identity.

The support and information quantifiers cannot be discarded. Testing only
regular graphs permits `g_i=a_{d_i}*sum_j sin(delta_ij)/d_i` with arbitrary
degree-dependent gains. Consuming neighbor degrees instead permits

\[
g_i=\frac1{d_i}\sum_{j\sim i}(d_i+d_j)\sin\delta_{ij},
\]

which conserves the same weighted form on every graph by edge cancellation,
but has no common singleton kernel across supports. It lies outside the
stated gap-only information class. Ordinary locality, relabeling covariance
or equal replication does not supply the missing restriction.

#### Cosine exchange selects sine, with a separate phase-row premise

Combine this conservation premise with the global comparison domain and
storage assumptions of [section 11.6](#global-closure-pressure-comparison):
continuous circular pressure on the full phase torus, the retained storage
`E_D+beta*V_phi`, its exact loss `-e*q^T*N*D^-1*q`, and finite phase
velocities that freeze at zero own capacity. The complete law must admit
all nonnegative held capacity vectors; weighted form itself is used only
on the positive-capacity part of that domain.

Activate only a star's root capacity and choose `q_root!=0`. All other
phase rows freeze, so exact loss leaves

\[
\beta S_{\rm root}F_{\rm root}
 =w\nu_{\rm root}q_{\rm root}P_d(A).
\]

Whenever `sum_j sin(delta_j)=0`, finiteness forces
`sum_j f(delta_j)=0`. The balanced-star argument in section 11.6 then gives
`f(delta)=k*sin(delta)`; matching the native consensus tangent fixes
`k=1/pi`. Thus conservation plus the stated global exchange premises
selects the normalized-sine **pressure** without independently assuming
pairwise superposition. This reuses the existing classification rather
than deriving cosine storage or conservation from it.

Own-capacity separability is an additional requirement for selecting the
complete phase row. With that premise and continuity of the phase law,
capacity isolation at each node first forces the row where `S_i!=0`,
transfers it to arbitrary capacity vectors, and extends it by continuity
to `S_i=0`:

\[
g_i=\frac{S_i}{\pi d_i},\qquad
F_i=\frac{w\nu_iq_i}{\beta\pi d_i}.
\]

Without the extra phase-row premise, the pressure selection does not remove
all globally work-null phase velocities. The required conservation law,
cosine storage, prescribed loss, capacity behavior, full phase domain and
consensus normalization remain independent constitutive assumptions.

#### The combined premises also cover induced-neighborhood pressure

The sine-pressure conclusion survives a larger information class when both
conservation and exact cosine exchange are imposed. Allow pressure to consume
the phases and topology of the induced rooted ball `G[{i} union N(i)]`,
including edges between neighbors. Require one rule invariant under rooted
isomorphism and common phase rotation across all supports, independent of
form and capacity. External neighbor degrees, remote state and hidden
support-specific coefficients remain excluded. This does not extend the
conservation-alone equivalence to arbitrary local sources.

First isolate one active capacity in the global storage identity. For every
node, `S_i=0` forces `g_i=0`: choose `q_i!=0`, use finiteness of its phase
row and freezing of all other phase rows. This implication holds at every
capacity assignment because pressure is capacity-independent. On P2,
whole-graph balance again makes the common singleton kernel `f` odd.

Adapt the [induced-neighborhood completion](RELATIONAL_EXCHANGE_ADMISSION.md#primitive-locality-exchange-selection).
Retain an arbitrary root ball with all its internal edges and phases. For
each root neighbor `j` and every retained incidence `j-k`, attach one private
leaf to `j` with phase `2*theta_j-theta_k`. No new leaf touches the root, so
its entire source information is unchanged. Every retained neighbor now has
`S_j=0`, and hence `g_j=0`, because each old sine contribution has its
opposite at the reflected leaf. Unlike the native regular-domain completion,
no aligned padding is needed: the full phase torus admits zero or negative
resultants.

Assign strictly positive capacities throughout this completed graph before
using whole-graph form balance. A reflected leaf at `j-k` contributes
`f(theta_k-theta_j)`. Leaves associated with the two incidences of an edge
between root neighbors cancel by oddness. The remaining leaves correspond
to root edges and contribute `-sum_{j~i}f(theta_j-theta_i)`. Therefore

\[
d_i g_i=\sum_{j\sim i}f(\theta_j-\theta_i).
\]

The original root inherits this identity by uniform rooted locality. The
preceding balanced-star classification now selects normalized sine. No
locality or capacity separability of the phase row was used to select this
pressure; uniqueness of its complete phase row retains the earlier extra
premise. The proof never evaluates inverse-capacity form weights at zero
capacity. These completion graphs are mathematical comparison states, not
support events. The [locality controls](../../tests/physics/test_relational_locality_selection.py)
check the retained root ball and reflected-leaf cancellation separately
from the existing native-domain completion.

#### Branching tests information that paths and cycles cannot determine

Even conservation, composition, smoothness, the consensus tangent and exact
cosine-storage loss on every path and cycle do not select sine. For
`epsilon>0`, take the common odd kernel

\[
f_\epsilon(\delta)=\frac{\sin\delta+\epsilon\sin^3\delta}{\pi}.
\]

On graphs of maximum degree two, let `s_j=sin(delta_j)` at a root and use
the finite positive mobility

\[
m_1=\frac{1+\epsilon s_1^2}{\pi},\qquad
m_2=\frac{1+\epsilon(s_1^2-s_1s_2+s_2^2)}{2\pi}.
\]

The identity `s_1^3+s_2^3=(s_1+s_2)(s_1^2-s_1*s_2+s_2^2)` gives
`P_d=m_d*S`. Consequently `F_i=(w/beta)*nu_i*m_i*q_i` supplies a smooth
full-state exchange completion with the prescribed exact loss, zero-capacity
freezing and own-capacity separability on this restricted support class.
Its tangent is the normalized-sine tangent, but its nonlinear pressure
differs.

A degree-three star rejects that same kernel. Choose the acute leaf gaps
`(arcsin(1/4),arcsin(1/4),-pi/6)`. Their sine sum is zero while their sine
cubes sum to `-3/32`, giving

\[
P_3=-\frac{\epsilon}{32\pi}\ne0.
\]

With only the root capacity active and nonzero root form contrast, its
phase work is zero and its source work is nonzero. No finite phase row
with the stated freezing can supply the prescribed loss. The obstruction
occurs strictly inside an acute phase chart, not only at a zero resultant or
an antipodal boundary. Degree-three branching is therefore indispensable
to this countercontrol; further path or cycle trajectories cannot resolve
the same constitutive ambiguity.

<a id="phase-storage-selection-boundary"></a>

The fixed cosine-storage premise is essential to that obstruction. The
same cubic pressure has an all-degree completion if its phase potential is
changed explicitly to

\[
U_\epsilon(\delta)=1-\cos\delta+
\epsilon\left(\frac23-\cos\delta+\frac{\cos^3\delta}{3}\right),
\qquad \epsilon>0.
\]

For `c=cos(delta)` this is
`(1-c)+epsilon*(1-c)^2*(c+2)/3>=0`, with `U_epsilon(0)=0`.
Moreover `U_epsilon'=sin(delta)+epsilon*sin(delta)^3` and
`U_epsilon''(0)=1`. On any admitted finite support define

\[
\widetilde S_i=\sum_{j\sim i}U_\epsilon'(\theta_j-\theta_i),\qquad
g_i=\frac{\widetilde S_i}{\pi d_i},\qquad
F_i=\frac{w\nu_iq_i}{\beta\pi d_i},\qquad
E_\epsilon=E_D+\beta\sum_{\{i,j\}}U_\epsilon(\theta_j-\theta_i).
\]

Then `grad_theta E_epsilon=-beta*tilde S`, so the same constant
reciprocal Poisson tensor and form-diffusion block reproduce both rows,
and `dot E_epsilon=-e*q^T*N*D^-1*q`. Odd edge currents preserve weighted
form balance at positive held capacities. The pressure is smooth and
pairwise, and its complete consensus Jacobian equals the normalized-sine
one, although its nonlinear phase potential and pressure differ.

At the degree-three witness the original sine sum vanishes, but the
changed phase gradient does not; the exchange therefore has the missing
phase work. This countermodel shows why conservation, Jacobi, smoothness
and the consensus response still do not select sine without a phase-storage
premise. It changes that premise, rather than evading the fixed-cosine
selection theorem. Cosine-storage capture certificates and the earlier
native-pressure-derived storage classification cannot be transferred to
it without checking their changed hypotheses. It supplies no new default
law, runtime setting or physical storage identification.

#### Exact local agreement does not select global winding passage

The following complete-law comparison reuses that mechanism to test the

**An explicit smooth continuation.** Define

\[

\exp(1-1/y),&y>0,\end{cases}

\]

The function `exp(-1/y)` and every derivative tend to zero at `y=0+`.

<a id="first-phase-moment-sufficiency"></a>

#### What restricting primitive phase information selects

The alternative potential above leaves a precise information question: must
the primitive phase source retain the whole incident-gap multiset, or can it
discard everything except degree and the first complex circular moment?
This is an additional constitutive premise, not a consequence of the nodal
identity, reciprocity or the shared consensus tangent.

Let a bounded real kernel `j(delta)` be defined on the full phase circle;
ordinary continuity suffices for boundedness. For any finite nonempty list
of incident gaps, retain

\[
d=\#\{\delta_a\},\qquad Z_1=\sum_a e^{\mathrm i\delta_a},\qquad
P=\sum_a j(\delta_a),\qquad g=P/(\pi d).
\]

Here `j` is the unscaled edge current, so the preceding normalized edge
kernel is `j/pi`. Repeated phases represent distinct neighboring nodes;
simple stars realize these multisets without parallel edges. The information
premise says that equal `d,Z_1` imply equal `P` for **all** such lists.
Degree is retained separately; this is not a direction-only `Arg(Z_1)`
contract, and it does not add nonlocal data or future observations.

**Classification.** This premise holds if and only if

\[
\boxed{j(\delta)=c+a\cos\delta+b\sin\delta.}
\]

If the edge current is odd, it follows that `j(delta)=kappa*sin(delta)`.
Consequently `g=kappa*Im(Z_1)/(pi*d)`. The value of `kappa` still needs a
normalization or constitutive scale; no coefficient is fitted to a response.

**Elementary proof.** Every point `z` of the closed disk `|z|<=2` is the sum
of two unit phasors. Degree-two sufficiency makes their current sum a
well-defined function `F_2(z)`, including different antipodal decompositions
of zero. Comparing two degree-four lists gives

\[
F_2(z)+F_2(w)=F_2(z+w)+F_2(0)
\quad\text{when }z,w,z+w\text{ lie in the disk}.
\]

Thus `G(z)=F_2(z)-F_2(0)` is bounded and locally additive. If `|z|<=2/n`,
additivity yields `G(nz)=n*G(z)`; boundedness makes `G` continuous at zero.
Integer subdivision, rational homogeneity and continuity give
`G(z)=a*Re(z)+b*Im(z)` throughout the disk. Evaluating the repeated pair
at `z=2*exp(i*delta)` yields the displayed classification, with
`c=F_2(0)/2`. Conversely its sum is exactly `cd+a*Re(Z_1)+b*Im(Z_1)`.
Oddness eliminates the constant and cosine terms. No differentiability or
pressure dynamics is used in this information theorem.

If the premise is initially restricted to lists with nonzero `Z_1`, two
equal-zero lists can each be padded with one aligned entry. Their resultants
become one and their degrees remain equal; canceling the common contribution
proves the needed equality at zero. The same proof then applies. This
comparison does not assign a direction or a native pressure at zero resultant.
Degree two alone is insufficient to select sine. A nonzero resultant already
determines its unordered pair of unit phasors; zero leaves the antipodal pair
`(delta,delta+pi)` undetermined. Cancellation on every such pair requires
the additional half-turn condition `j(delta+pi)=-j(delta)`, not merely
oddness `j(-delta)=-j(delta)`. The non-sine cubic current above and
`sin(3*delta)` satisfy both conditions and therefore pass every degree-two
comparison. The classification also compares degree-four compositions;
its premise ranges over all finite lists, not just pairs.

For example, `j_2(delta)=sin(2*delta)/2` is odd, has unit consensus slope
and derives from the even nonnegative potential `U_2=sin(delta)^2/2`.
Nevertheless its sum on an antipodal pair is `sin(2*delta)`, which depends
on the unresolved orientation. Thus some odd currents can be distinguished
already at degree two. For a smooth odd periodic current, half-turn
antisymmetry removes the even sine harmonics; oddness alone does not. The
all-degree information premise then removes the remaining higher harmonics.
These are distinct restrictions on the supplied law, not interchangeable
consequences of reciprocity or an even phase potential.

**Storage consequence and independent premises.** When the current is the
derivative of a periodic potential, `j=U'`, integrating the odd result and
normalizing `U(0)=0` gives `U=kappa*(1-cos(delta))`. Nonnegative storage
requires `kappa>=0`; unit consensus curvature selects `kappa=1`. Alternatively,
applying the classification directly to an even additive potential whose sum
is determined by `d,Z_1` gives the same normalized shape. These are conditional
selection statements, not a derivation of first-moment sufficiency itself.

The two central premises are independent. Native argument pressure consumes
the first resultant but is not an additive sum of one common edge current;
the existing [composition boundary](#common-exchange-geometry-and-composition)
retains that distinction. Conversely the smooth cubic-sine current is
additive, odd and conservative under its supplied companion law, but consumes
information beyond the first moment. The next control exposes exactly that
discarded information, rather than merely comparing two unequal laws.

<a id="balanced-neighborhood-information-admission"></a>

#### Balanced geometry does not independently select the phase current

Consider the structural proposal that every finite phase neighborhood whose
unit vectors sum to zero must exert zero net phase current:

\[
\sum_a e^{\mathrm i\delta_a}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sum_a j(\delta_a)=0. \tag{BC}
\]

For a bounded additive odd kernel on the full phase circle, this proposal is
**equivalent** to first-moment sufficiency, not an independent deduction of
it. A two-member balanced list first gives
\(j(\delta+\pi)=-j(\delta)\). For any equal-resultant lists \(A,B\), append
the half-turn shift of \(B\) to \(A\). The combined resultant vanishes, so
(BC) gives \(\sum_A j=\sum_B j\). Applying the preceding classification and
oddness selects \(j(\delta)=\kappa\sin\delta\); conversely that current obeys
(BC). Without oddness, (BC) permits a cosine term as well. It forbids the
constant term which degree-aware first-moment sufficiency alone permits.

Thus calling the first phase resultant a geometric balance does not prove
that it contains all the information used by pressure. The smooth cubic
alternative above supplies an exact control. On the rotated balanced triad
\(\delta_a=\pi/6+2\pi a/3,\ a=0,1,2\), both the resultant and sine sum vanish,
but \(\sum_a\sin^3\delta_a=-3/4\). Its normalized phase pressure is therefore
\(-\epsilon/(4\pi)\). Both complete smooth laws admit this configuration.
The native argument-pressure law does not: its zero-resultant direction is
undefined. This test does not extend that native domain by convention.

Checking only pairs and balanced triads is insufficient. For any \(\eta>0\),

\[
j_\eta(\delta)=\frac{\sin\delta+\eta\sin(5\delta)}{1+5\eta},
\qquad
U_\eta(\delta)=
\frac{1-\cos\delta+(\eta/5)(1-\cos(5\delta))}{1+5\eta}
\]

is odd, has unit consensus slope, and derives from a nonnegative even
potential. It cancels on every antipodal pair and every rotated balanced
unit triad, but not on a regular pentagon rotated by \(\pi/10\): its current
sum there is \(5\eta/(1+5\eta)>0\). A balanced unit triad is necessarily an
equilateral phase arrangement; the fifth harmonic survives its symmetry.
These are phase-space configurations, not physical spatial polyhedra.
This counterexample is an algebraic admission control, not a new engine law.

The premise audit consequently closes with a **nonselection result**: (BC)
is a meaningful candidate constitutive restriction, but its unrestricted
version restates the information choice and its pair/triad version leaves
other smooth conservative potentials. None of the nodal identity,
reciprocity or existing persistence results establishes (BC). An independent
structural principle or observation is still required.

#### Phase information must retain its pairing with motion

The [existing resultant chain rule](RELATIONAL_DOMAIN_AND_CAPTURE.md#regular-domain-continuation-and-boundary-access)
already differentiates the first moment. Its higher-harmonic version connects
the preceding static source comparison to response and work without adding a
primitive coordinate or changing a law. On fixed support, let each relative
gap have a declared angular rate `r_a=dot(delta_a)` in one specified clock, and
define

\[
Z_m=\sum_a e^{\mathrm i m\delta_a},\qquad
M_m=\sum_a r_a e^{\mathrm i m\delta_a},\qquad
\boxed{\dot Z_m=\mathrm i m M_m.}
\]

`B=epsilon/4`. Its source and incident potential are
`pi*d*g=A*Im(Z_1)-B*Im(Z_3)` and
`V_inc=d*(1+2*epsilon/3)-A*Re(Z_1)+(B/3)*Re(Z_3)`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\pi\dot g=
\frac{A\operatorname{Re}M_1-3B\operatorname{Re}M_3}{d},
\qquad
\dot V_{\rm inc}=A\operatorname{Im}M_1-B\operatorname{Im}M_3.}
\]

These identities assume fixed coefficients and support. The potential rate
counts root incidences once; it is not a whole-network storage balance.
Phase rates are supplied with their provenance or derived from the complete
chosen law, never reconstructed from the source response being predicted.

**A complete-law pairing control.** On the three-node star `0--1,0--2`, use
unit capacity, `e=0,w=beta=1` and the conservative sine law in `tau=t/pi`.
Keep the labeled relative phasors fixed at `(3/5,4/5)` and `(4/5,3/5)` and
compare

and the respective relative-rate lists `(1,0)` and `(0,1)`, where `B` is
the unnormalized graph Laplacian and primes here use `tau`. Both states
have the same labeled phases, hence every unweighted circular moment, the
same form and rate multisets, the same degree-weighted form mean `1/8`
and the same full storage `73/80`. Nevertheless

\[
x'_0(A)=x'_0(B)=7/10,\qquad
x''_0(A)=3/10,\quad x''_0(B)=2/5,
\]
\[
V'_{\rm inc}(A)=4/5,\qquad V'_{\rm inc}(B)=3/5.
\]

Thus even all phase moments together with a separate rate multiset discard
information needed for the next response: which motion belongs to which
phase. The example changes that association, not the pressure law. The
different incident work is compatible with conservation of total storage.

The shared

returns `PhaseMomentMotion` from exact unit phasors and declared gap rates,

by this sine or cubic derivative.

Retaining `M_m` still does not establish an autonomous reduction. Where the
rates are differentiable,

\[
\dot M_m=\sum_a\dot r_a e^{\mathrm i m\delta_a}
 +\mathrm i m\sum_a r_a^2 e^{\mathrm i m\delta_a}.
\]

Accelerations and further phase/rate correlations therefore enter unless an

the same information-retention issue addressed by the existing complete-law

There is a scoped sufficient-state exception, already connected to the

#### Existing acute-star observations separate the premises

Reuse the preceding composition control's incident-gap preparation: a star

More generally, at uniform form and arbitrary positive held capacities,

\[
\dot M_{\rm native}
 =\frac{w}{\pi W}\left[

### 11.9 Form balance, common origins and the primitive information contract

<a id="form-balance-common-origin"></a>

The conservation premise in section 11.8 has different content on a relative
state and on a primitive nodal law. Fix a connected simple unit graph with at
least two nodes, held strictly positive capacities, the original clock and
an unforced complete field

\[
F=\dot x=Np,\qquad p=-eD^{-1}Bx+wg(\theta),\qquad
\dot\theta=H(x,\theta).
\]

Assume both rows are invariant under `x -> x+s*1` for constant real `s`.
The native and sine exchange rows have this property on their respective
phase domains. Use their local existence/uniqueness domains; no global
continuation through a native phase singularity is assumed. Put

\[
a_i=d_i/\nu_i,\quad W=a^T\mathbf1,\quad Q=a^Tx,\quad
m=Q/W,\quad P=I-\mathbf1a^T/W,\quad
\sigma(\theta)=\sum_i d_i g_i(\theta),\quad
h=\dot Q/W=w\sigma/W.
\]

The weights are held, so `P^2=P`, `P*1=0` and `a^T*P=0`.
Changing capacities, support, clock or inputs requires the separate
[continuous/event accounting](FORCED_GEOMETRIC_IDENTITY.md#prescribed-capacity-and-event-mean-balance).

#### Exact relative equivalence and its retained reconstruction coordinate

The unique correction along the common form direction that makes this Q
constant while preserving the phase row is

\[
\widehat F=F-h\mathbf1=PF,\qquad \widehat H=H.
\]

It satisfies `a^T*Fhat=0`. Every form difference has the same derivative,
`Fhat_i-Fhat_j=F_i-F_j`; both full fields induce the same phase derivative.
Because both rows depend only on the form quotient, this is a trajectory
equivalence on the quotient, not just an instantaneous identity. With the
same initial complete state, throughout their common existence interval,

\[
\widehat x(t)=x(t)-c(t)\mathbf1,\qquad
\widehat\theta(t)=\theta(t),\qquad
c(0)=0,\quad \dot c=h(Px(t),\theta(t)).
\]

The notation for h allows the general shift-invariant case; the displayed
phase-only pressure makes h a function of phase and held capacities alone.
Every fixed state-observation map invariant under common form shifts has
the same history. Model-dependent pressure and raw-rate reports use different
maps for the two fields and need not agree. In particular, `grad_x E=Bx=q`
and `1^T*q=0`, so the original storage `E=E_D+beta*V_phi` and its total
exchange work are unchanged by this correction, including along the paired
trajectories. An exact loss identity already satisfied by the selected
original law is therefore preserved; the arbitrary phase row displayed above
does not establish it by itself. Nodal form-work contributions instead change
by `-h*q_i`, whose sum is zero. This statement
compares a law with its own projection, not native trajectories with sine
trajectories.

The complete coordinate description `(y,m)=(Px,Q/W)` instead has

\[
\dot y=PF(y,\theta),\qquad
\dot m=h(y,\theta),\qquad x=y+m\mathbf1.
\]

Dropping m loses the absolute common form origin. Keeping its initial value
but imposing `mdot=0` changes the full law whenever h is nonzero. A
trajectory-dependent moving frame must retain c and its reconstruction row;
it is not a proof of conservation of the original Q. A time-independent
invertible origin chart requires the separate derivative equation and
domain hypotheses of the
[asymptotic-origin construction](RELATIONAL_PHASE_MEMORY.md#asymptotic-origin-coordinate-scope).
Constant translation symmetry alone supplies neither that chart nor Q balance.

#### The balancing pressure consumes global state and capacity ratios

On positive capacities the corrected nodal pressure is

\[
\widehat p_i=p_i-\frac{w\sigma(\theta)}{\nu_i W}.
\]

Unless sigma vanishes identically on the admitted domain, this is generally
outside the primitive information class in section 11.8: it reads the whole
support's phase-source sum and the capacity ratios in `nu_i W`. Uniformly
rescaling all capacities cancels from this pressure correction, but changing
one remote capacity generally does not. Conservation can therefore be
enforced on a chosen relative model without deriving a primitive-local,
capacity-independent conserving law.

There is a stronger uniqueness restriction. Suppose two pressures p and
`p_tilde` in the same original form chart are independent of capacities.
If their nodal rows `N*p` and `N*p_tilde` give the same relative-form rates
at every state for every independently varied positive capacity vector, then
they are equal. Indeed, writing `r=p_tilde-p`, quotient equality requires
`nu_i*r_i=nu_j*r_j` for every pair. Vary `nu_i` alone while retaining all
other capacities and the same form/phase state: `r_i=0`, then `r_j=0`.
Thus no nontrivial common-direction freedom survives these primitive
premises across the capacity family. This is not a derivation of conservation
or capacity independence. It distinguishes those added premises from an
unobserved common offset at one fixed capacity assignment.

#### Zero-capacity rates and pressures have different limits

The correction need not diverge at a freezing boundary. For positive
capacities,

\[
\left|\widehat p_i-p_i\right|
 \le \frac{|w\sigma|}{d_i},\qquad
|h|\le |w\sigma|\frac{\nu_k}{d_k}\quad\text{for every }k.
\]

At fixed regular form/phase state, h tends to zero whenever any capacity
tends to zero. If the original full field is continuous there, the projected
full field extends continuously by that original field on each zero-capacity
face. Its form row still freezes at every zero-capacity node. This extension
does not define inverse-capacity Q on the face.

Pressure has a subtler boundary. If only `nu_i -> 0` while all other
capacities stay positive, its correction tends to `-w*sigma/d_i`; the
corrections at the other nodes vanish. If two capacities satisfy
`nu_i=r*epsilon`, `nu_j=epsilon`, with fixed `r>0` and every other capacity
positive, then

\[
\widehat p_i-p_i\longrightarrow-\frac{w\sigma}{d_i+r d_j},\qquad
\widehat p_j-p_j\longrightarrow-\frac{w\sigma}{d_i/r+d_j}.
\]

For nonzero sigma these limits depend on r. There is no continuous
single-valued pressure extension through that corner, although the full
velocity extension exists. The nodal product at zero capacity cannot resolve
that pressure ambiguity. These claims hold at retained regular states; they
do not control a simultaneous phase singularity or a changed capacity law.

#### Frozen static discriminators and existing consumers

The [origin controls](../../tests/physics/test_relational_form_origin.py)
fix a native acute `K1,3` preparation before evaluation: root-first phases
`(0,0,0,t)`, `t=atan(3/4)`, uniform zero form, capacities `(1,2,3,4)` and
`e=0,w=beta=1`, admitted through the regular-domain reader. A separate
shift/work control uses form `(0,1,-1,2)`, a common shift of 8 and
`e=w=1/2,beta=1`. There is no trajectory or parameter fit. Write
`alpha=atan(3/14)`. Since `sin(t)=3/5`, `cos(t)=4/5` and
`tan(3*alpha)=1737/2366<3/4` on the same acute branch,

\[
\sigma_{\rm native}=(3\alpha-t)/\pi<0,\qquad
\sigma_{\rm sine}=0.
\]

Projection conserves Q but preserves the native difference between the
nonaligned and an aligned leaf, `-4*w*t/pi`. Sine instead gives
`-4*w*sin(t)/pi`; hence the projection does not recover sine even after
removing the common origin. At this preparation an originally zero form
rate on an aligned leaf also becomes `-h!=0`, excluding a positive common
clock rescaling. All phase rates and the instantaneous storage loss vanish
because form is uniform; zero storage loss does not certify zero full motion.

A second fixed support has edges `0-1,0-2,0-3,3-4,4-5,4-6`, zero form,
unit capacities and phases `(0,0,t,0,0,0,u)`. Changing the remote `u=0` to
`u=t` preserves the complete induced root ball at node 0, but doubles the
nonzero sigma with `W=12` unchanged. The original root pressure stays the
same while its projected pressure changes. Holding `u=0` and changing only
the capacity at node 6 from 1 to 2 instead preserves sigma and changes
`W` from 12 to `23/2`. Exact symbolic identities
and the shared native reader are checked separately from binary64 rounding;
no support or evolution law is installed by these tests.

Existing [relative-state reports](../../src/tnfr/physics/relational_sine_pattern.py)
already retain moving-reference rates and reconstruction information.
[Recovery admission](../../src/tnfr/physics/relational_sine_recovery.py) refuses
absolute mean-slab membership from relative observations, while
[replica membership](../../src/tnfr/physics/relational_sine_scale.py) consumes
the absolute mean when its declared family requires it. These consumers
cannot silently inherit a quotient-only conservation convention.

Nor can two disconnected patterns be recentered independently before a
join: their bridge form gap contains the difference between their two
origins. One common translation preserves it; two independent translations
do not. The internal quotient descriptions must retain that relative offset
or explicitly leave contact observations unavailable. The same information
obligation holds for relative phase origins. A native attachment observer
does not transfer its complete law to a sine composition.

**Conclusion.** Common-origin symmetry permits a closed relative description
and a conserving lift when its extra information is admitted. It does not
derive conservation of the original primitive Q. Primitive locality and
capacity independence make that balance an additional restriction, not a
free relabeling. Together with section 11.8 this closes the equivalence
question without selecting a unique microscopic law, justifying cosine
storage, changing an executor or revising any retained response.
