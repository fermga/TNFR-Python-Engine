    Global pulse, emergence and resonance: a hypothesis audit¶

    This chapter audits primordial structural activity with local rhythms, and the hypothesis that a collective pulse and clock arise through their coordination. It distinguishes derived internal motion, emergent synchronization, resonant susceptibility, supplied excitation and a reciprocal source with its own state. None is promoted to a universal law or a physical identification by naming it.

    The research program owns the sole active question. The resonance owner and existing internal pulse retain their original assumptions and proofs.

    1. What would have to emerge?¶

    The intended hypothesis starts with basic activity and local rhythms. A global pulse, and a collective reading of time, would arise through coordination or synchronization of those local processes. It does not require an externally imposed universal beat or initially equal phases.

    This separates two scientific questions: whether local organized activity arises and persists, and whether interactions coordinate its local timing. The existing pulse theorem supports a conditional realization of the first; it does not by itself solve either generic acquisition or the second. The supplied-clock and source alternatives below are discriminators and counterexamples, not replacements for this local-first hypothesis.

    A primordial pulse could mean a common periodic state, a shared phase reference, a persistent capacity for internal motion, or a cause that excites otherwise quiet organizations. These are different hypotheses. Existence of one periodic solution does not select that solution, make its period universal, or make all constituents share its phase. An explicit oscillator postulate is a legitimate constitutive candidate, but must retain what was assumed and what then follows. The current sine law is itself conditional, not a uniquely selected fundamental law that excludes every alternative.

    The prepared replica pulse already derives persistent internal circulation from a complete autonomous law. On its supplied invariant preparation, with alpha=2*pi/5, it obeys

    \[ \dot u=-a\nu\cos\alpha\cos\delta\sin\delta,\qquad \dot\delta=b\nu u,\qquad H=u^2+\beta\cos\alpha\sin^2\delta. \]

    Here a=w/pi, b=w/(beta*pi), and H is conserved. For 0<m=H/(beta*cos(alpha))<1, the labeled period is

    \[ T=\frac{4K(m)}{\Omega_{\rm int}},\qquad \Omega_{\rm int}=\frac{w\nu\sqrt{\cos\alpha}}{\pi\sqrt\beta}. \]

    K(m) is the complete elliptic integral defined in that owner. The period depends on supplied geometry, coefficients and amplitude. The unordered pair state returns after T/2; the collective means remain fixed. This is a positive existence result for an internal pulse, not a proof of a unique global rhythm or its formation from arbitrary preparations.

    The complete pulse variation and small-amplitude splitting already show that a derived autonomous pulse can produce periodic transverse coefficients and parametric feedback. Sufficiently small nonzero members of that particular prepared waveform are transversely unstable. Meanwhile an open family can preserve the larger collective identity and active internal constituents, as proved in the joint persistence result. Neither synchronized periodicity nor convergence to that waveform follows for the surrounding family.

    The existing obstructions have precise domains:

    • An exact equilibrium of an autonomous smooth law stays there by uniqueness. Thus the present state space contains counterexamples to the assertion that every preparation must start pulsing.
    • On finite connected support with held nonnegative capacities and e>0, the closed unforced sine model approaches its equilibrium set. Its storage argument excludes nonstationary full-state recurrence; it does not select one final geometry or a universal relaxation rate.
    • At e=0 with strictly positive held capacities, the admitted finite invariant slabs preserve volume. For a closed invariant set, the basin converging from outside is confined to the recurrence-null exceptional set in that ambient volume. In particular a periodic orbit cannot acquire an attracting basin of positive ambient volume there. The capture boundary also excludes entry from outside into a two-sided invariant family.

    These statements do not exclude finite observational formation, persistent activity without a common period, different complete laws, retained open subsystems, or infinite-system hypotheses with different compactness premises. They do rule out using the current finite closed examples as a proof of universal pulse selection. Failure of that proof is not a general theorem that a primordial pulse cannot emerge from any TNFR realization.

    2. Specify what is observed to pulse¶

    The repository's coherence read-out is

    \[ C=\frac1{1+\operatorname{mean}|p_i|+ \operatorname{mean}|\dot x_i|}, \]

    in declared input scales and with admitted pressure/rate data. It is a selected diagnostic. It is not an independent state coordinate or a law for how coherence must evolve. Promoting an independently prescribed C(t) to a cause would require compatibility with the full state and its evolution, including the nondifferentiable absolute-value boundaries.

    Two exact controls prevent misleading identifications. At a crossing of the prepared pulse, delta=0,u!=0, every form rate and its corresponding unforced pressure vanish. The live read-out is therefore C=1, while delta_dot=b*nu*u!=0. Maximal C does not imply a stationary full state. Moreover, its ten phase phasors satisfy

    \[ \sum_{i=0}^4\sum_{\sigma=\pm1} e^{\mathrm i(\bar\Theta+2\pi i/5+\sigma\delta)} =2e^{\mathrm i\bar\Theta}\cos\delta \sum_{i=0}^4e^{2\pi\mathrm i i/5}=0. \]

    The normalized magnitude of this global phase sum is identically zero during the nonzero internal pulse. It is a separate phase-order observation, not the repository's C. Organized winding and internal circulation need not produce global phase alignment. A proposed global coherence pulse must name its retained state and observable instead of relying on that phrase alone.

    3. A common clock or origin can leave relative dynamics unchanged¶

    Fix the existing finite connected simple unit graph, positive held capacities, and the complete normalized-sine law

    \[ K=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_i/d_i),\quad q=Lx,\quad S_i=\sum_{j\sim i}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i), \]
    \[ \dot x=-eKq+aKS,\qquad \dot\theta=bKq, \qquad a=w/\pi=\beta b,\quad b=w/(\beta\pi). \]

    Its storage is E=x^T L x/2+beta*V(theta), where V=sum_edges[1-cos(theta_j-theta_i)]. Its exact balance is E_dot=-e*q^T K q. Both rows, all coefficients and the original clock belong to the model. Subsequent alternatives are explicitly changed models; none changes engine defaults.

    3.1. Common positive speed modulation¶

    Write the full autonomous vector field as F(z). For an independently specified positive continuous gamma(t), the alternative

    \[ \dot z=\gamma(t)F(z),\qquad \tau(t)=\int_0^t\gamma(s)\,ds \]

    has the exact solution z(t)=Phi_(tau(t))(z(0)) wherever defined. It follows the same state-space orbits, in the same orientation. It cannot release an invariant family, turn an equilibrium into motion, or create a new relative configuration along that orbit. Finite-time windows in the original clock can change. Infinite-time accessibility also requires that tau(t) tend to infinity; periodic positive gamma has that property.

    Every consumed row must be multiplied. In the conservative sector, changing only the common w scales both sine rows together. With positive fixed e, changing only w leaves the loss term unscaled and is generally a constitutive change. Inputs, output units and timing must also transform in a clock comparison. A nonconstant clock need not preserve a time-invariant laboratory frequency-response experiment, as the clock owner explains.

    A second-order calculation exposes a common false resonance mechanism. For a real mode with constants A,B>0,d>=0, suppose

    \[ \dot X=\gamma(-dX-A\Theta),\qquad \dot\Theta=\gamma BX. \]

    For differentiable positive gamma, differentiation of the actual second row gives

    \[ \boxed{\ddot\Theta+ \left(\gamma d-\frac{\dot\gamma}{\gamma}\right)\dot\Theta +\gamma^2AB\Theta=0.} \]

    Dropping -gamma_dot/gamma changes the model. At an equilibrium, a prescribed periodic clock produces tangent return exp(A_full*integral(gamma)). For a conservative stable mode its multipliers remain on the unit circle; a periodic clock alone does not create parametric growth of that mode. This says nothing about removing an instability already present in F.

    3.2. Common form and phase rates¶

    Add prescribed scalar rates f_x(t)*1 and f_theta(t)*1 to the respective full rows. Subtracting their time integrals from the corresponding common origins removes both additions exactly: L*1=0, and S depends only on phase differences. Relative forms, relative phases, joint pair distances and cycle windings follow their original dynamics. The added work is zero, since 1^T q=1^T S=0. Absolute-origin observations would require their own measurement convention; this result concerns the stated relative structure.

    Uniform pressure is different from a uniform form rate. Adding the same nodal pressure P(t) gives the rate nu_i*P(t). If capacities differ, this vector need not be a common origin. For example, on two nodes with nu_0!=nu_1, initially equal forms and equal phases acquire form-contrast rate (nu_0-nu_1)*P. The spatial vector actually entering the full equation, not the adjective "global", determines what can be excited.

    4. Intrinsic susceptibility, supplied excitation and observation¶

    For standard terminology, Georgi's The Physics of Waves, Chapter 2 distinguishes driving frequency, natural response and supplied work in a forced oscillator. The TNFR-model equations below are derived independently from their stated full law; that reference is not validation of this paradigm.

    An additive form-row port f*u(t), with fixed real spatial vector f, has work-conjugate output y=f^T q. Direct differentiation gives

    \[ \dot E=-e q^TKq+u(t)y(t). \]

    The input can transfer structural storage. Its amplitude, spatial pattern, phase and timing are preparation data. The autonomous law's susceptibility to it does not produce that input. Conversely a supplied pulse cannot excite an invariant modal sector to which its admitted input has no coupling, or make an output observe a mode that it annihilates.

    For an exact illustration, take phase consensus on regular support with K=kappa*I. Let L*v=lambda*v, lambda>0, and ||v||=1. Use a pure modal input f=f_m*v and perturbations delta x=X*v,delta theta=Theta*v. Define

    \[ A=a\kappa\lambda,\quad B=b\kappa\lambda,\quad d=e\kappa\lambda,\quad k=AB>0. \]

    The complete tangent rows and three outputs give

    \[ \dot X=-dX-A\Theta+f_m u,\qquad \dot\Theta=BX, \qquad y=f_m\lambda X, \]
    \[ D(s)=s^2+ds+k,\qquad \mathcal H_X=\frac{f_m s}{D(s)},\quad \mathcal H_\Theta=\frac{Bf_m}{D(s)},\quad \mathcal H_y=\frac{f_m^2\lambda s}{D(s)}. \]

    For e>0 these are stable quotient transfers. With f_m!=0, the form and work gains peak at omega^2=k for every d>0. Phase gain has a positive frequency maximum only if d^2<2k, at omega^2=k-d^2/2; otherwise its maximum is at zero. The pole condition d^2<4k is different again. These follow by differentiating the squared gains in omega^2, and agree with the existing modal theorem. A pulse can excite a pre-existing susceptibility; it need not create it.

    A uniform rate input has f_m=0 for every nonconstant Laplacian mode on this regular example.
