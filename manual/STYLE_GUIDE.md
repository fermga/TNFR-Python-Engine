## 3) Reader-level targeting

Primary audience: high-school readers with scientific curiosity.

Required readability choices:

- Short paragraphs (2–5 lines)
- One idea per paragraph
- Prefer concrete verbs and observable nouns
- Avoid unexplained jargon
- Define terms on first use in ordinary language

Forbidden readability failures:

- Dense abstraction blocks without examples
- Definition cascades (more than 3 new terms in one page)
- Long symbolic passages without plain-language paraphrase

Cognitive-load drafting target (an editorial heuristic, not a measured learning law):

- Per page target: 1 primary idea, 1 secondary idea, 0 unnecessary concepts.
- If a page introduces more than 2 genuinely new concepts, split or defer.
- High-school readability has priority over rhetorical density.

## 4) Chapter logic and continuity

Use this question-driven progression:

1. Show a phenomenon.
2. Raise a question.
3. Identify what the selected description explains and leaves unspecified.
4. Introduce a concept whose usefulness can be checked.
5. Open a new unresolved question.

Close with a motivated next question, usually under "¿Y ahora qué?".
Narrative continuity does not establish that TNFR is the only possible answer.

Note on visible structure:

- These functions are mandatory, but headings are optional.
- Prefer integrating them organically in prose when that improves narrative flow.

---

## 5) Concept introduction protocol

Before adding a concept, verify:

- The motivating question already exists in the reader's mind.
- Relevant simpler explanations and their actual scope have been considered.
- The concept has a declared purpose; unresolved questions remain visible.
- The concept prepares a specific next question.

If any condition fails, postpone concept introduction.

## 6) Narrative voice rules

Narrator role: scientist-explorer, never doctrinal authority.

Required voice moves:

- Ask before asserting.
- Build from observation to explanation.
- Treat intuitive mistakes as useful steps.
- Use confident but non-dogmatic phrasing.

Preferred language patterns:

- "Let us test this idea."
- "What would this predict in a river / flame / swarm?"
- "This explains X, but leaves Y unresolved."

Avoid:

- "Obviously..."
- "It is clear that..." (without evidence)
- "You must accept..."

Narrative cadence standard (mandatory):

- Chapters must follow this loop repeatedly: **observation → question → intuition → example → reflection → next question**.
- Do not open a chapter with recap language (e.g., “In the previous chapter…”). Open with scene or question pressure.
- Use smooth connective prose; avoid excessive fragmentation into isolated one-line paragraphs.
- Preserve reader momentum: each major section must end with a forward-pulling sentence.

Continuity with neighboring chapters:

- Chapter N must begin as a natural continuation of Chapter N-1’s closing question.
- Chapter N must end with one precise question that Chapter N+1 will answer.

Meta-phrasing moderation (from Chapter 4 onward):

- Reduce explicit scaffolding phrases such as “the central idea of this chapter” or “the question that opens the next chapter.”
- Prefer implicit narrative transitions when reader familiarity with the book rhythm is already established.
- Use explicit scaffolding only when cognitive load or conceptual novelty requires it.

Long-rhythm rule (book-level):

- Insert one interlude every five chapters.
- Interludes introduce no new concepts; they consolidate through visual maps, recaps, and guiding questions.
- Interludes function as cognitive recovery and integration checkpoints.

Chapter identity rule (mandatory):

- Each chapter must have one **protagonist phenomenon** (e.g., flame, melody, wave, swarm).
- Supporting examples are allowed, but the protagonist phenomenon anchors the chapter’s memory.
Part III framing rule (chapters 11–16):

- These chapters function as clue-gathering, not isolated object-lessons. Each one should surface a property or clue that a future unified language would need to have, rather than only answering a standalone question about one phenomenon.
- Prefer framing like "what property does this share with what we already found?" over a purely self-contained "what happens here?"
- Definitional sentences that name a new candidate property (e.g., "a esto lo llamamos resonancia...") may be isolated with horizontal rules and italics for typographic emphasis, echoing the chapter's core discovery.

Clue-tracking checklist (chapters 12–16, after the bridge section):

- Close the "¿Y ahora qué?" section with a minimal, unexplained checklist tallying the clues gathered so far toward the new language (✓ for topics introduced with stated scope, □ for topics still pending), under its own heading so the reader immediately understands what the list represents, e.g.:

  ```
  ## Lo que ya empezamos a reconocer

  ✓ Resonancia
  ✓ Fractalidad
  □ Relaciones en red
  □ …
  ```

- Keep the checklist brief, but include a scope qualifier when an unqualified label would overstate the evidence.
- Update it to reflect topics covered, not theories confirmed. An empty box should identify a real next question rather than promise a predetermined discovery.
Symbolic triad rule (mandatory):

- Each chapter must define and keep coherent a **protagonist triad**:
	1) protagonist phenomenon,
	2) protagonist illustration plan,
	3) protagonist sentence (memorable line).
- The triad must allow chapter recall by symbolic object (e.g., “the bicycle chapter”).
- If any of the three elements is weak or generic, revise before approval.

Entry variation rule (book-level):

- Keep the pedagogical architecture stable, but vary chapter openings to avoid structural monotony.
- Rotate opening modes across chapters: scene, question, micro-story, paradox, observation test.
- Do not repeat the same opening mode more than two chapters in a row.

## 7) Mathematics deployment rules

Mathematics is compression, not introduction.

For each equation, mandatory order:

1. Prior question (why this equation is needed)
2. Plain-language meaning of each term
3. Observable interpretation
4. One boundary condition (where not to over-extend)

Equation placement rule:

- Never place a foundational equation before reader expectation.
- If the equation feels abrupt during review, rewrite the runway.

Formatting:

- Inline math for short expressions
- Display math for structural equations
- Immediately follow display math with plain-language paraphrase

## 8) Intellectual honesty rules

Every chapter must include explicit honesty elements:

- What this chapter explains well
- What this chapter cannot explain yet
- Which claims remain open or tentative

Do not:

- Present open questions as solved
- Hide assumptions in endnotes
- Frame disagreement as ignorance

Neutrality-toward-alternatives rule (mandatory):

- When comparing the book's emerging language against existing frameworks, tools, or disciplines, never frame them as failed attempts or as competitors being eliminated.
- Frame each one as excellent at answering its own specific question — and show precisely which question that is — rather than as falling short of ours.
- State the book's question specifically without claiming novelty that has not been established. Existing results may already answer it; compare them fairly before motivating a TNFR contribution.

Many-named-items rule (chapters introducing several named items at once, e.g. operators, grammar rules):

- Never present a long list of named items as a flat catalog ("Item 1: definition. Item 2: definition. ...").
- Group items into a small number of families first (ideally reusing a grouping already established in earlier chapters), and make the families themselves the protagonist of the chapter, not the individual item count.
- Within each family, tell one continuous narrative using the chapter's single protagonist phenomenon, with item names appearing as brief in-line labels ("eso es X") rather than as separate headings.
- Reference tables or lists at the chapter's end should visually foreground the small number of families (as bold headers) with item names listed compactly underneath, not a flat grid that gives every item equal visual weight.
- The chapter's takeaway should be the grouping principle, not mastery of every individual name; memorization of specific items can happen naturally across later chapters.

## 9) Wonder design rules

Each chapter must include one authentic "moment of wonder".

Validation criteria:

- Uses an everyday phenomenon
- Produces a perception shift
- Changes subsequent reasoning in the chapter

Weak wonder (reject):

- Beautiful but irrelevant image
- Rhetorical flourish without explanatory consequence

Strong wonder (accept):

- Reader can no longer "unsee" the new structure

Memorable line rule (mandatory):

- Each chapter must contain **exactly one** high-impact, underlinable sentence.
- The sentence must summarize the chapter’s core insight in plain language.
- Do not add multiple competing “slogan” lines.

Heading minimalism policy:

- Avoid excessive meta-subtitles (e.g., "moment of wonder", "honesty box") in chapter text unless pedagogically necessary.
- Preserve those elements as narrative functions embedded in the chapter flow.

Sensory grounding rule (mandatory):

- If two abstract terms appear close together (e.g., continuity/structure/organization), immediately anchor with a concrete image from nature or daily life.

Silence rule (recommended, at least once per chapter):

- Include one deliberate pause after a major question (short paragraph break or single reflective line) before giving explanation.

Suggested drafting balance (not an empirical learning metric):

- 1 major wonder moment
- 3 surprising observations (scene-based)
- 5 reader-facing questions (explicit or implicit)
- 1 memorable line (and only one)

Use these counts as prompts; do not add redundant questions or manufacture observations to meet them.

Observation/Explanation/Reflection pacing target:

- 50% observation
- 30% explanation
- 20% reflection

These proportions are editorial prompts, not validated optimum values or pass/fail measurements.

Abstract-to-observable pairing rule (mandatory):

- For every abstract concept introduced, provide at least one observable phenomenon.
- If a paragraph introduces abstraction without observation anchor, revise before approval.

Experiment protocol (mandatory in chapter activities):

- Add a pre-observation prediction step: **What do you expect to observe before looking?**
- Add a post-observation contrast step: **What did you actually observe?**
- Require one sentence on mismatch: **What changed in your explanation after observing?**

## 10) Visual-text integration rules

All major figures must answer three questions:

1. What chapter question does this figure answer?
2. What misconception does it correct?
3. What next concept does it prepare?

Caption structure:

- One-line claim
- One-line interpretation
- Optional one-line bridge to next section

Chapter opening field-note card (recommended standard):

- Use a short "field notebook" card at chapter opening with:
	- Observation of the day
	- Place
	- Phenomenon
	- Suggested observation time
	- Guiding question

Important production note:

- The opening observation plate is required at planning level for every chapter.
- Image execution can be deferred; during writing stage, keep it as a text specification only.

## 11) Typography and layout rules (author-facing)

- Body text: serif-oriented manuscript style
- Auxiliary elements: restrained sans-serif
- Handwritten style: margin notes only, never body paragraphs
- Keep generous whitespace between conceptual blocks
- Limit dense bullet clusters; alternate with prose

Page rhythm target:

- 1 dense conceptual spread followed by 1 breathing spread

---

## 12) Diagram and figure style rules

- Prefer hand-drawn appearance and line hierarchy
- Avoid sterile UI-like diagram aesthetics
- Use grayscale-safe contrasts
- Keep labels legible when printed in reduced size

Forbidden visual shortcuts:

- 3D renders as conceptual core visuals
- Overuse of color coding as sole information channel

## 13) Chapter-level quality gate rubric

Score 0–2 per criterion:

- Question-seeded concept introduction
- Standalone essay quality
- Intuition-before-formalism discipline
- Wonder authenticity
- Intellectual honesty clarity
- Clear motivation for the next question

Additional pass/fail checks:

- Narrative cadence loop is present at least twice.
- Chapter opens with observation/question, not recap.
- Exactly one memorable line is clearly identifiable.
- At least one deliberate reflective pause exists.
- Abstract claims are consistently tied to concrete scenes.
- The suggested observation/question balance serves the argument without padding.
- Observation, explanation and reflection support the argument; the suggested 50/30/20 split is not a pass/fail count.
- One protagonist phenomenon clearly anchors the chapter.
- Chapter includes one final takeaway sentence ("La idea que nos llevamos").

Decision:

- 10–12: ready for further editorial review; scientific and reader checks remain separate
- 7–9: revise
- 0–6: redesign

## 14) Editorial workflow

Mandatory production sequence:

1. Use `CHAPTER_TEMPLATE.md`
2. Draft chapter v1
3. Run quality gate rubric
4. Revise for math runway and wonder quality
5. Visual pass against `VISUAL_PHILOSOPHY.md`
6. Final editorial sign-off
7. External reader pilot checkpoint (when scheduled by phase policy)
8. Chapter lock decision and handoff to layout

Ruthless editor test (mandatory before sign-off):

- Ask: **If I remove one third of this chapter, does the main idea remain equally clear?**
- If yes, simplify further (there is still removable redundancy).
- If no, retain the necessary material and continue normal review; this heuristic does not prove an optimal chapter length.

Execution-phase priority rule:

- Once architecture, index, and style system are validated, do not reopen foundational design debates by default.
- Primary bottleneck becomes **quality consistency across full book scale** (26+ chapters).
- Optimize for repeatable excellence, not perpetual reinvention.

## 15) External reader pilot protocol

Purpose:

- Collect qualitative feedback on comprehension and reading experience.
- This small editorial sample does not establish general educational efficacy.

Initial checkpoint:

- Evaluate Chapters 1–4 before claiming reader readiness. All 26 chapters already
  exist as drafts; the blank response form does not establish a completed pilot.

Proposed rolling checkpoint:

- Run one pilot after each 5-chapter block (including interludes).

Pilot participants (minimum):

1. One 16-year-old student
2. One high-school teacher
3. One non-specialist adult reader

Ask exactly these five questions:

1. What idea do you remember most?
2. Did your way of thinking change? If so, at what moment; if not, say so.
3. Was any part heavy or hard to follow?
4. Do you want to keep reading?
5. What do you think will happen next?

Pass criteria:

- At least 2/3 readers state they want to continue.
- All readers can name at least one retained core idea.
- At least 2/3 can predict the next conceptual step in plausible terms.
- Any "heavy" section reported by 2 or more readers triggers revision.

Evidence requirement:

- Record raw responses with the manuscript revision before claiming a pilot outcome.
- Use [the response form](READER_PILOT_CH01_CH04.md); do not duplicate this protocol there.
- Factual corrections can proceed while a reader pilot remains pending.

---

## 16) Chapter freeze rule (mandatory)

- A layout lock requires a recorded editorial decision, factual review and the
  applicable reader feedback. A rubric score alone does not confer a lock.
- After lock, only accept edits for:
	- factual errors,
	- continuity breaks with adjacent chapters,
	- critical readability issues confirmed by pilot readers.
- Do not reopen stylistic micro-optimizations after lock.

Rationale:

- Prevent infinite polishing loops and ensure full-book completion.

---

## 17) Non-negotiable fail conditions

A chapter is blocked from publication if any applies:

- Introduces formalism before conceptual need
- Lacks a genuine moment of wonder
- Has no motivated transition to the next question
- Cannot stand alone as a coherent scientific essay
- Violates intellectual honesty requirements

---

## 18) Final instruction to authors

Write as if the reader is discovering with you, not receiving doctrine from you.
When in doubt, return to observation, then rebuild the concept.
